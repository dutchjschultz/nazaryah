## The Old Path

At Sinai, Yahuah called the whole nation, not one class, "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). When Joshua wanted two men stopped from prophesying in the camp, Moses answered, "would God that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Peter gave the same words to every believer: "a royal priesthood, an holy nation" (1 Peter 2:9). John wrote that Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). Yahushua forbade the titles that lift one man over the rest: "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah; and all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). Elders and overseers still serve, teach, and watch over the flock, but as brothers among brothers, never as a priesthood standing between the people and Yahuah. "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). There is no second.

## What Judaism Teaches

When the Temple fell in 70 AD, the Levitical priests lost their altar. Jewish leadership passed to the rabbis, the teachers of the law. Judaism today teaches that along with the written Torah, Moses received an Oral Torah at Sinai, passed down by word of mouth and later written in the Mishnah and Talmud. The rabbis are its keepers, and their rulings govern daily Jewish life: what may be eaten, how the Sabbath is kept, how prayers are said. A rabbi is ordained by other rabbis, and Orthodox communities bring their questions of law to him. The families of the *kohanim*, the priests descended from Aaron, are still honored and still give the priestly blessing in the synagogue, but they hold no office over the people.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the gathering of the people around the Word. The synagogue is a house of reading and prayer, not of sacrifice, and any Jewish man may read from the Torah before the congregation. Every Jew prays to Yahuah directly. Judaism also kept the call to teach the Law at home: fathers are to teach the words "diligently unto thy children" (Deuteronomy 6:7). And it remembered that a priesthood was given at Sinai and has not forgotten the line of Aaron.

## Where Judaism Falls

**The rabbi is the very title Yahushua forbade.** *Rabbi* means "my great one," "my master." Yahushua spoke against the scribes and Pharisees who loved to be called "Rabbi, Rabbi," and said, "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master" (Matthew 23:7-8). The office Judaism built after the Temple fell rests on the exact title Yahushua warned against forty years earlier.

**The oral law stands over the written.** Yahushua told the Pharisees, "Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition," and that they were "making the word of God of none effect through your tradition" (Mark 7:9, 13). When the people must ask a rabbi how to keep the Law, and the rabbi answers from a tradition said to have been given beside the Torah, the Law has a gatekeeper. Yahuah said His word was "very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). It needs no oral key.

**It refuses the High Priest Yahuah appointed.** David wrote of a priest "for ever after the order of Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4). Hebrews shows that Yahushua is that priest, made "not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life" (Hebrews 7:16). Since the Temple fell, Judaism has had no altar, no blood, and no high priest. Yahuah had already provided all three in His Son, and Judaism still waits without them.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the Word at the center of its gatherings, but it set up rabbis over the people and an oral law over the written one, under the very title Yahushua forbade. It lost the Temple priesthood and would not receive the High Priest after Melchizedek. The kingdom of priests is not a nation ruled by teachers; it is a people who come near through the blood and keep the Law themselves.

Revelation shows Yahuah living among His people with no go-between at all: "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God" (Revelation 21:3). No seat of Moses, no rabbi, no oral key. Yahuah Himself with His people.
