## The Old Path

Yahuah promised, "then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent" (Zephaniah 3:9). The Hebrew is *safah berurah*, a pure lip. At Babel Yahuah confounded the lip so that men "may not understand one another's speech" (Genesis 11:7); the pure lip undoes Babel. When the Spirit came upon the seventy elders they prophesied, and Moses wished "that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Joel promised the same Spirit on sons, daughters, servants, and handmaids (Joel 2:28-29). At Shavuot that promise came, and every man asked, "how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?" (Acts 2:8). Yahuah "is not the author of confusion" (1 Corinthians 14:33). The gift He promised is a language understood, used to call on His Name. Every religion's answer is measured against that promise.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism's oldest scriptures, the Vedas, are in Sanskrit, and Hindus have long called Sanskrit the language of the gods. Its sacred verses, called *mantras*, are believed to have power in their very sound. For that reason they must be pronounced exactly, and for centuries priests memorized the Vedas syllable by syllable and passed them down by mouth without change.

The best-known sacred sound is *Om*, a single syllable chanted at the start of prayers and meditation and held to contain the whole of reality. Mantras are repeated hundreds or thousands of times, often on strings of beads, to please a god, to bring a blessing, to ward off harm, or to quiet the mind. At temples and in home ceremonies, priests chant Sanskrit verses that most of the worshippers present do not understand.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept one true thing: words matter, and sacred words should be kept exactly. The care its priests took to pass down the Vedas without changing a syllable is the kind of care Moses commanded for Yahuah's words: "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). Solomon said, "Every word of God is pure… Add thou not unto his words" (Proverbs 30:5-6). Hinduism guarded its words carefully. They were simply the wrong words.

Beyond that, Hinduism kept nothing of the pure lip. Its sacred speech calls on other gods and trusts in sound rather than meaning.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It treats sound as power.** A word repeated because its sound is thought to work on the world is a charm, and Torah forbids it. Moses listed among the abominations of the nations "a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits" (Deuteronomy 18:11). Isaiah warned against those "that peep, and that mutter," and asked, "should not a people seek unto their God?" (Isaiah 8:19). Yahuah's words are not sounds with power in them; they are His meaning spoken to men who can understand it.

**Its holy words call on other gods.** Zephaniah gave the reason for the pure lip: "that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah." Hindu mantras call on Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, the goddess, and many more. David refused even to "take up their names into my lips" (Psalm 16:4), and Torah says of other gods, "neither let it be heard out of thy mouth" (Exodus 23:13). The lip that chants their names is the opposite of a pure lip.

**It does not ask to be understood.** In a Hindu ceremony the worshipper need not follow the words, because the sound is thought to be enough. Paul said the opposite: "except ye utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how shall it be known what is spoken? for ye shall speak into the air" (1 Corinthians 14:9). Yahushua warned against "vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking" (Matthew 6:7).

## The Verdict

Hinduism keeps sacred words with great care and uses them for the very things Yahuah forbade: charms, repeated sounds trusted for their power, and the names of other gods. Its holy speech is not meant to be understood, and it does not call on Yahuah. Zephaniah's pure lip is understood speech calling on the one Name. Hinduism offers sound without sense, spoken to gods that are not God.

Revelation sends one message to every tongue on earth: "the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people, saying with a loud voice, Fear God, and give glory to him" (Revelation 14:6-7). Every tongue is called to speak to the One who made them.
