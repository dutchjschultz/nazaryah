## The Old Path

Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That belief was credited at the door, and the credit was real. Two chapters later Yahuah told him, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (Genesis 17:1), and afterward He said Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Moses said the same to a people already brought "out of Egypt with a mighty hand" (Deuteronomy 6:21): "it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25). The psalmist said, "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), and Ezekiel said, "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20), upon him and not on another man's record. James joined the two: Abraham's belief "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Belief is credited first. Feet on the road follow.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism today teaches that a Jew lives righteously by keeping the commandments, counted as 613. It also teaches that an oral law was given at Sinai alongside the written Law, and that this oral law, later written down, tells how each commandment is to be kept. So a Jew's righteousness is measured by the commandments as tradition interprets them.

Since the Temple fell in 70 AD, Judaism has had no sacrifice. It teaches that repentance, prayer, and charity now take the place of the offerings. The Hebrew word for charity, *tzedakah*, is the same word as righteousness. Yom Kippur is kept each year as a day of fasting and repentance with no blood. Judaism does not accept that any one man's death can bear another's sin.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the verse. Deuteronomy 6:25 is its own Scripture, and it has never let go of it. It holds that righteousness is walked, not just believed, that Yahuah's commandments are good, and that the Sabbath and the feasts are to be kept. It agrees with the psalmist that "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172). On the walk, Judaism stands closer to Abraham's feet than the church that calls the commandments a burden.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It keeps the walk and refuses the door.** Moses spoke of righteousness to people who had already been passed over by the blood. Yahuah said, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13), and only after that did Israel walk out toward Sinai. Abraham was counted righteous before the covenant of circumcision (Romans 4:10). Judaism keeps the road and turns away the Lamb who opened it. Paul said this of his own people: "going about to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted themselves unto the righteousness of God" (Romans 10:3).

**It puts charity where the blood belongs.** Giving to the poor is commanded, and it is righteousness in the walk. It was never the atonement. Yahuah said, "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). When the Temple fell, Yahuah did not change the Law of atonement. He had already provided the Lamb whose blood the earthly altar pictured, and Judaism refused Him.

**It fences the Law with tradition.** Yahuah said, "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). Yahushua told the leaders of His day that they were "making the word of God of none effect through your tradition" (Mark 7:13). A righteousness measured by men's rulings on the commandments is no longer the righteousness of "all these commandments" as Yahuah gave them. The tradition even moved the Sabbath onto Saturday by a calculated calendar, so the very day is kept by the rulings of men and not by the lights Yahuah set.

## The Verdict

Judaism is right that righteousness is walked, and wrong about where the walk begins. It keeps Deuteronomy 6:25 and refuses Exodus 12:13. It sets charity and repentance in the place of the blood, and it walks the commandments through a fence of tradition. The feet are on the road, but the road has no gate.

Revelation joins the two things Judaism has pulled apart. Those who overcome "sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3). Moses and the Lamb, the walk and the blood, belong in one song.
