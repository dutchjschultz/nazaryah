## The Old Path

Yahuah charged the priests to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (Leviticus 10:10). In Leviticus 11 He named the beasts, fish, and birds Himself, so the line was never left to appetite or conscience. He gave the reason, "ye shall be holy; for I am holy," right after naming Himself "Yahuah that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 11:44-45). Deliverance came first, and holiness followed. Noah already knew the clean beasts from the unclean long before Sinai (Genesis 7:2), and Ezekiel condemned the priests who "put no difference" (Ezekiel 22:26). Yahuah told Abram, "walk before me, and be thou perfect," *tamim*, whole (Genesis 17:1), and Peter carried the same command to the believers: "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16). Holiness is not a feeling or a guess. It is the line Yahuah drew, walked by a people He has already delivered.

## What Hinduism Teaches

In Hinduism the cow is sacred. She is honored as a mother because she gives milk, and she is linked with the god Krishna, the cowherd. Most Hindus will not eat beef, and most states of India forbid killing cows. The cow's milk, butter, curds, dung, and urine are mixed together and used in rites of purification.

Many Hindus, especially among the Brahmin caste and the followers of Vishnu, eat no meat at all. This rests on *ahimsa*, the teaching of doing no harm to any living thing, and on karma, since killing a creature is thought to bring bad results in this life or the next. Some strict Hindus also avoid onions, garlic, and eggs. Many other Hindus eat chicken, goat, and fish. Hinduism also has a long-standing teaching of purity and pollution tied to caste, so that food cooked or touched by a person of a lower caste was held to defile a person of a higher one.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the idea that food is a holy matter. It knows there is a line between what may be eaten and what may not, and it knows that a person's diet is bound up with his standing before heaven. Hinduism also kept a care for animals that Scripture shares: "A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast" (Proverbs 12:10).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It serves the creature instead of the Creator.** The ox is one of the clean beasts. Yahuah named it first in His list of what Israel may eat: "the ox, the sheep, and the goat" (Deuteronomy 14:4). He accepted it on His altar. Hinduism took a clean animal Yahuah gave for food and offering and raised it to a goddess. Paul described exactly this: men who "worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator" (Romans 1:25). Service goes to Yahuah alone, not to any beast He made.

**It draws the line where Yahuah never drew it.** After the flood Yahuah gave Noah flesh for food: "Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you" (Genesis 9:3), given to a man who already knew clean from unclean (Genesis 7:2). Yahuah's line runs between clean and unclean, not between meat and no meat. Hinduism forbids what He allowed, while the onion and the egg are counted impure by men, not by the Law.

**It makes men unclean by birth.** The caste rules of purity treat whole classes of people as defiling. Peter learned the opposite in his vision: "God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean" (Acts 10:28). The vision was about men, not food. Yahuah's line runs through the animals, never through human birth.

**It seeks purity with no deliverance first.** Hinduism purifies with the products of a cow and with bathing, and hopes to escape bad karma. Yahuah's order runs the other way: He brought His people out first, and then said "ye shall therefore be holy" (Leviticus 11:45). A man is made clean by the blood of the Lamb Yahuah provided, and only then does the clean table become his walk.

## The Verdict

Hinduism knows that food and holiness belong together, but it drew the line by karma and caste instead of by the word of Yahuah. It made a goddess of a clean beast, forbade the meat Yahuah gave, and called men unclean whom Yahuah never called unclean. Its holiness is built on the creature, not on the Holy One.

Revelation calls every nation back to the One who made the cattle: "Fear God, and give glory to him… and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Revelation 14:7). The cow is not holy. Her Maker is.
