## The Old Path

At the burning bush Yahuah gave Moses His Name and bound it to time: "this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations" (Exodus 3:15). Moses later warned Israel to "fear this glorious and fearful name, Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 28:58). The psalmist sang, "Thy name, O Yahuah, endureth for ever" (Psalm 135:13), and Malachi saw a book of remembrance written for those who "thought upon his name" (Malachi 3:16). A title tells what someone is; a name tells who He is. Yahuah gave a Name to be remembered and spoken, and nothing in Scripture ever withdrew it. Every religion's answer is measured against that standing command.

## What Buddhism Teaches

The Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, taught in northern India about five centuries before Messiah. He taught no Creator. Questions about how the world began he set aside as questions that do not lead to release from suffering. The early Buddhist writings even tell of the god Brahma wrongly believing he had made the world.

Buddhism does speak of gods, called *devas*, but they are not creators. They are beings who live long, happy lives in higher realms and then die and are reborn like everyone else. They need the Buddha's teaching as much as any man. The goal of Buddhism is not to know God but to end craving and reach *nirvana*, the end of rebirth.

Where Buddhists do call on a name, it belongs to a Buddha. In Pure Land Buddhism, widely followed in China and Japan, believers repeat the name of Amitabha Buddha, in Japanese *Namu Amida Butsu*, trusting that he will bring them at death into his Pure Land.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept nothing of the Name, because it has no God to give one. The one thing it saw rightly is that the gods of India are not the Creator. It treats them as beings who are born and die. Scripture says the same of the gods of the nations: "The gods that have not made the heavens and the earth, even they shall perish from the earth" (Jeremiah 10:11). Buddhism looked at the gods of its neighbors and saw that they were not God. It never went on to find the One who is.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has no Creator, so it has no Name.** Scripture points every man to the sky: "Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things… he calleth them all by names" (Isaiah 40:26). The One who names the stars has a Name of His own, and He gave it. Paul said the Creator is "clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made," so that men "are without excuse" (Romans 1:20). Buddhism looked at the same creation and set the question of its Maker aside.

**It set aside the very question Moses asked.** Moses stood before the fire and asked first for the Name (Exodus 3:13). The Buddha treated the question of who made the world as a distraction. Yet that question is where deliverance begins. Israel's deliverance from Egypt came in the Name Moses asked for and received. A man who will not ask who made him will never learn who saves him.

**It calls on a name Scripture never knew.** The Pure Land believer is right that deliverance comes by calling on a name, but he calls on the wrong one. Joel wrote, "whosoever shall call on the name of Yahuah shall be delivered" (Joel 2:32). Solomon said, "The name of Yahuah is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe" (Proverbs 18:10). A name is only as strong as the one who carries it. Amitabha is a figure of Buddhist teaching, not the Maker of heaven and earth, and his name has no power to save.

## The Verdict

Buddhism has no Name because it has no God. It saw that the many gods of India were not the Creator, and then it stopped, leaving the Maker of the world unasked about and unnamed. Where it does call on a name for deliverance, it calls on one Scripture never gave. Yahuah's Name is the answer to the first question Buddhism refused to ask.

Revelation sends the everlasting gospel to every nation with one command: "Fear God, and give glory to him… and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Revelation 14:7). The call goes out to the Maker, the very One Buddhism set aside.
