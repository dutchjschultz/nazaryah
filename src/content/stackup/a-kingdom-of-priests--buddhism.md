## The Old Path

At Sinai, Yahuah called the whole nation, not one class, "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). When Joshua wanted two men stopped from prophesying in the camp, Moses answered, "would God that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Peter gave the same words to every believer: "a royal priesthood, an holy nation" (1 Peter 2:9). John wrote that Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). Yahushua forbade the titles that lift one man over the rest: "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah; and all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). Elders and overseers still serve, teach, and watch over the flock, but as brothers among brothers, never as a priesthood standing between the people and Yahuah. "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). There is no second.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism is built around the *Sangha*, the order of monks and nuns. Monks shave their heads, wear robes, keep a long code of rules, and live on what lay people give them. Lay people are taught to make merit by feeding the monks, giving to the temple, and supporting the order. The Sangha is often called a "field of merit," the best ground in which to plant good deeds. In Theravada lands such as Thailand, monks walk the streets at dawn and lay people kneel to put food in their bowls. Many young Thai men ordain as monks for a short time, often to make merit for their parents. Lay people bow to monks, and monks do not bow to lay people. In most Buddhist teaching, the full path to awakening is lived out in the robe, and the lay life is a lower step.

## What Buddhism Kept

The Buddha himself stood against the caste priesthood of the Brahmins. An early Buddhist text, the Vasala Sutta, teaches that a man is not made a Brahmin, or an outcast, by birth, but by what he does. The early Sangha took in men of every caste. In that, Buddhism saw that holiness does not run in a family's blood, and that is true. Peter learned the same: "God is no respecter of persons" (Acts 10:34).

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It traded a birth class for a robe class.** Buddhism tore down the Brahmin wall and built another. The people still kneel, and a set-apart order still receives their gifts and their bows. Yahushua gave no order of robed men. He said, "all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). The elders Paul appointed were to be the husband of one wife, ruling their own houses well (1 Timothy 3:2, 4), living among the people, not above them.

**Merit is bought through the monks.** The lay Buddhist hopes to improve his future by what he gives to the order. That makes the monk a kind of go-between, the channel through which the layman's good deeds count for more. Scripture knows only one Mediator (1 Timothy 2:5), and deliverance is not earned by gifts. Yahuah saves by His own favor through the blood, and only then does a man walk in good works (Ephesians 2:8-10).

**A priest needs a God to serve.** A priest is one who stands before God for the people and brings the people near. Buddhism teaches no Creator, so its order has no one to stand before. The Sangha can teach, beg, and meditate, but it cannot do what a priest is for. Israel's priests were set apart "that they may minister unto me in the priest's office" (Exodus 28:41). Without the One to minister to, there is no priesthood, only a class.

## The Verdict

Buddhism was right that holiness is not handed down by birth, and then it put a robe where the caste had been. Its people still kneel to a set-apart class and still hope to gain merit through them, before no God at all. The Old Path makes every redeemed man a priest, serving Yahuah directly through the one Mediator.

Revelation shows the only robe that makes a priest. The great multitude "have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb. Therefore are they before the throne of God, and serve him day and night in his temple" (Revelation 7:14-15). Not a saffron robe given by an order, but a white robe washed in the blood, and it is given to all who come.
