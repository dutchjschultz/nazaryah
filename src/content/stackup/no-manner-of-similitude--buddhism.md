## The Old Path

At Horeb, Israel "heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice" (Deuteronomy 4:12). Moses drew the command straight from that: take heed "lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure" (Deuteronomy 4:15-16). The second commandment forbids "any graven image, or any likeness," and forbids bowing down to them (Exodus 20:4-5). Even the brass serpent Yahuah Himself commanded (Numbers 21:8-9) became an idol, and Hezekiah broke it in pieces and called it "Nehushtan," a piece of brass (2 Kings 18:4). Isaiah asked, "To whom then will ye liken God?" (Isaiah 40:18), and Paul told Athens that the divine is not "like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device" (Acts 17:29). Yahuah gave His people a reminder that is no image at all: fringes on the garment, so that they would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39).

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism does not teach a Creator God, and most Buddhists say the Buddha was a man who found the way out of suffering, not a god. Yet statues of the Buddha fill every Buddhist land. They stand in temples, homes, shops, and roadside shrines, from small amulets worn on the neck to giant figures many stories high. Worshippers bow three times before the image, light candles and incense, and offer flowers, food, and gold leaf pressed onto the statue. Many lands hold an eye-opening or consecration rite before a new image is honored. Relics said to be the Buddha's bones or teeth are kept in shrines called stupas and visited by pilgrims. In Thailand the Emerald Buddha in Bangkok, the nation's most honored image, has its golden robes changed with each season in a royal ceremony.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism began without images. For its first few centuries, its art showed the Buddha only by signs: an empty seat, a footprint, a wheel, or the tree under which he sat. Statues of the Buddha himself appear only around the first century AD, in the regions of Gandhara and Mathura in what is now Pakistan and northern India. The earliest Buddhists, without the Law of Moses, still sensed that it was wrong to make the figure of the one they honored. That was closer to Horeb than anything their descendants built.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It bows to the image of a man.** The second commandment forbids the likeness of anything "in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath" and forbids bowing to it (Exodus 20:4-5). Buddhists who say the Buddha is only a man still kneel, light incense, and press gold onto his statue. Paul named this exact turn: men "changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man" (Romans 1:23).

**It honors bones and relics.** Moses' own brass serpent, a thing Yahuah commanded, was broken when Israel began to burn incense to it (2 Kings 18:4). How much more a tooth or a fragment of bone carried in procession and bowed to. Yahuah even hid the body of Moses so that no grave would become a shrine: "no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day" (Deuteronomy 34:6).

**It has images and no God.** A statue in Scripture is a counterfeit of the One who cannot be seen. Buddhism has the counterfeit without the One. It sets the figure of a teacher where the Creator should be, and so its people bow without knowing to whom service belongs. Paul preached to such people "the God that made the world and all things therein," who "dwelleth not in temples made with hands" (Acts 17:24).

## The Verdict

Buddhism started with an empty seat and filled it with a golden man. Its first followers would not carve the Buddha; their children carved him everywhere and bowed. A religion that denies a Creator ended up serving the work of its own hands, which is the oldest idolatry there is.

Revelation calls every nation back to the One the statues have replaced: "Fear God, and give glory to him… and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Revelation 14:7). Not a teacher who sat under a tree, but the One who made the tree.
