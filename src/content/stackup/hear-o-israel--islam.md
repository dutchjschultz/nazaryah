## The Question He Asked

At Caesarea Philippi, Yahushua asked His disciples, "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?" Peter answered, "Thou art the Messiah, the Son of the living God," and Yahushua said that answer came from "my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 16:13-17). Peter's answer has two parts: He is the Messiah, and He is the Son of the living God. Every religion's answer is measured against those two parts.

## What Islam Teaches

The Quran calls him Isa, son of Maryam. It says he was born of a virgin (Surah 3:45-47; 19:16-22) and gives him the title al-Masih, the Messiah (Surah 3:45). It says he spoke as a newborn in the cradle, healed the blind and the leper, and raised the dead by God's permission (Surah 3:49; 5:110). It calls him a word from God and a spirit from Him (Surah 4:171).

Then it takes away the rest. The Quran says he is only a servant and a messenger, and that God is far above having a son (Surah 4:171; 19:35). It says the Jews "killed him not, nor crucified him, but it was made to appear so unto them," and that God raised him up to Himself alive (Surah 4:157-158). Islam teaches that Isa will return before the Day of Judgment, but as a follower of Islam, and that Muhammad came after him as the last of the prophets (Surah 33:40).

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept more of the truth about the Messiah than any religion except Scripture itself. It holds that he was born of a virgin, that he is the Messiah, that he worked miracles by God's power and not his own, and that he will return. Mary was told the same: "The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee… therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

Islam also refuses to make him God. The Quran says, "the likeness of Isa with God is as the likeness of Adam: He created him of dust" (Surah 3:59). Paul calls Yahushua "the last Adam" (1 Corinthians 15:45), and Peter called him a "man approved of God… by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him" (Acts 2:22). The miracles were God's work through a man. On this point the Muslim stands closer to the apostles than the church that voted Him into a Trinity.

Islam even calls him a servant, and so does Scripture. Isaiah wrote, "Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth" (Isaiah 42:1), and Peter preached that "the God of Abraham… hath glorified his Son Yahushua" (Acts 3:13), using the Greek *pais*, a word meaning both child and servant.

## Where Islam Falls

**It denies the Son.** Scripture calls him servant and Son together. At the Jordan the Father spoke from heaven, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" (Matthew 3:17). Islam keeps the servant and throws away the Son. John answered that directly: "Whosoever denieth the Son, the same hath not the Father" (1 John 2:23).

**It denies the death.** Yahushua foretold His own death again and again: He "must go unto Jerusalem, and suffer many things… and be killed, and be raised again the third day" (Matthew 16:21). Paul said the first thing he delivered was "how that Messiah died for our sins according to the scriptures; and that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). A Messiah who never died could not have shed blood, and "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). Islam has a Messiah with no cross, no blood, and no resurrection.

**It makes him serve another prophet.** Islam says Isa foretold Muhammad (Surah 61:6) and will come back as a follower of Islam. Scripture says the opposite. Yahuah gave the Son "a name which is above every name" (Philippians 2:9), and at the end it is the Son, not another prophet, who comes in the glory of His Father (Matthew 16:27).

## The Verdict

Islam answered half of Peter's confession. It said "the Messiah" and refused "the Son of the living God." It honors him as a man and denies the one thing he came to do: to die as the Lamb Yahuah provided.

Revelation answers Islam in the Son's own words: "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore" (Revelation 1:18). He was dead. The One the Quran says never died is the One who "washed us from our sins in his own blood" (Revelation 1:5).
