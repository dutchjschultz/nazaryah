## The Old Path

Moses set two roads before Israel: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Joshua said the same: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). Yahuah takes "no pleasure in the death of him that dieth... wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9). A choice made can be unmade. "Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33), and the righteous man who turns away dies in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). The blood on the door opened the road out of Egypt (Exodus 12:13), but the people still had to walk it, and "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Yahuah declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), but knowing is not forcing. The choice was always the person's to make.

## What Christianity Teaches

John Calvin taught in the 1500s that God, before the world began, chose some people for eternal life and passed over the rest for damnation, and that this choice did not depend on anything in them. The Synod of Dort in 1618 and 1619 set his followers' teaching in five points, later summed up in English as TULIP: total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, and perseverance of the saints. The Westminster Confession of 1646 says that some are predestined to everlasting life and others foreordained to everlasting death.

Under this teaching, a person cannot choose God until God first chooses him, the one chosen cannot refuse, Jesus died only for the chosen, and the chosen cannot be lost. Many Baptists who reject the rest of Calvin still hold the last point as "once saved, always saved." Romans 9 is the passage most often quoted.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the truth that Yahuah begins the work. Yahushua said, "No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him" (John 6:44). Deliverance starts with Yahuah's call and His provided Lamb, not with a man's cleverness. The church also kept the truth that Yahuah knows the end from the beginning (Isaiah 46:10).

## Where Christianity Falls

**It settles the choice before birth.** Moses did not tell Israel that some were chosen for life and others for death. He set both before every one of them and said, "choose life." Yahuah said He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked and calls him to "turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). Paul said He "will have all men to be saved" (1 Timothy 2:4). A choice already made by someone else is no choice. Yahuah foreknows, but knowing is not forcing.

**It narrows the cross.** Limited atonement says the Son died only for the elect. John said He is the atoning sacrifice "for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world" (1 John 2:2). The door was opened for all. Not all walk through it.

**It says the chosen cannot fall.** Yahuah said He would blot out of His book whoever sins against Him (Exodus 32:33). Ezekiel said the righteous man who turns away will die in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). Yahushua said it is the one who endures "unto the end" who shall be saved (Matthew 24:13). Israel was passed over by the blood, and a whole generation still fell in the wilderness. The blood starts the journey. It does not walk it for anyone.

**It misreads Romans 9.** Paul quotes "Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated" (Romans 9:13). That line comes from Malachi 1:2-3, where it speaks of Edom's land laid waste, and it rests on Genesis 25:23: "Two nations are in thy womb." Paul was speaking of which line would carry the promise, not of which babies would burn.

## The Verdict

Calvin's predestination takes the choice Moses set before every person and hands it to a decree made before anyone was born. It narrows the Son's death to a few, and it promises the chosen that they cannot fall. Scripture says the opposite on every point. Yahuah calls, the blood opens the door, and the person must choose to walk through it and keep walking.

Revelation ends with an invitation, not a decree. "Whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17). Whosoever will. The last call in the Book is the same as the first one Moses gave: choose life.
