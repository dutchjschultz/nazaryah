## The Old Path

At the burning bush Yahuah gave Moses His Name and bound it to time: "this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations" (Exodus 3:15). Moses later warned Israel to "fear this glorious and fearful name, Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 28:58). The psalmist sang, "Thy name, O Yahuah, endureth for ever" (Psalm 135:13), and Malachi saw a book of remembrance written for those who "thought upon his name" (Malachi 3:16). A title tells what someone is; a name tells who He is. Yahuah gave a Name to be remembered and spoken, and nothing in Scripture ever withdrew it. Every religion's answer is measured against that standing command.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism has a great many gods, traditionally numbered in the millions, and each of the major gods carries many names. Hindus recite the *Vishnu Sahasranama*, a hymn of a thousand names of Vishnu, and similar thousand-name hymns are sung to Shiva and to the great goddess. Krishna alone is called by dozens of names in Hindu devotion.

Repeating a god's name, called *nama japa*, is one of the most common Hindu practices. A worshipper may run a string of 108 beads through his fingers, saying the name once on each bead, believing the repetition purifies the heart and moves the soul toward release from the cycle of rebirth.

Many Hindus also teach that behind all the gods stands *Brahman*, one ultimate reality without form, and that every god and every name is only one face of it. In that view a man may call on Vishnu, Shiva, or Durga, and all of it reaches the same end.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept very little of the Name. What it kept is the belief that names matter. No Hindu teacher would say, as the modern pulpit does, that it makes no difference what God is called. Hinduism holds that a name carries the one who is named, and that it is to be remembered, repeated, and honored. Scripture agrees that a name is to be remembered: "I will bless thy name for ever and ever" (Psalm 145:1). Hinduism has the habit of honoring a name. It has never had the Name.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It takes up the names of other gods.** Torah is plain: "make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth" (Exodus 23:13). David said of those who run after other gods, "nor take up their names into my lips" (Psalm 16:4). The practice Hinduism counts most holy, the long repetition of a god's name, is the very thing Yahuah forbade. When Israel came into the land, they were to destroy the images of the nations "and destroy the names of them out of that place" (Deuteronomy 12:3).

**It makes every name the same God.** The teaching that all names point to one reality sounds generous, but Yahuah refused it outright: "I am Yahuah: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images" (Isaiah 42:8). He does not answer to the names of other gods, and He said He would take the names of Baalim out of Israel's mouth so "they shall no more be remembered by their name" (Hosea 2:17). The gods of India are not other faces of the Creator. Jeremiah said what they are: "The gods that have not made the heavens and the earth, even they shall perish" (Jeremiah 10:11).

**It trusts in repetition.** Yahushua warned, "use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking" (Matthew 6:7). A name said ten thousand times on a string of beads is counted for nothing if the one named is not the living God. Malachi's book of remembrance was written for those who "thought upon his name" (Malachi 3:16), not for those who counted beads.

## The Verdict

Hinduism gathered thousands of names and never found the one that matters. It honors names as holy, but every name it honors belongs to a god Scripture forbids Israel to mention. Its kindest teaching, that all names lead to the same God, is the one Yahuah answered most directly: His glory He will not give to another.

Revelation sets the Name against every god of the nations. The saints who overcome sing, "who shall not fear thee… and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy" (Revelation 15:4). One Name, glorified because He alone is holy. Not one name among a thousand.
