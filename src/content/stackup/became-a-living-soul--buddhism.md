## The Old Path

Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Adam did not receive a soul; he became one, a *nephesh chayyah*, the same words Genesis uses for the "living creature" of the field (Genesis 1:24). The sentence on sin was "unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). Ezekiel says it twice: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20). When the breath goes, man "returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4), for "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Immortality is not something man has. It is something he must "seek for" (Romans 2:7) and will "put on" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53).

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism teaches *anatta*, no-self. There is no permanent soul hiding inside a person. What people call a self is a bundle of five parts that come together for a time: the body, feelings, perceptions, mental habits, and consciousness. When those parts come apart at death, there is no undying soul left over to go anywhere.

Yet Buddhism still teaches rebirth. The force of a person's deeds, his karma, carries on and sets off a new life, the way one candle lights another. Nothing permanent passes over, but the chain continues. The goal is to end the chain and reach nirvana, a word that means blowing out, as a flame is blown out.

## What Buddhism Kept

On this one point Buddhism stands closer to Genesis 2:7 than Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, or the church. It refuses the immortal soul. It sees that a man is not a ghost living in a body but a living whole that comes apart at death. Genesis says the same: dust plus breath made a living soul, and when the breath goes the dust returns to the earth (Genesis 3:19; Ecclesiastes 12:7). Solomon saw that in death man has "no preeminence above a beast" (Ecclesiastes 3:19). The Buddhist and the Preacher agree that nothing in man is undying by nature.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has a creature with no Creator.** Buddhism looks at the parts that make a man and never asks who put them together. Genesis begins there: Yahuah "formed" man and "breathed" into him (Genesis 2:7). The life in man is on loan from the One who gave it, and it "shall return unto God who gave it" (Ecclesiastes 12:7). A man made of parts with no Maker has no one to answer to and no one to hope in.

**It denies the soul and then keeps it.** Buddhism says no self passes from life to life, yet it sends karma on into a new birth that pays for what the old one did. That is a soul by another name. Scripture is consistent: the man who dies is the man who sinned, and nothing of him slips into another womb. "It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27).

**It aims at being blown out.** The Buddhist hope is that the flame goes out and the chain stops. Scripture's hope is the opposite. The dead in the dust are asleep, and they will "awake and sing" (Isaiah 26:19). Paul did not hope to vanish. He hoped that "this mortal must put on immortality" (1 Corinthians 15:53). Death is the enemy (1 Corinthians 15:26), not the doorway to peace.

**It has no one who conquered death.** Buddhism offers a path out of rebirth by a man's own effort. Scripture offers a resurrection by Yahuah's power, already proven. Yahuah raised His Son from the dead as "the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20). The Son did not reach nirvana. He came back to life in His body, ate fish before His disciples (Luke 24:42-43), and said, "a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have" (Luke 24:39).

## The Verdict

Buddhism got the soul half right. Man has no undying soul, and Genesis agrees. But Buddhism took away the soul and kept rebirth, took away the Creator and kept the cycle, and took away the resurrection and offered extinction. Scripture closes the gap Buddhism leaves open. The man who dies sleeps, and Yahuah, who formed him the first time, will raise him the second.

Revelation shows that waking. "The sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them" (Revelation 20:13). Then the One on the throne says, "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5). Not blown out. Made new.
