## The Old Path

Yahuah charged the priests to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (Leviticus 10:10). In Leviticus 11 He named the beasts, fish, and birds Himself, so the line was never left to appetite or conscience. He gave the reason, "ye shall be holy; for I am holy," right after naming Himself "Yahuah that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 11:44-45). Deliverance came first, and holiness followed. Noah already knew the clean beasts from the unclean long before Sinai (Genesis 7:2), and Ezekiel condemned the priests who "put no difference" (Ezekiel 22:26). Yahuah told Abram, "walk before me, and be thou perfect," *tamim*, whole (Genesis 17:1), and Peter carried the same command to the believers: "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16). Holiness is not a feeling or a guess. It is the line Yahuah drew, walked by a people He has already delivered.

## What Christianity Teaches

The church teaches that the food laws were for Israel only and ended at the cross. It points to three passages. In Mark 7, Jesus is said to have declared all foods clean; many modern Bibles add those very words to verse 19, though the King James reads only "purging all meats." In Acts 10, Peter's vision of a sheet full of unclean animals is taught as God lifting the ban on pork and shellfish. And in 1 Timothy 4:4, "every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused," is read to mean every animal is now food. The Roman church kept its own food rule instead, forbidding meat on Fridays in Lent, a law Yahuah never gave.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the word "holy." It reads Leviticus and 1 Peter and agrees that believers must be set apart. It kept the truth that deliverance comes first, through the blood. What it lost is the walk that follows.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It reads Mark 7 backward.** The question in Mark 7 was not pork. It was eating "with unwashen hands," a tradition of the elders (Mark 7:2-5). Yahushua answered that food passing through the belly does not defile the heart, and it goes out "purging all meats" (Mark 7:19), meaning the body clears out what is eaten. Nobody at that table was holding a plate of swine. The words "declared all foods clean" are not written out in the Greek. They are the translators' explanation of the one word the King James renders "purging," and they carry the church's view into a verse about handwashing.

**It reads Acts 10 against Peter's own words.** Peter, years after the cross, said, "I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean" (Acts 10:14). If Yahushua had made all food clean in Mark 7, Peter never heard about it. Then Peter told what the vision meant: "God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean" (Acts 10:28). The vision was about Gentile men, not about animals. Peter never ate the sheet.

**It cuts 1 Timothy 4 in half.** Paul warned against those "commanding to abstain from meats, which God hath created to be received" (1 Timothy 4:3), and said every creature of God is good, "for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer" (1 Timothy 4:5). The word of God that sanctifies a meat is Leviticus 11. The swine was never sanctified by it.

**It removes the line the priests were charged to teach.** Ezekiel condemned the priests who "put no difference between the holy and profane, neither have they shewed difference between the unclean and the clean" (Ezekiel 22:26). That is a description of the modern pulpit. Isaiah warned that when Yahuah comes with fire, those "eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be consumed together" (Isaiah 66:15-17). That is not a verse about Israel before the cross. It is about His coming.

## The Verdict

Christianity entered by the right door and then refused the walk. It kept the blood and threw out the bread, the commandments that the delivered are meant to live by. Its three proof texts, read in their own setting, are about washing hands, about Gentile men, and about meats the Word itself sanctifies. Not one of them makes the swine clean. The command still stands: "Be ye holy; for I am holy."

Revelation describes the fall of the great city that taught the nations to put no difference: "Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and is become… a cage of every unclean and hateful bird." Then comes the call: "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins" (Revelation 18:2, 4). The call to holiness is still being given.
