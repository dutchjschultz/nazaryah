## The Old Path

Yahuah has only ever had one people. At the first Pesach the Law said, "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger" (Exodus 12:49). The stranger who joins himself to Yahuah and keeps His Sabbaths is brought to His holy mountain, "for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:6-7). Paul pictures that one people as one olive tree. The wild branch is "graffed in" and partakes "of the root and fatness of the olive tree," and he is warned, "thou bearest not the root, but the root thee" (Romans 11:17-18). Gentiles in Messiah are no longer "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" but "fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:12, 19). The door into that tree is the Seed, "which is Messiah," and "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:16, 29). One tree, one root, one law, entered by belief and not by birth.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism has no single founder and no single people. It holds many gods and many paths. Its oldest social order is the varna system of four classes: Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers). A hymn in the Rig Veda pictures these four coming from the mouth, arms, thighs, and feet of a cosmic man. Below and outside the four stood the people once called "untouchables," now called Dalits.

A person's caste comes from birth, and Hindu teaching on karma explains that birth as the fruit of deeds in a past life. For centuries caste decided whom a man could marry, eat with, or touch. India's constitution of 1950 abolished untouchability, and many Hindus today reject caste, but it still shapes life across much of India.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept little of the olive tree. It does hold that a man belongs to a people larger than himself and owes duties within it, and that a holy order should mark how people live. Some of its own reformers and devotional poets stood against caste and taught that devotion was open to every class.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It ranks men by birth.** In Israel, Levi was set apart by birth to serve, but Levi received no land, and the stranger ate the same Pesach under one law (Exodus 12:48-49). Birth never made one Israelite cleaner than another. Now even that priesthood has given way to one "not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life" (Hebrews 7:16). Paul closed the matter: "there is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free... for ye are all one in Messiah Yahushua" (Galatians 3:28).

**It blames a man for a life he never lived.** Caste teaching says a low birth is the debt of a former life. The disciples asked something close to this about the man born blind: "Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents?" Yahushua answered, "Neither" (John 9:2-3). Yahuah said, "The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father" (Ezekiel 18:20), and Scripture knows nothing of earlier lives, for "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27).

**It makes some people untouchable.** Yahushua "put forth his hand, and touched" the leper (Matthew 8:3), the most untouchable man in Israel. Peter, raised to keep apart from Gentiles, learned at Cornelius's door that "I should not call any man common or unclean" (Acts 10:28). No birth puts a man beyond the reach of the tree.

**It has many trees and no root.** The olive tree has one root and draws from one God. Hinduism has countless gods, so it can have no single household. Joshua told Israel to choose between Yahuah and "the gods which your fathers served" (Joshua 24:15). There is one root, and branches are grafted into it, not sorted beneath it.

## The Verdict

Hinduism builds its people on birth, ranks them from mouth to feet, and explains the ranking by lives no one remembers. Scripture builds one people on one root, enters them by belief in the Seed, and sets them under one law with no class above another. Caste is not a branch of the olive tree. It is a different kind of forest.

Revelation calls every servant into the same praise with no rank between them: "Praise our God, all ye his servants, and ye that fear him, both small and great" (Revelation 19:5). Before that throne stand people "of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" (Revelation 7:9), and not one of them is sorted by the house he was born in.
