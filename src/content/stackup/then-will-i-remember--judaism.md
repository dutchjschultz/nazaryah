## The Old Path

Leviticus 26 is a long warning, and at the bottom of it Yahuah wrote the road home. If Israel would "confess their iniquity" and their "uncircumcised hearts be humbled," then "will I remember my covenant with Jacob" (Leviticus 26:40-42). The return was provided for before the curse ever fell. To repent is *shuv*, to turn around and walk the other way; to confess is *yadah*, to acknowledge openly, out loud. Joel cried, "turn ye even to me with all your heart… rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13), and Ezekiel gave the reason: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth… wherefore turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). One God receives the turn, and He Himself remembers. The turn was never the price. The price was blood Yahuah gave "upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls" (Leviticus 17:11). So Hebrews sets "repentance from dead works" first among the foundations (Hebrews 6:1).

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism calls repentance *teshuvah*, from the same root *shuv*. It is the center of the Jewish year. The month before Rosh Hashanah is spent in self-examination, and the ten Days of Awe run from Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur. On Yom Kippur the people fast and stand in the synagogue most of the day, reciting a confession together, "we have sinned," naming sin after sin aloud.

There is no altar. The temple fell in 70 AD, and with it every sacrifice. In place of the blood, rabbinic Judaism teaches that repentance, prayer, and charity turn aside the evil decree, and the High Holy Day prayer book says so in those words. Then the cycle begins again the next year.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept more of Leviticus 26 than any religion outside of Scripture. It kept the word itself, *shuv*. It kept confession spoken aloud, *yadah*, just as Leviticus commands. It kept the humbled heart: the fast of Yom Kippur is the very thing Torah ordered, "ye shall afflict your souls" (Leviticus 16:29).

It kept the One God. Every word of Leviticus 26 is singular, "I will remember," and the synagogue has never prayed to three. On that point Judaism stands with Moses against the creeds.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It kept the turn and lost the blood.** Leviticus 26 sits inside a Torah where the altar ran red every morning and every evening. Yahuah never gave prayer and charity "to make an atonement for your souls." He gave blood (Leviticus 17:11). When the temple fell, Judaism did not ask why Yahuah took the altar away. It wrote a substitute. But no prayer book can overrule Torah: "without shedding of blood is no remission" (Hebrews 9:22).

**It was told the altar would end, and why.** Daniel said Messiah would "be cut off, but not for himself," and that "in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease" (Daniel 9:26-27). Messiah was cut off at Pesach in 31 AD. Within forty years the temple was gone. The sacrifice did not cease because Rome won. It ceased because the Lamb Yahuah provided had been offered, and nothing was left to offer.

**It makes the turn yearly instead of once.** The old Yom Kippur was itself "a remembrance again made of sins every year" (Hebrews 10:3). Even that pointed forward to the High Priest who "by his own blood… entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption" (Hebrews 9:12). Judaism keeps the yearly remembrance with neither the goat nor the High Priest it pictured.

**It refuses the One it pierced.** Zechariah wrote that Yahuah would pour out "the spirit of grace and of supplications," and "they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him" (Zechariah 12:10). That is the deepest repentance in the prophets, and it is a turn toward the pierced One.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the shape of repentance almost perfectly: the turn, the open confession, the humbled soul, the One God who remembers. What it lost is the ground under it. A turn with no blood is a turn that must be made again every year, because it never reaches the place where sin is put away. The old path home ends at the Lamb Yahuah provided, and Judaism has walked it for two thousand years without arriving.

Revelation shows the day the turn of Zechariah 12 is finished: "Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him" (Revelation 1:7). The mourning Zechariah promised will come. Blessed are those who make it now.
