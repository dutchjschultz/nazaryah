## The Old Path

Revelation opens by saying its message was "signified" (Revelation 1:1), shown in signs, and nearly every sign was already written. The trumpets and vials repeat the plagues of Egypt: hail and fire, water turned to blood, darkness, locusts, sores, and frogs (Exodus 7-12). The sevenfold measure is the "seven times more" of the covenant curses (Leviticus 26:18). The beasts rise out of Daniel's sea (Daniel 7). John eats a little book that is "sweet as honey" in the mouth, as Ezekiel did (Ezekiel 3:3; Revelation 10:9), and is handed a measuring reed like the one Ezekiel saw (Ezekiel 40:3; Revelation 11:1). The two olive trees and the lampstands are Zechariah's (Zechariah 4; Revelation 11:4), and so are the colored horses (Zechariah 6:2-3; Revelation 6). Read against the Law and the Prophets, Revelation explains itself. Read as something brand new, it can be made to mean whatever each century wants.

## What Christianity Teaches

Much of modern Christianity, especially in America, reads Revelation as a coded forecast of the evening news. The Scofield Reference Bible of 1909 told its readers that Gog in Ezekiel 38, who returns in Revelation 20:8, meant Russia. Hal Lindsey's *The Late Great Planet Earth* (1970), one of the best-selling Christian books of its century, tied the prophecies to the Cold War and suggested the end would come within about forty years of Israel's founding in 1948. Edgar Whisenant published *88 Reasons Why the Rapture Will Be in 1988*. In 2013 John Hagee's *Four Blood Moons* pointed to the eclipses of 2014 and 2015.

In this way of reading, the locusts become attack helicopters and the mark of the beast becomes a barcode or a microchip. When the date passes, a new book takes the old one's place.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept Revelation in the Bible as true prophecy from Yahuah, given "to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass" (Revelation 1:1), and it kept the hope that the Son is returning. Many of its study Bibles still print in the margins the very cross-references to Exodus, Daniel, Ezekiel, and Zechariah that unlock the book. The keys are in the church's own hands. It simply stopped using them.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It reads the newspaper instead of the Book.** Every sign in Revelation was "signified," given as a sign drawn from earlier Scripture. When John sees hail and fire mingled with blood (Revelation 8:7), the reader is meant to remember the hail and fire of Egypt (Exodus 9:23-24). When the waters turn to blood (Revelation 8:8; 16:3), it is Moses' rod over the Nile (Exodus 7:20). When locusts come up out of the smoke (Revelation 9:3), it is the eighth plague (Exodus 10:12-15). The trumpets are Yahuah's war on Egypt fought again against spiritual Babylon all through the assembly age, which is why the saints sing "the song of Moses" (Revelation 15:3). A helicopter teaches none of that.

**It forgets who the letter was written to.** Revelation was sent to seven real assemblies in Asia (Revelation 1:4, 11), and John was told, "Seal not the sayings of the prophecy of this book: for the time is at hand" (Revelation 22:10). Daniel was told to seal his book (Daniel 12:4). John was told not to seal his. A book in code for twentieth-century readers would have been sealed to the very people it was sent to. They knew Moses and the Prophets, and that was all they needed.

**It lets Daniel's beasts mean anything.** The beast of Revelation 13 is "like unto a leopard," with the feet "of a bear" and the mouth "of a lion" (Revelation 13:2). Those are Daniel's three beasts rolled into one (Daniel 7:4-6), and Daniel says what his beasts are: "four kings, which shall arise out of the earth" (Daniel 7:17). Read with Daniel, the beast is the kingdoms of man, gathered into one power. Read with the newspaper, it is whichever nation the preacher fears that year.

**It keeps setting dates that fail.** Moses gave the test: "if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which Yahuah hath not spoken" (Deuteronomy 18:22). The Book never failed. The headline readers did.

## The Verdict

Revelation is not a puzzle about tomorrow's news. It is the Law and the Prophets gathered into one final picture, and it explains itself to anyone who knows the books it quotes. Christianity kept the Book and traded its key for the morning paper.

Revelation itself promises a blessing on the plain reading of its words aloud: "Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein" (Revelation 1:3). It closes with a warning against adding to it (Revelation 22:18), and a reading that fills it with headlines does exactly that.
