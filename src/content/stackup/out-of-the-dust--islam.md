## The Old Path

Man was formed "of the dust of the ground" and became "a living soul" (Genesis 2:7), and to dust he returns (Genesis 3:19). The One who formed him from the dust can call him out of it. In the Song of Moses, before Israel ever entered the land, Yahuah said, "I kill, and I make alive," and in the same verse, "there is no god with me" (Deuteronomy 32:39). Hannah sang that He "bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up" (1 Samuel 2:6). Job knew that "in my flesh shall I see God" (Job 19:26). Isaiah wrote that "the earth shall cast out the dead" (Isaiah 26:19), and Daniel saw "many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth" awake (Daniel 12:2). Until that day "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). The resurrection of the dead is no late borrowing from Persia. It stands in the Law, and Hebrews names it among the first principles (Hebrews 6:1-2).

## What Islam Teaches

Islam teaches a bodily resurrection on the Last Day, which it calls the Day of Standing. When doubters in the Quran ask who can give life to bones that have rotted away, the answer given is that the One who made them the first time will give them life again (Surah 36:78-79). The Quran points to man's creation from dust as the proof that the resurrection will come (Surah 22:5). The trumpet is blown, and blown a second time, and the dead stand up (Surah 39:68).

Between death and that day lies the *barzakh*, a barrier the dead cannot cross back over (Surah 23:100). Islamic tradition teaches that the dead are questioned in the grave by two angels and feel comfort or punishment there while they wait. The Quran also says those killed in God's cause are not to be thought of as dead, but are alive with their Lord and provided for (Surah 3:169).

As for Isa, Islam says he was never crucified (Surah 4:157). So in Islam he never died and was never raised from the dead.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept a great deal. It holds that the body itself rises, not a ghost or a spirit. It argues from dust to resurrection, the same ground Moses stood on: the God who formed man from the ground can bring him back from it. It keeps the trumpet, as Paul did (1 Corinthians 15:52). And it gives the raising to one God with no partner, just as Yahuah said, "there is no god with me" (Deuteronomy 32:39). On the plain fact of a bodily resurrection at the last day, the Muslim stands closer to Moses than the church that sends its dead to heaven at the funeral.

## Where Islam Falls

**It has a harvest with no firstfruits.** In the Law, a sheaf of the firstfruits was waved before Yahuah before the harvest could be gathered (Leviticus 23:10-11). Paul tied the resurrection to that pattern: "now is Messiah risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept," and then "every man in his own order: Messiah the firstfruits; afterward they that are Messiah's at his coming" (1 Corinthians 15:20, 23). Islam denies the death of Isa, so it has no one who rose first. Paul said what follows from that: "if Messiah be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17).

**It wakes the dead before the day.** Islam questions the dead in the grave, comforts or punishes them there, and calls its martyrs alive. Scripture says the dead sleep. "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4). Yahushua said, "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth" (John 11:11). A man punished in the grave has been sentenced before his hearing.

**It raises the body without the One who went before.** Yahuah raises the dead by the same power that raised His Son: "God hath both raised up the Master, and will also raise up us by his own power" (1 Corinthians 6:14). Islam keeps the hope and removes the empty tomb it hangs on.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the resurrection of the body and cut off its firstfruits. It believes the dead will rise but denies the one resurrection that guarantees the rest, and it fills the grave with questions and punishments that Scripture never put there. Its resurrection is a harvest no one has gone ahead of.

Revelation answers in the words of the One Islam says never died: "These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive" (Revelation 2:8). He was dead, He is alive, and because Yahuah raised Him, the dust will give up the rest.
