## The Old Path

Melchizedek met Abram with "bread and wine" (Genesis 14:18). The Law never separated the two: every burnt offering carried a meal offering and a drink offering beside it (Numbers 15:4-5). The night before He died, Yahushua gave His disciples both, the bread, "my body which is given for you," and the cup, "the new testament in my blood" (Luke 22:19-20). The cup is the blood and the entry, for "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). The bread is the body and the walk, living "by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4). Paul joined them: "justified by his blood… saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10). The cup starts a man. The bread finishes him.

## What Christianity Teaches

The church calls the meal the Eucharist, from the Greek for thanksgiving, or Communion. The Roman Catholic Church teaches that at the priest's words the bread and wine become the body and blood of Jesus in substance, a teaching it calls transubstantiation. The Council of Trent said that the whole Christ, body, blood, soul, and divinity, is present in the host. The host, a thin round wafer of unleavened bread, is lifted up and adored, sometimes in a gold sunburst stand called a monstrance. For centuries the people received only the wafer while the cup was kept for the priest.

Most Protestant churches call the bread and cup symbols, served as a cracker and a thimble of grape juice once a month or once a quarter. Grape juice took the place of wine in many churches after a Methodist dentist named Thomas Welch began bottling unfermented juice for communion in 1869.

Across nearly all of it, the church teaches that a man is saved at the moment he believes, that the Law ended at the cross, and that obedience is a fruit that may or may not follow.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the cup. It preaches the blood of Yahushua, it says no man is saved without it, and it keeps a meal in memory of His death, as He asked: "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). It still reads Paul's words that "as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Master's death till he come" (1 Corinthians 11:26). Of the five religions, only the church kept the door.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It kept the cup and threw away the bread.** The church took the entry and refused the walk. Paul says a man is "justified by his blood" and then "saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10), two stages, one after the other. Salvation still has a finish line: "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). The bread is the life of the commandments, every word out of Yahuah's mouth (Matthew 4:4). The church shrank deliverance to the doorway and called the walk optional.

**It shrank the bread to a wafer and called it God.** All four gospels name the bread of that night *artos*, ordinary bread, not *azymos*, the unleavened bread of the feast, for it was a preparation meal on the thirteenth of Aviv. The church replaced the broken loaf with a round white wafer, said it holds the "divinity" of Jesus, and held it up to be adored. Yahuah said, "Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them" (Exodus 20:5). The Son called Himself "a man that hath told you the truth, which I have heard of God" (John 8:40). He gave a man's body, and Yahuah raised it. The Trinity turned that memorial into the adoring of a wafer as God.

**It set the memorial aside.** Yahuah called Pesach "a memorial… a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations" (Exodus 12:14), and Paul said, "let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:8). The church traded the feast for a Greek word and a few minutes once a quarter.

## The Verdict

The church kept the door and refused the house. Its people are told they have arrived the moment they enter. A man who comes through the door and sits down on the step has not finished the journey.

Revelation names the saints by both halves of the meal. They "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12). The faith of Yahushua is the cup. The commandments of God are the bread. The saints at the end hold both, as Melchizedek carried both out to Abram at the beginning.
