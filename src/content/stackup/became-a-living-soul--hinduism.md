## The Old Path

Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Adam did not receive a soul; he became one, a *nephesh chayyah*, the same words Genesis uses for the "living creature" of the field (Genesis 1:24). The sentence on sin was "unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). Ezekiel says it twice: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20). When the breath goes, man "returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4), for "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Immortality is not something man has. It is something he must "seek for" (Romans 2:7) and will "put on" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53).

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism teaches that the true self of every person is the atman, which was never born and can never die. The Bhagavad Gita, one of Hinduism's most loved scriptures, says the self is not slain when the body is slain (Bhagavad Gita 2:20), and that just as a man puts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, the self puts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones (2:22). The body is only a garment. At death the atman moves into another body, higher or lower according to its karma, and this goes on through many lives until it is released. Many Hindu schools teach that the atman is, at bottom, one with Brahman, the divine reality behind all things.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept very little of Genesis 2:7, but it kept something. It sees that the body is made of the earth and goes back to it, and it sees that life in man is tied to breath. Hindu teaching speaks of *prana*, the life-breath, as the thing that keeps the body alive. Scripture agrees that breath is life: "All in whose nostrils was the breath of life… died" (Genesis 7:22). Hinduism also takes seriously that what a man does in this life matters after it.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It makes man uncreated.** Genesis says man was "formed" (Genesis 2:7). He had a beginning, on a day, from the dust. Only Yahuah is "from everlasting to everlasting" (Psalm 90:2), and only He "hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:16). A self that was never born and cannot die is not a creature at all. It is a god, and Hinduism, in its schools that make the atman one with Brahman, says so outright.

**It repeats the serpent's two lies.** In the garden the serpent said, "Ye shall not surely die," and "ye shall be as gods" (Genesis 3:4-5). The Gita's undying self that is one with the divine is both lies in a single doctrine. Yahuah's word stands against both. Man dies: "dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). And man is not divine: "I am Yahuah, and there is none else, there is no God beside me" (Isaiah 45:5).

**It gives many lives where Scripture gives one.** The Gita's change of clothes has the self moving from body to body. Scripture gives one body, one death, and one judgment: "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). Job asked, "If a man die, shall he live again?" and answered that he would wait "till my change come" (Job 14:14), a single change at the end, not a string of rebirths.

**It throws away the body Yahuah will raise.** To Hinduism the body is a worn-out garment to be shed. To Scripture it is the very thing Yahuah will raise. "This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality" (1 Corinthians 15:53). Job said, "yet in my flesh shall I see God" (Job 19:26). The hope is not escape from the body but a body made new.

## The Verdict

Hinduism took the breath of life and turned it into an eternal god living inside a man. Genesis says the opposite. Man is dust given breath, a creature with a beginning, and when the breath goes he dies. He has no undying self and does not move from body to body. He sleeps until the one resurrection, and immortality is a gift given then, not a nature held now.

Revelation keeps that gift in Yahuah's hand. "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life" (Revelation 2:7). Adam was barred from that tree "lest he… live for ever" (Genesis 3:22). If man already had an undying self, he would never need the tree. In Revelation it stands open again for those who "do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life" (Revelation 22:14).
