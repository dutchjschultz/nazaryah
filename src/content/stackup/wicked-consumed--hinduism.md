## The Old Path

Fire went out from Yahuah and "devoured" Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:2). Sodom was left "brimstone, and salt, and burning" (Deuteronomy 29:23), and Peter says Yahuah turned those cities "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). Malachi says the coming day "shall burn as an oven," leaving the wicked "neither root nor branch," and they "shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:1, 3). David says the wicked "shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away" (Psalm 37:20). Yahushua warned of Him who "is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28), and Paul calls the end "everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). The fire is eternal in what it does, not in how long it keeps burning. "The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23), not endless life in pain.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism speaks of many hells, called Naraka. Later Hindu writings such as the Puranas describe them in detail, each with its own punishment for its own kind of sin. Over them stands Yama, the god of death, who judges the dead by the record of their deeds. But no Hindu hell is final. A soul is punished there until the bad karma of its deeds is used up, and then it is born again into another body on earth, perhaps as an animal, perhaps as a man of low birth. Hell is a stop on a long road, not the end of it.

Beneath this lies the Hindu belief in the atman, the self, which Hindu scripture says is never born and never dies. Because the self cannot die, it can never be ended. It can only move on.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the idea that wrongdoing is punished and that what a man does in this life follows him after it. Scripture agrees that "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). Hinduism also sensed that a finite life of sin does not call for a punishment without end. Its hells all close. On that one point it has more sense than the church that preaches a torment with no limit, because the Judge of Scripture "will by no means clear the guilty" (Exodus 34:7) and yet takes "no pleasure in the death of the wicked" (Ezekiel 33:11).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It cannot let the wicked die.** If the self can never die, then the sentence Yahuah gave can never be carried out. Scripture says the opposite of Hindu teaching: "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4). Yahushua said the soul itself can be destroyed in hell (Matthew 10:28). Only Yahuah "hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:16). A man has no undying self for the fire to circle around. When the fire comes, there is nothing left but ash (Malachi 4:3).

**It replaces the judgment with a recycling.** Hinduism sends the punished soul back to earth for another try. Scripture gives one life and one judgment: "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). There is no second birth into another body to work off the first one. The new birth Scripture speaks of happens in this life, by water and the Spirit (John 3:5), not after death in a new womb.

**It puts another god on the judgment seat.** Yama is not the Judge of all the earth. Abraham asked, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25), and that Judge is Yahuah alone. He has committed the judgment to the Son He raised (John 5:22, 27; Acts 17:31), a man appointed for the work, not a god of death with a ledger.

**It never ends wickedness.** Because every hell closes and every soul returns, the wheel of sin keeps turning forever. Evil is never finished. Scripture promises that it will be. "For yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be" (Psalm 37:10). Yahuah will "make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time" (Nahum 1:9).

## The Verdict

Hinduism was right that hell is not forever and wrong about why. Its hells end because the soul moves on, not because the soul dies. Scripture's fire ends because it has finished the wicked. One teaching leaves sin turning without end. The other closes the book.

Revelation shows that closing. Death itself, the thing Hinduism says the self escapes again and again, is "cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death" (Revelation 20:14). There is no third birth after the second death. Then Yahuah says, "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5), and the wheel stops for good.
