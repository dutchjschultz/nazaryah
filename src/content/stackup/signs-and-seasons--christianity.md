## The Old Path

On the fourth day Yahuah set lights in the sky "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years" (Genesis 1:14). The seasons are *moadim*, appointed meetings, and Yahuah calls them "my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). "He appointed the moon for seasons" (Psalm 104:19). The sun turns the year, and its first month is Abib (Deuteronomy 16:1; Exodus 12:2). The moon numbers the month from the new moon (Psalm 81:3). The Sabbaths fall on the eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second, and twenty-ninth of each month, and the day runs from dawn to dusk. The appointments still stand, kept "from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another" (Isaiah 66:23).

## What Christianity Teaches

The church keeps the Gregorian calendar. It is Julius Caesar's sun calendar of 45 BC, corrected by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582, when ten days were cut from October to bring Easter back where the Council of Nicaea had placed it. The year begins on January 1, the month of Janus. The months have nothing to do with the moon. The day begins at midnight. The week is the Roman planetary week, running without a break, with its days named for the sun, the moon, and the gods of the planets.

The church rests on Sunday. Constantine ordered rest on "the venerable day of the Sun" in AD 321, and the Council of Laodicea, around AD 363, told Christians not to rest on the Sabbath but to honor the Lord's day. The church teaches that the feasts of Leviticus were Jewish and ended at the cross, and it keeps Christmas on December 25 and Easter in their place. A Roman almanac of AD 354 lists December 25 as the birthday of the Unconquered Sun, and it is the first record to place the birth of Jesus on that day. Easter is set as the first Sunday after the church's full moon on or after March 21. Its fifty-day count from Easter lands in late spring.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the sun turning the year, and its calendar holds the seasons well. It kept a faint memory of Pesach in Easter, still found by a full moon after the spring turn of the year, because Israel left Egypt under a full moon in the first month (Numbers 33:3). It kept a seven-day count. Beyond those leftovers it kept almost nothing. It dropped the moon, the Sabbath, the feasts, and the Hebrew day.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It threw the moon off the calendar.** "He appointed the moon for seasons" (Psalm 104:19). The Roman month has no new moon, no full moon, and no tie to the sky. A month that does not follow the moon is not a month in the sense the Hebrew word *chodesh*, renewal, carries. Two of the three lights of Genesis 1:14 do nothing in the church's calendar.

**It moved the Sabbath onto Rome's week.** Yahuah called the Sabbath "my holy day" (Isaiah 58:13). The church left the seventh day for the day of the Sun, and the Saturday-keepers who protest that change still keep Saturn's day on the same Roman wheel. Scripture's Sabbath falls by the moon on the eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second, and twenty-ninth, and the count starts over each month. Daniel saw a power that would "think to change times and laws" (Daniel 7:25). A pope rewrote the calendar of the Western world, and the church still keeps it.

**It traded Yahuah's feasts for Rome's.** The feasts are "the feasts of Yahuah" (Leviticus 23:2), not Israel's or the Jews'. The church calls them Jewish and keeps Christmas and Easter instead. It reads Paul's "Ye observe days, and months, and times, and years" (Galatians 4:10) as a ban on Leviticus 23. Paul wrote it to men who "did service unto them which by nature are no gods" and were turning "again to the weak and beggarly elements" (Galatians 4:8-9). They were turning back to their pagan calendar. That is the calendar the church now keeps.

**It moved the day itself.** "God called the light Day" (Genesis 1:5). The day runs from dawn to dusk. Rome began it at midnight, in the dark.

## The Verdict

The church kept Rome's calendar and called it Scripture. It has the sun without the moon, a Sabbath without the seventh day, feasts without Leviticus, and a day that begins in the dark. Of the five religions, it holds the calendar furthest from the sky Yahuah made.

John was "in the Spirit on the Master's day" (Revelation 1:10). The church reads it as Sunday. The Master named His own day: "the Son of man is Master also of the sabbath" (Mark 2:28), and Yahuah called the Sabbath "my holy day" (Isaiah 58:13). The Master's day in Revelation is the day the Master of the Sabbath already claimed.
