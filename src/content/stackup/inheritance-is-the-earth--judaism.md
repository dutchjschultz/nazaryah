## The Old Path

Yahuah promised Abraham "all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession" (Genesis 17:8). The Psalmist wrote, "The heaven, even the heavens, are Yahuah's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men" (Psalm 115:16), and "The righteous shall inherit the land, and dwell therein for ever" (Psalm 37:29). Yahushua said the same: "the meek… shall inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5). The dead do not go anywhere at death. They "sleep in the dust of the earth" until they awake (Daniel 12:2), and Job knew his Redeemer would "stand at the latter day upon the earth" (Job 19:25). The hope is resurrection to "new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). The inheritance was never heaven. It is the earth made new.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism speaks of *Olam Ha-Ba*, the world to come. Its Thirteen Principles of Faith end with belief in the resurrection of the dead, and the daily prayers praise God as the One who gives life to the dead. Alongside this, Jewish teaching commonly holds that the souls of the righteous go at death to *Gan Eden*, the Garden of Eden, to wait for the resurrection, while the wicked pass through a purging before they are released or cut off.

Judaism also holds tightly to the land. The prayers ask three times a day for the exiles to be gathered and Jerusalem rebuilt. Many religious Jews see the return of Jews to the land and the founding of the modern State of Israel in 1948 as the first stage of the promised redemption. The inheritance is generally understood as belonging to the physical descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, though Judaism does accept converts.

## What Judaism Kept

Of the five religions, Judaism kept the most of this doctrine. It kept the land. It kept the promise to Abraham as a promise about real ground on a real earth. It kept the resurrection of the body as a pillar of its faith. It kept the Psalms that say the righteous inherit the land, and it never traded them for a heaven in the clouds.
## Where Judaism Falls

**It sends the soul to the Garden at death.** Daniel did not see souls waiting in Eden. He saw the dead sleeping "in the dust of the earth" (Daniel 12:2). "The dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). A soul living on in a garden while the body lies in the ground came from the Greek world, not from Moses.

**It shrinks the promise to a border.** Abraham lived in the land "as in a strange country," because "he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (Hebrews 11:9-10). The promise was larger than Canaan from the start. Abraham was to be "the heir of the world" (Romans 4:13). A state with borders drawn by British and United Nations maps is not the inheritance. The earth made new is.

**It ties the inheritance to the bloodline, not the Seed.** Paul pointed out that the promise was made to Abraham's "seed," not "seeds," "as of one, And to thy seed, which is Messiah" (Galatians 3:16). The inheritance goes to the one Seed and to all who are in Him: "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29). Descent from Jacob was never the deed to the land.

**It refused the Heir.** Yahushua told the parable of the vineyard, where the husbandmen saw the son and said, "This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and let us seize on his inheritance" (Matthew 21:38). Entry comes through the Heir's blood. After that, the heirs walk in the Torah Judaism has guarded, as a people already brought in.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the earth, the land, and the resurrection, and on these points it stands nearer the old path than any other religion. It fell by sending souls to a garden at death, by shrinking the world to a border, and by holding the inheritance through the bloodline while rejecting the Seed to whom it was promised.

Revelation shows where the promise ends. John saw "the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven" (Revelation 21:2). On its gates are written "the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel," and on its foundations "the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb" (Revelation 21:12, 14). The city comes down to the earth, and it carries Israel's tribes and the Lamb's apostles together. No one inherits it apart from the Lamb.
