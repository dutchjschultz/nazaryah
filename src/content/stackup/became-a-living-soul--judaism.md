## The Old Path

Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Adam did not receive a soul; he became one, a *nephesh chayyah*, the same words Genesis uses for the "living creature" of the field (Genesis 1:24). The sentence on sin was "unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). Ezekiel says it twice: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20). When the breath goes, man "returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4), for "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Immortality is not something man has. It is something he must "seek for" (Romans 2:7) and will "put on" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53).

## What Judaism Teaches

Traditional Judaism teaches that every person has a soul that lives on after the body dies. After death the soul is believed to go on to a spiritual existence, often called the world to come, and Jewish mourning customs, such as saying the Kaddish and lighting a memorial candle on the anniversary of a death, grow out of that belief. Traditional Judaism also holds to the resurrection of the dead at the end of days, and its daily prayer praises God as the One who gives life to the dead. Many modern Jews, especially outside Orthodox circles, believe in the soul but have let the resurrection go, or hold neither.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the books. Every verse that answers the immortal soul is in the Hebrew Scriptures that Judaism guards and reads. Genesis 2:7 is theirs. So is Ezekiel's "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4), and so is Solomon's "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5).

Judaism also kept the resurrection, which is the true hope. Daniel wrote that "many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake" (Daniel 12:2). Isaiah wrote, "Thy dead men shall live… Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust" (Isaiah 26:19). In the days of the apostles, the Pharisees held to the resurrection against the Sadducees, who said "there is no resurrection" (Acts 23:8).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It reads its own Scriptures through Greek eyes.** The Hebrew prophets never taught a soul that floats free of the body. The idea of a deathless soul belonged to the Greeks, and it entered Jewish thought in the centuries after Alexander, when Greek learning filled the lands of Israel and Egypt. Judaism kept the Hebrew words and filled them with a Greek meaning. In Hebrew, *nephesh* is the whole living creature, man or beast (Genesis 1:24; 2:7). A *nephesh* can be killed (Numbers 31:19), can hunger (Proverbs 27:7), and can die (Ezekiel 18:4).

**It contradicts its own Psalms.** David says, "in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?" (Psalm 6:5). "The dead praise not Yahuah, neither any that go down into silence" (Psalm 115:17). If the souls of the righteous were already in the world to come, they would be praising. The Psalms say they are silent until the morning of the resurrection.

**It makes the resurrection unneeded.** If the soul already lives in bliss, raising the body adds little. That is why so many Jews today keep the soul and drop the resurrection. Scripture puts everything on the waking. Peter told the men of Jerusalem that "David is not ascended into the heavens" (Acts 2:34) and that "his sepulchre is with us unto this day" (Acts 2:29). David is in the grave, waiting.

**It refuses the One who opened the grave.** The resurrection Judaism still prays for has already begun. Yahuah raised His Son as "the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20), the wave sheaf of the harvest to come. Judaism waves no sheaf for Him and so has a harvest with no firstfruits.

## The Verdict

Judaism holds the Scriptures that settle the question and then answers it with Greek thought. Man is dust and breath. He became a living soul, and when the breath goes he sleeps in the dust until Yahuah wakes him. His hope is not a soul that escapes the grave but a body called up out of it.

Revelation gives the morning Daniel saw. "The sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them" (Revelation 20:13). The dead are given up from the grave, because that is where Daniel said they sleep. "Blessed are the dead which die in the Master… that they may rest from their labours" (Revelation 14:13). They rest, and then they rise.
