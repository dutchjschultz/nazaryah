## The Old Path

On the fourth day Yahuah set lights in the sky "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years" (Genesis 1:14). The seasons are *moadim*, appointed meetings, and Yahuah calls them "my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). "He appointed the moon for seasons" (Psalm 104:19). The sun turns the year, and its first month is Abib (Deuteronomy 16:1; Exodus 12:2). The moon numbers the month from the new moon (Psalm 81:3). The Sabbaths fall on the eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second, and twenty-ninth of each month, and the day runs from dawn to dusk. The appointments still stand, kept "from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another" (Isaiah 66:23).

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hindus set their festivals by a sun and moon calendar called the *panchang*, or almanac. There are many regional versions, but they share the same parts. The month is counted by the moon in lunar days, called *tithis*, with a bright half as the moon grows and a dark half as it shrinks. The year is held to the sun. When the moon months fall too far behind, an extra month, the *adhika masa*, is added. The sun's months are marked by its entry into each sign of the zodiac, counted against the fixed stars.

The festivals are set by this calendar. Diwali, the festival of lamps for the goddess Lakshmi, falls on a new moon in autumn. Holi falls on the full moon of spring and is tied to stories of Krishna and of the boy Prahlad. Many Hindus fast on the eleventh lunar day of each half month, *Ekadashi*, in honor of Vishnu. The sun is honored as the god Surya, and the moon as Chandra. The days of the week carry the names of the nine planetary powers, the *navagraha*, and astrologers read the stars to choose lucky hours for weddings, journeys, and business.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the frame of the old calendar better than most. It keeps both lights at work, as Genesis 1:14 does. It counts the month by the moon and turns the year by the sun. It adds a month when the year runs short, so its spring feasts stay in spring and its harvest feasts stay at harvest. It marks the new moon and the full moon as the two great points of the month, which matches the psalm that names both (Psalm 81:3). Hinduism knows the sky is a clock. It has the right clock and puts the wrong names on the hours.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It serves the lights instead of their Maker.** Moses warned Israel not to "lift up thine eyes unto heaven, and when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars… shouldest be driven to worship them, and serve them" (Deuteronomy 4:19). Ezekiel saw men in Yahuah's own house "with their backs toward the temple… and they worshipped the sun toward the east" (Ezekiel 8:16). Hinduism prays to Surya at dawn and names the days for planetary gods. The lights were made as signs. They were never made as lords.

**It keeps the appointments for other gods.** Yahuah calls His appointed times "my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). Hinduism keeps its new moons and full moons for Lakshmi, Krishna, Vishnu, and Shiva. The command was plain: "make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth" (Exodus 23:13).

**It makes the stars into rulers.** Scripture uses the stars as witnesses, never as masters of a man's life. Isaiah mocked Babylon's "astrologers, the stargazers, the monthly prognosticators," and said they could not save even themselves (Isaiah 47:13-14). Hindu astrology chooses the day for a wedding by the stars. Its zodiac count, held to the fixed stars, also walks away from the seasons. The winter festival of Makar Sankranti once marked the sun's turn at midwinter and now falls more than three weeks after it. A star rule always drifts. The sun at the east gate does not.

## The Verdict

Hinduism kept the clock and gave the hours to other gods. Its months are counted by the moon and its year turned by the sun, but every appointment on it is kept for a god Yahuah never named. A right calendar spent on the wrong gods is still idolatry.

Revelation calls every nation back with one cry: "Fear God, and give glory to him… and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Revelation 14:7). The sun and the moon are things He made. Glory goes to the Maker, and the feasts on His calendar are His.
