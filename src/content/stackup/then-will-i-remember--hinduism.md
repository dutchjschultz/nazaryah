## The Old Path

Leviticus 26 is a long warning, and at the bottom of it Yahuah wrote the road home. If Israel would "confess their iniquity" and their "uncircumcised hearts be humbled," then "will I remember my covenant with Jacob" (Leviticus 26:40-42). The return was provided for before the curse ever fell. To repent is *shuv*, to turn around and walk the other way; to confess is *yadah*, to acknowledge openly, out loud. Joel cried, "turn ye even to me with all your heart… rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13), and Ezekiel gave the reason: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth… wherefore turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). One God receives the turn, and He Himself remembers. The turn was never the price. The price was blood Yahuah gave "upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls" (Leviticus 17:11). So Hebrews sets "repentance from dead works" first among the foundations (Hebrews 6:1).

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism has a word for making up for sin: *prayashchitta*, usually translated "penance" or "expiation." The old law books, such as the Laws of Manu, set out penances to match each wrong: fasting, gifts to priests, repeating sacred words, and long journeys on foot. The penance is sized to the sin, so that the deed is worked off.

The best-known penance is water. Hindus go on pilgrimage to holy rivers, above all the Ganges, which they honor as the goddess Ganga, and bathe in it to wash away sins. At the great Kumbh Mela gatherings, held in turn at four river cities, millions step into the water on the most favored days for that reason.

Behind it all stands karma. Every deed carries a result that must be paid out, in this life or in another. Penance is a way to pay some of that debt down before it comes due.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the knowledge that sin is real, that it defiles a man, and that something must be done about it. It never pretends that wrong does not matter. Isaiah began in the same place: "Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes" (Isaiah 1:16).

It also kept the humbled body. Fasting, walking barefoot, and going without are outward forms of the low heart Leviticus 26:41 asks for. The Torah itself joined humbling with fasting on Yom Kippur (Leviticus 16:29).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It rends the garment, not the heart.** Joel said, "rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:13). Penance in Hinduism is a work done with the body: a fast, a journey, a bath. But Leviticus 26 asks for a turn and a confession. A man can walk a thousand miles and never turn around inside. That is exactly what Hebrews means by "dead works."

**No river can wash out sin.** Yahuah answered the hope of the holy river through Jeremiah: "though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me" (Jeremiah 2:22). Sin is not dirt on the skin. It is a debt written before Yahuah, and only the blood He gave can pay it (Leviticus 17:11).

**It turns to many gods, so no one remembers a covenant.** Leviticus 26 ends with one God saying "I will remember." Hinduism offers its penances to a crowd of gods and goddesses, and even to a river. David knew whom his sin was against: "Against thee, thee only, have I sinned" (Psalm 51:4). A man cannot confess to a thousand gods and turn to any one of them.

**Karma has no mercy in it.** Under karma the debt must be worked off, life after life. Under the covenant, Yahuah forgives: "I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins" (Isaiah 43:25). Karma remembers every sin. Yahuah promises to remember His covenant and forget the sin.

## The Verdict

Hinduism knows that sin defiles, and it has spent three thousand years trying to wash it away. But its washing is a work of the body, its rivers are gods, and its debt is never forgiven, only worked down. That is penance, not repentance. The road home in Leviticus 26 is a turn of the heart toward one God who has already provided the blood, and Hinduism has never found it.

Revelation names this very error among those who would not turn: "the rest of the men… repented not of the works of their hands, that they should not worship devils, and idols of gold, and silver, and brass, and stone, and of wood" (Revelation 9:20). Repentance from dead works begins by leaving the idols at the river and turning to the living God.
