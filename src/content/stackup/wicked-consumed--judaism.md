## The Old Path

Fire went out from Yahuah and "devoured" Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:2). Sodom was left "brimstone, and salt, and burning" (Deuteronomy 29:23), and Peter says Yahuah turned those cities "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). Malachi says the coming day "shall burn as an oven," leaving the wicked "neither root nor branch," and they "shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:1, 3). David says the wicked "shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away" (Psalm 37:20). Yahushua warned of Him who "is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28), and Paul calls the end "everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). The fire is eternal in what it does, not in how long it keeps burning. "The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23), not endless life in pain.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism has no single settled teaching on the end of the wicked. The word it uses is Gehinnom, taken from the Valley of Hinnom just south of Jerusalem. Many religious Jews today think of Gehinnom as a place of cleansing rather than endless punishment. A common teaching holds that the soul is purged there for no more than twelve months, and for that reason mourners say the Kaddish prayer for a parent for eleven months, so as not to suggest the parent needed the full term. Some traditional teaching says a small number of the very worst are destroyed or never leave. Many modern Jews, especially in the Reform movement, do not believe in any hell at all.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the place name, and the place name preaches. The Valley of Hinnom was where Judah built high places "to burn their sons and their daughters in the fire" (Jeremiah 7:31). Josiah defiled it so no one could do that again (2 Kings 23:10), and Jeremiah said it would be called "the valley of slaughter," a place to bury the dead "till there be no place" (Jeremiah 7:32). It was a valley of corpses, not of living men. When Yahushua warned of Gehenna, He was pointing at that valley, the same picture Isaiah gave of the "carcases of the men that have transgressed" (Isaiah 66:24). Dead bodies, not living souls.

Judaism also refused the church's endless torture chamber. In that refusal it stands nearer to its own Scriptures than most of Christendom, for its own Psalms say "yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be" (Psalm 37:10).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It makes the fire a cleanser for the wicked.** Scripture does speak of a refining fire, but it is for Yahuah's own people: He "shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver" (Malachi 3:3). In the very next chapter the same prophet tells what fire does to the wicked: it burns them "as stubble" and leaves "neither root nor branch" (Malachi 4:1). Judaism took the fire meant for the righteous and handed it to the wicked. The refiner's fire and the oven are two different fires.

**It lets suffering pay for sin.** If twelve months of purging can clean a soul, then pain has become the sacrifice. Torah never taught that. "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). Judaism has had no altar since the Temple fell, and it has filled that gap with prayer, repentance, charity, and a cleansing fire. None of those is blood. The Lamb Yahuah provided (Genesis 22:8) is the only thing that removes sin, and Judaism has refused Him.

**It prays for the dead, who cannot be helped.** The Kaddish custom assumes the dead are conscious and that the living can affect their outcome. Judaism's own Scriptures deny both. "The dead know not any thing… neither have they any more a portion for ever" (Ecclesiastes 9:5-6). "None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him" (Psalm 49:7). The time to be redeemed is in this life.

## The Verdict

Judaism walked away from endless torment, and that was right. But it walked toward a fire that heals the wicked, and that is just as wrong. Scripture's fire for the wicked does not clean them. It ends them. The Valley of Hinnom itself was the proof: a place of the dead, burned and buried, not a school for souls.

Revelation closes the question with Malachi's own ending. Fire comes "down from God out of heaven, and devoured them" (Revelation 20:9). Devoured is the word Moses used of Nadab and Abihu. The ashes under the feet in Malachi and the devouring fire in Revelation are one judgment, and nothing walks out of it.
