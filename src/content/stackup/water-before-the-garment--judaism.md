## The Old Path

Before Aaron and his sons wore a single holy garment, Moses brought them to the door of the tabernacle "and wash them with water" (Exodus 29:4); only then were they clothed and anointed (Exodus 29:5-7). Nobody served until he was washed. On Yom Kippur the order held: "therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on" (Leviticus 16:4). Torah is full of washings: the priests at the laver (Exodus 30:20), the leper (Leviticus 14:8-9), the one who touched the dead (Numbers 19:19), the Levites (Numbers 8:7). So Hebrews names "the doctrine of baptisms," plural, among the foundations (Hebrews 6:2), and calls the old rites "divers washings" by the same Greek word (Hebrews 9:10). One God commanded every washing in His own singular voice, and none of them named three. In Acts the believer was immersed "in the name of Yahushua Messiah for the remission of sins" (Acts 2:38), and then wore the garment: "as many of you as have been baptized into Messiah have put on Messiah" (Galatians 3:27).

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism keeps the *mikveh*, a pool for full immersion. By Jewish law it must hold natural water, gathered from rain or fed by a spring, and enough of it to cover a whole body. The immersion itself is called *tevilah*. The one who goes down must be bare, with nothing between the skin and the water, and must go fully under.

Today the mikveh is used most by married Orthodox women, who immerse each month after their time of separation. Every convert to Judaism must immerse as part of conversion. Many observant men immerse before the Sabbath or before Yom Kippur, and new dishes are dipped before use.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept more of the doctrine of baptisms than any religion outside Scripture. It kept full immersion, not sprinkling. It kept living water, the "running water" of Leviticus 15:13. It kept the many washings, plural, just as Hebrews 6:2 names them. And it kept immersion as the door for an outsider coming in, which is exactly how Acts uses it.

It also kept the one God. The mikveh has never been entered in three names. When John immersed in the Jordan, he was not inventing something strange. Israel already knew what it meant to go down into the water, which is why the leaders only asked him, "Why baptizest thou then?" (John 1:25).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It kept the water and refused the Lamb the water pointed to.** John immersed so that Messiah "should be made manifest to Israel" (John 1:31), and the first thing he said when he saw Yahushua was, "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29). Every washing in Torah stood beside an altar. The mikveh now stands beside nothing. Judaism keeps the water and refuses the blood.

**It skipped the time of reformation.** Hebrews says the old washings were "carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation" (Hebrews 9:10). That time came when Messiah entered the heavenly sanctuary "by his own blood" (Hebrews 9:12). The washings did not vanish. They found their meaning in immersion into His name. Judaism keeps the shadow after the substance has come.

**It refused the Name over the water.** Peter told Israel itself at Shavuot, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Yahushua Messiah" (Acts 2:38). Three thousand Jews went down into the water that day (Acts 2:41). The immersion of Acts was a Jewish washing, given first to Jews, in the name of the Son of the God of Israel.

**It washes the body, but never receives the garment.** In Exodus 29 the water came before the clothing. Ezekiel's promise of clean water (Ezekiel 36:25) led to "a new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit" (Ezekiel 36:26). Zechariah promised "a fountain opened to the house of David… for sin and for uncleanness" (Zechariah 13:1). That fountain was opened at the tree, and the mikveh has never led anyone to it.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the water almost exactly as Torah gave it: full immersion, living water, many washings, one God. It is closer to the doctrine of baptisms than the church that sprinkles infants. But the water was always the step before the garment, and the garment is Messiah. Living water with no Lamb is a washing that prepares a priest who is never dressed and never serves.

Revelation shows the priests the water was preparing: "Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, and hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:5-6). Washed, then made priests. The order of Exodus 29 stands, and it ends at the blood.
