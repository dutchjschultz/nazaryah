## The Old Path

When Yahuah told Abraham about Sodom, He said, "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21). Judgment in Scripture is a court case. The Hebrew *shaphat* means to judge, to decide a case, to pass sentence after a hearing. Abraham pleaded, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). The two men went on toward Sodom while Abraham "stood yet before Yahuah" (Genesis 18:22), and those two are named "two angels" (Genesis 19:1): one Judge, not three. Then the smoke of the plain went up "as the smoke of a furnace" (Genesis 19:28). Sodom was the demonstration, turned "into ashes" as "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Peter 2:6). Solomon wrote that "God shall bring every work into judgment" (Ecclesiastes 12:14), and Daniel saw the dead rise, "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). Hebrews names eternal judgment among the first principles (Hebrews 6:2). The sentence is sure, the day is set, and the Judge is one.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism teaches karma: every act carries its own result, and that result comes back to the one who did it, in this life or in a later one. Good deeds lead to a better birth, and evil deeds to a worse one. Karma works like a law of nature. It needs no one to hear a case.

Popular Hindu belief does give a place to Yama, lord of the dead, who receives souls and sends them to heavens or hells for a time. But those heavens and hells are only stops between births, and the soul goes round again. Time itself moves in vast cycles of ages that end and begin again, so there is no final day. The way off the wheel is *moksha*, release, sought through knowledge, devotion, or selfless action.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the truth that a man reaps what he sows. Scripture says it the same way: "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7), and "they that plow iniquity, and sow wickedness, reap the same" (Job 4:8). Hosea warned Israel, "they have sown the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind" (Hosea 8:7). Hinduism holds that every deed counts and none is forgotten, and in its duties it keeps much of the second tablet of the Law. On that it stands closer to Scripture than the church that calls obedience optional.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It has a law with no Lawgiver on the bench.** Karma is a machine, and a machine does not hear a case. Yahuah does. Before Sodom burned He said, "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21), and Abraham pleaded before "the Judge of all the earth" (Genesis 18:25). David wrote, "God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another" (Psalm 75:7). Judgment in Scripture is a Person passing sentence, not a force paying out results. Yama is only one god among many, and his courts are rooms on the wheel.

**It has no final day.** In Hinduism the ages turn without end. In Scripture the end is fixed: Yahuah "hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained" (Acts 17:31). A man dies once, "but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). There is no next round and no new birth to work off the old one.

**It has no pardon.** Karma cannot forgive. Every debt must be paid by the one who owes it, over as many lives as it takes. Micah asked, "Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity" (Micah 7:18)? Yahuah offers what karma never can: "though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow" (Isaiah 1:18). He did it by providing the Lamb Himself (Genesis 22:8). Under karma the sinner is his own sacrifice, over and over, and never finished.

## The Verdict

Hinduism kept the harvest and lost the Judge. It knows that deeds bear fruit, but it puts an impersonal law where Scripture puts a living Judge, endless cycles where Scripture puts one appointed day, and a debt paid life after life where Scripture puts a pardon bought with blood. A sentence with no Judge is not judgment. It is only a wheel, and Scripture has no wheel.

Revelation shows the Person behind the reward: "behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be" (Revelation 22:12). The reward comes in the hand of the man Yahuah ordained, not from the turning of a wheel.
