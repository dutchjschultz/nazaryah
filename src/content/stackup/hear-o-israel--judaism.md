## The Question He Asked

At Caesarea Philippi, Yahushua asked His disciples, "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?" Peter answered, "Thou art the Messiah, the Son of the living God," and Yahushua said that answer came from "my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 16:13-17). Peter's answer has two parts: He is the Messiah, and He is the Son of the living God. Every religion's answer is measured against those two parts.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism teaches that the Messiah has not yet come. It looks for a man from the line of David who will rule as king, gather Israel back to the land, rebuild the Temple, and bring peace to the nations. Judaism says Yahushua of Nazareth was not that man. To most Jews today he is a Jewish teacher at best, and at worst a false messiah whose followers turned him into a god.

Judaism reads the Suffering Servant of Isaiah 53 as the nation of Israel suffering among the nations, not as one man dying for sin.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the truth that the Messiah is a man. He is a Son of David, a "righteous Branch" raised up to David (Jeremiah 23:5). Judaism never voted him into a Godhead. It holds to the Shema, "Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 6:4), and it rejects the Trinity outright. On that point the synagogue and the apostles agree against the church.

Judaism also kept the very Scriptures that name the Messiah as Son. Yahuah promised David a seed after him and said, "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:12-14). The Psalm says of the anointed King, "Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee" (Psalm 2:7). Paul applied that verse to Yahushua's resurrection (Acts 13:33). The promise of a Son who is a man, from David's line, sits in Judaism's own Bible.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It refused the One who came.** "He came unto his own, and his own received him not" (John 1:11). Yahushua told the leaders of His day, "had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me" (John 5:46). Judaism held to the promise and turned away from its fulfilment.

**It missed the timing.** Daniel said Messiah would be "cut off, but not for himself," and only after that "the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary" (Daniel 9:26). The city and the Temple fell in 70 AD. By Daniel's own order, the Messiah had to come and be cut off before that. A Messiah still awaited cannot be the one Daniel saw.

**It looked for a king and refused a Lamb.** Judaism wants the crown without the cross. Isaiah gave both. The Servant was "wounded for our transgressions" and "brought as a lamb to the slaughter," and then Yahuah would "divide him a portion with the great" (Isaiah 53:5-12). The nation of Israel cannot be that Servant, because the Servant suffers "for the transgression of my people" (Isaiah 53:8). He dies for the people. He is not the people.

**It hid the Son behind a veil.** Paul said that when Moses is read, "the vail is upon their heart. Nevertheless when it shall turn to the Master, the vail shall be taken away" (2 Corinthians 3:15-16).

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the man and lost the Messiah. It holds rightly that the Anointed One is a son of David and not a second God. Then it refuses the Son of David who came, died, and rose exactly as its own prophets wrote.

Revelation says the day is coming when the veil comes off: "Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him" (Revelation 1:7). Zechariah saw the same day: "they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son" (Zechariah 12:10).
