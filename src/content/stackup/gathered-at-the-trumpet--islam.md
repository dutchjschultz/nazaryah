## The Old Path

At Sinai, "the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud" sounded, and Moses "brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God" (Exodus 19:16-17). Yahuah ordered two silver trumpets "for the calling of the assembly" (Numbers 10:2), and set "a memorial of blowing of trumpets" in the seventh month (Leviticus 23:24). Isaiah saw "the great trumpet" blown to gather the outcasts home (Isaiah 27:13). Yahushua gave the order with no gap: "Immediately after the tribulation of those days," the Son of man comes, and His angels gather His elect "with a great sound of a trumpet" (Matthew 24:29-31). Paul calls it "the last trump" (1 Corinthians 15:52), and says that day will not come "except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed" (2 Thessalonians 2:3). One coming, one trumpet, one gathering, after the trouble.

## What Islam Teaches

The Quran speaks often of the Trumpet blown on the Last Day. It says that when the Trumpet is blown, all who are in the heavens and the earth fall dead except whom God wills, and then it is blown a second time and they stand up, looking on (Surah 39:68). It says that at the blowing of the Trumpet the dead come out of their graves and hurry to God (Surah 36:51). Islamic tradition names the angel who blows it as Israfil.

Islam also teaches that signs come before that Day. Among them is the Dajjal, a great deceiver, and the return of Isa, the son of Maryam, who Islam says will come back, defeat the Dajjal, and rule as a follower of Islam. Then come the end, the Trumpet, the resurrection, and the weighing of deeds.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept a good deal of the Old Path. It has one Day, not two. It has a trumpet that everyone hears, not a secret disappearance. It has the dead raised bodily out of their graves at the sound, just as Paul wrote: "the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised" (1 Corinthians 15:52). And it puts a great deceiver on the earth before the end, not after believers have been taken away. On that order, the Muslim stands closer to Paul's "man of sin be revealed" first (2 Thessalonians 2:3) than the church that teaches a secret rapture.

## Where Islam Falls

**Its trumpet kills the living.** The Quran's first blast strikes down everyone alive. Scripture's trumpet does the opposite. "We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump" (1 Corinthians 15:51-52). Those "which are alive and remain" are not struck dead but "caught up together with them" (1 Thessalonians 4:17). Yahuah's trumpet is a call to assemble, as at Sinai and as with the silver trumpets of Numbers 10. It was never a death blow to His own people.

**It has no one to gather to.** In Scripture the trumpet gathers the elect to a Person. The Son of man "shall send his angels… and they shall gather together his elect" (Matthew 24:31). Paul calls it "our gathering together unto him" (2 Thessalonians 2:1). Islam brings Isa back, but as a follower of another prophet, not as the One who sends the angels. A gathering with no one to gather to is only a crowd.

**It gathers for weighing, not for meeting.** Islam's Trumpet calls the dead to have their deeds placed on the scales. Sinai shows what the trumpet is for: Moses led the people out "to meet with God" (Exodus 19:17), a people already brought out of Egypt by the blood of the lamb. The trumpet gathers those already delivered, to meet the One who delivered them. Judgment by weighing deeds has no blood in it at all, and "without shedding of blood is no remission" (Hebrews 9:22).

## The Verdict

Islam heard the trumpet and kept its order, one Day after the trouble, with the dead raised from the graves. But it made the trumpet a weapon against the living and left out the Son who gathers. Scripture's trumpet changes the living, raises the dead, and brings them together to the Messiah Yahuah sent.

Revelation answers in its first chapter: "Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him" (Revelation 1:7). Every eye sees Him, so the coming is public, as Islam says. And the One who comes is the One who was pierced, the Son who died, which Islam denies.
