## The Old Path

Leviticus 26 is a long warning, and at the bottom of it Yahuah wrote the road home. If Israel would "confess their iniquity" and their "uncircumcised hearts be humbled," then "will I remember my covenant with Jacob" (Leviticus 26:40-42). The return was provided for before the curse ever fell. To repent is *shuv*, to turn around and walk the other way; to confess is *yadah*, to acknowledge openly, out loud. Joel cried, "turn ye even to me with all your heart… rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13), and Ezekiel gave the reason: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth… wherefore turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). One God receives the turn, and He Himself remembers. The turn was never the price. The price was blood Yahuah gave "upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls" (Leviticus 17:11). So Hebrews sets "repentance from dead works" first among the foundations (Hebrews 6:1).

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism has confession, but it belongs mainly to the monks. In Theravada lands such as Thailand, Sri Lanka, and Burma, the monks gather on the observance days at the new moon and the full moon to recite the monastic rules, called the Patimokkha. Before the recitation, a monk who has broken a rule admits it to a fellow monk, and the fellow monk tells him to guard himself in the future. In parts of the Mahayana world, monks and laypeople also hold repentance services, reciting their faults before images of buddhas.

But there is no God in Buddhism to forgive. Confession does not cancel karma. A deed still bears its fruit. Confession is meant to clear the mind of guilt, restore harmony in the community, and strengthen the resolve to do better. The one who repents is, in the end, answering to himself and to the rules of the order.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept the open admission of wrong. A monk names his fault out loud to another person, which is close to *yadah*. James taught the same thing: "Confess your faults one to another" (James 5:16). Buddhism also kept the resolve not to go back to the sin, which is the heart of *shuv*. Turning around means walking away.

Beyond that, Buddhism kept very little of Leviticus 26, because Leviticus 26 is about a Person who remembers, and Buddhism has none.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**There is no one to repent to.** David said, "Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight" (Psalm 51:4). Every sin is first a sin against Yahuah. Buddhism has no Lawgiver, so it has no one to sin against, and so no one to turn back to. Confessing to another monk is right, but it is only the half of confession that faces man.

**A turn needs somewhere to turn toward.** *Shuv* means to come back to someone. Joel said "turn ye even to me," and Moses promised that Israel "shalt return unto Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 30:2). Buddhism has a turn away from sin with nowhere to go. A man who turns from evil but not toward Yahuah has only turned in a circle.

**Nobody remembers a covenant.** The last words of the road home are "then will I remember my covenant." Repentance in Scripture works because Yahuah promised beforehand to receive it. Buddhism has no covenant, no promise, and no one to remember. Its confession is a hope with no one behind it.

**Karma never forgives.** In Buddhism a confessed wrong still ripens. In Scripture, Yahuah says, "I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more" (Jeremiah 31:34). That forgiveness rests on the blood He gave, which Buddhism has never heard of. Without it, the debt only waits.

## The Verdict

Buddhism kept the honest admission of fault and the will to do better, and both are good. But repentance in Scripture is a turn back to a Person who wrote the road home, gave the blood to pay the debt, and promised to remember His covenant. Buddhism has the turning but not the One. It is confession with no one listening, and so its debt is never forgiven.

Revelation calls every nation to the One Buddhism left out: "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Revelation 14:7). The Maker of heaven and earth is the One to turn to, and He is listening.
