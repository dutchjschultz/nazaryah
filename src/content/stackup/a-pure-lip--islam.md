## The Old Path

Yahuah promised, "then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent" (Zephaniah 3:9). The Hebrew is *safah berurah*, a pure lip. At Babel Yahuah confounded the lip so that men "may not understand one another's speech" (Genesis 11:7); the pure lip undoes Babel. When the Spirit came upon the seventy elders they prophesied, and Moses wished "that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Joel promised the same Spirit on sons, daughters, servants, and handmaids (Joel 2:28-29). At Shavuot that promise came, and every man asked, "how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?" (Acts 2:8). Yahuah "is not the author of confusion" (1 Corinthians 14:33). The gift He promised is a language understood, used to call on His Name. Every religion's answer is measured against that promise.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam teaches that the Quran was sent down in Arabic, and that the Arabic words themselves are the speech of God. Translations into other languages are treated as explanations of its meaning, not as the Quran itself. The Quran says of itself, "Indeed, We have sent it down as an Arabic Quran that you might understand" (Surah 12:2).

The five daily prayers are said in Arabic wherever a Muslim lives. Most Muslims in the world are not Arabs, and a great many of them recite the prayers and memorize long portions of the Quran without understanding what the words mean.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the reason a holy word is given. The Quran's own stated purpose for its Arabic is "that you might understand" (Surah 12:2). That is the purpose Scripture gives too: when Ezra read the Law to the people, the Levites "gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8).

Islam also kept order in prayer. Its worshippers pray together, at set times, with one voice, and nothing in Muslim prayer looks like the babbling and falling down seen in some churches. In that sense Islam has something of "one consent" (Zephaniah 3:9), and it has never confused noise with the Spirit.

## Where Islam Falls

**It made one nation's tongue holy for every nation.** The Quran says it came in Arabic so that the Arabs who first heard it could understand. Then Islam made that same Arabic the only tongue of prayer for a world that does not speak it, and the reason was lost. At Shavuot the Spirit did not make every nation learn one tongue. He gave each man the words "in our own tongue, wherein we were born" (Acts 2:8). Paul said, "except ye utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how shall it be known what is spoken? for ye shall speak into the air" (1 Corinthians 14:9).

**It prays words the worshipper does not understand.** Paul would rather "speak five words with my understanding… than ten thousand words in an unknown tongue" (1 Corinthians 14:19). A prayer said in sounds the speaker cannot follow is not a pure lip. It is the confusion of Babel, kept on purpose. Yahuah complained of a people who "draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me" (Isaiah 29:13). Words that bypass the mind cannot come from the heart.

**The lip does not call on the Name.** Zephaniah gave the reason for the pure lip: "that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah." Islam's Arabic prayers call on Allah, the word for God, and never on Yahuah. Even if every Muslim understood every word, the pure lip would still be missing its purpose. The tongue is not what makes speech pure. The Name it calls on, and the understanding behind it, make it pure.

## The Verdict

Islam rightly refused noise and rightly said holy words are for understanding. Then it made one people's language the only tongue of prayer for every people, so that millions pray words they do not know to a title that is not the Name. That is not Zephaniah's pure lip. It is Babel's confusion with good order.

Revelation shows the pure lip fulfilled. John saw "a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" standing before the throne (Revelation 7:9). Every tongue was still there. Yahuah did not erase the languages of the nations; He gathered them, each one calling on Him in words it understood.
