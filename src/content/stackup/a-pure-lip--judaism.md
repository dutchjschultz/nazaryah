## The Old Path

Yahuah promised, "then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent" (Zephaniah 3:9). The Hebrew is *safah berurah*, a pure lip. At Babel Yahuah confounded the lip so that men "may not understand one another's speech" (Genesis 11:7); the pure lip undoes Babel. When the Spirit came upon the seventy elders they prophesied, and Moses wished "that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Joel promised the same Spirit on sons, daughters, servants, and handmaids (Joel 2:28-29). At Shavuot that promise came, and every man asked, "how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?" (Acts 2:8). Yahuah "is not the author of confusion" (1 Corinthians 14:33). The gift He promised is a language understood, used to call on His Name. Every religion's answer is measured against that promise.

## What Judaism Teaches

Jewish prayer is set down in the *siddur*, the prayer book, written mostly in Hebrew with some prayers in Aramaic. Traditional synagogues pray the services in Hebrew, at fixed times each day, in fixed words handed down over many centuries. In much of the world, Jews who do not speak Hebrew in daily life still pray in it, often reading words they can sound out but cannot fully follow. The Reform movement, beginning in the 1800s, brought the local language into its prayers, while traditional Judaism kept the Hebrew.

In the modern land of Israel, Hebrew was brought back as a spoken language from the late 1800s, and today millions use it in daily life.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the tongue of the prophets. Moses, David, Isaiah, and Zephaniah himself wrote in Hebrew, and the words of the Hebrew Scriptures are still read in the language they were given in. Judaism kept the Scriptures read aloud in order through the year, the way Moses commanded the Law to be read before all Israel (Deuteronomy 31:11).

Judaism also kept order and one voice in prayer. Its services are not noise, and no one falls to the floor babbling. When the Hebrew prayers are understood, they are prayers of real words with real meaning, much of it taken from the Psalms themselves.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It prays words many cannot follow.** The fault is not in Hebrew. The fault is in praying a language without understanding it. After the exile, many of the people had lost Hebrew. Nehemiah found children who "could not speak in the Jews' language" (Nehemiah 13:24), and so when Ezra read the Law the Levites "gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8). Ezra did not count the sound of Hebrew as enough. The people had to understand. Paul said the same: "five words with my understanding" outweigh "ten thousand words in an unknown tongue" (1 Corinthians 14:19).

**Set words can leave the heart behind.** A prayer repeated for centuries can become lips without a heart. Yahuah said, "this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men" (Isaiah 29:13). The pure lip of Zephaniah is a lip joined to a heart.

**The lip will not say the Name.** This is the deepest fault. Zephaniah gave the reason for the pure lip: "that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah." Judaism holds the right tongue and the right letters, and then, every time the Name appears in its prayers, it says *Adonai*, a title, instead. The one language on earth that carries the Name as Moses wrote it is the language in which Judaism refuses to speak it. The pure lip was promised so that the Name would be called on, and Judaism keeps the lip and withholds the Name.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the language of the prophets and lost the purpose of the pure lip. Too many of its people pray Hebrew they cannot follow, and every one of them, when the Name comes, passes over it in silence. Zephaniah's promise was not a holy language; it was a people who "call upon the name of Yahuah" with understanding and "with one consent."

Revelation shows the lip made pure. John heard the redeemed on Mount Sion sing "a new song before the throne… and no man could learn that song but the hundred and forty and four thousand" (Revelation 14:3), the same company who carry "his Father's name written in their foreheads" (Revelation 14:1). The new song and the Father's Name belong together.
