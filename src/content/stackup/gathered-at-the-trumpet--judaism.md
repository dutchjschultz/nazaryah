## The Old Path

At Sinai, "the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud" sounded, and Moses "brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God" (Exodus 19:16-17). Yahuah ordered two silver trumpets "for the calling of the assembly" (Numbers 10:2), and set "a memorial of blowing of trumpets" in the seventh month (Leviticus 23:24). Isaiah saw "the great trumpet" blown to gather the outcasts home (Isaiah 27:13). Yahushua gave the order with no gap: "Immediately after the tribulation of those days," the Son of man comes, and His angels gather His elect "with a great sound of a trumpet" (Matthew 24:29-31). Paul calls it "the last trump" (1 Corinthians 15:52), and says that day will not come "except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed" (2 Thessalonians 2:3). One coming, one trumpet, one gathering, after the trouble.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism prays for the great shofar every day. In the central prayer of the synagogue service, the Amidah, one blessing asks God to sound the great shofar for Israel's freedom and to gather the exiles from the four corners of the earth. The words come from Isaiah 27:13. Judaism expects that ingathering to come with the Messiah it still awaits.

The shofar is blown in the synagogue on the first day of the seventh month, which Judaism calls Rosh Hashanah, the new year, and again at the close of Yom Kippur. Many religious Jews today see the return of Jews to the land over the last century, and the founding of the State of Israel in 1948, as the beginning of the promised gathering.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the trumpet and kept it tied to gathering, which is exactly where Torah puts it. The silver trumpets called the assembly (Numbers 10:2). The great trumpet of Isaiah gathers the outcasts (Isaiah 27:13). Zechariah says Yahuah Himself "shall blow the trumpet" (Zechariah 9:14). Judaism still blows a real shofar on the first of the seventh month, keeping the "memorial of blowing of trumpets" (Leviticus 23:24) while most of the church has forgotten the day exists.

Judaism also kept the order. Its gathering is public, it comes after long affliction, and it is the work of God in the open. No Jewish prayer asks to be secretly taken away before the trouble.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It made Yom Teruah a new year.** Torah calls the first of the seventh month a memorial of blowing (Leviticus 23:24), *Yom Teruah*, the day of the trumpet blast. It is the seventh month, not the first. Yahuah set the head of the year in the spring: "This month shall be unto you the beginning of months" (Exodus 12:2), the month of Aviv (Exodus 13:4). The name Rosh Hashanah is not in Torah. By turning the day into a new year, Judaism hid what the day points to, the trumpet of the gathering.

**It prays for the gathering and refuses the Gatherer.** Yahushua stood over Jerusalem and said, "how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!" (Matthew 23:37). The great trumpet of Isaiah is blown by the Son of man's angels, and they gather "his elect" (Matthew 24:31). Judaism prays every day for a gathering it already turned down when the Gatherer stood in its streets.

**It looks for the gathering in politics.** A return by ships and airplanes, settled by votes and wars, is not the great trumpet. Yahushua said the elect are gathered "from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other" (Matthew 24:31), and the dead are raised at the same sound (1 Thessalonians 4:16). The elect are not one bloodline but all who belong to Messiah: "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29). The ingathering is of the one olive tree, Jew and Gentile grafted in together (Romans 11:17-24), not of a nation back to a map.

## The Verdict

Judaism holds the trumpet, the memorial day, and the promise in Isaiah, and it prays rightly that the gathering will come. But it renamed the trumpet day, refused the Son who sends the angels, and looks for the gathering in the news from the Middle East. The great shofar sounds once, at the end, after the trouble, and it gathers the elect to Yahuah's Messiah.

Revelation shows the gathering Isaiah saw: "a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" (Revelation 7:9). The elder tells John who they are: "These are they which came out of great tribulation" (Revelation 7:14). They came out of it, through it, and were gathered after it.
