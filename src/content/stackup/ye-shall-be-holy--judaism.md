## The Old Path

Yahuah charged the priests to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (Leviticus 10:10). In Leviticus 11 He named the beasts, fish, and birds Himself, so the line was never left to appetite or conscience. He gave the reason, "ye shall be holy; for I am holy," right after naming Himself "Yahuah that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 11:44-45). Deliverance came first, and holiness followed. Noah already knew the clean beasts from the unclean long before Sinai (Genesis 7:2), and Ezekiel condemned the priests who "put no difference" (Ezekiel 22:26). Yahuah told Abram, "walk before me, and be thou perfect," *tamim*, whole (Genesis 17:1), and Peter carried the same command to the believers: "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16). Holiness is not a feeling or a guess. It is the line Yahuah drew, walked by a people He has already delivered.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism keeps the food laws under the name *kashrut*, and food that passes is called kosher. Observant Jews eat only the beasts that chew the cud and part the hoof, only fish with fins and scales, and only the birds not named as unclean. Meat must come from an animal slaughtered by a trained slaughterer with one swift cut, and the meat is then soaked and salted to draw out the blood.

On top of this Judaism has built a large set of added rules. From the command "Thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk" (Exodus 23:19), it forbids eating any meat with any dairy, including chicken with cheese. Observant homes keep two sets of dishes, pots, and sinks, one for meat and one for dairy, and wait a set number of hours after eating meat before eating dairy. Most packaged food is approved by rabbinic agencies that print their mark on the label. Judaism describes such added rules as fences built around the Law to keep a man from coming near breaking it.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the line. Of the five religions it is the only one that still eats by Leviticus 11 as written. The observant Jew will not eat swine, camel, rabbit, shrimp, or catfish. He drains the blood, as Yahuah commanded: "ye shall eat no manner of blood" (Leviticus 7:26). Through exile and persecution the Jewish people have refused unclean food, "severed… from other people" as Yahuah said (Leviticus 20:26). The church that mocks kosher food is mocking the commandment itself.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It added to what Yahuah said.** Moses warned, "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). The command about a kid in its mother's milk is one short line, repeated three times. Nothing in it mentions chicken, cheese, separate sinks, or waiting hours. When the fence becomes the law, the law is no longer Yahuah's. Yahushua said of the traditions of His day, "in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men," and "full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition" (Mark 7:7, 9).

**It moved the teaching of holiness to the wrong office.** Yahuah gave the priests the charge to teach the difference between clean and unclean (Ezekiel 44:23). Judaism gave it to rabbinic boards and their seals on a package. A believer who reads Leviticus 11 can know what is clean without a stamp.

**It walks the holy way without the Lamb who opens it.** In Leviticus the order is plain: Yahuah brought Israel out of Egypt, and then said "ye shall therefore be holy" (Leviticus 11:45). The deliverance came by the blood of a lamb. Judaism keeps the food law and refuses the Lamb Yahuah provided, so it walks the second step without the first. Clean food cannot cleanse a man. The blood delivers. The commandments are then the walk of those already brought out.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the food laws better than any religion on earth, and it deserves credit the church will not give it. But it fenced the Law with commandments of men until the fence stood taller than the Law, and it keeps the walk of holiness while refusing the Lamb whose blood comes first. It has the table of Leviticus without the door of Exodus.

Revelation shows who stands clean before the throne: "These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:14). The robes are made white in blood, not in salt and separate dishes. The clean table follows the washed robe.
