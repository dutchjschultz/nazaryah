## The Old Path

Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That belief was credited at the door, and the credit was real. Two chapters later Yahuah told him, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (Genesis 17:1), and afterward He said Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Moses said the same to a people already brought "out of Egypt with a mighty hand" (Deuteronomy 6:21): "it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25). The psalmist said, "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), and Ezekiel said, "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20), upon him and not on another man's record. James joined the two: Abraham's belief "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Belief is credited first. Feet on the road follow.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam teaches that on the Day of Judgment every person's deeds will be weighed on scales, the Mizan. The Quran says God will set up "the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection" (Surah 21:47). The one whose scales are heavy with good deeds will live in a pleasing life, and the one whose scales are light will fall into the pit (Surah 101:6-9).

A Muslim adds weight to the good side through the Five Pillars: the confession of faith, the five daily prayers, giving to the poor, the fast of Ramadan, and the pilgrimage to Mecca. Islam teaches that sincere repentance and good deeds can wipe out bad deeds, and that in the end every person depends on the mercy of God. There is no sacrifice for sin and no one whose death opens the way.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the feet. It refuses the idea that a man can believe and then live as he pleases. It teaches that righteousness is done, that prayer and giving and fasting are commanded, and that every person will answer for his deeds. Scripture agrees that the dead will be "judged every man according to their works" (Revelation 20:13). On this point the Muslim takes the commandments of his religion more seriously than many in the church take the commandments of Yahuah.

## Where Islam Falls

**It has no door.** Abraham was counted righteous before he did anything. Paul pointed out that it was "not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision" (Romans 4:10). The credit came first, and the walk followed. Islam begins with the walk and hopes that it will be enough at the end. Moses spoke Deuteronomy 6:25 to people who had already been passed over by the blood of the lamb (Exodus 12:13). Islam takes the commandments of Sinai's road and removes the Pesach that opened it.

**It weighs instead of cleanses.** Scales assume that enough good can outweigh the bad. Yahuah's Law does not work that way. Ezekiel said that when the righteous man turns to sin, "all his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned" (Ezekiel 18:24). Good deeds do not cancel a debt. Only blood does, for "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11), and "without shedding of blood is no remission" (Hebrews 9:22). Islam denies that the Messiah died, so it has no blood to offer.

**It sets its own commandments on the scale.** The righteousness Moses named was "all these commandments" that Yahuah gave (Deuteronomy 6:25). Islam weighs prayers toward Mecca, a fast in a moon-only month, and a pilgrimage to the Kaaba, none of which Yahuah commanded. Its Friday gathering stands where the Sabbath should be. Righteousness is not any deed a religion names. It is the deeds Yahuah named.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the walk and lost the door. It teaches that righteousness must be done, which is true, and then tries to earn by the walk what Yahuah only gives by the blood. It weighs deeds that Yahuah never commanded, and it hopes the scales tip the right way. A man whose entry rests on his own weight has no entry at all.

Revelation shows how the robes of the righteous were made white. The great multitude before the throne "have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:14). No scale whitened them. The blood made them clean, and then they served Him "day and night in his temple" (Revelation 7:15).
