## The Old Path

At Horeb, Israel "heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice" (Deuteronomy 4:12). Moses drew the command straight from that: take heed "lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure" (Deuteronomy 4:15-16). The second commandment forbids "any graven image, or any likeness," and forbids bowing down to them (Exodus 20:4-5). Even the brass serpent Yahuah Himself commanded (Numbers 21:8-9) became an idol, and Hezekiah broke it in pieces and called it "Nehushtan," a piece of brass (2 Kings 18:4). Isaiah asked, "To whom then will ye liken God?" (Isaiah 40:18), and Paul told Athens that the divine is not "like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device" (Acts 17:29). Yahuah gave His people a reminder that is no image at all: fringes on the garment, so that they would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39).

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism today forbids any image of God. A synagogue has no statue to bow to and no picture of the Most High. Jews wear the fringes, the *tzitzit*, on the corners of a garment, and many bind small leather boxes holding Scripture passages, the *tefillin*, on the arm and forehead during morning prayer. A small case holding the words of the Shema, the *mezuzah*, is fixed to the doorpost, and many touch it and kiss their fingers as they pass. The Torah scroll is dressed in a cloth mantle and often a silver crown, carried through the synagogue, and kissed with the fringe or the prayer book as it goes by.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the commandment against images more faithfully than any other religion on this list. It learned the hard way. The golden calf at Sinai (Exodus 32:4), the calves of Jeroboam at Bethel and Dan (1 Kings 12:28-29), and the idols that brought on the Babylonian exile all stand in Israel's own record. After the exile, the Jews never again set up an idol of their own. They also kept the fringes, the one visible reminder Yahuah gave (Numbers 15:38-39).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It turned the words into objects.** Yahuah said His words were to be "in thine heart," taught to the children, and bound "for a sign upon thine hand" and "as frontlets between thine eyes" (Deuteronomy 6:6-8). The sign was His words living in the heart, the hand, and the mind. Judaism made the sign into leather boxes and cased scrolls, and made those objects things to kiss and to crown. A scroll is not an idol, but the habit of treating the holy thing as the thing itself is the road that led Israel to burn incense to the brass serpent.

**History shows the slide.** Judaism has not always kept its own line. Archaeologists have uncovered synagogue floors from the fourth through sixth centuries AD in the land of Israel, at places like Hammat Tiberias and Beit Alpha, laid with mosaics of the zodiac wheel and the Greek sun god Helios riding his chariot at the center. Moses had warned against just this, lest "when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars… shouldest be driven to worship them" (Deuteronomy 4:19).

**It refused the one image Yahuah did give.** Yahuah forbade man to make an image of Him because He would provide His own. Paul calls Yahushua "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15), and Hebrews calls Him "the express image of his person" (Hebrews 1:3). An image is not the one it shows. A son bears his father's likeness and is not his father. The Son is the living likeness of Yahuah, born of a woman, not carved by a craftsman. Judaism, which rightly refused every carved likeness, also refused the living one, and so missed the very reason no carving was ever allowed.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the letter of the second commandment better than anyone, and that deserves honor. But it gave holy weight to scrolls and boxes and doorposts, and it turned away from the Son who is the true image of the invisible God. The commandment was never only "make no image." It was "make no image, because Yahuah will show Himself His own way."

Revelation finishes what Horeb began. At Horeb they saw no form. In the city to come, "his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads" (Revelation 22:3-4). The Name will be on the forehead, not in a box tied there, and the face no one was allowed to carve will be seen.
