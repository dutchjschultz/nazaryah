## The Old Path

Before Sinai, before circumcision, before the knife on Moriah, Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). To believe is *aman*, to lean your whole weight on something; it is the root of "amen." To count is *chashab*, a bookkeeper's word: righteousness was credited to an account that had not earned it. Abram had no written commandments, no covenant sign, no priesthood, and no tabernacle. He had a promise of a seed, and he leaned on it. Every pronoun in the chapter is singular: one God speaks, one man trusts, one God credits. The same word runs through the prophets: "Believe in Yahuah your God, so shall ye be established" (2 Chronicles 20:20), and "the just shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). Hebrews lays "faith toward God" among the first stones (Hebrews 6:1), and Genesis had already set it in place.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism calls Abraham *Avraham Avinu*, "Abraham our father," and the title is meant by descent. A Jew is a son of Abraham through Isaac and Jacob. In Orthodox practice, Jewish identity passes through the mother: a child born to a Jewish mother is Jewish, and anyone else must convert. Every Jewish boy receives circumcision on the eighth day as the sign of the covenant of Abraham, the *brit milah*.

The word for faith, *emunah*, comes from *aman*, and Judaism holds firmly to the one God. But its weight falls on belonging to the people and doing the commandments with the tradition built around them. Jewish teaching also holds that the children of Abraham are favored for the sake of the patriarchs, an idea called the merit of the fathers.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept Genesis 15:6 itself, in Hebrew, in its own Torah scroll. It kept the very words *aman* and *chashab*. It kept the one God of Abraham, and it has never bowed to a creed of three persons. Every pronoun in Genesis 15 is singular, and the synagogue reads it that way.

It kept the promise of the land and of a seed as many as the stars, and it kept the covenant sign Yahuah gave Abraham (Genesis 17:10-11). Paul himself said the Jew has much advantage, "chiefly, because that unto them were committed the oracles of God" (Romans 3:2).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It traces Abraham by blood, but Yahuah counted him by belief.** In Yahushua's day the leaders said, "We be Abraham's seed" (John 8:33). He answered, "If ye were Abraham's children, ye would do the works of Abraham" (John 8:39). John the Immerser warned them not to say "We have Abraham to our father," for "God is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham" (Matthew 3:9). The family of Abraham is known by its likeness to him, not by its birth certificate.

**The credit came before the sign.** Paul asked when righteousness was counted to Abraham: "when he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision" (Romans 4:10). Genesis 15 comes before Genesis 17. Circumcision was "a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised" (Romans 4:11). The sign sealed belief. It never replaced it.

**The promised Seed is one man.** Paul says the promise was "not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Messiah" (Galatians 3:16). Judaism reads the seed as the nation. But the nation was promised through the Seed, and "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29). To refuse the Seed is to stand outside the promise while holding the scroll it is written in.

**Abraham believed in the One they refused.** Yahushua said, "Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad" (John 8:56). The father they claim by blood looked ahead to the Son they turned away.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the text, the words, and the one God of Genesis 15, and kept them faithfully for thousands of years. But it made Abraham a father by blood when Yahuah made him a father by belief. Righteousness was credited to Abram as an uncircumcised man leaning on a promise, and that promise was a single Seed. A son of Abraham is one who leans where Abraham leaned.

Revelation shows Abraham's true family, the "many nations" he was promised (Genesis 17:5): "a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9). They are counted by belief, not by blood.
