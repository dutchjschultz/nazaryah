## The Old Path

Yahuah promised, "then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent" (Zephaniah 3:9). The Hebrew is *safah berurah*, a pure lip. At Babel Yahuah confounded the lip so that men "may not understand one another's speech" (Genesis 11:7); the pure lip undoes Babel. When the Spirit came upon the seventy elders they prophesied, and Moses wished "that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Joel promised the same Spirit on sons, daughters, servants, and handmaids (Joel 2:28-29). At Shavuot that promise came, and every man asked, "how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?" (Acts 2:8). Yahuah "is not the author of confusion" (1 Corinthians 14:33). The gift He promised is a language understood, used to call on His Name. Every religion's answer is measured against that promise.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhist worship is full of chanting. In the Buddhist lands of Southeast Asia, such as Thailand, Burma, and Sri Lanka, monks and lay people chant in Pali, an ancient Indian language that almost none of them speak. The most common chant takes refuge in the Buddha, his teaching, and the order of monks. In Tibet, China, Korea, and Japan, Buddhists chant Sanskrit sounds and sutras in old forms of Chinese that ordinary worshippers often cannot follow.

Chanting is believed to bring merit, calm the mind, and protect from harm. Some chants, called *dharanis*, are short strings of syllables valued for their sound more than their meaning. Tibetan Buddhists carve and print mantras such as *Om mani padme hum* and spin them in prayer wheels, believing each turn of the wheel sends out the merit of the mantra written inside.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism, at its start, kept something worth noting. The early monastic rules record that when two monks asked to recast the Buddha's sayings into the formal language of the Hindu priests, he refused and allowed each to learn the teaching in his own speech. That is the pattern of Shavuot, when each man heard "in our own tongue, wherein we were born" (Acts 2:8), and of Ezra's Levites, who "caused them to understand the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8).

Beyond that, Buddhism kept nothing of the pure lip, because its chants call on no God.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It chants in tongues the worshipper does not know.** Buddhism turned away from its own early rule. Pali in Thailand and old Chinese in Japan are no more understood by the worshipper than Latin was by a European peasant. Paul said, "except ye utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how shall it be known what is spoken? for ye shall speak into the air" (1 Corinthians 14:9). A chant no one understands is speaking into the air.

**It counts sound as merit.** A prayer wheel turns words that no mouth speaks and no ear hears, and the turning is counted as merit. Yahushua warned against "vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking" (Matthew 6:7). Isaiah warned against those "that peep, and that mutter" (Isaiah 8:19). Speech Yahuah honors comes from understanding and a heart, not from a wheel.

**It calls on no one.** Zephaniah gave the reason for the pure lip: "that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah." Buddhist chants take refuge in a teacher, a teaching, and an order of monks. Scripture names a different refuge: "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" (Psalm 46:1), and "my refuge, is in God" (Psalm 62:7). A chant that calls on no God cannot be the pure lip.

## The Verdict

Buddhism began with a founder who wanted his teaching heard in each man's own tongue and ended with chants in languages its people do not speak, sounds counted as merit, and wheels that pray by turning. It calls on no God and asks no one for help. Zephaniah's pure lip is a people calling on Yahuah's Name with understanding and one consent. Buddhism has the chanting and none of the calling.

Revelation shows who the redeemed speak to: "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation" (Revelation 5:9). Every tongue is there, and every one is singing to Someone. The pure lip is never a chant into the air; it is a song of the redeemed to the One who redeemed them.
