## The Old Path

Fire went out from Yahuah and "devoured" Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:2). Sodom was left "brimstone, and salt, and burning" (Deuteronomy 29:23), and Peter says Yahuah turned those cities "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). Malachi says the coming day "shall burn as an oven," leaving the wicked "neither root nor branch," and they "shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:1, 3). David says the wicked "shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away" (Psalm 37:20). Yahushua warned of Him who "is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28), and Paul calls the end "everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). The fire is eternal in what it does, not in how long it keeps burning. "The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23), not endless life in pain.

## What Christianity Teaches

Most of Christianity, Catholic and Protestant, teaches that the wicked will be kept alive and conscious in hell forever. Augustine argued for it at length in the fifth century, and it became the church's settled position. The Westminster Confession of 1646 says the wicked "shall be cast into eternal torments." Preachers have described it as a fire that burns and never consumes, where a sinner suffers for billions of years and has not shortened his sentence by a single day.

The teaching rests on one belief underneath it: that every human soul is immortal and cannot die. If the soul cannot die, then the fire cannot end it, and the punishment must go on forever.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the truth that there is a judgment, that it is by fire, and that the outcome is eternal. Yahushua did speak of "everlasting fire" (Matthew 25:41) and "everlasting punishment" (Matthew 25:46). The church is right that those words are in the Book and right that the sentence can never be reversed. It went wrong on what the sentence is.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It confuses the punishment with the punishing.** "Everlasting punishment" (Matthew 25:46) is a punishment whose result lasts forever. Paul says what that result is: "everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). Destruction that lasts forever is destruction that is never undone, not a destroying that never gets done. Sodom shows it. Jude says Sodom suffered "the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 7), and Sodom is not burning today. Peter says Yahuah turned it "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). Eternal fire left ash.

**It builds on an immortal soul that Scripture never gives.** Only Yahuah "hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:16). Man is told to "seek for" it (Romans 2:7) and will "put on immortality" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53). The wicked never put it on. The idea of a soul that cannot die came from Greek philosophy, not from Moses, and it was the serpent who first preached it: "Ye shall not surely die" (Genesis 3:4). Eternal torment is that first lie carried into the lake of fire.

**It reads Revelation without the books Revelation quotes.** The church's strongest verse is that "the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever" (Revelation 14:11). But John took those words from Isaiah, who said of Edom that "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever" (Isaiah 34:10). Edom is not burning today. The rising smoke is a picture of a judgment that is complete and permanent, the same picture Abraham saw when the smoke of Sodom "went up as the smoke of a furnace" (Genesis 19:28) over a city already gone.

**It makes Yahuah crueler than His Word.** Yahuah says, "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked" (Ezekiel 33:11). Eternal torment has Him keeping billions alive for the single purpose of hurting them without end. That is not the Judge who weighs a case. It is the Molech of the Valley of Hinnom, the god to whom Judah burned its children alive, a thing Yahuah said He "commanded them not, neither came it into my heart" (Jeremiah 7:31).

## The Verdict

Christianity is right that the sentence is eternal and wrong about what the sentence is. The wages of sin is death (Romans 6:23), and the church has turned it into an eternal life spent in pain. The fire of Scripture consumes. The wicked become smoke and ashes, and they are gone.

Revelation names the end in plain words: "death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death" (Revelation 20:14). It is called death, not torment. And after it, "there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain" (Revelation 21:4). No more pain anywhere, including in a hell burning off in some corner of the new creation.
