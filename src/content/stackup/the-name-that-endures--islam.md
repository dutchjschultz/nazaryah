## The Old Path

At the burning bush Yahuah gave Moses His Name and bound it to time: "this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations" (Exodus 3:15). Moses later warned Israel to "fear this glorious and fearful name, Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 28:58). The psalmist sang, "Thy name, O Yahuah, endureth for ever" (Psalm 135:13), and Malachi saw a book of remembrance written for those who "thought upon his name" (Malachi 3:16). A title tells what someone is; a name tells who He is. Yahuah gave a Name to be remembered and spoken, and nothing in Scripture ever withdrew it. Every religion's answer is measured against that standing command.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam calls God Allah. The word is Arabic for "God," from the same family of words as the Hebrew *Eloah* and *Elohim*, and Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews use it the same way in their own Bibles and prayers. It is the word for Deity. It is a title, not a personal name.

Islam also teaches that God has beautiful names. The Quran says, "to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them" (Surah 7:180), and near the close of one surah it gathers several together: the King, the Holy, the Creator, the Mighty (Surah 59:22-24). Muslim tradition counts ninety-nine of these names, and devout Muslims recite them on prayer beads. Every one of them is a description: the Merciful, the Forgiving, the All-Hearing. Not one of them is Yahuah.

The Quran also tells of Moses at the burning bush. In its account the voice from the fire says, "Indeed, I am Allah. There is no deity except Me" (Surah 20:14).

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept that God is one, and it never let anyone divide Him into three. It kept that God is to be called on and remembered. Remembrance of God, *dhikr*, stands at the center of Muslim devotion, and Scripture commands the same: "call upon his name" (Psalm 105:1).

Islam also kept that the descriptions of God are true and worth saying. Scripture is full of them. When Yahuah passed before Moses, He proclaimed Himself "merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth" (Exodus 34:6). A Muslim reciting "the Merciful" is saying something Moses heard on the mountain.

## Where Islam Falls

**It answered the bush with a title.** In Exodus, Moses asked plainly what name to give Israel (Exodus 3:13), and Yahuah answered with His Name and said it was "for ever" (Exodus 3:15). The one moment in Scripture when the Creator was asked His Name, and gave it, is the moment the Quran retells with the word for God in its place.

**It kept the descriptions and dropped the Name they belong to.** On the mountain, Yahuah "proclaimed the name of Yahuah" first, and the descriptions came after it (Exodus 34:5-6). In Scripture the mercy and the patience describe the One whose Name was just spoken. In Islam the descriptions stand alone, ninety-nine of them, and the Name is missing from the list. Yahuah said, "I am Yahuah: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another" (Isaiah 42:8), and the psalmist prayed that men would know "that thou, whose name alone is Yahuah, art the most high over all the earth" (Psalm 83:18).

**A title can be worn by any god.** Before Muhammad, the Arabs of Mecca already used the word Allah for a high god, and they served other gods beside him. The Quran itself names three of them, al-Lat, al-Uzza, and Manat (Surah 53:19-20). Islam swept the other gods away, but it kept the shared title. That is the very danger Yahuah warned of through Hosea, when He told Israel to stop calling Him by a word that also belonged to Baal: "I will take away the names of Baalim out of her mouth" (Hosea 2:16-17). A title can be shared. A Name cannot.

## The Verdict

Islam guarded the oneness of God better than the church that made Him three, and it is right that He is merciful, mighty, and the Creator. But it guarded a title. Moses asked for the Name and received it, and Islam retold the scene without it. Ninety-nine names were counted, and the one Name that Yahuah said would stand "for ever" was not among them.

Revelation shows what those who belong to Him carry. John saw the Lamb on Mount Sion with the redeemed, "having his Father's name written in their foreheads" (Revelation 14:1). Not a title, not a description, but the Father's Name, the same one given at the bush.
