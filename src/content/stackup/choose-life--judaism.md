## The Old Path

Moses set two roads before Israel: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Joshua said the same: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). Yahuah takes "no pleasure in the death of him that dieth... wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9). A choice made can be unmade. "Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33), and the righteous man who turns away dies in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). The blood on the door opened the road out of Egypt (Exodus 12:13), but the people still had to walk it, and "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Yahuah declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), but knowing is not forcing. The choice was always the person's to make.

## What Judaism Teaches

Of the five religions, Judaism holds most firmly to free choice. Deuteronomy 30:19 is one of its central texts on the subject. Judaism teaches that every person is free to choose good or evil, that Yahuah knows all things and still leaves the choice to man, and that each person is judged for what he chooses. It teaches that a man can always turn back through repentance, and it keeps a yearly season of repentance from Yom Teruah, which it calls Rosh Hashanah, to Yom Kippur.

Judaism does not teach that anyone is chosen for eternal life or death before birth. It also rejects Yahushua as the Messiah and looks for a Messiah still to come.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the Old Path on this doctrine almost whole. It reads Deuteronomy 30:19 as Moses meant it. It holds with Ezekiel that the soul that sins shall die and the one who turns shall live (Ezekiel 18:20-21). It refuses any teaching that the choice was settled before a man was born. On choice, the Jew stands far closer to Moses than the Calvinist does, and closer than Islam's decree or the karma of the East.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It chooses the road and refuses the Life.** Moses set life before Israel, and that life was always tied to the one Yahuah would send. Yahushua told the leaders of His day, "ye will not come to me, that ye might have life" (John 5:40). John wrote, "He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life" (1 John 5:12). Judaism keeps the freedom to choose and uses it to turn away from the Son.

**It refuses the Prophet Moses named.** Moses said Yahuah would raise up "a Prophet... like unto me," and "whosoever will not hearken unto my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him" (Deuteronomy 18:18-19). Peter preached that this Prophet had come (Acts 3:22-23). The choice Moses set before Israel included this one. Saying no to the Prophet is a choice with the very cost Moses named.

**It chooses without the blood.** The people who heard "choose life" had already been passed over by the lamb's blood in Egypt (Exodus 12:13). The choice was a choice to walk after deliverance. Judaism keeps Yom Kippur with no sacrifice and teaches that repentance alone opens the way. But "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). Repentance is the turning. The blood is the door.

## The Verdict

Judaism understands the choice better than any other religion here. It keeps Moses's words, it rejects the idea of a fixed fate, and it holds every person to account. But the choice Moses set was between life and death, and life is in the Son whom Yahuah sent. Judaism has the freedom to choose and has chosen the road without the gate.

Revelation shows that the choice is still open to every one who hears. The Son says, "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him" (Revelation 3:20). The door is not forced. It is opened by the one who chooses, and it is still open to the natural branches.
