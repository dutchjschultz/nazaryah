## The Old Path

At Sinai, Yahuah called the whole nation, not one class, "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). When Joshua wanted two men stopped from prophesying in the camp, Moses answered, "would God that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Peter gave the same words to every believer: "a royal priesthood, an holy nation" (1 Peter 2:9). John wrote that Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). Yahushua forbade the titles that lift one man over the rest: "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah; and all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). Elders and overseers still serve, teach, and watch over the flock, but as brothers among brothers, never as a priesthood standing between the people and Yahuah. "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). There is no second.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Traditional Hinduism divides society into four classes, called *varnas*. At the top are the Brahmins, the priests and teachers. Below them are the warriors and rulers, then the merchants and farmers, then the laborers. Below all four, and outside them, were those once called untouchables, now known as Dalits. A hymn in the Rig Veda, the Purusha Sukta, says the Brahmin came from the mouth of the cosmic man, the warrior from his arms, the merchant from his thighs, and the laborer from his feet. A man's class is fixed by his birth.

Traditionally, only Brahmins may recite the Vedas in the great rituals, serve as temple priests, and perform the rites at weddings and funerals. For centuries Dalits were barred from many temples. India's constitution of 1950 outlawed untouchability, but caste still shapes temple life in much of the country.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism knows that man needs a priest. It understands that coming near the holy is a serious thing and that the priest must be set apart and clean. Israel was taught the same: the priests were to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (Leviticus 10:10).

Hinduism has also heard protest from inside its own house. The *bhakti* movements of medieval India taught that any person, of any caste, could love and approach the divine directly, and some of their best-known poets, such as Ravidas, came from the lowest castes.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It makes the few the priests and the many their servants.** At Sinai the whole nation was named "a kingdom of priests" (Exodus 19:6), and Peter gave that name to every believer (1 Peter 2:9). Hinduism turned it upside down. It gave the priesthood to one class and taught the rest that they were made lower from the very body of their god.

**It ranks men by birth, and Yahuah does not.** Israel's priests of Aaron were indeed born to their office, but that was a shadow and it pointed beyond itself. The High Priest Yahuah appointed came "after the order of Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4), a priest with no recorded line, made "not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life" (Hebrews 7:16). Through Him, the stranger once kept outside is brought in: "mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:7). Peter learned that "God is no respecter of persons" (Acts 10:34). No man is born unclean before Yahuah because of his father's trade.

**Its priests serve other gods.** A priest is a servant of the One he stands before. The Brahmin serves many gods and their images. Yahuah said, "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" (Exodus 20:3). A priesthood to other gods is not a lesser priesthood. It is the priesthood of Baal that Elijah faced on Carmel (1 Kings 18:19-21).

## The Verdict

Hinduism kept the idea that man needs a priest and then shut the priesthood inside one caste, leaving the rest of its people born below the altar. The Old Path is the opposite in every part: one God, one Mediator, and a whole people made priests through Him, with no man shut out by birth.

Revelation shows who stands in that priesthood at the end. The song of the redeemed says of the Lamb, "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation; And hast made us unto our God kings and priests" (Revelation 5:9-10). Out of every kindred. Not one caste, but every people the blood has reached.
