## The Old Path

Melchizedek met Abram with "bread and wine" (Genesis 14:18). The Law never separated the two: every burnt offering carried a meal offering and a drink offering beside it (Numbers 15:4-5). The night before He died, Yahushua gave His disciples both, the bread, "my body which is given for you," and the cup, "the new testament in my blood" (Luke 22:19-20). The cup is the blood and the entry, for "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). The bread is the body and the walk, living "by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4). Paul joined them: "justified by his blood… saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10). The cup starts a man. The bread finishes him.

## What Judaism Teaches

Since the Romans destroyed the temple in AD 70, Judaism has offered no sacrifices. It teaches that prayer, repentance, and charity now take the place of the altar. On the High Holy Days the prayer book says that repentance, prayer, and charity turn aside the severe decree. Yom Kippur is kept as a day of fasting and confession, with no goat, no high priest, and no blood. Judaism often points to Hosea's words, "so will we render the calves of our lips" (Hosea 14:2), as the warrant for prayer in place of offerings.

Wine and bread are still at the center of Jewish life. Every Sabbath begins with a blessing over a cup of wine, called *Kiddush*, followed by a blessing over two loaves of bread. At the Passover Seder four cups of wine are drunk and unleavened bread is eaten. A roasted shank bone sits on the Seder plate as a reminder of the lamb, but in most Jewish homes no lamb is eaten.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the table. It never dropped the wine and the bread, and it still blesses them together every week, as Melchizedek brought them out together to Abram. It kept the Torah walk, the commandments, the Sabbath, and the feasts, which is the bread in the old pattern. It kept Pesach as a memorial forever, as it was commanded: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations" (Exodus 12:14). It has never said obedience is optional.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It put prayer where the blood belongs.** "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). On the Day of Atonement the high priest carried the blood within the veil and sprinkled it before the mercy seat (Leviticus 16:14-15). Repentance always mattered, but it was never the covering. Hosea's "calves of our lips" is praise that follows forgiveness; the same verse begins with "take away all iniquity, and receive us graciously" (Hosea 14:2). Prayer is the answer of a forgiven man. It is not the price of his forgiveness.

**It refused the Lamb the shank bone remembers.** The Seder plate holds a bone in memory of a lamb, and Isaiah wrote of the one "brought as a lamb to the slaughter" (Isaiah 53:7), on whom "Yahuah hath laid… the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:6). The Lamb Yahuah provided came, and the house that keeps the memorial of the lamb refused Him.

**It reads the lost temple the wrong way.** Daniel foretold that Messiah would "cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease" (Daniel 9:27), and He did, at His death in AD 31. Forty years later Yahuah let the temple fall. The copy was taken away because the real offering had been made once for all (Hebrews 9:11-12). Judaism reads AD 70 as a gap to be filled with prayer. It was the end of the shadow because the body had come.

**It walks the road without entering the door.** The bread is the walk, and Judaism walks. But the walk follows the entry, and the entry is the blood. A people keeping Torah without the blood are keeping the bread without the cup.

## The Verdict

Judaism set the table every Sabbath and left the cup's meaning out. It kept the wine and the bread, the walk and the memorial, and refused the blood they point to. Prayer, repentance, and charity are the fruit of a man brought in by the blood. They cannot bring him in.

Revelation names the One Judaism waits for without knowing He came. He is "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" (Revelation 13:8). The Lamb in Revelation is the One Yahuah provided, and His blood opens the door the Torah walk was always meant to follow.
