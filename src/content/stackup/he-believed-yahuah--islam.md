## The Old Path

Before Sinai, before circumcision, before the knife on Moriah, Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). To believe is *aman*, to lean your whole weight on something; it is the root of "amen." To count is *chashab*, a bookkeeper's word: righteousness was credited to an account that had not earned it. Abram had no written commandments, no covenant sign, no priesthood, and no tabernacle. He had a promise of a seed, and he leaned on it. Every pronoun in the chapter is singular: one God speaks, one man trusts, one God credits. The same word runs through the prophets: "Believe in Yahuah your God, so shall ye be established" (2 Chronicles 20:20), and "the just shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). Hebrews lays "faith toward God" among the first stones (Hebrews 6:1), and Genesis had already set it in place.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam makes Abraham, called Ibrahim, its model believer. The Quran says Ibrahim was neither a Jew nor a Christian, but upright in faith and one who submitted to Allah (Surah 3:67). The Arabic word there is *muslim*, one who submits. When his Lord told him to submit, he answered that he submitted to the Lord of the worlds (Surah 2:131). The Quran calls him *hanif*, one who turned from idols to the one God, and says Allah took him as a friend (Surah 4:125). It tells how he broke his people's idols (Surah 21:51-70).

The word *Islam* itself means submission. In Muslim teaching, faith (*iman*) is belief in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and His decree, and it must be shown by the duties of prayer, fasting, alms, and pilgrimage. Abraham is honored as the one who submitted perfectly, above all when he was ready to sacrifice his son, whom most Muslims hold to be Ishmael (Surah 37:102-107).

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the one God. It is right that Abraham was neither Jew nor Christian, for he lived before both, and right that he turned from idols. Joshua said Abraham's father "served other gods" beyond the river (Joshua 24:2), and Yahuah called Abram out of that (Genesis 12:1).

It kept Abraham as the friend of God. Scripture calls him that three times: "Abraham my friend" (Isaiah 41:8), "Abraham thy friend for ever" (2 Chronicles 20:7), and "he was called the Friend of God" (James 2:23).

And it kept a ransom on the mountain. The Quran says the son was ransomed with a great sacrifice (Surah 37:107). That is a faint memory of Moriah, where Abraham said "God will provide himself a lamb" (Genesis 22:8).

## Where Islam Falls

**It puts submission where trust belongs.** Submission is a servant bowing to a master's order. *Aman* is a man leaning his weight on a promise. Genesis 15 has no order in it at all. Yahuah promised, Abram leaned, and righteousness was credited. Abram obeyed later and obeyed well (Genesis 26:5), but the obeying came after the believing, never in place of it. Islam turns the credit into wages.

**It changes the son of the promise.** The promise Abram leaned on was a seed through Sarah. Yahuah said, "in Isaac shall thy seed be called" (Genesis 21:12), and on Moriah He named "thine only son Isaac" (Genesis 22:2). Islam moves the mountain to Ishmael and loses the line of the promise.

**It denies the Seed he believed in.** Paul says the promise was not to many seeds, "but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Messiah" (Galatians 3:16). Yahushua said, "Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad" (John 8:56). Abraham's belief looked forward to the Son. Islam denies that Yahuah has a Son (Surah 19:35), and so it honors Abraham while denying the one thing he trusted.

**It keeps the ram and loses the Lamb.** Moriah was a picture. The ram caught in the thicket stood in place of the son; then the Lamb Yahuah provided stood in place of us. Islam keeps the ransom on the mountain and rejects the Lamb it pointed to.

## The Verdict

Islam kept Abraham's one God and his break with idols, and it kept him as the friend of God. But it rebuilt him as a man who earned his place by submitting, and stripped away the promise he leaned on. Abraham was counted righteous because he believed Yahuah about a Seed. Islam honors the man and rejects the Seed. That is submission in place of trust.

Revelation shows who the ram on Moriah pointed to: "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" (Revelation 13:8). Yahuah provided the Lamb before Abraham ever climbed the mountain, and the Lamb Abraham trusted Him to provide is the one Islam says was never slain.
