## The Old Path

When Yahuah told Abraham about Sodom, He said, "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21). Judgment in Scripture is a court case. The Hebrew *shaphat* means to judge, to decide a case, to pass sentence after a hearing. Abraham pleaded, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). The two men went on toward Sodom while Abraham "stood yet before Yahuah" (Genesis 18:22), and those two are named "two angels" (Genesis 19:1): one Judge, not three. Then the smoke of the plain went up "as the smoke of a furnace" (Genesis 19:28). Sodom was the demonstration, turned "into ashes" as "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Peter 2:6). Solomon wrote that "God shall bring every work into judgment" (Ecclesiastes 12:14), and Daniel saw the dead rise, "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). Hebrews names eternal judgment among the first principles (Hebrews 6:2). The sentence is sure, the day is set, and the Judge is one.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism teaches karma as a law of cause and effect with no Creator behind it. The Buddha taught that intention itself is karma: what a person means in his heart, and acts on, shapes what comes to him. The results ripen over many lifetimes, in births among gods, humans, animals, hungry ghosts, or beings in the hells. Those hells are long and painful, but they are not forever. When the karma that sent a being there runs out, he is born again somewhere else.

No judge is needed, because the law runs itself. The way out is the Eightfold Path, walked by one's own effort, until craving ends and the cycle stops in *nirvana*.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept the truth that the heart matters. Yahuah said it to Samuel: "Yahuah looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7). The Buddha's teaching that intention is the root of a deed agrees with Yahuah's word that He gives "every man according to his ways" (Jeremiah 17:10). Buddhism also kept sowing and reaping, and its five precepts against killing, stealing, sexual wrong, lying, and drunkenness hold much of the second tablet of the Law.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has a heart to judge but no one to judge it.** The same verse that names the heart names the Judge: "I Yahuah search the heart, I try the reins" (Jeremiah 17:10). The verse before it explains why a Judge is needed: "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?" (Jeremiah 17:9). A heart cannot sit in judgment on itself. Abraham did not plead with a law of cause and effect. He stood before "the Judge of all the earth" (Genesis 18:25).

**It has no final sentence.** Buddhism's hells end and the wheel turns again. Scripture's sentence is final. The wicked "shall be punished with everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9), a destruction that is never undone, not a stay in a dark room before the next birth. Sodom was the demonstration. It was turned "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6), and it has not come back.

**It offers release by effort, with no pardon.** The Eightfold Path asks a man to free himself. The Psalms answer that no man can: "None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him" (Psalm 49:7). Then the psalmist names the only one who can: "But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave" (Psalm 49:15). Yahuah is the Redeemer, and the ransom is the Lamb He provided. Release earned by effort is no release from a sentence already passed.

## The Verdict

Buddhism saw that every deed begins in the heart and bears fruit, and then left the heart alone in an empty courtroom. With no Judge it has no pardon, and with no final day it has no end, only a wheel of reward and punishment that turns until a man frees himself. Scripture puts a Judge on the bench, a day on the calendar, and a ransom in the hand of the Redeemer. Cause and effect is not a court.

Revelation shows the court Buddhism has no room for: "I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it" (Revelation 20:11). The throne has someone on it, and before Him "the dead, small and great, stand" (Revelation 20:12).
