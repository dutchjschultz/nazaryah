## The Old Path

Leviticus 26 is a long warning, and at the bottom of it Yahuah wrote the road home. If Israel would "confess their iniquity" and their "uncircumcised hearts be humbled," then "will I remember my covenant with Jacob" (Leviticus 26:40-42). The return was provided for before the curse ever fell. To repent is *shuv*, to turn around and walk the other way; to confess is *yadah*, to acknowledge openly, out loud. Joel cried, "turn ye even to me with all your heart… rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13), and Ezekiel gave the reason: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth… wherefore turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). One God receives the turn, and He Himself remembers. The turn was never the price. The price was blood Yahuah gave "upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls" (Leviticus 17:11). So Hebrews sets "repentance from dead works" first among the foundations (Hebrews 6:1).

## What Christianity Teaches

The church has two answers, and both put something between the sinner and Yahuah.

The Roman church made confession a sacrament called penance. The sinner tells his sins to a priest, the priest assigns a penance, and the priest pronounces the absolution. In 1215 the Fourth Lateran Council required every Catholic to confess to a priest at least once a year. The wooden confession booth came later, in the 1500s, after the Council of Trent.

The Protestant revival churches built a different thing. In the 1830s Charles Finney set out an "anxious bench" at the front of his meetings, and from it came the altar call: the sinner walks the aisle, repeats a short prayer after the preacher, and is told he is saved. Repentance became one moment, done once.

Both are usually joined to a third teaching: that the Law was nailed to the cross and no longer tells a believer what sin is.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the blood. It knows that "the blood of Yahushua Messiah his Son cleanseth us from all sin" (1 John 1:7), and that the turn alone cannot pay. On that point it stands above every other religion in this series.

It kept confession aloud, too. The Catholic says his sins to a man's face, and the altar call makes a man say out loud that he is a sinner. Both are a broken echo of *yadah*.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It put a priest in the booth.** Leviticus 26 has no man standing between Israel and Yahuah. David confessed straight to Him: "I acknowledge my sin unto thee… and thou forgavest" (Psalm 32:5). John wrote, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us" (1 John 1:9). There is "one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5), and He is not a priest in a box. Yahushua said, "call no man your father upon the earth" (Matthew 23:9).

**It shrank the turn to a walk down an aisle.** *Shuv* is a change of direction for the whole life. Ezekiel said, "turn yourselves from all your transgressions" (Ezekiel 18:30). Paul preached that men "should repent and turn to God, and do works meet for repentance" (Acts 26:20). A prayer repeated once, with no new walk after it, is not a turn. It is a pause.

**It threw away the map of the road.** John defined sin: "sin is the transgression of the law" (1 John 3:4). Paul said, "I had not known sin, but by the law" (Romans 7:7). A church that says the Law is gone cannot tell a man what to repent of. The blood opens the door; the commandments are the walk beyond it. Remove the Law, and repentance from dead works becomes repentance from nothing in particular.

**It made the One who remembers into three.** Every word in Leviticus 26 is singular: "I will remember." The God who receives the turn is one, and Yahushua prayed to Him as "the only true God" (John 17:3). A creed that sends repentance to three persons has added to the road home.

## The Verdict

Christianity kept the blood, which every other religion lost, and then built a booth in front of it and an aisle away from it. The priest stands where Yahuah alone should stand, the altar call shortens a life's turn to a moment, and the Law that shows sin has been thrown out. The road home in Leviticus 26 is still open: turn, confess to Yahuah, stand on His blood, and walk in His commandments.

Revelation gives the church this same call in the Son's own words: "Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works" (Revelation 2:5). Remember, repent, and do. That was the old path, and it still is.
