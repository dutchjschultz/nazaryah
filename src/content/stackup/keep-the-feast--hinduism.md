## The Old Path

Yahuah gave Israel a memorial in Egypt, and it was a feast: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever" (Exodus 12:14). In Scripture, to remember is to do. The fringes were given so that Israel would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39). The feasts belong to Him: "the feasts of Yahuah… even these are my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). On the night before He died, at a preparation meal on the 13th of Aviv, Yahushua took bread and said, "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). He was not starting a church service. He was laying the Pesach memorial on Himself, the Lamb who died on the 14th at the hour the lambs were slain. Paul drew the line plainly: "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8). The memorial is the feast, kept as Yahuah gave it.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism has more festivals than any religion on earth, and each belongs to one or more of its gods. Diwali, the festival of lights, falls on the new moon of the autumn month of Kartika. Homes are lit with clay lamps, fireworks are set off, and families pray to Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth. In North India it also marks the god Rama's return to Ayodhya. Holi comes on the full moon of the spring month of Phalguna. The night before, bonfires are lit to recall the burning of the demoness Holika, and the next day people throw colored powder and water on each other, a custom tied to stories of the god Krishna. At Ganesh Chaturthi, clay images of the elephant-headed god are worshipped, then carried in procession and sunk in rivers or the sea.

The Hindu religious calendar is lunisolar. Its months follow the moon, and an extra month is added from time to time to keep the festivals in their seasons.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept something most of the world lost. It reckons its months by the moon and keeps its year in step with the sun, so its festivals return in their proper seasons. That is close to the pattern of Genesis 1:14, where the lights were given "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years." It also never forgot that a people must gather and feast together.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**Its feasts are kept to other gods.** Yahuah commanded, "make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth," and in the same breath, "Three times thou shalt keep a feast unto me in the year" (Exodus 23:13-14). Every great Hindu festival is kept in the name of another god. A feast to Lakshmi or Krishna is the very thing the commandment forbids.

**Israel was told not to learn the nations' feasts.** Moses warned Israel not to ask, "How did these nations serve their gods? even so will I do likewise." Yahuah answered, "Thou shalt not do so unto Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 12:30-31). Even when Israel tried to keep "a feast to Yahuah" with a golden calf at its center, it was a great sin (Exodus 32:5, 31). Joy does not make a feast holy. The One who appointed it does.

**It has no memorial of deliverance.** The feasts of Yahuah tell one story: the Lamb slain, the bread without leaven, the firstfruits raised, the harvest gathered, and the Judge who comes. Hindu festivals tell the stories of many gods and their battles. None remembers a people bought out of bondage by blood. "When I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13) has no place in them.

## The Verdict

Hinduism kept the moon and the sun in their places, and kept the habit of feasting, but it keeps every feast to the wrong god. Diwali's lamps honor a goddess of wealth, Holi's fires honor the gods of India, and none of it remembers the Lamb. The feasts Yahuah called "my feasts" are not on its calendar.

Revelation shows the city where no festival of lamps is needed: "the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof" (Revelation 21:23). The word the King James renders "light" is the Greek *luchnos*, a lamp. The glory is Yahuah's, and the Lamb is the lamp that carries it. That is the only festival of light that lasts.
