## The Old Path

Melchizedek met Abram with "bread and wine" (Genesis 14:18). The Law never separated the two: every burnt offering carried a meal offering and a drink offering beside it (Numbers 15:4-5). The night before He died, Yahushua gave His disciples both, the bread, "my body which is given for you," and the cup, "the new testament in my blood" (Luke 22:19-20). The cup is the blood and the entry, for "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). The bread is the body and the walk, living "by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4). Paul joined them: "justified by his blood… saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10). The cup starts a man. The bread finishes him.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism teaches karma, the law that every deed brings back its own result. Good deeds bring good, and evil deeds bring suffering, in this life or the next. The soul, called the *atman*, is reborn again and again in a cycle called *samsara*, and each new life is shaped by the karma of the last. The goal is *moksha*, release from the cycle altogether.

Hindus teach several paths to that release: the path of knowledge, the path of right action and duty, and the path of devotion to a chosen god. Wrongs may be lessened by penance, called *prayaschitta*: fasting, pilgrimage, gifts, and bathing in holy rivers such as the Ganges. In its oldest scriptures, the Vedas, Hindu worship centered on fire offerings, and some of those offerings were animals. Animal sacrifice still goes on in some temples today, especially to the goddesses Kali and Durga. In daily worship food is offered to an image of a god and then eaten by the worshipper as a blessing, called *prasad*.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the truth that sin has a cost and that the cost does not go away by being ignored. Scripture agrees: "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). It kept the old memory that men came before God with offerings on fire, as Noah and Abraham did (Genesis 8:20; 22:13). It also kept a walk of duty, much of which follows the second tablet of the Law: honor for parents, care for the poor, and a ban on killing, stealing, and lying. Hinduism knows a debt is owed. It does not know who paid it.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It makes a man pay his own debt over many lives.** Karma says each soul must work off what it owes, life after life. Scripture says "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). There is one life, not a wheel of them. And the debt is too great for any man: "None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him" (Psalm 49:7). Hinduism hands the bill to the debtor and gives him a thousand lifetimes to pay what one lifetime shows he never can.

**It has penance but no blood.** Micah asked whether thousands of rams or "the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul" could please Yahuah (Micah 6:7). The answer was no. No man's gift buys his own covering. Yahuah provided the covering Himself, "a lamb for a burnt offering" (Genesis 22:8). A bath in the Ganges washes the skin. "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11), and that blood is the Lamb Yahuah gave, not a goat offered to Kali.

**It sets a table for other gods.** Prasad is a meal first set before an idol. Paul warned that "the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God," and said, "Ye cannot be partakers of the Master's table, and of the table of devils" (1 Corinthians 10:20-21). Hinduism has a holy meal, and it is eaten at the wrong table.

## The Verdict

Hinduism knows the debt and refuses the payment. It keeps a walk of duty with no door at the start, and it sets a table with bread offered to other gods. The cup of the Lamb is the one thing karma cannot hold, because it is a debt paid by Yahuah through the Son He gave, not by the debtor.

Revelation shows a great crowd from "all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" in white. They did not work their robes clean through many lives. They "washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9, 14). One washing, in one blood, finished what a thousand rebirths never could.
