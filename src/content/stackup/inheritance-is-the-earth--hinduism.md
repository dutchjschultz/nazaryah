## The Old Path

Yahuah promised Abraham "all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession" (Genesis 17:8). The Psalmist wrote, "The heaven, even the heavens, are Yahuah's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men" (Psalm 115:16), and "The righteous shall inherit the land, and dwell therein for ever" (Psalm 37:29). Yahushua said the same: "the meek… shall inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5). The dead do not go anywhere at death. They "sleep in the dust of the earth" until they awake (Daniel 12:2), and Job knew his Redeemer would "stand at the latter day upon the earth" (Job 19:25). The hope is resurrection to "new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). The inheritance was never heaven. It is the earth made new.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism teaches that every living being has an *atman*, an eternal self, that passes from body to body through birth, death, and rebirth. This endless round is called *samsara*, and what a person does in one life, his karma, shapes the life he is born into next. The goal of life is *moksha*, release from the round.

Hindu schools describe that release in different ways. In the school called Advaita, the self is found to be one with Brahman, the single reality behind all things, and the person as a separate person is gone, like a drop returning to the ocean. In the devotional schools, the released soul goes to live forever in the presence of its chosen god, as in Vaikuntha, the heavenly realm of Vishnu. In both, the goal is to leave this world behind. The earth is a place to escape, not a place to inherit.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept very little here, but not nothing. It knows that this present life is not all there is, and that death is not the final word. It knows the world as it now stands is full of suffering and is not what it ought to be. Scripture agrees that "the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now" (Romans 8:22). Hinduism also holds that what a man does matters and follows him, and Scripture agrees that "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It runs from the earth Yahuah gave.** Yahuah made the earth and called it "very good" (Genesis 1:31), then gave it to the children of men (Psalm 115:16). Creation groans, but Paul says it groans in hope, because it "shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption" (Romans 8:21). The answer to a broken earth is an earth restored, not an earth abandoned. Hinduism looks for the exit. Scripture looks for the new earth.

**It gives man an eternal self that Scripture never gave him.** Man did not have a soul placed in him. He "became a living soul" when Yahuah breathed into the dust (Genesis 2:7). At death "his breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth" (Psalm 146:4). There is no self that moves from body to body. There is one life, one death, and then the resurrection, for "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27).

**It ends the person instead of raising him.** In the highest goal of Advaita, the person dissolves into the All. Scripture's hope is the opposite. Job said, "in my flesh shall I see God: whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another" (Job 19:26-27). The inheritance belongs to persons raised whole, not to drops lost in an ocean.

**It earns release by its own path.** Moksha is reached by knowledge, devotion, or works carried through many lives. The inheritance of Abraham is not reached that way. It comes as a gift to the heirs, those who are Messiah's (Galatians 3:29), through the blood Yahuah provided. The delivered then walk in His commandments as heirs, not as souls working off karma.

## The Verdict

Hinduism stands at the opposite pole from the old path. Scripture promises a people raised from the dust to inherit a renewed earth. Hinduism promises an eternal self released from the earth, either dissolved into the All or carried off to a god's heaven. One is an inheritance. The other is an escape.

Revelation gives the song sung before the Lamb who was slain. The redeemed were gathered "out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation," and they sing, "we shall reign on the earth" (Revelation 5:9-10). Not off the earth, not out of it, but on it. India is in that song too, and its hope is the earth made new.
