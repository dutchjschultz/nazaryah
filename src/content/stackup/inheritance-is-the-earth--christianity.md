## The Old Path

Yahuah promised Abraham "all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession" (Genesis 17:8). The Psalmist wrote, "The heaven, even the heavens, are Yahuah's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men" (Psalm 115:16), and "The righteous shall inherit the land, and dwell therein for ever" (Psalm 37:29). Yahushua said the same: "the meek… shall inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5). The dead do not go anywhere at death. They "sleep in the dust of the earth" until they awake (Daniel 12:2), and Job knew his Redeemer would "stand at the latter day upon the earth" (Job 19:25). The hope is resurrection to "new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). The inheritance was never heaven. It is the earth made new.

## What Christianity Teaches

The church teaches that every person has an immortal soul, and that at death the soul of the believer leaves the body and goes straight to heaven to be with Jesus. Funerals say that the departed is "in a better place" and "looking down on us." Hymns speak of flying away to a home beyond the sky, of crossing over to mansions of glory, of streets of gold far above. John 14:2, "In my Father's house are many mansions," is read as a promise of a house in the clouds. Heaven, not the earth, is held out as the believer's final home.

The church's own creeds still confess "the resurrection of the body," but in most preaching it is an afterthought. If the soul is already in heaven, the resurrection adds little.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the words. Its Bibles still say "the meek shall inherit the earth," still promise "new heavens and a new earth," and still end with a city coming down. Its creeds still confess the resurrection of the body and the return of Messiah to judge. Many believers have read Revelation 21 and wondered why the city comes down instead of the saints going up.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It sends the dead to heaven, and Scripture says they sleep.** Daniel saw the dead "that sleep in the dust of the earth" (Daniel 12:2). "The dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Yahushua said plainly, "no man hath ascended up to heaven" (John 3:13). On the day of Shavuot, Peter said of King David, the man after Yahuah's own heart, "David is not ascended into the heavens" (Acts 2:34). If David is not there, the church's dead are not there either. They wait in the grave for the voice that calls them out (John 5:28-29).

**It trades the earth for the sky.** Yahuah kept the heavens for Himself and gave the earth to the children of men (Psalm 115:16). Abraham was promised land, the righteous are promised land, and the meek are promised the earth. Nowhere is the believer promised a home in the clouds. Even the "many mansions" are in the Father's house, and the Father's house comes down: "the tabernacle of God is with men" (Revelation 21:3).

**It got its heaven from the Greeks.** Plato taught that each soul was assigned to a star, and that the soul that lived well would return at death to dwell in its native star. Hesiod sang of the Isles of the Blessed, where heroes lived after death far from the toil of men. A soul flying off at death to a blessed place above is Greek thought dressed in church clothing. The Hebrew hope is resurrection and land.

**It makes the resurrection unnecessary.** Paul said that if there is no resurrection, "then they also which are fallen asleep in Messiah are perished" (1 Corinthians 15:18). He staked everything on the dead rising, because without it they stay dead. The church that says the dead are already in glory has quietly removed the one hope Paul preached.

## The Verdict

Christianity kept the verses and taught the opposite. It sends the believer up at death, while Scripture lays him down in the dust to wait. It promises the sky, while Yahuah promised the earth. It confesses the resurrection in its creeds and empties it in its pulpits. The inheritance is not heaven. It is the earth, made new, received by the dead raised from their graves.

Revelation ends the matter: "I saw a new heaven and a new earth," and the holy city came "down from God out of heaven," with a great voice saying, "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:1-3). The saints do not go up to Yahuah. Yahuah comes down to dwell with them on the earth.
