## The Old Path

On the fourth day Yahuah set lights in the sky "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years" (Genesis 1:14). The seasons are *moadim*, appointed meetings, and Yahuah calls them "my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). "He appointed the moon for seasons" (Psalm 104:19). The sun turns the year, and its first month is Abib (Deuteronomy 16:1; Exodus 12:2). The moon numbers the month from the new moon (Psalm 81:3). The Sabbaths fall on the eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second, and twenty-ninth of each month, and the day runs from dawn to dusk. The appointments still stand, kept "from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another" (Isaiah 66:23).

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism keeps holy days by the moon, called *uposatha*. Its own scriptures say the Buddha took up the practice because other teachers of his day already gathered on the moon days, and the king of Magadha asked that his monks do the same. In the Buddhist lands of Southeast Asia the observance days fall at the four faces of the moon: the eighth and fifteenth days of the waxing moon, the eighth day of the waning moon, and the last day of the month at the dark moon. In Thailand these are called *wan phra*, the monk's days.

On those days lay people go to the temple, bring food to the monks, hear teaching, and many take eight precepts instead of the usual five. They give up eating after noon, give up entertainment and adornment, and sleep on a plain mat. On the new and full moons the monks gather to recite their rule of discipline and confess their faults. The calendar is a sun and moon one, with a month added when needed. The great feasts fall on full moons: Vesak, which marks the Buddha's birth, enlightenment, and death, and the full moon that opens the three-month rains retreat.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept something no other religion in this series kept. Its observance days fall on the moon's quarters, about every seventh or eighth day, and they begin again with each month. That is the shape of the Sabbath in Scripture. The month opens on its new moon, and the Sabbaths fall on the eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second, and twenty-ninth, a count that starts over every month. "From one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another" (Isaiah 66:23) joins the two. The old peoples of India knew the moon set apart holy days in the month, and Buddhism kept that memory when the Roman world had buried it under a planetary week. It also kept a sun and moon year, so its feasts stay in their seasons.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**Its holy days belong to no one.** Yahuah says, "the seventh day is the sabbath of Yahuah thy God" (Exodus 20:10), and "my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you" (Exodus 31:13). A sign must point to someone. Buddhism has no Creator and no Lawgiver, so its moon days point to no one at all. A day set apart by the moon, with no Maker behind the moon, is a clock with no owner.

**Its day is spent on effort, not rest.** The Sabbath is rest from work because Yahuah rested and blessed the day (Genesis 2:2-3). The uposatha is a day of extra discipline, a harder fast and stricter vows, kept to gather merit toward one's own release. The prophet called the Sabbath "a delight, the holy of Yahuah, honourable," kept by "not doing thine own ways" (Isaiah 58:13). Buddhism makes the holy day a day for doing one's own way more strictly.

**Its feasts honor a man.** Yahuah's feasts tell the story of deliverance: Pesach, the wave sheaf, Shavuot, the trumpets, the atonement, the booths (Leviticus 23). Buddhism's great full moons celebrate the birth, awakening, and death of the Buddha. The appointed times have been given to a teacher who said there was no God to appoint them.

## The Verdict

Buddhism kept the shape and lost the Owner. Its moon days fall close to where Yahuah set His Sabbaths, and that is a real witness against the church's Roman week. But the days serve no one, the rest has become labor, and the feasts honor a man. A Sabbath without Yahuah is not His Sabbath.

Revelation shows the elders before the throne giving glory to the One who made the clock: "thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created" (Revelation 4:11). The moon that marks the Buddhist holy day was made, and its Maker is the One who owns the day.
