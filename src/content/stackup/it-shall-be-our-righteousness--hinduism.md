## The Old Path

Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That belief was credited at the door, and the credit was real. Two chapters later Yahuah told him, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (Genesis 17:1), and afterward He said Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Moses said the same to a people already brought "out of Egypt with a mighty hand" (Deuteronomy 6:21): "it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25). The psalmist said, "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), and Ezekiel said, "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20), upon him and not on another man's record. James joined the two: Abraham's belief "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Belief is credited first. Feet on the road follow.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism's word for right living is dharma, the order and duty that hold life together. Each person has his own duty, set largely by his caste and his stage of life. A priest's duty is not a warrior's, and a warrior's is not a laborer's. The Bhagavad Gita says it is better to do one's own duty poorly than another's duty well (Gita 3:35).

Doing one's duty, worshipping the gods, giving gifts, going on pilgrimage, and bathing in holy rivers gather merit, called *punya*. Wrong deeds gather the opposite. By the law of karma, that store of merit and demerit carries over from life to life and decides the next birth. Hinduism teaches several paths toward final release, called moksha: the path of works, the path of knowledge, and the path of devotion to a chosen god.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the truth that righteousness is lived and not only believed. Dharma is a whole way of life, from the home to the market, and Hinduism holds that a man must do what is right whether anyone sees it or not. It also teaches that deeds have results, which Scripture affirms: "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**Its righteousness changes with a man's birth.** Moses gave one standard: "all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25), one law for the native and the stranger (Exodus 12:49). Hinduism gives each caste its own duty, so what is right for one man is wrong for another. Yahuah's righteousness does not rise and fall with the house a man was born in. "All thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), for every man alike.

**No one counts it.** Abraham's righteousness was counted by Someone: "he counted it to him" (Genesis 15:6). Karma has no counter. It is an impersonal law, a store that fills and empties by itself, with no God who credits belief and no Judge who forgives. Merit in Hinduism is earned, never given. The door Abraham walked through was a gift from a Person before it was ever a walk.

**It spreads the account over many lives.** Hinduism lets a man work off his debt across birth after birth. Scripture gives one life to walk: "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). The righteousness of the righteous is upon him, and the sin of the sinner is upon him, in this life and no other (Ezekiel 18:20).

**It gathers merit from service to other gods.** The pilgrimages, the temple gifts, and the river baths are offered to the gods of Hinduism. Yahuah's first word to Israel was "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" (Exodus 20:3). Deeds done to other gods are not righteousness at all. They are the breaking of the first commandment.

## The Verdict

Hinduism saw that righteousness must be lived and that deeds bear fruit. But it measures righteousness by caste instead of by Yahuah's commandments, stores it up with no God to count it, stretches it across lives no one has lived twice, and spends it on gods who are not God. That is merit, not righteousness, and merit cannot open a door.

Revelation shows that the account is kept by a Judge and not by a wheel. John saw "the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened," and they "were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works" (Revelation 20:12). The books are His, the judgment is once, and the record belongs to the man himself.
