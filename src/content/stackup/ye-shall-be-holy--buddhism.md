## The Old Path

Yahuah charged the priests to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (Leviticus 10:10). In Leviticus 11 He named the beasts, fish, and birds Himself, so the line was never left to appetite or conscience. He gave the reason, "ye shall be holy; for I am holy," right after naming Himself "Yahuah that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 11:44-45). Deliverance came first, and holiness followed. Noah already knew the clean beasts from the unclean long before Sinai (Genesis 7:2), and Ezekiel condemned the priests who "put no difference" (Ezekiel 22:26). Yahuah told Abram, "walk before me, and be thou perfect," *tamim*, whole (Genesis 17:1), and Peter carried the same command to the believers: "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16). Holiness is not a feeling or a guess. It is the line Yahuah drew, walked by a people He has already delivered.

## What Buddhism Teaches

The first of the Buddhist precepts is to refrain from taking life. Buddhism teaches that every living creature is caught in the same round of rebirth, so that an animal may be a being who was once human, and a man may be reborn as an animal. Killing a creature brings bad karma that follows the killer into future lives.

Buddhist practice on food differs by region. In the Theravada lands, such as Thailand, Sri Lanka, and Burma, monks eat whatever is placed in their bowls on the morning alms round, meat included, as long as they did not see, hear, or suspect that the animal was killed for them. In the Mahayana lands, such as China, Korea, and Vietnam, monks and nuns take vows to eat no meat at all, and many lay Buddhists eat vegetarian on certain moon days. Some Mahayana writings teach that every animal has at some point been one's own mother or father in a past life.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept restraint. It knows that the appetite must be ruled and that a man's table is not a matter of mere pleasure. It kept a care for living creatures that Scripture shares: "A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast" (Proverbs 12:10), and Yahuah's Law forbade muzzling the ox that treads out the corn (Deuteronomy 25:4). Beyond that, Buddhism kept nothing of the line between clean and unclean. It has no list, because it has no one to give a list.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has no Holy One to be holy like.** The command is "ye shall be holy; for I am holy" (Leviticus 11:44). Holiness in Scripture means being set apart to Someone. Buddhism has no Creator and no Lawgiver, so it can teach discipline but never holiness. There is no one for a Buddhist to be set apart to.

**It rests its food rules on rebirth, and rebirth is false.** Man "became a living soul" when Yahuah breathed into him (Genesis 2:7), and "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). No man comes back as a goat, and no chicken was ever anyone's mother. Yahuah gave man rule over the beasts (Genesis 1:26), not kinship with them.

**It forbids what Yahuah created to be eaten.** Paul warned of teachers "commanding to abstain from meats, which God hath created to be received with thanksgiving of them which believe and know the truth" (1 Timothy 4:3). He said such meats are "sanctified by the word of God" (1 Timothy 4:5), and the word that sets them apart is Leviticus 11. A vow against all meat goes past the Law, while the alms bowl takes in pork and shellfish, so Yahuah's line is missing on both sides.

**It looks for purity with no deliverance.** The Buddhist hopes to work off bad karma by his own effort over many lives. Yahuah's order is deliverance first: He brought His people out, and then said "ye shall therefore be holy" (Leviticus 11:45). The blood of the Lamb He provided makes the man clean, and the clean table follows as the walk of the delivered.

## The Verdict

Buddhism has rules for eating but no holiness, because holiness needs a Holy One. It forbids meat for the sake of a rebirth that never happens, while it ignores the clean and unclean line that Yahuah set. Some of its monks refuse a clean lamb, and others accept unclean pork from the alms bowl. Discipline without the Holy One is only discipline.

Revelation gives the song the Buddhist has never sung: "Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy" (Revelation 15:4). Holiness begins with the One who alone is holy, and Buddhism has no one to sing it to.
