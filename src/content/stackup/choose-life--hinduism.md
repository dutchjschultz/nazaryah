## The Old Path

Moses set two roads before Israel: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Joshua said the same: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). Yahuah takes "no pleasure in the death of him that dieth... wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9). A choice made can be unmade. "Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33), and the righteous man who turns away dies in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). The blood on the door opened the road out of Egypt (Exodus 12:13), but the people still had to walk it, and "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Yahuah declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), but knowing is not forcing. The choice was always the person's to make.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism teaches that the soul, the *atman*, passes from body to body through a long cycle of births and deaths called *samsara*. What carries it from one life to the next is karma, the sum of a person's deeds. Good deeds bring a better birth, and evil deeds bring a worse one. A person's caste, health, wealth, and suffering in this life are taught to be the fruit of deeds done in earlier lives he cannot remember.

A person does make choices now, and those choices will shape his next birth. But the life he is living was set by what came before. The goal is not a better life but release from the wheel altogether, called *moksha*, by works, knowledge, or devotion to a chosen god.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the truth that choices bear fruit. Scripture says the same: "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). Hosea told Israel, "Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy" (Hosea 10:12). Hinduism does not teach that a man's deeds are meaningless or that all is fixed by a god's whim. It holds that what a person does matters.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It binds a man to a life he never lived.** Moses set the choice before the people standing in front of him, "this day" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Karma loads each person at birth with the debt of a stranger's past. Scripture refuses that chain: "The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father" (Ezekiel 18:20), and Yahushua said the blind man's blindness was not from his sin or his parents' (John 9:3). There are no earlier lives to pay for, for "it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27).

**It leaves no one to choose.** Moses called on "heaven and earth to record" and set the choice before Yahuah Himself. Joshua's choice was between Yahuah and "the gods which your fathers served" (Joshua 24:15). Karma has no one behind it. It is a law that turns by itself, with no Judge who forgives and no Father who calls a son home. Where Hinduism does offer a god to choose, it offers many, and Joshua's choice was to put those gods away.

**It seeks escape instead of life.** Moses said choose life, "that thou mayest dwell in the land" (Deuteronomy 30:20). The promise was life in a body on the earth. Hinduism's highest goal is release from bodies and from the world. Scripture's choice is to live; Hinduism's goal is to stop being born.

**It has no door out of the debt.** If karma must be worked off, no one can cancel it. Yahuah cancels debt through the blood He provided, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13). Then the delivered walk forward from a clean start, not from a debt carried over.

## The Verdict

Hinduism sees that deeds have fruit, and then chains each person to a past he never chose and cannot remember. It replaces a living God who sets life and death before men with a wheel that turns by itself, and it trades the promise of life for the hope of escape. Scripture gives one life, one choice, one judgment, and a door through the blood. Karma gives none of these.

Revelation shows the end the wheel can never reach. "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5). There is no next turn, no new birth into a lower caste, and no "more death" (Revelation 21:4). The one who chose life receives it, made new, once and for good.
