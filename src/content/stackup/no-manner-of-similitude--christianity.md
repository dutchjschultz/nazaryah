## The Old Path

At Horeb, Israel "heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice" (Deuteronomy 4:12). Moses drew the command straight from that: take heed "lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure" (Deuteronomy 4:15-16). The second commandment forbids "any graven image, or any likeness," and forbids bowing down to them (Exodus 20:4-5). Even the brass serpent Yahuah Himself commanded (Numbers 21:8-9) became an idol, and Hezekiah broke it in pieces and called it "Nehushtan," a piece of brass (2 Kings 18:4). Isaiah asked, "To whom then will ye liken God?" (Isaiah 40:18), and Paul told Athens that the divine is not "like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device" (Acts 17:29). Yahuah gave His people a reminder that is no image at all: fringes on the garment, so that they would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39).

## What Christianity Teaches

The Catholic and Orthodox churches fill their buildings with images: crucifixes, statues of Mary and the saints, and painted icons of Jesus. Believers kneel before them, kiss them, light candles to them, and carry them in procession. In the eighth and ninth centuries the Eastern church fought over this, and the Second Council of Nicaea in 787 AD ruled that icons are to be honored and kissed. The churches teach that the honor passes through the image to the one it shows, and they draw a line between the "veneration" given to images and the "adoration" given to God alone.

The Catholic and Lutheran way of counting the Ten Commandments folds the command against images into the first commandment and splits the command against coveting into two to keep the number at ten. Catechisms built on that count often leave the words about graven images out of the short list taught to children.

Protestants mostly removed the statues but kept the pictures. Warner Sallman's painting *Head of Christ* (1940) has been printed hundreds of millions of times Crosses stand on steeples, altars, and necklaces.

## What Christianity Kept

The Reformation did keep part of the truth. Many Reformers read the second commandment as its own commandment, and some churches in the Reformed tradition stripped out images altogether. Under the iconoclast emperors, even part of the Eastern church once called icon worship idolatry.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It made a picture of a man and called it God.** The church teaches that Jesus is God. So by its own teaching, every picture of Jesus is a picture of God, the very thing Deuteronomy 4 forbids. Scripture does not have that problem. Yahushua is "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15), the likeness, not the one He shows. Yahuah is the One "whom no man hath seen, nor can see" (1 Timothy 6:16), yet many saw Yahushua. An image is not the original, and the Son is not the Father. The Trinity forced the church into idolatry: once a man is called God, his portrait becomes an image of God.

**"Veneration" is still bowing.** The commandment does not say "do not adore them." It says "Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them" (Exodus 20:5). Kneeling, kissing, and burning candles before a statue is bowing, whatever name the council gave it.

**The cross became Nehushtan.** Yahushua Himself compared His lifting up to the brass serpent (John 3:14). The serpent pointed to deliverance, and Israel ended up burning incense to it. The church has done the same with the cross, setting up crucifixes to kneel before.

**It folded away the commandment.** A command Yahuah spoke from the mount and wrote with His finger was quietly merged and dropped from the children's lessons. "Ye shall not add unto the word… neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). The very chapter that gives "no manner of similitude" also forbids trimming the word.

## The Verdict

Christianity broke the second commandment and then renumbered the list so the break would not show. It painted a man, called him God, and bowed. The Old Path gave no image, no bowing, and no trimmed list.

Revelation shows where an honored image leads. The false prophet tells the earth "that they should make an image to the beast," and gives life to it, "that the image of the beast should both speak, and cause that as many as would not worship the image of the beast should be killed" (Revelation 13:14-15). The last great test of the world is a test about an image. Those who learn now to bow to none will stand then.
