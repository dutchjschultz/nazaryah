## The Old Path

Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6), and afterward Yahuah said Abraham "kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Israel was spared in Egypt by the blood on the door, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13), and only then was led to Sinai. Moses said the commandment "is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). The psalmist said, "I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45), and Solomon called the commandment "a lamp" and the law "light" (Proverbs 6:23). Deliverance comes through the blood Yahuah provided; Torah is what walking looks like on the other side of it. Torah was never the door, and the door was never the end of the road. Every religion's answer is measured against that order.

## What Christianity Teaches

The church teaches that a man is saved by grace through faith in Jesus, not by works. Then it adds that the Law of Moses was "nailed to the cross," that believers are "not under law but under grace," and that the Sabbath, the feasts, and the food laws belonged to the Jews and have passed away. The Sabbath was moved to the first day of the week. Many churches teach "once saved, always saved," so that a man who has prayed the prayer is secure whatever he does next. Salvation is spoken of as one moment in the past, finished at the altar.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the door. It preaches that deliverance comes by favor through belief, "not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8-9). It preaches the blood of the Lamb, and that no man can make himself clean by keeping rules. On that point the church stands where Abraham stood, counted righteous because he believed (Genesis 15:6), and where Israel stood on the night the blood went on the doors.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It stops at the door.** Israel was delivered by blood in Egypt and then led to Sinai to receive the walk. The church brings men out of Egypt and leaves them standing on the far shore of the sea. Paul said salvation runs on past the door: "being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him… being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10). Yahushua said, "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). The door is entered once. The walk is the rest of life.

**It calls lawlessness favor.** Paul asked the church's own question and answered it: "shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? God forbid" (Romans 6:15). John wrote that "sin is the transgression of the law" (1 John 3:4), and, "He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar" (1 John 2:4). Favor does not cancel the Law. Favor brings a man through the door so that he can walk in it. Yahushua said He did not come "to destroy the law" (Matthew 5:17), and He will say to those who called Him Master without walking, "depart from me, ye that work iniquity" (Matthew 7:23), a word that means lawlessness.

**It mistook the end of the penalty for the end of the instruction.** What was taken out of the way at the cross was the record against us, the sentence of death, not the instruction of a Father to His children. Torah still describes what a righteous life looks like, and Yahuah promised to write it inward, not erase it: "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33). A law written on the heart is a law still in force.

## The Verdict

Christianity has the door and refuses the walk. It rightly preaches that Torah was never the way in, and then wrongly preaches that Torah has no place after. That is the second of the two errors born from collapsing the door and the walk into one: since a man is not saved by keeping the commandments, the church concludes he need not keep them at all. Abraham believed, and then he kept Yahuah's charge. Both halves stand.

Revelation names the people the dragon makes war against: those "which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Yahushua Messiah" (Revelation 12:17). The testimony is the door. The commandments are the walk. The remnant has both.
