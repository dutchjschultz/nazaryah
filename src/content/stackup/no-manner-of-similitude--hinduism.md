## The Old Path

At Horeb, Israel "heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice" (Deuteronomy 4:12). Moses drew the command straight from that: take heed "lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure" (Deuteronomy 4:15-16). The second commandment forbids "any graven image, or any likeness," and forbids bowing down to them (Exodus 20:4-5). Even the brass serpent Yahuah Himself commanded (Numbers 21:8-9) became an idol, and Hezekiah broke it in pieces and called it "Nehushtan," a piece of brass (2 Kings 18:4). Isaiah asked, "To whom then will ye liken God?" (Isaiah 40:18), and Paul told Athens that the divine is not "like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device" (Acts 17:29). Yahuah gave His people a reminder that is no image at all: fringes on the garment, so that they would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39).

## What Hinduism Teaches

In Hinduism the image of the god stands at the center of worship. The image is called a *murti*, a form, and it may be stone, metal, or wood. Before a temple image is used, priests perform a rite to invite the god to dwell in it, and from then on it is treated as the god present. It is bathed, dressed, fed, fanned, and put to bed. Worshippers offer it flowers, fruit, incense, and lamps in the daily rite called *puja*. Much of a temple visit is *darshan*, the act of seeing the god and being seen by it. Homes keep a small shrine with images of the family's chosen gods, such as Krishna, Shiva, Ganesh, or Lakshmi.

Hindu teachers often explain that the image is a doorway, a help for the mind to reach the divine, which in itself has no form.

## What Hinduism Kept

A thread of the truth runs through Hinduism's own writings. The Upanishads speak of the highest reality as beyond every form, and a line in the Shvetashvatara Upanishad says of the Supreme that "there is no likeness of him." In 1875 a Hindu reformer named Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj, which rejected idol worship altogether and called Hindus back to what he saw as the older Vedas. So Hinduism has heard, from inside its own house, that the divine cannot be carved. That is an echo of Horeb: "ye saw no manner of similitude" (Deuteronomy 4:15).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It does the very thing the commandment names.** Exodus 20:4-5 does not only forbid making an image. It forbids bowing to it and serving it. Hindu worship bows to the image, washes it, and feeds it. The "doorway" explanation does not help. Israel said the same of the golden calf: Aaron built an altar before it and proclaimed "a feast to Yahuah" (Exodus 32:5). The calf was meant as a picture of the true God, and Yahuah still called it a "molten calf" and "a great sin" (Exodus 32:8, 31).

**The prophets mocked this exact practice.** Isaiah described a man who cuts down a tree, burns half of it to bake his bread and warm himself, and of the rest "he maketh a god, even his graven image: he falleth down unto it, and worshippeth it" (Isaiah 44:15-17). The Psalm says of such images, "They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not," and then adds, "They that make them are like unto them" (Psalm 115:5, 8). An image that is bathed and fed cannot bathe or feed anyone.

**It multiplies gods.** The images are not of one God but of many. Yahuah said, "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" (Exodus 20:3), and "I am Yahuah: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images" (Isaiah 42:8).

## The Verdict

Hinduism heard, in its own writings, that the highest has no likeness, and then built more likenesses than any religion on earth. The Old Path stands opposite on every point: no image, no bowing, no other gods. A god a man must carry, wash, and feed is the god Isaiah laughed at.

Revelation names this worship among the sins men refused to leave even under judgment: they repented not of "idols of gold, and silver, and brass, and stone, and of wood: which neither can see, nor hear, nor walk" (Revelation 9:20). The God who made the tree does not live in the half of it that was not burned.
