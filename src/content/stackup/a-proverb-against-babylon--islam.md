## The Old Path

Isaiah tells Israel to "take up this proverb against the king of Babylon" (Isaiah 14:4). It is a taunt song over a dead king, whose pomp is "brought down to the grave" with worms for his bed (Isaiah 14:11). He had boasted, "I will exalt my throne above the stars of God… I will be like the most High" (Isaiah 14:13-14), and those who see his body ask, "Is this the man that made the earth to tremble?" (Isaiah 14:16). His title *heylel*, shining one, son of the dawn (Isaiah 14:12), was stolen, for the morning star belongs to the Son (Revelation 22:16; 2 Peter 1:19). Ezekiel's prince of Tyrus heard the same answer: "thou art a man, and not God" (Ezekiel 28:2). Scripture names the enemy by what he does, never by a title of light. *Satan* means adversary, a word even used of Yahuah's own messenger who stood against Balaam (Numbers 22:22), and the serpent is named by his craft, "more subtil than any beast of the field" (Genesis 3:1).

## What Islam Teaches

The Quran tells the story of Iblis several times (Surah 2:34; 7:11-18; 15:28-42; 17:61-65; 38:71-85). God made Adam from clay and commanded the angels to bow down to him. All of them bowed except Iblis. Iblis answered that he was better than Adam, because God made him from fire and Adam from clay (Surah 7:12). For that pride he was cast out and cursed. He asked for a delay until the day of raising, received it, and swore to lead men astray (Surah 7:14-17).

The Quran says Iblis "was of the jinn" (Surah 18:50), spirit beings it describes as made from smokeless fire (Surah 55:15). Muslim scholars have long disagreed over whether he was an angel or a jinn. After his fall he is called *al-Shaytan*, and the Quran describes him as one who whispers into the hearts of men and then draws back (Surah 114:4-5).

Islam has no Lucifer. The Quran does not use Isaiah 14 or Ezekiel 28, and it never gives Iblis a title of light.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the right name. *Shaytan* is the same word as the Hebrew *satan*, adversary, so the Quran names the enemy by his work, as Scripture does. It never hands him the morning star. Its picture of a whisperer who deceives and slips away fits the serpent, named for his craft (Genesis 3:1). It also kept pride as the root of the enemy's ruin, the very sin of the king of Babylon (Isaiah 14:14). On the name, Islam stands closer to Scripture than the church does.

## Where Islam Falls

**It writes a backstory Scripture never wrote.** Moses gives the serpent no history before the garden. The Quran gives the enemy a heavenly career: a command to bow to Adam, a refusal, a casting out. That story did not begin with the Quran. An older writing outside Scripture, known as the Life of Adam and Eve, already told of the devil refusing to bow to Adam and being cast down for it. The Quran carries a tale that was told outside the Word before it.

**It gives the adversary a reprieve and a mission.** The Quran's reprieve (Surah 15:36-40) makes Iblis a rival with his own career. Scripture never dignifies him so. It names him by his works only: "a liar, and the father of it" and "a murderer from the beginning" (John 8:44), and "the accuser of our brethren" (Revelation 12:10).

**It moves the warning off men.** Scripture puts the boast "I will be like the most High" in the mouth of a king (Isaiah 14:13-14), the claim "I am a God" in the mouth of a prince of Tyre (Ezekiel 28:2), and "is not this great Babylon, that I have built" in the mouth of Nebuchadnezzar (Daniel 4:30). The lesson is for rulers of men: "the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men" (Daniel 4:32). The Iblis story, like the church's Lucifer, makes pride a tale about a spirit and lets proud men off the page.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the right name and told the wrong story. It never made the enemy a shining one. But it gave him a heavenly history Moses never wrote, a tale already told outside Scripture, and sent the warning of pride off kings and onto a spirit.

Revelation names the enemy the way Scripture always has, by what he does: "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world" (Revelation 12:9). Serpent, slanderer, adversary, deceiver. Not one title of light among them.
