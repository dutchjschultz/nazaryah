## The Old Path

Yahuah gave Israel a memorial in Egypt, and it was a feast: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever" (Exodus 12:14). In Scripture, to remember is to do. The fringes were given so that Israel would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39). The feasts belong to Him: "the feasts of Yahuah… even these are my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). On the night before He died, at a preparation meal on the 13th of Aviv, Yahushua took bread and said, "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). He was not starting a church service. He was laying the Pesach memorial on Himself, the Lamb who died on the 14th at the hour the lambs were slain. Paul drew the line plainly: "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8). The memorial is the feast, kept as Yahuah gave it.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism's holy days are set by the moon. The greatest is Vesak, held on the full moon of the month of Vesakha, usually in May. In Theravada lands it remembers three events said to have fallen on that same full moon: the Buddha's birth, his enlightenment, and his death. Buddhists visit temples, light candles, walk three times around the shrine, and give to the monks. Other full-moon festivals mark other events in the Buddha's life and teaching, such as Magha Puja and Asalha Puja.

Beside these, Theravada Buddhists keep the *uposatha*, the observance days, four times in each lunar month: at the new moon, the full moon, and the two half moons. In Thailand these are called *wan phra*. On those days devout lay people go to the temple, hear teaching, and take on eight precepts instead of the usual five, often fasting from noon to the next dawn.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept a striking piece of the old pattern. It sets its holy days by the moon, and it marks four days in every lunar month by the moon's quarters, beginning again with each new moon. That is a shadow of the lunar Sabbath of Scripture, which falls on the 8th, 15th, 22nd, and 29th of every month and resets at each new moon. The moon was given "for seasons" (Psalm 104:19), and Israel blew the trumpet "in the new moon, in the time appointed, on our solemn feast day" (Psalm 81:3). Buddhism remembered that the moon numbers the holy days.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**Its holy days are kept to no one.** The Sabbath is "the sabbath of Yahuah thy God" (Exodus 20:10), and the feasts are "the feasts of Yahuah" (Leviticus 23:2). A holy day is holy because the Maker set it apart. Buddhism teaches no Creator, so its moon days honor a teacher's path, not the One who hung the moon. It kept the clock and lost the One who keeps the appointments.

**Vesak remembers a man who stayed dead.** Vesak marks the Buddha's death and his passing out of the round of rebirth. His death delivered no one. Pesach remembers a death that did: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13). The Lamb Yahuah provided died on the 14th and was raised again, and Paul calls Him "the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20). One feast mourns a teacher's end. The other keeps the memorial of a Lamb who lives.

**Merit is not a memorial.** On Buddhist holy days the lay person gives to the monks and keeps extra precepts to build up merit. Yahuah's feasts are not wages. They remember what He has already done: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial" (Exodus 12:14). Deliverance comes first through the blood, and the feast is kept by those already brought out, never as the price of getting out.

## The Verdict

Buddhism kept the moon as the numberer of holy days, closer than almost any other religion on earth. But its feasts honor a man and are kept before no God, and its greatest festival remembers a death that saved no one. The memorial Yahuah gave remembers the Lamb, and Buddhism has no Lamb.

Revelation shows the feast that waits for those who keep His: "Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9). The moon will still keep its appointments then, and "from one new moon to another… shall all flesh come to worship before me" (Isaiah 66:23). The clock was always pointing to Him.
