## The Question He Asked

At Caesarea Philippi, Yahushua asked His disciples, "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?" Peter answered, "Thou art the Messiah, the Son of the living God," and Yahushua said that answer came from "my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 16:13-17). Peter's answer has two parts: He is the Messiah, and He is the Son of the living God. Every religion's answer is measured against those two parts.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism has no single teaching on Yahushua, because it has no single creed. Most Hindus who think about him gladly honour him as a holy man, a great teacher, or a guru who reached union with the divine. Many go further and call him an avatar, a god come down in human form, standing beside Rama and Krishna as one more descent of the divine into flesh.

A popular legend says Yahushua spent his hidden years in India learning from Hindu and Buddhist teachers. The story was spread in 1894 by a Russian writer, Nicolas Notovitch, who claimed to have found a lost manuscript in a Tibetan monastery. No such manuscript has ever been produced, and scholars who checked his account at the time found it false. Scripture gives those years in one line: He "increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man" (Luke 2:52), in Nazareth, "as his custom was" in the synagogue (Luke 4:16).

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept respect for him. Hindus do not curse his name, and many keep his picture beside their own holy men. They honour his teaching on love, humility, and peace. Yahushua said, "Blessed are the meek" and "Blessed are the peacemakers" (Matthew 5:5, 9), and a Hindu will agree with every word.

Beyond respect, Hinduism kept almost nothing. It has no Messiah, because it has no promise to Abraham, no line of David, and no Israel. It has no Son, because it has no one Father.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It makes him one of many.** An avatar is one descent among many, and Hinduism welcomes as many as it likes. Scripture allows only one. "There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). Peter said, "there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12). One Messiah cannot be placed on a shelf of gods.

**It makes him a god come down.** The avatar is a god wearing a human body for a season. Scripture says, "God is not a man" (Numbers 23:19). Yahushua did not descend from a heaven of gods into a borrowed body. He was conceived in Mary, and "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). He is a man who is the Son, not a god dressed as a man.

**It puts his death on a wheel.** Hinduism teaches that souls die and are born again, over and over. Scripture says the Messiah died once and does not die again: "Messiah being raised from the dead dieth no more; death hath no more dominion over him. For in that he died, he died unto sin once" (Romans 6:9-10). A death repeated on a wheel atones for nothing. A death once, for all, was enough.

## The Verdict

Hinduism gives Yahushua honour and takes away everything that makes Him who He is. It calls Him holy and denies He is the only Messiah. It calls Him divine and denies He is the Son of the one God. It respects Him as a teacher and has no place for His blood.

Revelation shows Him not as one god among many, but as the one Lamb standing in the midst of the throne. The heavenly host sings, "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain" (Revelation 5:12). There is one Lamb, and He was slain once.
