## The Old Path

Yahuah gave Israel a memorial in Egypt, and it was a feast: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever" (Exodus 12:14). In Scripture, to remember is to do. The fringes were given so that Israel would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39). The feasts belong to Him: "the feasts of Yahuah… even these are my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). On the night before He died, at a preparation meal on the 13th of Aviv, Yahushua took bread and said, "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). He was not starting a church service. He was laying the Pesach memorial on Himself, the Lamb who died on the 14th at the hour the lambs were slain. Paul drew the line plainly: "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8). The memorial is the feast, kept as Yahuah gave it.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism keeps Pesach every spring with the Seder, a home meal held on the first night of the feast. The family reads from the Haggadah, a book of prayers, questions, and stories that retells the Exodus. Four cups of wine are drunk. Matzah and bitter herbs are eaten. A piece of matzah called the *afikoman* is hidden and found by the children. Since the Temple fell in 70 AD, no lamb is slain. A roasted shank bone sits on the Seder plate to stand for it and is not eaten. All leaven is removed from the house beforehand.

The date comes from the fixed Hebrew calendar that Jewish tradition credits to Hillel II in the fourth century AD. The months begin by an average new moon worked out by formula, a thirteenth month is added on a fixed nineteen-year cycle, and postponement rules can move a month's start so that certain holy days do not fall on certain days of the Roman week. The day is reckoned from dusk to dusk, and the month of Aviv goes by the Babylonian name Nisan.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the feast itself, more faithfully than any other religion on this list. Through two thousand years of exile, Jewish families have put away leaven, eaten unleavened bread, and told their children of the night Yahuah passed over. That is what Yahuah commanded: "when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service? That ye shall say, It is the sacrifice of Yahuah's passover" (Exodus 12:26-27).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It keeps the feast and refuses the Lamb.** The shank bone on the plate is an empty place where the lamb should be. Yahuah filled it. John said, "Behold the Lamb of God" (John 1:29), and the Lamb died on the 14th at the hour the Pesach lambs were slain. Paul said, "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us" (1 Corinthians 5:7). Judaism tells the whole story of the blood on the doorposts and turns away from the blood it pointed to.

**The Seder has been dressed in tradition.** The four cups, the Haggadah, and the hidden afikoman are not in the Torah. Yahuah gave the lamb, the unleavened bread, and the bitter herbs (Exodus 12:8). Yahushua warned of "the tradition of men" laid over "the commandment of God" (Mark 7:8).

**The date is calculated by rule, not read from the lights.** Yahuah gave the sun, moon, and stars "for signs, and for seasons" (Genesis 1:14) and commanded, "Observe the month of Abib" (Deuteronomy 16:1). The month opens at the first dawn after the moon passes the sun, and the year turns when the sun crosses the fourth gate in the east. A fixed formula, a thirteenth month by cycle, and days shifted to avoid certain weekdays are a man-made calendar, and its Babylonian month names show its source.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the feast that the rest of the world threw away, and that deserves honor. But it keeps it on a calculated date, in a tradition-built form, with an empty place where the Lamb belongs. The memorial Yahuah gave is the feast kept as He gave it, and its center is the Lamb He provided.

Revelation shows the feast finished. Those who overcame "sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3). Moses' song was sung beyond the sea after the first Pesach. In the end it is sung with the song of the Lamb: one deliverance, one memorial.
