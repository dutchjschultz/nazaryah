## The Old Path

Yahuah gave Israel a memorial in Egypt, and it was a feast: "this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to Yahuah throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever" (Exodus 12:14). In Scripture, to remember is to do. The fringes were given so that Israel would "remember all the commandments of Yahuah, and do them" (Numbers 15:39). The feasts belong to Him: "the feasts of Yahuah… even these are my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). On the night before He died, at a preparation meal on the 13th of Aviv, Yahushua took bread and said, "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). He was not starting a church service. He was laying the Pesach memorial on Himself, the Lamb who died on the 14th at the hour the lambs were slain. Paul drew the line plainly: "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8). The memorial is the feast, kept as Yahuah gave it.

## What Christianity Teaches

The church keeps two great holidays. Christmas celebrates the birth of Jesus on December 25. The earliest record of that date being kept in Rome is from 336 AD, a city whose winter festivals already filled late December. Easter celebrates the resurrection on a Sunday in spring. At the Council of Nicaea in 325 AD, the church agreed that all should keep Easter on the same Sunday, and the emperor Constantine's letter after the council said that Christians should have nothing in common with the Jews in setting the date. Many churches hold Easter services at dawn, facing the rising sun.

The memorial Yahushua spoke of became a church rite called Communion, the Eucharist, or the Lord's Supper. The Catholic Church teaches that at the Mass the bread and wine become the body and blood of Jesus, defined as transubstantiation at the Fourth Lateran Council in 1215. Many Protestant churches hold communion monthly or quarterly, with a small wafer and a thimble cup of juice.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the words "this do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19) and the bread and the cup. It still keeps its spring holiday near the time of Pesach. Its people remember that something happened in that season; they have forgotten what Yahuah told them to do about it.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It dropped His feasts and kept its own.** Yahuah called Pesach "a feast by an ordinance for ever" (Exodus 12:14) and named His appointed times "my feasts" (Leviticus 23:2). The church dropped every one of them. Christmas is nowhere commanded. Easter was set apart from Pesach on purpose, so the church would not keep the date the Jews kept. Moses warned against just this: "How did these nations serve their gods? even so will I do likewise… Thou shalt not do so unto Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 12:30-31). Ezekiel saw men in Yahuah's house "with their backs toward the temple… and they worshipped the sun toward the east" (Ezekiel 8:16). A dawn service facing the sun is that very picture.

**It turned the memorial into a ritual.** "This do in remembrance of me" was not spoken at a church service. Yahushua said it at a preparation meal the night before He died, and He was pointing to the Pesach that would be eaten while His body lay in the tomb. In Hebrew thought, to remember is to do (Numbers 15:39). The remembrance is the feast itself. The church traded a feast kept in its season for a wafer taken a few times a year.

**It tore the Lamb out of the Pesach.** Paul did not say, "Messiah our passover is sacrificed, therefore the feast is over." He said, "Therefore let us keep the feast… with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:8). The church kept the Lamb and threw away the feast He fulfilled, as if the death of the Lamb ended the memorial of the Lamb.

## The Verdict

Christianity keeps Christmas, which Yahuah never gave, and Easter, which was cut loose from Pesach on purpose, and it shrank the memorial into an occasional crumb and sip. Delivered by the blood, the believer is then called to keep the feast as Yahuah gave it. That is the Old Path.

Revelation ends with a blessing on those who return to it: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city" (Revelation 22:14). The feasts are His commandments. The ones who keep them walk through the gates.
