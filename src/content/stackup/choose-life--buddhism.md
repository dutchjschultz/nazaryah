## The Old Path

Moses set two roads before Israel: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Joshua said the same: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). Yahuah takes "no pleasure in the death of him that dieth... wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9). A choice made can be unmade. "Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33), and the righteous man who turns away dies in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). The blood on the door opened the road out of Egypt (Exodus 12:13), but the people still had to walk it, and "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Yahuah declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), but knowing is not forcing. The choice was always the person's to make.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism teaches that all beings are caught in a cycle of rebirth driven by karma. Unlike Hinduism, it denies that there is a lasting soul that moves from life to life. What carries on is a stream of causes and effects, each moment giving rise to the next. The Buddha taught that karma is above all intention: what a person chooses in his heart is the deed that bears fruit.

The cause of the cycle, Buddhism says, is craving. The way out is to end craving through the Eightfold Path until the fire of rebirth goes out. That going out is nirvana. The Buddha also rejected the fatalists of his own day, teachers who said that everything was fixed and that a man's effort changed nothing.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept the weight of choice. It refused fatalism outright, and it taught that what a man intends and chooses shapes what he becomes. That is near to Moses, who set the choice before each person "this day" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Buddhism also teaches that a person is not doomed by his past and can turn, which agrees with Ezekiel: "if the wicked will turn from all his sins... he shall surely live" (Ezekiel 18:21).

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It chooses to stop instead of to live.** Moses said choose life, "that both thou and thy seed may live." Buddhism's highest choice is to end the stream of becoming, so that nothing is reborn. Scripture never offers extinction as the prize. It offers life, and life is the gift of Yahuah, who "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7).

**It has no one setting the choice.** Moses said, "I have set before you." Someone set the roads, and Someone gave the blessing and the curse. Buddhism has no God, so the choice is only between causes and effects, with no one to bless the right road and no one to forgive the wrong one. A choice of life needs a Giver of life.

**It has no self to answer.** Buddhism teaches that there is no lasting self. But the choice in Scripture belongs to a person, and a person answers for it: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4). On this point Buddhism stands nearer to Genesis 2:7 than the immortal-soul churches, for man does not hold a soul that lives on by itself. But Scripture still makes him a whole person, one who chooses, endures, and is raised to stand before his Judge.

**It has no door.** The choice Moses set came after the blood on the door (Exodus 12:13). Buddhism offers a path walked by a man's own effort, with no Lamb and no one to open the way. The walk is real in Scripture, but it begins at a door that only Yahuah could open.

## The Verdict

Buddhism was right to reject fatalism and right that choice shapes a man. But it aims every choice at stopping, not at living. It has no God to set the roads, no self to stand in the judgment, and no blood to open the door. Moses said choose life. Buddhism says choose to cease. Those are not two paths to the same place. They are life and death set side by side.

Revelation gives the choice its prize, and the prize is a crown, not an ending. "Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life" (Revelation 2:10). A Giver promises it, a faithful person receives it, and what he receives is life.
