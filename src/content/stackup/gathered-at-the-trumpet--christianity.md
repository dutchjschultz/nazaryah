## The Old Path

At Sinai, "the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud" sounded, and Moses "brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God" (Exodus 19:16-17). Yahuah ordered two silver trumpets "for the calling of the assembly" (Numbers 10:2), and set "a memorial of blowing of trumpets" in the seventh month (Leviticus 23:24). Isaiah saw "the great trumpet" blown to gather the outcasts home (Isaiah 27:13). Yahushua gave the order with no gap: "Immediately after the tribulation of those days," the Son of man comes, and His angels gather His elect "with a great sound of a trumpet" (Matthew 24:29-31). Paul calls it "the last trump" (1 Corinthians 15:52), and says that day will not come "except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed" (2 Thessalonians 2:3). One coming, one trumpet, one gathering, after the trouble.

## What Christianity Teaches

A large part of modern Christianity, especially in America, teaches a secret rapture. In this teaching, Jesus comes invisibly before a seven-year tribulation and snatches the church away. The world is left behind to suffer under the Antichrist, and seven years later Jesus comes again, this time in public.

The teaching is young. It was shaped by John Nelson Darby and the Plymouth Brethren in the 1830s, spread across America by the notes of the Scofield Reference Bible in 1909, and sold to millions through the *Left Behind* novels beginning in 1995. No major church taught a coming in two stages, seven years apart, before the nineteenth century.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the gathering itself. Believers will be "caught up together… to meet the Master in the air" (1 Thessalonians 4:17). It kept the trumpet, the shout, and the raising of "the dead in Messiah" (1 Thessalonians 4:16). The error is not in whether the gathering happens but in when and how.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It moves the gathering before the tribulation.** Yahushua put it after. "Immediately after the tribulation of those days… they shall see the Son of man coming… And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect" (Matthew 24:29-31). There is one gathering in the passage, and it comes after. Yahushua even prayed against an escape: "I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil" (John 17:15).

**It makes the loudest day in history a secret.** Paul describes the gathering with "a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God" (1 Thessalonians 4:16). Yahushua compared it to lightning from east to west (Matthew 24:27). And Paul calls the gathering trumpet "the last trump" (1 Corinthians 15:52). A rapture seven years before the end would need a trumpet followed by more trumpets, which makes Paul's last trump the first. In Revelation, the mystery of God is finished "in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound" (Revelation 10:7). That is the last, and there is none after it.

**It has the church gone before the man of sin appears.** Concerning "our gathering together unto him," he said, "let no man deceive you… for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed" (2 Thessalonians 2:1-3). The rapture teaching puts the gathering first and the man of sin after. Paul put them the other way around.

**It borrows its seven years from a prophecy already fulfilled.** The seven-year tribulation is Daniel's seventieth week cut off and pushed two thousand years into the future. Daniel gave no gap. In the midst of that week Messiah would "cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease" (Daniel 9:27), and He did, when He died at Pesach and the sacrifices lost their meaning. The other half ran out a few years later, when the message turned to the Gentiles. All seventy weeks are finished, and the rapture preachers' tribulation has no week left to stand on.

## The Verdict

There is no secret rapture. There is one coming, at the last trumpet, after the tribulation and after the man of sin is revealed, and every eye will see it. The saints are not lifted out before the trouble. They are kept through it and gathered at the end of it.

Revelation sounds that last trumpet in plain view. When the seventh angel sounds, great voices in heaven say that the kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of Yahuah "and of his Messiah" (Revelation 11:15). Two are named, Yahuah and His anointed one, the Father and the Son He anointed, not three persons in one. The trumpet that ends the age does not sound in secret, and it does not sound twice.
