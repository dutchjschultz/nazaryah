## The Old Path

Yahuah has only ever had one people. At the first Pesach the Law said, "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger" (Exodus 12:49). The stranger who joins himself to Yahuah and keeps His Sabbaths is brought to His holy mountain, "for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:6-7). Paul pictures that one people as one olive tree. The wild branch is "graffed in" and partakes "of the root and fatness of the olive tree," and he is warned, "thou bearest not the root, but the root thee" (Romans 11:17-18). Gentiles in Messiah are no longer "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" but "fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:12, 19). The door into that tree is the Seed, "which is Messiah," and "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:16, 29). One tree, one root, one law, entered by belief and not by birth.

## What Christianity Teaches

Much of the modern church, especially in America, teaches that Israel and the church are two separate peoples with two separate plans. This teaching, called dispensationalism, was shaped by John Nelson Darby in the 1830s and spread through the Scofield Reference Bible of 1909. It says the church began in Acts 2, that the church is under grace while Israel was under law, that the church age is a pause in Yahuah's plan for Israel, and that the church will be taken away so Yahuah can return to dealing with the Jews. Its followers often quote "rightly dividing the word of truth" (2 Timothy 2:15) to mean dividing Israel from the church.

Older churches fall into the opposite ditch, teaching that the church replaced Israel and that Israel's promises now belong to the church alone. Both views end with the Gentile believer apart from Israel's Law.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the door. It teaches that a Gentile is joined to Yahuah by belief in Messiah, not by birth or by a ceremony, and that is the way Paul describes the grafting: "thou standest by faith" (Romans 11:20). Dispensationalism also kept the truth that Yahuah has not forgotten the natural branches, for "God is able to graff them in again" (Romans 11:23).

## Where Christianity Falls

**It plants a second tree.** Paul never described two trees. He described one olive tree with natural and wild branches drawing on one root (Romans 11:17-18). He said Messiah "hath made both one" (Ephesians 2:14), and that the Gentiles are now citizens of "the commonwealth of Israel" (Ephesians 2:12). A Gentile church with its own plan, its own promises, and its own destiny is a second people, the very thing Yahuah never started.

**It dates the church wrong.** The word *ekklesia*, church, is the word the Greek Old Testament used for the assembly of Israel. Stephen called Israel at Sinai "the church in the wilderness" (Acts 7:38). The church did not begin at Shavuot in Acts 2. The assembly that gathered at Shavuot was the same assembly that stood at Sinai, now opened wide to the nations.

**It gives the branch a different law.** Yahuah said, "One law and one manner shall be for you, and for the stranger" (Numbers 15:16). The church says Israel had the Law and the church has grace. But the stranger Isaiah welcomed keeps the Sabbath and holds the covenant (Isaiah 56:6). The wild branch draws from the same root and the same "fatness" (Romans 11:17). It is delivered first by the blood, and then it walks in the same commandments as the natural branch.

**It boasts against the branches.** Replacement teaching says the church took Israel's place. Paul answered, "Boast not against the branches... thou bearest not the root, but the root thee" (Romans 11:18). The Gentile did not replace Israel. He was grafted into Israel.

## The Verdict

The church found the door and then built a second house. Whether it divides Israel from the church or replaces Israel with the church, it ends in the same place: two peoples, and a Gentile believer cut off from the Law that Yahuah gave to the native and the stranger alike. Scripture knows one olive tree, one root, and one law.

Revelation shows the one city. On its gates are written "the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel," and on its foundations "the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb" (Revelation 21:12, 14). The tribes and the apostles are not two peoples in two places. They are the gates and the foundations of one city.
