## The Old Path

Fire went out from Yahuah and "devoured" Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:2). Sodom was left "brimstone, and salt, and burning" (Deuteronomy 29:23), and Peter says Yahuah turned those cities "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). Malachi says the coming day "shall burn as an oven," leaving the wicked "neither root nor branch," and they "shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:1, 3). David says the wicked "shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away" (Psalm 37:20). Yahushua warned of Him who "is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28), and Paul calls the end "everlasting destruction" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). The fire is eternal in what it does, not in how long it keeps burning. "The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23), not endless life in pain.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism teaches that beings are born again and again in different realms, and one of those realms is hell, called naraka. Buddhist writings describe hot hells and cold hells, with the deepest, Avici, reserved for the gravest deeds. A being may stay in a hell for an age so long it can hardly be counted. Yet no hell lasts forever. When the bad karma that brought a being there is used up, it is reborn somewhere else.

No god sends anyone to hell in Buddhism. Karma works like a law of cause and effect. Bad deeds bring bad births, and good deeds bring better ones. The goal is not to stand acquitted before a Judge but to escape the whole round of births and reach nirvana.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept the truth that deeds have consequences that reach past the grave. It refuses to say that wrongdoing simply disappears. Scripture says the same: "Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). Buddhism also sensed that endless torment is not justice, and its hells all come to an end.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has punishment without a Judge.** In Buddhism, no one weighs the case, no one hears a plea, and no one can show mercy. Karma simply runs. Scripture puts a Person on the seat. "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). Yahuah "hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained" (Acts 17:31). A law with no Lawgiver can only grind. A Judge can also forgive, and Yahuah does: "Come now, and let us reason together… though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow" (Isaiah 1:18).

**It punishes a self it says does not exist.** Buddhism teaches that there is no lasting self, yet it sends a being through hell and back into another life. Scripture is plainer and more honest. There is a real man, formed from dust, who answers for his own deeds: "every one of us shall give account of himself to God" (Romans 14:12). And when that man is judged and found wicked, he is not passed along to another life. He dies.

**It never finishes evil.** Because every hell ends in another birth, the cycle of wrongdoing never closes. The wicked are always somewhere, sinning again. Scripture promises an end. Yahuah "will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time" (Nahum 1:9). Malachi says the wicked "shall be ashes" (Malachi 4:3). Ashes do not sin again.

**It leaves no way out but self-effort.** The only escape Buddhism offers from hell and rebirth is a man's own long discipline. Scripture says no man can climb out by his own hand, for "none of them can by any means redeem his brother" (Psalm 49:7). The way out was given: "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). Yahuah provided the Lamb (Genesis 22:8). The Buddhist is left to save himself, and no man ever has.

## The Verdict

Buddhism sees that hell cannot be endless, and in that it is wiser than much of the church. But it trades the fire of Yahuah for a machine of cause and effect that never stops running. Scripture's judgment is personal, it is final, and it ends. The wicked are not cycled. They are consumed.

Revelation shows both halves of the Judge's work. For the wicked, "the second death" (Revelation 20:14). For those who come to Him, "him that is athirst" receives "the fountain of the water of life freely" (Revelation 21:6). The water is free. No one has to work off his karma to drink it.
