## The Old Path

Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6), and afterward Yahuah said Abraham "kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Israel was spared in Egypt by the blood on the door, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13), and only then was led to Sinai. Moses said the commandment "is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). The psalmist said, "I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45), and Solomon called the commandment "a lamp" and the law "light" (Proverbs 6:23). Deliverance comes through the blood Yahuah provided; Torah is what walking looks like on the other side of it. Torah was never the door, and the door was never the end of the road. Every religion's answer is measured against that order.

## What Buddhism Teaches

The Buddha taught that life is marked by suffering, that suffering comes from craving, and that craving can be ended by walking the Noble Eightfold Path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. The end of the path is *nirvana*, the end of craving and of rebirth.

For ordinary Buddhists the walk begins with five precepts: do not kill, do not steal, do not commit sexual wrong, do not lie, and do not take intoxicants.

Buddhism is clear that the walk is one's own. The Dhammapada, a collection of the Buddha's sayings, says, "By oneself is evil done… by oneself is one made pure. Purity and impurity depend on oneself; no one can purify another" (Dhammapada 165). It also says, "You yourselves must strive; the Buddhas only point the way" (Dhammapada 276).

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept the idea of a path, a way that is walked day by day and not merely believed. It kept much of the second tablet: its first four precepts stand close to "Thou shalt not kill… Thou shalt not commit adultery. Thou shalt not steal. Thou shalt not bear false witness" (Exodus 20:13-16). It kept self-control, honest speech, and a sober mind, and Scripture asks the same: "Be sober, be vigilant" (1 Peter 5:8).

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It is walked by one's own effort.** "No one can purify another," says the Dhammapada. Scripture says the opposite: Yahuah purifies. "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean… A new heart also will I give you" (Ezekiel 36:25-26). Isaiah heard, "though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow" (Isaiah 1:18). A man cannot wash himself clean any more than Israel could walk out of Egypt before the blood was on the door. The walk Buddhism asks for is the walk Torah describes after deliverance, and Buddhism asks a man to walk it with no deliverance at all.

**It has a path with no Lawgiver.** The Eightfold Path came out of one man's insight under a tree. Torah came from Yahuah's mouth: "I will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him" (Deuteronomy 18:18). A path that a man worked out for himself can only lead as far as that man could see. The psalmist did not seek his own insight; he said, "I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45).

**It leaves out the first tablet.** The five precepts say nothing of the Creator, because Buddhism has none. Yet Yahushua called "Hear, O Israel; Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" the first of all the commandments (Mark 12:29). A walk with no one to walk with is not the walk of Enoch, who "walked with God" (Genesis 5:24).

**Its road ends in nothing.** The Old Path ends in life: Moses set before Israel "life and good" (Deuteronomy 30:15). The Buddhist road ends with the self blown out.

## The Verdict

Buddhism is a walk with no door and no Lawgiver. It keeps much of the second tablet and walks it with real discipline, but it tells a man that no one can make him clean, and so it leaves him to make himself clean. Scripture says Yahuah does the cleansing, through the blood He provided, and then gives the walk.

Revelation shows who stands at the end of the true road: "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12). The commandments are kept, but not alone. They are kept with the faith of the One who opened the door.
