## The Old Path

In the garden Yahuah promised that the seed of the woman would bruise the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). Yahushua cast out devils "by the Spirit of God" and said no man can spoil a strong man's goods "except he first bind the strong man" (Matthew 12:28-29). He saw Satan "as lightning fall from heaven" (Luke 10:18) and said "now shall the prince of this world be cast out" (John 12:31). Through death He destroyed "him that had the power of death" (Hebrews 2:14). John saw the dragon bound "that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled" (Revelation 20:3). A thousand in Scripture is fullness: Yahuah keeps covenant "to a thousand generations" (Deuteronomy 7:9), and "one day is with the Lord as a thousand years" (2 Peter 3:8). The binding began in Yahushua's ministry and was finished at the cross. The thousand years is the present age, when the nations are turned "from the power of Satan unto God" (Acts 26:18).

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism today still waits for the Messiah. Its statement of faith, the Thirteen Principles written in the twelfth century, includes belief in his coming, and the daily prayers ask for Jerusalem to be rebuilt and the throne of David restored. The Messiah Judaism expects is a man, a son of David, who will gather the exiles to the land, rebuild the Temple, and bring an age of peace on the earth when the nations learn the ways of the God of Israel. Many Jews read Isaiah's picture of swords beaten into plowshares (Isaiah 2:4) and the wolf dwelling with the lamb (Isaiah 11:6) as a description of that coming earthly age.

Judaism has no teaching of the enemy being bound, because mainstream Jewish teaching does not see a rebel holding the nations captive. The satan is generally treated as an accuser who serves under God, or as a name for the evil inclination inside every man.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the promise. It never stopped believing that a son of David would come, that the nations would be drawn to the God of Israel, and that Yahuah would undo the evil that came in at the garden. It kept the very scriptures that foretold the binding, Genesis 3:15 and Isaiah 49:24-25 among them. And it rightly refuses to make the Messiah a god. Judaism expects a man anointed by Yahuah, and Scripture agrees: Peter called Yahushua "a man approved of God" (Acts 2:22).

## Where Judaism Falls

**It missed the one who did the binding.** When Yahushua cast out devils, the leaders of His own people said He did it "by Beelzebub the prince of the devils" (Matthew 12:24). That accusation is the very setting of the strong man saying. He answered that a kingdom divided cannot stand, and that His works were the sign the kingdom "is come unto you" (Matthew 12:28). Judaism still waits for the sign it was shown and refused.

**It looks for a future age that has already begun.** Isaiah asked, "Shall the prey be taken from the mighty?" and Yahuah promised the captives would be "taken away" (Isaiah 49:24-25). That was answered at the cross, and the nations have been coming out ever since. Paul was sent to the Gentiles for exactly this work (Acts 26:18). The peace Judaism waits for is not a political age in the land. It is the turning of men from the enemy to Yahuah, and it has been going on for nearly two thousand years.

**It shrinks the enemy.** If the satan is only an accuser on Yahuah's side, or only a bad impulse, then there is no strong man to bind and no house to spoil. Scripture names him "that old serpent" who "deceiveth the whole world" (Revelation 12:9). A Messiah is needed because the enemy is real.

**It hopes for a rebuilt Temple.** A stone Temple with restored sacrifices goes back to the shadow. Yahushua entered the true sanctuary "by his own blood" and "obtained eternal redemption" (Hebrews 9:12). The Torah stands, but it is walked by those already delivered through that blood, not by men waiting for an altar in Jerusalem.

## The Verdict

Judaism holds the promise and refuses its fulfillment. It waits for a Messiah to free the captives, while the Messiah who bound the strong man has already freed them and is still taking the spoil. The binding is not coming. It came.

Revelation records the shout heard when the enemy was thrown down: "Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Messiah: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down" (Revelation 12:10). The word is "now." Judaism still says "someday" to what heaven announced as done.
