## The Old Path

Before Aaron and his sons wore a single holy garment, Moses brought them to the door of the tabernacle "and wash them with water" (Exodus 29:4); only then were they clothed and anointed (Exodus 29:5-7). Nobody served until he was washed. On Yom Kippur the order held: "therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on" (Leviticus 16:4). Torah is full of washings: the priests at the laver (Exodus 30:20), the leper (Leviticus 14:8-9), the one who touched the dead (Numbers 19:19), the Levites (Numbers 8:7). So Hebrews names "the doctrine of baptisms," plural, among the foundations (Hebrews 6:2), and calls the old rites "divers washings" by the same Greek word (Hebrews 9:10). One God commanded every washing in His own singular voice, and none of them named three. In Acts the believer was immersed "in the name of Yahushua Messiah for the remission of sins" (Acts 2:38), and then wore the garment: "as many of you as have been baptized into Messiah have put on Messiah" (Galatians 3:27).

## What Christianity Teaches

Most of the world's churches baptize babies. The Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican, Presbyterian, and Methodist churches all do it. In the Western churches the water is usually poured or sprinkled on the infant's head. The Catholic church teaches that baptism washes away original sin and makes the child a member of the church, whether or not he can understand anything. Parents and godparents make the promises for him.

In every one of these churches, and in most Baptist and evangelical churches as well, the words spoken are "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost," from Matthew 28:19. The church reads "one baptism" (Ephesians 4:5) as a single rite done once, and treats the plural "baptisms" of Hebrews 6:2 as a puzzle.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the link between water and the Son. It knows that immersion belongs to Messiah, and that Paul said "as many of you as have been baptized into Messiah have put on Messiah" (Galatians 3:27). The churches that immerse believers kept the full burial of Romans 6:4: "we are buried with him by baptism into death." The church also kept the idea that baptism comes at the start of the walk, the door into it.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It sprinkles a baby who cannot turn or believe.** In Acts the order never changes: repent, then be immersed (Acts 2:38). When the eunuch asked to be immersed, Philip answered, "If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest" (Acts 8:37). "He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved" (Mark 16:16). An infant cannot repent and cannot believe, so the water comes before the very things it was meant to answer. The baby is washed for a garment he never asked to wear.

**Sprinkling is not immersion.** The Greek *baptizo* means to dip or immerse. The eunuch and Philip "went down both into the water" and came "up out of the water" (Acts 8:38-39). Paul calls it a burial (Romans 6:4). Nobody is buried by a few drops on the forehead. The Torah washings that lie behind the word were full baths: "bathe himself in water" (Numbers 19:19).

**It uses three names where Acts used one.** Every immersion recorded in Acts was in the name of Yahushua: at Shavuot (Acts 2:38), in Samaria (Acts 8:16), at Cornelius' house (Acts 10:48), and at Ephesus (Acts 19:5). The triune formula rests on a single verse whose wording is disputed, while no Torah washing ever named three and Yahuah commanded every one in the first person singular. The apostles never once spoke the formula the church now requires.

**It lost the many washings.** Hebrews says "baptisms," plural, and uses the same word for the "divers washings" of Torah (Hebrews 9:10). The plural was never a puzzle. It was Leviticus. The church that thinks it has one baptism has forgotten where the doctrine came from.

## The Verdict

Christianity kept the tie between the water and Messiah, then broke the order the water stood in. It washes babies who cannot repent, sprinkles where Acts buried, speaks three names where the apostles spoke one, and forgets the Torah washings that give the word its meaning. Water before the garment means a man who has turned and believed goes fully down into the water in the name of Yahushua, and comes up to be clothed and to serve.

Revelation shows the garment that follows the washing: "to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints" (Revelation 19:8). The linen is the walk. The water came first, and it was meant for someone who chose to come.
