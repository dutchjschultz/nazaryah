## The Old Path

Before Aaron and his sons wore a single holy garment, Moses brought them to the door of the tabernacle "and wash them with water" (Exodus 29:4); only then were they clothed and anointed (Exodus 29:5-7). Nobody served until he was washed. On Yom Kippur the order held: "therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on" (Leviticus 16:4). Torah is full of washings: the priests at the laver (Exodus 30:20), the leper (Leviticus 14:8-9), the one who touched the dead (Numbers 19:19), the Levites (Numbers 8:7). So Hebrews names "the doctrine of baptisms," plural, among the foundations (Hebrews 6:2), and calls the old rites "divers washings" by the same Greek word (Hebrews 9:10). One God commanded every washing in His own singular voice, and none of them named three. In Acts the believer was immersed "in the name of Yahushua Messiah for the remission of sins" (Acts 2:38), and then wore the garment: "as many of you as have been baptized into Messiah have put on Messiah" (Galatians 3:27).

## What Islam Teaches

Islam requires washing before prayer. The Quran commands it: before prayer, wash the face and the hands to the elbows, wipe the head, and wash the feet to the ankles; if a man is in a state of major uncleanness, he must bathe the whole body; and if no water can be found, he may wipe with clean earth (Surah 5:6). The short washing is called *wudu*, the full bath *ghusl*, and the earth-wiping *tayammum*.

Wudu is broken by sleep, by using the toilet, and by other ordinary things of the body, so it is repeated many times a day, before each of the five daily prayers if needed. A well-known saying in Muslim tradition teaches that small sins fall away with the drops of water.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the order of Exodus 29: water before service. No Muslim stands to pray unwashed. The washing of hands and feet before coming near is almost exactly what Yahuah commanded the priests at the bronze laver: "they shall wash their hands and their feet, that they die not" (Exodus 30:21).

It also kept the full bath after major uncleanness, which matches the Torah washings after a discharge or after touching the dead (Leviticus 15:16; Numbers 19:19). And it kept the sense that coming near to God is a serious thing that a man must be made ready for. On these points the Muslim at the fountain is more careful than the church that sprinkles a sleeping baby.

## Where Islam Falls

**It washes, but it never clothes.** In Torah the water came first so the garment could come next. Aaron was washed to be dressed. In Islam the washing leads to no garment at all. Isaiah named the garment the water prepares for: "he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness" (Isaiah 61:10). Islam has the laver with no priestly robe after it.

**It repeats what was meant to point forward.** Hebrews calls the old washings "carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation" (Hebrews 9:10). They were pictures that pointed ahead. Islam took up the picture six hundred years after the time of reformation came, and made it a daily round that never arrives anywhere.

**Water cannot take away sin.** Jeremiah said, "though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me" (Jeremiah 2:22). Peter said immersion is "not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God" (1 Peter 3:21). The washing outside answers to the blood inside, and Islam has no blood. Only "the blood of Yahushua Messiah his Son cleanseth us from all sin" (1 John 1:7).

**It has no Name over the water.** Every washing in Acts was "in the name of Yahushua Messiah" (Acts 2:38; 10:48). Islam washes in the name of God but denies the Son whose name the water was meant to carry.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the old order better than most: water before approaching, hands and feet washed, the full bath after uncleanness. But it took a set of pictures and turned them into an endless routine with no garment at the end and no blood beneath. Washing before prayer is right. Washing in place of the Lamb is the counterfeit. The water in Torah prepared a priest to be clothed, and Islam never puts the robe on.

Revelation shows the garment the water was waiting for: "These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:14). The robes were washed in blood, not at a fountain five times a day.
