## The Question He Asked

At Caesarea Philippi, Yahushua asked His disciples, "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?" Peter answered, "Thou art the Messiah, the Son of the living God," and Yahushua said that answer came from "my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 16:13-17). Peter's answer has two parts: He is the Messiah, and He is the Son of the living God. Every religion's answer is measured against those two parts.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism has no teaching on Yahushua in its own scriptures, since the Buddha lived about five centuries before Him. Modern Buddhists who speak of Him tend to see Him as a wise and compassionate teacher, a man who reached a high spiritual state. Some call Him a bodhisattva, one who reached enlightenment and stayed in the world to help others reach it. The Dalai Lama has spoken of Him in those terms.

In this view Yahushua is a guide on the path. He showed the way of love and selflessness, and anyone who walks that way far enough can reach what He reached.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept respect for His moral teaching. Its call to compassion, patience, and the letting go of greed sits close to the Sermon on the Mount. Yahushua said, "Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you" (Matthew 5:44), and "Take heed, and beware of covetousness" (Luke 12:15). A Buddhist hears those words and nods.

Buddhism also kept the truth that He was a man. It does not make Him a god.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has no Father, so it can have no Son.** Buddhism has no Creator at its center. But Yahushua's whole message was about His Father. He called Him "the only true God" (John 17:3) and said, "my Father is greater than I" (John 14:28). He said, "I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world" (John 16:28). Take away the Father, and nothing is left of the Son but a teacher. Peter's confession is impossible in Buddhism, because there is no "living God" for Him to be the Son of.

**It makes Him a teacher of a path instead of the way.** In Buddhism every man walks his own road to enlightenment. Yahushua did not say He knew the way. He said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6). The door leads to the Father, and there is only one door (John 10:9).

**It wants His teaching and not His blood.** Yahushua did not come only to teach. He said He came "to give his life a ransom for many" (Matthew 20:28), and at the Last Supper, "this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins" (Matthew 26:28). A Buddhist can take the Sermon on the Mount and leave the cross. Scripture does not allow that split.

**It aims to escape, and He rose.** Buddhism's goal is release from existence, the self blown out like a flame. Yahushua rose from the grave in a body. "Behold my hands and my feet… handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have" (Luke 24:39). He did not escape life. He conquered death.

## The Verdict

Buddhism admires the teacher and has never met the Son. It keeps His words on love and covetousness and cuts away the Father He came from, the blood He shed, and the body He rose in.

Revelation names Him in a way no Buddhist teacher could be named: "the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead" (Revelation 1:5). He was not the first to be enlightened. He was the first to rise from the dead, and He rose as the Son of His Father.
