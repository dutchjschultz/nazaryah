## The Old Path

When Yahuah told Abraham about Sodom, He said, "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21). Judgment in Scripture is a court case. The Hebrew *shaphat* means to judge, to decide a case, to pass sentence after a hearing. Abraham pleaded, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). The two men went on toward Sodom while Abraham "stood yet before Yahuah" (Genesis 18:22), and those two are named "two angels" (Genesis 19:1): one Judge, not three. Then the smoke of the plain went up "as the smoke of a furnace" (Genesis 19:28). Sodom was the demonstration, turned "into ashes" as "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Peter 2:6). Solomon wrote that "God shall bring every work into judgment" (Ecclesiastes 12:14), and Daniel saw the dead rise, "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). Hebrews names eternal judgment among the first principles (Hebrews 6:2). The sentence is sure, the day is set, and the Judge is one.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam teaches a Day of Judgment, the Day of Reckoning, when all the dead are raised and stand before God. Each person receives the book of his deeds. The one given his record in his right hand rejoices, and the one given it in his left hand wishes he had never seen it (Surah 69:19, 25). Scales are set up on that day so that no soul is wronged in the least (Surah 21:47). The one whose scales are heavy with good deeds will live a pleasing life, and the one whose scales are light will have the pit for his home (Surah 101:6-9).

The Quran says more than once that no one bearing a burden will bear the burden of another (Surah 6:164; 17:15). Each person answers for himself. Isa has a part on that day, but only as a witness against the people of the Book (Surah 4:159). Unbelievers are sent to Jahannam, a fire Islam teaches is forever.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the frame of the courtroom. There is one Judge with no partner, one fixed Day, and a full record of every deed. Scripture keeps the same record: "a book of remembrance was written before him" (Malachi 3:16), and in Daniel's vision "the judgment was set, and the books were opened" (Daniel 7:10). Islam is right that every work will be brought into judgment and that the Judge of all the earth will do right.

## Where Islam Falls

**Its scales have no blood on them.** David asked the question no scale can answer: "If thou, Yahuah, shouldest mark iniquities… who shall stand?" (Psalm 130:3). Isaiah said that "all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags" (Isaiah 64:6). Islam says no one can bear another's burden. Isaiah says the opposite: "Yahuah hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:6). Deeds do count in the judgment, but only after the blood. Paul set the order: "being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him" and then "saved by his life" (Romans 5:9-10). The walk is weighed after the door is entered, never in place of it.

**It has no ordained man.** Paul told Athens that Yahuah "hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained," and He gave proof by raising him from the dead (Acts 17:31). Yahushua said the Father "hath given him authority to execute judgment also, because he is the Son of man" (John 5:27). Islam makes him a witness. Scripture makes him the man through whom Yahuah judges, proven by a resurrection Islam denies.

**Its fire never finishes.** Sodom suffered "the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 7), yet Sodom is not still burning. It was turned "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6). The fire is eternal in its result, not in its torment. Malachi said the day "shall burn as an oven" and "leave them neither root nor branch," and the wicked "shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:1, 3). Jahannam's endless burning is not the sentence Sodom demonstrated.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the courtroom and removed the mercy seat. It has one Judge, one Day, and full books, but it sends every man to the scales alone, with no blood and no Lamb. Under that sentence no one stands, because no man's good deeds outweigh his sin. Yahuah judges by the man He ordained, and those who enter by the blood are judged as His.

Revelation shows that the deciding book holds names, not weights: "whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire" (Revelation 20:15). Only "they which are written in the Lamb's book of life" enter the city (Revelation 21:27).
