## The Old Path

At Sinai, Yahuah called the whole nation, not one class, "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). When Joshua wanted two men stopped from prophesying in the camp, Moses answered, "would God that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Peter gave the same words to every believer: "a royal priesthood, an holy nation" (1 Peter 2:9). John wrote that Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). Yahushua forbade the titles that lift one man over the rest: "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah; and all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). Elders and overseers still serve, teach, and watch over the flock, but as brothers among brothers, never as a priesthood standing between the people and Yahuah. "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). There is no second.

## What Christianity Teaches

The Catholic and Orthodox churches split the people into two: the clergy and the laity. The clergy are ordained by a bishop, and Catholic teaching holds that ordination leaves a permanent mark on the soul. Only a priest may say the Mass, hear confession and grant absolution, and give most of the sacraments. The people call him "Father." Above the priests stand bishops, archbishops, cardinals, and the pope.

Martin Luther preached the priesthood of all believers, and Protestants dropped the confessional. But they kept the line between clergy and laity. A Protestant pastor is ordained and called "Reverend," stands behind the pulpit, and in most churches is the only one who preaches, baptizes, and leads communion. The people sit in rows and listen.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the words. Peter's "royal priesthood" (1 Peter 2:9) is read in its churches, and the Reformers fought to bring the common man back to the Scriptures in his own tongue, and to teach that he could pray to God without a priest. That was a true recovery, even where it stopped halfway.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It took the people's name for itself.** "Clergy" comes from the Greek *kleros*, a lot or portion. Peter used that very word when he told the elders not to act "as being lords over God's heritage" (1 Peter 5:3). The *kleros*, the heritage, is the flock itself. The church took the name Peter gave to the whole people and gave it to the men who rule over them, the very thing Peter told the elders not to be.

**It calls men "Father" and "Reverend."** Yahushua said, "call no man your father upon the earth: for one is your Father, which is in heaven" (Matthew 23:9). The word "reverend" appears once in the King James Bible, and it belongs to Yahuah: "holy and reverend is his name" (Psalm 111:9).

**It set up a second mediator.** Paul says there is "one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). He is a man standing between God and men, which is why He is not the God He stands before. The Trinity cannot hold that verse, and the clergy system adds a second go-between below Him. When Yahushua died, the veil of the Holy Place was torn "from the top to the bottom" (Matthew 27:51), opening the priestly room to every believer-priest. The church sewed it back up with an altar rail and a pulpit.

**It lets the people stay passive.** Moses wished all of Yahuah's people were prophets (Numbers 11:29). Paul wrote that when the assembly comes together, "every one of you hath a psalm, hath a doctrine" (1 Corinthians 14:26). The clergy system turned that into one man speaking and the rest paying for it.

## The Verdict

Christianity reads "a royal priesthood" from the pulpit and then keeps the people in the pews. It took the name of the flock for its ruling class, gave them titles that belong to Yahuah, and put a second mediator under the one Paul named. Elders are needed, but as brothers serving brothers, not as a priesthood over the laity.

Revelation records the Messiah's own word to one assembly: "this thou hast, that thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitanes, which I also hate" (Revelation 2:6). Whatever that sect taught, its name is made of two Greek words, *nikao*, to conquer, and *laos*, the people. The one who washed His people in His own blood "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). No one is called to conquer them.
