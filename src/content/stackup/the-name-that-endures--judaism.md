## The Old Path

At the burning bush Yahuah gave Moses His Name and bound it to time: "this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations" (Exodus 3:15). Moses later warned Israel to "fear this glorious and fearful name, Yahuah thy God" (Deuteronomy 28:58). The psalmist sang, "Thy name, O Yahuah, endureth for ever" (Psalm 135:13), and Malachi saw a book of remembrance written for those who "thought upon his name" (Malachi 3:16). A title tells what someone is; a name tells who He is. Yahuah gave a Name to be remembered and spoken, and nothing in Scripture ever withdrew it. Every religion's answer is measured against that standing command.

## What Judaism Teaches

The four letters of the Name, *Yod-He-Vav-He*, stand in the Hebrew Scriptures nearly seven thousand times, and Jewish scribes copied every one of them with great care. Yet Judaism today teaches that the Name must not be spoken. When the Scriptures are read aloud in the synagogue, the reader says *Adonai*, a title meaning "my Lord," wherever the Name is written. In ordinary talk many Jews say *HaShem*, "the Name," and many write "G-d" in English so that even the title is not erased.

The practice is old. By the later Second Temple period the Name had already dropped out of common speech. Centuries later, the scribes who added vowel marks to the Hebrew text placed the vowels of *Adonai* under the four letters, as a signal to the reader to say the title instead. The Name stayed on the page and left the mouth.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the letters. Without the care of the scribes, the four letters could have been lost from the text, and they were not. Every Hebrew Bible on earth still carries the Name where Moses wrote it.

Judaism also kept the fear. It never said, as the modern pulpit says, that the Name does not matter. It treats the Name as holy and refuses to use it lightly. Moses commanded exactly that fear (Deuteronomy 28:58). Judaism took the Name seriously. Its error is in what it did with that seriousness.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It turned fear into silence.** Moses commanded Israel to fear the Name, not to bury it. In the same Torah he told them to "swear by his name" (Deuteronomy 6:13). The priests were told to bless the people and so "put my name upon the children of Israel" (Numbers 6:27). In the fields of Bethlehem, Boaz greeted his reapers, "Yahuah be with you," and they answered, "Yahuah bless thee" (Ruth 2:4). Plain farm workers spoke the Name in the open. A Name put upon a whole people was never meant to be a Name no one may say.

**It misread the third commandment.** "Thou shalt not take the name of Yahuah thy God in vain" (Exodus 20:7). The Hebrew word for vain is *shav*, meaning emptiness or falsehood. The command forbids an empty or lying use of the Name. It does not forbid using it. Yahuah promised the opposite of silence: "my people shall know my name" (Isaiah 52:6), and He sets on high the one who "hath known my name" (Psalm 91:14).

**It put a title where Jeremiah said a title did the damage.** Jeremiah condemned prophets who "think to cause my people to forget my name… as their fathers have forgotten my name for Baal" (Jeremiah 23:27). *Baal* means lord, master, or owner. Israel did not forget the Name by hating Yahuah; they forgot it by calling Him by a word that meant "lord." Through Hosea, Yahuah said He would take that word out of their mouth (Hosea 2:16-17). Saying *Adonai* in place of the Name is a cleaner word, but it is the same trade.

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the letters and lost the voice. It guarded the Name so closely that Israel stopped saying it, and a fear meant to keep the Name holy became the very means by which it was forgotten. Yahuah said His Name was His memorial "unto all generations," and a memorial no one speaks is no memorial at all.

Revelation names the people Yahuah rewards when the time of judgment comes: "thy servants the prophets, and… the saints, and them that fear thy name, small and great" (Revelation 11:18). The fear Moses commanded is honored in the end. It is the fear that speaks the Name in truth, not the fear that hides it.
