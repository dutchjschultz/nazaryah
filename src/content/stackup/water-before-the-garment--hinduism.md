## The Old Path

Before Aaron and his sons wore a single holy garment, Moses brought them to the door of the tabernacle "and wash them with water" (Exodus 29:4); only then were they clothed and anointed (Exodus 29:5-7). Nobody served until he was washed. On Yom Kippur the order held: "therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on" (Leviticus 16:4). Torah is full of washings: the priests at the laver (Exodus 30:20), the leper (Leviticus 14:8-9), the one who touched the dead (Numbers 19:19), the Levites (Numbers 8:7). So Hebrews names "the doctrine of baptisms," plural, among the foundations (Hebrews 6:2), and calls the old rites "divers washings" by the same Greek word (Hebrews 9:10). One God commanded every washing in His own singular voice, and none of them named three. In Acts the believer was immersed "in the name of Yahushua Messiah for the remission of sins" (Acts 2:38), and then wore the garment: "as many of you as have been baptized into Messiah have put on Messiah" (Galatians 3:27).

## What Hinduism Teaches

Water is at the center of Hindu worship. A devout Hindu bathes before his morning prayers and before going into the presence of the gods.

Above all waters stands the Ganges. Hindus honor the river as the goddess Ganga, who in their stories came down from heaven to the earth. They teach that bathing in her washes away sins. Pilgrims go down into the river at holy cities such as Varanasi and Haridwar, and at the great Kumbh Mela festivals millions bathe on the most favored days. Families carry Ganges water home in bottles to sprinkle for purity, place a few drops in the mouth of the dying, and scatter the ashes of the dead on the river in the hope that it will help them toward release.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept the order of Exodus 29 in its daily life: wash first, then come near. The Hindu who will not enter worship unwashed understands something the casual churchgoer has forgotten. It kept the sense that a man is defiled and must be made clean before he approaches.

It also kept an old memory that cleansing should come from water that flows. Torah asked for "running water" for cleansing (Leviticus 15:13), and Ezekiel saw a river of healing flowing out from the house of Yahuah (Ezekiel 47:1, 9).

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It serves the river instead of the One who made it.** The Ganges is honored as a goddess. Paul described exactly this: men who "worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator" (Romans 1:25). Torah water was only water, set apart by the word of the God who commanded it. When the water itself becomes a god, the washing becomes idolatry.

**The power was never in the river.** When Elisha told Naaman to wash seven times in the Jordan, Naaman was angry: "Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel?" (2 Kings 5:12). He was cleansed in the Jordan, but not because the Jordan was holy. He was cleansed because he obeyed the word of Yahuah's prophet. No river cleanses. The God who commands the washing cleanses.

**Water does not take away sin.** Jeremiah said, "though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me" (Jeremiah 2:22). Peter said immersion is "not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God" (1 Peter 3:21). The outer washing stands on the blood. Hinduism has a river to wash in and no Lamb to stand on.

**It washes the dead, who cannot be clothed.** The garment in Exodus 29 was for living priests who would serve. Ashes on a river help no one: "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Water before the garment is for the living, in this life, by their own choice.

## The Verdict

Hinduism kept the instinct that a man must be washed before he comes near, and it kept going down into the water. But it made the river a goddess, put the cleansing power in the water itself, and offered it even to the dead. The water of Torah was plain water commanded by one God, preparing a living man to be clothed and to serve. A goddess for a river is not the doctrine of baptisms. It is the counterfeit of it.

Revelation ends with the only invitation to water that gives life: "let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17). That water is not a goddess and is not found in India. It is given by the God who calls.
