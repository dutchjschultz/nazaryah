## The Old Path

Yahuah has only ever had one people. At the first Pesach the Law said, "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger" (Exodus 12:49). The stranger who joins himself to Yahuah and keeps His Sabbaths is brought to His holy mountain, "for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:6-7). Paul pictures that one people as one olive tree. The wild branch is "graffed in" and partakes "of the root and fatness of the olive tree," and he is warned, "thou bearest not the root, but the root thee" (Romans 11:17-18). Gentiles in Messiah are no longer "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" but "fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:12, 19). The door into that tree is the Seed, "which is Messiah," and "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:16, 29). One tree, one root, one law, entered by belief and not by birth.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam calls its people the ummah, the one community of all Muslims across every nation. The Quran calls it "the best community raised up for mankind" (Surah 3:110) and "a middle nation" (Surah 2:143). A man enters it by confessing that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is his messenger. Race, tribe, and language do not decide who belongs.

Islam also claims Abraham. The Quran says Abraham "was neither a Jew nor a Christian," but upright and a Muslim (Surah 3:67), and that Abraham and Ishmael raised the foundations of the House at Mecca (Surah 2:127). Islam teaches that every true prophet was a Muslim, that the earlier scriptures were changed by those who held them, and that Muhammad came as the last prophet with the final law.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the truth that Yahuah's people are not a bloodline. Arab and non-Arab, black and white, enter the ummah the same way and pray in the same rows. Peter learned the same lesson at the house of Cornelius: "God is no respecter of persons" (Acts 10:34).

Islam also kept the truth that there is one people and not two. It does not divide its community into an earthly nation and a heavenly church with two plans. In that one point it stands nearer to the olive tree than the churches that teach two peoples.

## Where Islam Falls

**It plants a different tree.** Islam builds its community on Ishmael and the House at Mecca. Scripture sends the promise another way. Yahuah blessed Ishmael but said, "my covenant will I establish with Isaac" (Genesis 17:21), and "in Isaac shall thy seed be called" (Genesis 21:12). Paul quotes those same words to show who the children of promise are (Romans 9:7-8). The olive tree is Israel's tree. A community that sets itself apart from Israel's root is not a branch on it.

**It replaces the root.** Paul said plainly that the Seed of Abraham is one, and that Seed is Messiah (Galatians 3:16). Branches live because of the root they are joined to. Islam honors Isa as a prophet but makes Muhammad the seal and the Quran the final word, so the ummah is held together by a later prophet, not by the Son. Islam even denies that the Son died. But the wild branches were brought near "by the blood of Messiah" (Ephesians 2:13). With no blood there is no grafting.

**It brings a different law.** Yahuah gave one law for the native and the stranger, "one law and one manner" (Numbers 15:16). Islam teaches that the Law of Moses was set aside and that sharia is the final law for all people. The stranger in Israel kept Yahuah's Sabbath (Isaiah 56:6). A branch that brings its own law has not been grafted. It has been planted somewhere else.

## The Verdict

Islam saw rightly that Yahuah's people are one people and not a bloodline. But it built that one people on Ishmael, on Mecca, and on a later prophet, and it cut away the blood that opens the way in. The ummah is a single tree, but it is not the olive tree. It has its own root and its own law.

Revelation shows whose house it is. The new city has twelve gates, and on them are written "the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel" (Revelation 21:12). Every one who enters, from every nation, walks in through a gate named for Israel, and every one of them was "redeemed... to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation" (Revelation 5:9).
