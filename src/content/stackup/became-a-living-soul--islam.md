## The Old Path

Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Adam did not receive a soul; he became one, a *nephesh chayyah*, the same words Genesis uses for the "living creature" of the field (Genesis 1:24). The sentence on sin was "unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). Ezekiel says it twice: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20). When the breath goes, man "returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4), for "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Immortality is not something man has. It is something he must "seek for" (Romans 2:7) and will "put on" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53).

## What Islam Teaches

The Quran says God created man from clay (Surah 15:26; 55:14) and then breathed into him of His spirit (Surah 15:29; 38:72). The Arabic word is *ruh*. Islam teaches that this spirit, or soul, lives on when the body dies. The Quran says God takes the souls at the time of their death (Surah 39:42), and that behind the dead is a barrier, the *barzakh*, until the Day they are raised (Surah 23:100).

Islamic teaching holds that the soul is conscious in that waiting time. It is questioned in the grave by two angels, and the grave becomes a place of comfort for the believer and of punishment for the unbeliever. On the Last Day the bodies are raised and joined again to their souls for the final judgment.

## What Islam Kept

Islam holds that man was made from the earth and that life came into him when God breathed. It holds that "every soul shall taste death" (Surah 3:185; 21:35), which is close to Ezekiel's "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4). It also kept a bodily resurrection on the Last Day. The Quran answers the man who asks who can give life to rotten bones by saying the One who made them the first time (Surah 36:78-79). Ezekiel saw the same dry bones live (Ezekiel 37:5-6).

## Where Islam Falls

**It turns the breath into a separate person.** In Genesis the breath of life does not go into Adam as a second self. It goes into the dust, and the two together "became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). When the breath leaves, it "shall return unto God who gave it" (Ecclesiastes 12:7), the way it came, and the man is gone. The same breath is in the beasts: "they have all one breath" (Ecclesiastes 3:19). Breath is life, not a hidden man inside the man.

**It keeps the dead awake.** Islam has the dead questioned, comforted, or punished in the grave. Scripture says the dead do not know anything (Ecclesiastes 9:5), that "there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave" (Ecclesiastes 9:10), and that "in death there is no remembrance" (Psalm 6:5). Yahushua called death sleep. "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth," He said, and then told them plainly, "Lazarus is dead" (John 11:11, 14). Lazarus came out with nothing to report.

**It puts judgment before the Judgment.** If the grave is already a place of reward and punishment, then the case has been decided before the Day of Judgment. Scripture keeps the order: death, then sleep, then resurrection, then judgment. "Many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). No one is sorted until they awake.

**It has no one to give the life back.** Islam keeps the resurrection but denies that the Son ever died and rose (Surah 4:157). Paul says the resurrection hangs on His: "if Messiah be not raised, your faith is vain… Then they also which are fallen asleep in Messiah are perished" (1 Corinthians 15:17-18). Yahuah raised His Son first, as "the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20). A resurrection with no firstfruits has no harvest behind it.

## The Verdict

Islam began at the right place, with clay and breath, and then added a living soul that Genesis never mentions. The man made of dust became a living soul. He does not have one waiting to slip out. When he dies, he sleeps until the Day, and his only hope is the resurrection that Yahuah proved by raising His Son.

Revelation shows that hope kept. The Son says, "I am he that liveth, and was dead… and have the keys of hell and of death" (Revelation 1:18). On the Day, "death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them" (Revelation 20:13). The dead come up out of the grave because they were in it, asleep, and not anywhere else.
