## The Old Path

Moses set two roads before Israel: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). Joshua said the same: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). Yahuah takes "no pleasure in the death of him that dieth... wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9). A choice made can be unmade. "Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33), and the righteous man who turns away dies in his sin (Ezekiel 18:24). The blood on the door opened the road out of Egypt (Exodus 12:13), but the people still had to walk it, and "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Yahuah declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), but knowing is not forcing. The choice was always the person's to make.

## What Islam Teaches

Belief in *qadar*, the divine decree, is one of the six articles of faith in Islam. The Quran says, "Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (Surah 54:49), and that no disaster strikes the earth or a person except that it is written in a record before it happens (Surah 57:22). Muslims are taught to say, "Nothing will happen to us except what God has decreed for us" (Surah 9:51), and to add *insha'Allah*, "if God wills," to every plan.

The Quran also says that God "sends astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills" (Surah 14:4). At the same time it holds man responsible. It says there is "no compulsion in religion" (Surah 2:256) and "whoever wills, let him believe; and whoever wills, let him disbelieve" (Surah 18:29). Muslim scholars argued for centuries over how decree and choice fit together, and mainstream Sunni teaching holds both without fully explaining how.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the truth that Yahuah knows all things and that nothing surprises Him. Isaiah said He declares "the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10). Islam also kept the truth that man answers for his own choices. The Quran's "whoever wills, let him believe" stands closer to Joshua's "choose you this day" than the Calvinist who says the choice was made before birth.

## Where Islam Falls

**It makes knowing into writing.** Islam teaches that every event, good and evil, was written down and decreed before it came to be. Scripture separates what Yahuah knows from what He wills. Of the children burned to Molech, He said, "which I commanded them not, neither came it into my heart" (Jeremiah 7:31). Yahuah foreknew Israel's sin and still pleaded with them to turn. A decree that writes every evil deed in advance makes Yahuah the author of what He says never entered His heart.

**It says God sends men astray.** The Quran says God leads astray whom He wills. Scripture says the opposite of Yahuah's will: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth" (Ezekiel 18:32), and He is "not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9). Yahuah does harden those who harden themselves, as with Pharaoh, who "hardened his heart" against Him again and again (Exodus 8:15, 32). But He does not choose men for straying and then judge them for it.

**It offers the choice with no door.** Moses set life before a people who had already been passed over by the blood of the lamb. The choice to walk came after the blood. Islam denies that the Messiah died, so its choice is a choice to work, with no Lamb at the door. A man may choose the road, but he cannot open the gate by choosing.

## The Verdict

Islam holds two things at once and cannot join them: a decree that writes all things beforehand, and a man who answers for his choices. Scripture joins them plainly. Yahuah knows the end, and He still sets life and death before each person and says, choose life. His knowing does not force the hand. Islam's *qadar* makes the choice a script, and its denial of the cross leaves the choice with no way in.

Revelation shows that the book of life is not a fixed decree. The Son says, "He that overcometh... I will not blot out his name out of the book of life" (Revelation 3:5). A name can be blotted out, and a name can be kept. The overcomer chooses, and the book answers.
