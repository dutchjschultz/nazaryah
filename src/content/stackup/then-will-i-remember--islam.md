## The Old Path

Leviticus 26 is a long warning, and at the bottom of it Yahuah wrote the road home. If Israel would "confess their iniquity" and their "uncircumcised hearts be humbled," then "will I remember my covenant with Jacob" (Leviticus 26:40-42). The return was provided for before the curse ever fell. To repent is *shuv*, to turn around and walk the other way; to confess is *yadah*, to acknowledge openly, out loud. Joel cried, "turn ye even to me with all your heart… rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13), and Ezekiel gave the reason: "I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth… wherefore turn yourselves, and live" (Ezekiel 18:32). One God receives the turn, and He Himself remembers. The turn was never the price. The price was blood Yahuah gave "upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls" (Leviticus 17:11). So Hebrews sets "repentance from dead works" first among the foundations (Hebrews 6:1).

## What Islam Teaches

Islam calls repentance *tawbah*, which means to turn back. The Quran tells believers to "turn to Allah with sincere repentance" (Surah 66:8), and says that Allah "loves those who turn to Him constantly" (Surah 2:222). It tells sinners not to despair of mercy, for Allah forgives all sins (Surah 39:53). For the one who repents, believes, and does righteous work, it says Allah will change his evil deeds into good ones (Surah 25:70).

Muslim teachers list the conditions plainly: stop the sin, regret it, resolve never to return to it, and make right whatever was taken from another person. No priest hears the confession, and no one stands between the sinner and God. The Muslim turns straight to Allah in prayer.

There is no blood in it. Islam keeps an animal sacrifice at Eid al-Adha, but the Quran says of it, "It is not their meat nor their blood that reaches Allah; it is your piety that reaches Him" (Surah 22:37). And Islam teaches that Isa, the Messiah, was never crucified at all (Surah 4:157).

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept the turn. *Tawbah* and *shuv* say the same thing: a man stops, faces about, and walks back. It kept the hope of Leviticus 26, that no one has gone too far to come home.

Islam also kept the straight line between the sinner and God. There is no booth and no priest pronouncing pardon. David confessed the same way: "I acknowledge my sin unto thee… and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin" (Psalm 32:5). On this point the Muslim stands closer to Leviticus than the church that built a confession box.

And Islam kept restitution. Making right what was taken is Torah: the guilty man "shall recompense his trespass with the principal thereof, and add unto it the fifth part thereof" (Numbers 5:7).

## Where Islam Falls

**It has a turn with no altar.** In Leviticus the turn and the blood stand together. The same Torah that wrote the road home in chapter 26 wrote in chapter 17 that "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul." Islam keeps the road and tears out the altar at the end of it. Hebrews says it flatly: "without shedding of blood is no remission" (Hebrews 9:22).

**It denies the Lamb Yahuah provided.** The blood that answers repentance is not a man's piety. Yahuah gave it. Abraham told his son, "God will provide himself a lamb" (Genesis 22:8), and John pointed to Yahushua: "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29). A Messiah who was never crucified shed no blood, and a turn with no blood beneath it has nothing to stand on.

**It turns repentance into a payment.** When evil deeds become good deeds because a man repented and worked, the turn has become the price. But repentance from dead works cannot be made of more works. Isaiah said "all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags" (Isaiah 64:6). The turn opens the door; Yahuah's blood pays the debt; and only then does the walk of obedience begin.

## The Verdict

Islam kept the first half of Leviticus 26 and lost the ground it stands on. It turns, it confesses, it hopes, and it makes restitution, and all of that is right. But it turns toward a God it says wants no blood, and so it never reaches the covenant Yahuah promised to remember. Repentance without the altar is a man walking home to a locked door.

Revelation shows who opened it. The elders sing to the Lamb, "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation" (Revelation 5:9). The Muslim's turn is welcome there, but only through the blood his book says was never shed.
