## The Old Path

Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6), and afterward Yahuah said Abraham "kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Israel was spared in Egypt by the blood on the door, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13), and only then was led to Sinai. Moses said the commandment "is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). The psalmist said, "I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45), and Solomon called the commandment "a lamp" and the law "light" (Proverbs 6:23). Deliverance comes through the blood Yahuah provided; Torah is what walking looks like on the other side of it. Torah was never the door, and the door was never the end of the road. Every religion's answer is measured against that order.

## What Hinduism Teaches

Hinduism calls the right way of living *dharma*. It is duty, order, and the right path all in one word. But dharma is not the same for everyone. A man's duty depends on his caste, the station he was born into, and on his stage of life, whether student, householder, retiree, or one who has given up the world. The Bhagavad Gita, one of Hinduism's best-loved books, teaches that it is better to do one's own duty poorly than another man's duty well (Bhagavad Gita 3:35).

Hinduism speaks of three main paths toward the goal: the path of action and duty, the path of knowledge, and the path of devotion to a chosen god. The goal of every path is *moksha*, release from the long cycle of death and rebirth. That release is reached over many lifetimes, as a soul works off the weight of its past deeds.

## What Hinduism Kept

Hinduism kept much of the second tablet of the commandments. Its moral teaching lists restraints much like the ones Moses carried down: do not harm, do not lie, do not steal, keep sexual purity, and do not grasp after what is not yours. It honors father and mother. Scripture commands the same: "Thou shalt not kill… Thou shalt not steal… Thou shalt not bear false witness" (Exodus 20:13-16). Hinduism also kept the idea that life is a walk with duties, not a free-for-all. It does not teach that a man may do as he likes.

## Where Hinduism Falls

**It has no door.** Every Hindu path is walked toward release, not from it. Israel was delivered first, by blood on the door, and walked afterward (Exodus 12:13). Hinduism turns the order around: a man walks for lifetimes in the hope of being freed at the end. With no Lamb and no door, the walk becomes the means of deliverance, and it never ends. Scripture says a man dies once, and then comes judgment (Hebrews 9:27). There is no second life to walk better.

**It gives a different law to each man by birth.** Dharma tells the priest one thing and the laborer another, and a man's station is fixed before he is born. Torah gave one law to all: "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you" (Exodus 12:49). The walk Yahuah gave does not change with a man's birth. The stranger who joined Israel walked the same road as the son of Abraham.

**It keeps the second tablet and breaks the first.** The commandments begin, "Thou shalt have no other gods before me," and, "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image" (Exodus 20:3-4). Hindu duty includes service at the shrines of many gods and their images. A walk that keeps faith with men and breaks faith with Yahuah is a walk with half the road torn up. Yahushua named the first commandment first (Mark 12:29-30).

## The Verdict

Hinduism walks a long road of duty with no door at the start of it. It keeps much of what Moses wrote about loving one's neighbor and breaks what he wrote about Yahuah. It assigns the road by birth, and it promises release only after lifetimes of walking. Scripture puts the door first, gives one Torah to every man, and asks him to walk it in the one life he has.

Revelation speaks to the assembly that walked rightly: "behold, I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it: for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word" (Revelation 3:8). The door is set open first. Keeping His word is the walk through it.
