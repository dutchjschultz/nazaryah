## The Old Path

Man was formed "of the dust of the ground" and became "a living soul" (Genesis 2:7), and to dust he returns (Genesis 3:19). The One who formed him from the dust can call him out of it. In the Song of Moses, before Israel ever entered the land, Yahuah said, "I kill, and I make alive," and in the same verse, "there is no god with me" (Deuteronomy 32:39). Hannah sang that He "bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up" (1 Samuel 2:6). Job knew that "in my flesh shall I see God" (Job 19:26). Isaiah wrote that "the earth shall cast out the dead" (Isaiah 26:19), and Daniel saw "many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth" awake (Daniel 12:2). Until that day "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). The resurrection of the dead is no late borrowing from Persia. It stands in the Law, and Hebrews names it among the first principles (Hebrews 6:1-2).

## What Buddhism Teaches

The Buddha taught that there is no permanent self, a teaching called *anatta*. A person is a bundle of changing parts, body, feelings, thoughts, and awareness, with no lasting soul underneath. Yet Buddhism still teaches rebirth. Driven by karma, the stream of a life passes on at death into a new birth as a god, a human, an animal, a hungry ghost, or a being in one of the hells. Tibetan Buddhism teaches an in-between state, the *bardo*, of up to forty-nine days between one life and the next.

The goal is to end the cycle. *Nirvana* means a blowing out, as a flame is blown out. The Buddha is said to have reached it, and at his death he passed beyond rebirth for good. His remains were divided and enshrined, and relics said to be his are honored in shrines across Asia to this day.

## What Buddhism Kept

On one point Buddhism stands closer to Genesis than most of the world, the church included. It refuses the idea of an immortal soul living inside the body. Scripture never says man has an undying soul. It says man became a living soul when dust received breath (Genesis 2:7). Solomon put men and beasts side by side: "all are of the dust, and all turn to dust again" (Ecclesiastes 3:20). The Buddha saw that nothing in man is eternal by nature, and on that he was right.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It denies the soul and still keeps it moving.** Having said there is no lasting self, Buddhism sends something on to a new birth anyway, carried by karma from body to body. Scripture sends nothing anywhere. The dead lie in the grave, where "there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom" (Ecclesiastes 9:10). Nothing travels and nothing is reborn. The dead wait in the dust for the voice that calls them out.

**It seeks to be blown out; Scripture promises to be raised.** Nirvana is the flame put out for good. Solomon wrote that "the spirit of man is the candle of Yahuah" (Proverbs 20:27). The candle belongs to Yahuah, not to the man, and it is not the man's work to snuff it out. The hope of Scripture is not ending but waking: "Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust" (Isaiah 26:19), and the dead rise "to everlasting life" (Daniel 12:2).

**It has no one to raise the dead.** Buddhism has no Creator, so it has no one who can say, "I kill, and I make alive" (Deuteronomy 32:39). A path walked by one's own effort can lead out of desire, but it cannot lead out of a grave. The Buddha's relics are kept in shrines. The tomb of Yahushua held nothing to keep: "He is not here: for he is risen" (Matthew 28:6). Yahuah raised Him, and the same power will raise the rest.

## The Verdict

Buddhism saw the truth that man has no immortal soul, then built on it a wheel of rebirth with an exit and no resurrection. Its highest hope is to be put out like a flame. Scripture's hope is to be called out of the dust by the One who formed it. Rebirth and escape are not resurrection. Only the Life-giver raises the dead, and Buddhism has no Life-giver.

Revelation gives the answer to every shrine of relics: "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore" (Revelation 1:18). Yahushua was not blown out. He was raised, and He holds the keys of the grave for all who sleep in it.
