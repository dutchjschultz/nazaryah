## The Old Path

When Yahuah told Abraham about Sodom, He said, "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21). Judgment in Scripture is a court case. The Hebrew *shaphat* means to judge, to decide a case, to pass sentence after a hearing. Abraham pleaded, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). The two men went on toward Sodom while Abraham "stood yet before Yahuah" (Genesis 18:22), and those two are named "two angels" (Genesis 19:1): one Judge, not three. Then the smoke of the plain went up "as the smoke of a furnace" (Genesis 19:28). Sodom was the demonstration, turned "into ashes" as "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Peter 2:6). Solomon wrote that "God shall bring every work into judgment" (Ecclesiastes 12:14), and Daniel saw the dead rise, "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). Hebrews names eternal judgment among the first principles (Hebrews 6:2). The sentence is sure, the day is set, and the Judge is one.

## What Christianity Teaches

The Catholic Church teaches a particular judgment at the moment of death. Each soul is judged as it leaves the body and goes to heaven, to hell, or to purgatory, a place where souls are purified by suffering before they can enter heaven. Prayers and Masses are offered to shorten their time there. The sale of indulgences for that purpose stirred Martin Luther to post his Ninety-five Theses in 1517.

The Protestants threw out purgatory but kept judgment at death. In most churches the soul goes straight to heaven or to hell when the body dies. The creed still says Jesus "shall come to judge the quick and the dead," but by then the verdict has long been carried out. Hell is taught as conscious torment that never ends. Many teachers also point to the three visitors at Abraham's tent as a picture of the Trinity.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept a coming day of judgment in its creed, and it kept the truth that Yahushua returns to judge. The Protestants were right to reject purgatory. The church still teaches that the stakes are real and the sentence is final, and that much agrees with Scripture.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It passes sentence before the hearing.** Yahushua will "judge the quick and the dead at his appearing" (2 Timothy 4:1). "When the Son of man shall come in his glory… before him shall be gathered all nations" (Matthew 25:31-32). Yahuah "hath appointed a day" for it (Acts 17:31). Even with Sodom, Yahuah heard first and sentenced after: "I will go down now, and see" (Genesis 18:21). If every soul is judged at death, the day of judgment is only a rerun of verdicts already served.

**It invents a fire that cleans and a fire that never finishes.** Purgatory is found nowhere in Scripture. A man dies once, "but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27), and "by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified" (Hebrews 10:14). Nothing is left for a fire to purge. Endless torment fails the same test. Sodom suffered "the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 7), yet it was turned "into ashes" (2 Peter 2:6) and is not burning today. Malachi said the day will "leave them neither root nor branch" (Malachi 4:1). The fire is eternal in its result. The wicked are consumed.

**It reads three Judges into Abraham's tent.** The two men "went toward Sodom: but Abraham stood yet before Yahuah" (Genesis 18:22), and those two are "two angels" (Genesis 19:1). Abraham spoke to one Judge. When Yahushua judges, it is by authority received: "the Father judgeth no man, but hath committed all judgment unto the Son," and has given him that authority "because he is the Son of man" (John 5:22, 27). Yahuah judges "by that man whom he hath ordained" (Acts 17:31). A co-equal God needs nothing committed to him. A man ordained by the one God does.

## The Verdict

The church kept the judgment day in its creed and emptied it in its preaching. It sentences souls at death, purges them in a fire Scripture never lit, torments the wicked in a fire Sodom never showed, and divides the one Judge into three. The sentence is sure, but it is passed once, at the appearing, by the man Yahuah ordained, and it ends in ashes, not endless burning.

Revelation names the end of the matter: "death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death" (Revelation 20:14). Not a second life in torment, but a second death, and that is final.
