## The Old Path

Yahuah promised Abraham "all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession" (Genesis 17:8). The Psalmist wrote, "The heaven, even the heavens, are Yahuah's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men" (Psalm 115:16), and "The righteous shall inherit the land, and dwell therein for ever" (Psalm 37:29). Yahushua said the same: "the meek… shall inherit the earth" (Matthew 5:5). The dead do not go anywhere at death. They "sleep in the dust of the earth" until they awake (Daniel 12:2), and Job knew his Redeemer would "stand at the latter day upon the earth" (Job 19:25). The hope is resurrection to "new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). The inheritance was never heaven. It is the earth made new.

## What Buddhism Teaches

The Buddha taught that all life is bound up in suffering, that suffering comes from craving, and that craving keeps beings trapped in the round of rebirth. The way out is the Eightfold Path, and the goal at the end of it is *nirvana*. The word means "blowing out," the way a flame goes out when its fuel is gone. When craving, hatred, and delusion are blown out, there is no more fuel for rebirth, and the round ends.

Buddhism also teaches that there is no permanent self. What passes from life to life is not a soul but a stream of causes. When the Buddha was asked whether an enlightened one exists after death, he refused to give an answer. Buddhism has heavens, but they are temporary stops in the round, not the goal. In Pure Land Buddhism, popular in China and Japan, the faithful hope to be reborn in the Pure Land of the Buddha Amitabha, a land of beauty to the west, where enlightenment can be reached more easily.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept two things worth noticing. First, it saw that craving lies at the root of man's misery. James said the same: "every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust," and lust, when it is finished, "bringeth forth death" (James 1:14-15). John wrote that "the world passeth away, and the lust thereof" (1 John 2:17).

Second, it denied that man carries an immortal soul. That stands closer to Genesis than most of the world's religions, and closer than most churches. Man "became a living soul" when Yahuah breathed into the dust (Genesis 2:7), and at death "his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4).

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has no Father, so it has no inheritance.** An inheritance needs someone to leave it. Buddhism has no Creator, no Giver, and no will. Scripture says Yahuah gave the earth to the children of men (Psalm 115:16), and that those He calls are "heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Messiah" (Romans 8:17). Without a Father there are no heirs, and without heirs there is no promise. Buddhism can only offer an ending.

**It puts out the flame instead of ending death.** The goal of nirvana is that nothing remains to be reborn. Scripture's goal is that death itself is destroyed: "the last enemy that shall be destroyed is death" (1 Corinthians 15:26). Buddhism solves the problem of death by ending the one who dies. Yahuah solves it by raising him. Daniel's sleepers "shall awake, some to everlasting life" (Daniel 12:2).

**It looks for a land somewhere else.** The Pure Land lies far to the west, in another realm. The promised land is this earth, made new. The meek inherit "the earth" (Matthew 5:5), not a distant field of jewels.

**It walks out by its own effort.** The Eightfold Path is walked by one's own discipline, with no one to deliver. The inheritance of Abraham does not come that way. It comes to those who are Messiah's (Galatians 3:29), entered through the blood Yahuah provided, and only then walked out in His commandments.

## The Verdict

Buddhism saw the disease better than most. It knew craving brings death, and it knew man has no immortal soul. But it has no Father to give an inheritance, no resurrection to restore the sleeper, and no new earth to receive him. Its best hope is that the flame goes out. The old path promises that the dead rise and inherit the earth.

Revelation gives what Buddhism cannot: "He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his God, and he shall be my son" (Revelation 21:7). The inheritance is real because the Father is real, and the one who receives it is not blown out but raised up as a son.
