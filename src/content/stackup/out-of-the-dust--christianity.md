## The Old Path

Man was formed "of the dust of the ground" and became "a living soul" (Genesis 2:7), and to dust he returns (Genesis 3:19). The One who formed him from the dust can call him out of it. In the Song of Moses, before Israel ever entered the land, Yahuah said, "I kill, and I make alive," and in the same verse, "there is no god with me" (Deuteronomy 32:39). Hannah sang that He "bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up" (1 Samuel 2:6). Job knew that "in my flesh shall I see God" (Job 19:26). Isaiah wrote that "the earth shall cast out the dead" (Isaiah 26:19), and Daniel saw "many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth" awake (Daniel 12:2). Until that day "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). The resurrection of the dead is no late borrowing from Persia. It stands in the Law, and Hebrews names it among the first principles (Hebrews 6:1-2).

## What Christianity Teaches

Most of the church teaches that at death the soul leaves the body and goes at once to heaven or to hell. At the funeral the family is told the departed is in a better place, already with Jesus, and perhaps looking down on them. Catholic teaching adds that the saints in heaven hear prayers and pray for the living. The church also says Jesus raised himself from the dead, and points to that as proof that he is God.

The creeds still say "the resurrection of the body," and the church still expects the dead to rise when Jesus returns. But since the soul already has its reward, the resurrection is left as an afterthought, a body reunited with a soul that never needed it. The idea of a soul that is immortal and set free from the body at death was taught by Plato long before the church took it up.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the most important fact: Yahushua died, was buried, and rose bodily from the grave. It kept the trumpet and the dead rising at His return, and it kept the words of the creed. Those words are true. The church has simply stopped living by them.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It sends the dead to heaven before the trumpet.** Peter, preaching at Shavuot, said plainly, "David is not ascended into the heavens" (Acts 2:34). Yahushua said, "no man hath ascended up to heaven" (John 3:13). The dead sleep: "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth" (John 11:11), and "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). The church has its members in heaven ahead of David.

**It makes the resurrection pointless.** Paul gave the grieving one comfort, and it was not that the dead had gone to heaven. It was that "the Master himself shall descend from heaven… and the dead in Messiah shall rise first," and "wherefore comfort one another with these words" (1 Thessalonians 4:16, 18). Paul said that if the dead do not rise, "let us eat and drink; for to morrow we die" (1 Corinthians 15:32). He saw no gain at death without the resurrection. The church hands out the whole reward at the graveside and leaves the resurrection with nothing to do. Paul warned of men who said "the resurrection is past already" (2 Timothy 2:18). The funeral sermon says the same in softer words.

**It gives the raising to the wrong one.** The church claims Jesus raised himself to prove he is God. Scripture says, "This Yahushua hath God raised up" (Acts 2:32), and Paul wrote of "God the Father, who raised him from the dead" (Galatians 1:1). When Yahushua said He would take His life again, He added, "This commandment have I received of my Father" (John 10:18). The power was received, not owned. The One who raises the dead is the One who said, "there is no god with me" (Deuteronomy 32:39). There is one Raiser, not three.

## The Verdict

The church kept the resurrection in its creed and buried it in its funerals. By sending souls to heaven at death, it took the Greek immortal soul in place of the Hebrew dust, made the trumpet a formality, and robbed the resurrection of its purpose. The dead are not in heaven. They are asleep in the dust, and they will stay there until Yahuah calls them out.

Revelation shows where the dead are on that day: "the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them" (Revelation 20:13). They come up out of the grave, not down out of heaven.
