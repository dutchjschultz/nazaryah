## The Old Path

Yahuah has only ever had one people. At the first Pesach the Law said, "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger" (Exodus 12:49). The stranger who joins himself to Yahuah and keeps His Sabbaths is brought to His holy mountain, "for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:6-7). Paul pictures that one people as one olive tree. The wild branch is "graffed in" and partakes "of the root and fatness of the olive tree," and he is warned, "thou bearest not the root, but the root thee" (Romans 11:17-18). Gentiles in Messiah are no longer "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" but "fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:12, 19). The door into that tree is the Seed, "which is Messiah," and "if ye be Messiah's, then are ye Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:16, 29). One tree, one root, one law, entered by belief and not by birth.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism today teaches that the Jewish people are the chosen people, descended from Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. In Orthodox and Conservative Judaism a person is Jewish if born to a Jewish mother. Reform Judaism also accepts a Jewish father. A Gentile may convert through a formal process before a rabbinic court, with immersion in a mikveh and, for men, circumcision.

For the Gentile who does not convert, Judaism teaches a separate standard. He is expected to keep only seven basic laws said to be given to the sons of Noah, and he is not asked to keep the Sabbath or the rest of the Law. So Judaism holds two kinds of righteous people under two kinds of law. It also rejects Yahushua as the Messiah.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the root's name. The tree is Israel's tree, and Judaism has never let the world forget that Yahuah chose Israel. Moses said He set His love on them, not because they were many, but because they were "the fewest of all people" (Deuteronomy 7:7).

Judaism also kept the door open to the stranger. It has always received converts, and it can point to Ruth the Moabitess, who said, "thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God" (Ruth 1:16). And it kept the Sabbath and the commandments, the very Law that Yahuah said was one for the native and the stranger.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It counts the people by birth.** Paul answered this directly: "They which are the children of the flesh, these are not the children of God: but the children of the promise are counted for the seed" (Romans 9:8). John the Baptist told the crowds not to say "We have Abraham to our father," for "God is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham" (Matthew 3:9). Abraham's family was always counted by belief and by walk, never by the mother's line alone.

**It keeps two laws for two peoples.** Yahuah said, "One law and one manner shall be for you, and for the stranger that sojourneth with you" (Numbers 15:16). Isaiah's stranger keeps the Sabbath and takes hold of the covenant (Isaiah 56:6). A Gentile told to keep seven laws while Israel keeps the whole Law is a second people with a second arrangement, the very thing Yahuah never set up.

**It refuses the root.** The tree lives by the Seed, Messiah (Galatians 3:16). Paul said the natural branches were broken off "because of unbelief," and he did not say they were gone for good: "if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again" (Romans 11:20, 23). The way back for the natural branch is the same as the way in for the wild one. Both come through the Son.

## The Verdict

Judaism held on to the right tree and the right Law, and it shut out the Root that holds the tree together. It measures Abraham's family by birth, sets the Gentile under a lesser law, and refuses the Messiah through whom both Jew and Gentile are joined. The natural branch stays broken off, not because Yahuah closed the tree, but because of unbelief.

Revelation shows how the tree is filled. After the sealed of the tribes of Israel, John saw "a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues," standing "before the throne, and before the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9). They were not counted by their mothers. They "washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:14).
