## The Old Path

Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That belief was credited at the door, and the credit was real. Two chapters later Yahuah told him, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (Genesis 17:1), and afterward He said Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Moses said the same to a people already brought "out of Egypt with a mighty hand" (Deuteronomy 6:21): "it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25). The psalmist said, "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), and Ezekiel said, "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20), upon him and not on another man's record. James joined the two: Abraham's belief "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Belief is credited first. Feet on the road follow.

## What Buddhism Teaches

Buddhism has no God who gives commandments, so it has no righteousness credited by Him. What it has is merit, *puñña* in Pali. In Theravada lands such as Thailand, making merit is the center of daily religious life. Buddhist teaching names three main ways to make it: giving, keeping the precepts, and meditation. A layperson gives food to the monks at dawn, donates to the temple, frees caged birds, and keeps the five precepts: not to kill, not to steal, not to commit sexual wrong, not to lie, and not to take intoxicants.

Merit is believed to bring a better rebirth and to move a person along the path toward nirvana. Buddhists also teach that merit can be shared or passed on. Families make merit and dedicate it to dead relatives, and young men ordain as monks for a season to make merit for their parents.

## What Buddhism Kept

Buddhism kept some of the walk. Its five precepts match part of Yahuah's Law: "Thou shalt not kill... Thou shalt not commit adultery... Thou shalt not steal... Thou shalt not bear false witness" (Exodus 20:13-16). It holds that a person must live rightly and not only think rightly, and it teaches generosity and self-control. Those are fruits Scripture also asks for.

## Where Buddhism Falls

**It has no one to count righteousness.** Genesis 15:6 needs two parties: Abraham believed, and Yahuah "counted it to him." Buddhism has a store of merit and no one keeping the books. It has precepts but no Lawgiver, deeds but no door. Moses called the commandments righteousness because they were "before Yahuah our God, as he hath commanded us" (Deuteronomy 6:25). A precept with no God behind it is good advice, not righteousness.

**It passes merit from one man to another.** Ezekiel said "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20). The psalmist said, "None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him" (Psalm 49:7). A son in robes cannot make his mother righteous, and merit dedicated to the dead does not change the record of the dead. Each man's walk is his own.

**It stops halfway through the Law.** The five precepts cover only part of the second table of the commandments. They say nothing of the first table: no other gods, no images, the Name, and the Sabbath (Exodus 20:3-11). Temples filled with statues of the Buddha break the second commandment while the people make merit before them. A righteousness that keeps five of Yahuah's ten words and breaks the first ones is not the righteousness of "all these commandments."

## The Verdict

Buddhism kept a few of the commandments and lost the One who gave them. It makes merit with no God to credit it, shares merit that Scripture says cannot be shared, and keeps the precepts about neighbors while breaking the ones about Yahuah. Merit-making is effort without a door, and it reaches no righteousness, because there is no one to count it.

Revelation names whose commandments open the city. "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city" (Revelation 22:14). They are His commandments, kept by people He first washed, and the gates open only to them.
