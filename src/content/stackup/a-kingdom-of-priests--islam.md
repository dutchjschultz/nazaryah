## The Old Path

At Sinai, Yahuah called the whole nation, not one class, "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). When Joshua wanted two men stopped from prophesying in the camp, Moses answered, "would God that all Yahuah's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). Peter gave the same words to every believer: "a royal priesthood, an holy nation" (1 Peter 2:9). John wrote that Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6). Yahushua forbade the titles that lift one man over the rest: "be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah; and all ye are brethren" (Matthew 23:8). Elders and overseers still serve, teach, and watch over the flock, but as brothers among brothers, never as a priesthood standing between the people and Yahuah. "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). There is no second.

## What Islam Teaches

Islam says it has no priesthood. No one is ordained to stand between a Muslim and God, and there is no sacrament only a priest can give. Any Muslim man who knows the prayers may lead them, and every Muslim prays straight to God.

In practice, a class of learned men stands over the people. The imam leads the mosque and gives the Friday sermon. The scholars, called the *ulama*, interpret the Quran and the hadith and lay out Islamic law. A mufti issues rulings called fatwas, and ordinary Muslims are taught to follow the rulings of the scholars rather than reason the law out for themselves. In Shia Islam this goes further: each believer is expected to choose a senior cleric, an ayatollah, as his "source of emulation" and to follow that man's rulings in matters of the law. In Iran, since 1979, a senior cleric has stood at the head of the state itself.

## What Islam Kept

Islam kept a real piece of the truth. It refuses to put a man between the worshipper and God, and it teaches every believer to pray directly. That is nearer to Moses' wish than the priest-and-confessional systems of the world. Every Muslim is also expected to know and keep the commands for himself, which matches the call that every man of Israel know the Law and teach it to his children (Deuteronomy 6:6-7).

## Where Islam Falls

**It has no priesthood because it has no blood.** In Scripture, the kingdom of priests came after the blood. Israel was called "a kingdom of priests" at Sinai (Exodus 19:6), only after the Pesach lamb was slain and its blood put on the doorposts in Egypt (Exodus 12:7, 13). John keeps the same order: Yahushua "washed us from our sins in his own blood, And hath made us kings and priests" (Revelation 1:5-6). Islam does not reject priests because it found the one Mediator. It rejects them because it rejects the blood altogether, and the Quran denies that the Messiah was ever crucified (Surah 4:157). A people with no blood on the door is not a kingdom of priests at all. It is a people with no access.

**It refuses the one Mediator.** Paul says there is one mediator, "the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). Note that he is a man, standing between God and men, not God Himself. Islam is right that God has no equal, but it throws out the Man whom God appointed to stand in the gap. Without Him, no one comes near: "No man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6).

**The scholars took the place it emptied.** Yahushua warned against the men who sit in Moses' seat and "bind heavy burdens and grievous to be borne, and lay them on men's shoulders" (Matthew 23:2-4). Where every believer is told to follow the ruling of a scholar he cannot question, the scholar has become the go-between that Islam claims not to have.

## The Verdict

Islam is right that no man should stand between a believer and God, and wrong about how anyone gets near Him. It has no priests because it has no Lamb, no blood, and no High Priest. Then it filled the empty room with scholars whose rulings the people must follow.

Revelation shows the only road into the priesthood. The song of the redeemed says of the Lamb, "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation; And hast made us unto our God kings and priests" (Revelation 5:9-10). First the blood, then the priesthood. There is no other order.
