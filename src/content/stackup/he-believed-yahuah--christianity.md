## The Old Path

Before Sinai, before circumcision, before the knife on Moriah, Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). To believe is *aman*, to lean your whole weight on something; it is the root of "amen." To count is *chashab*, a bookkeeper's word: righteousness was credited to an account that had not earned it. Abram had no written commandments, no covenant sign, no priesthood, and no tabernacle. He had a promise of a seed, and he leaned on it. Every pronoun in the chapter is singular: one God speaks, one man trusts, one God credits. The same word runs through the prophets: "Believe in Yahuah your God, so shall ye be established" (2 Chronicles 20:20), and "the just shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). Hebrews lays "faith toward God" among the first stones (Hebrews 6:1), and Genesis had already set it in place.

## What Christianity Teaches

Christianity teaches that a man is saved by faith alone, and it quotes Genesis 15:6 to prove it. But in practice it has made faith two things that Abram never knew.

First, faith became agreement with a creed. The Athanasian Creed opens, "Whosoever will be saved, before all things it is necessary that he hold the catholic faith," and goes on to say that faith is to worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in unity. Those who do not keep it whole, it says, will perish everlastingly.

Second, in much of the modern church faith became a moment of decision. A person agrees that Jesus died for him, prays a prayer, and is told he is saved forever. How he lives afterward, and whether he keeps the commandments, is treated as a separate matter that does not touch his salvation.

## What Christianity Kept

Christianity kept the central truth of Genesis 15:6: righteousness is counted, not earned. Paul's whole argument stands on it: "to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness" (Romans 4:5). The church kept that the credit comes before the walk, and it kept the Seed. It knows the promise to Abraham was fulfilled in Yahushua (Galatians 3:16), which Islam and Judaism both deny.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It added a creed Abram never heard.** Abram was counted righteous for believing Yahuah, one God, speaking in the singular. Nobody taught him a Trinity, and he was not faulted for lacking one. Either Abram met the requirement, or the requirement was added later. Yahushua named the belief that brings life: "that they might know thee the only true God, and Yahushua Messiah, whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3). The One God, and the Son He sent. That is Abraham's belief, not Athanasius'.

**It turned leaning into agreeing.** *Aman* is putting your full weight on something. Agreeing that a fact is true costs nothing. James said, "the devils also believe, and tremble" (James 2:19). Devils agree with every true fact about Yahuah, and no righteousness is counted to them. Belief that has no weight on it is not the belief of Genesis 15.

**It cut belief off from the walk.** Abram's belief did not stay in a single moment. "By faith Abraham, when he was called… obeyed" (Hebrews 11:8). Yahuah later said of him, "Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). That was not how he was counted righteous. It was what his belief did afterward. James put the two together: "Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?" (James 2:22). The belief enters by the promise. The walk follows by the commandments.

## The Verdict

Christianity kept the credit and the Seed, more than any other religion here, and then emptied belief of its weight. It made faith either a creed about three persons that Abram never heard, or a moment of agreement that asks nothing afterward. Abram leaned his whole life on one God's promise, and the leaning carried him into obedience. Belief that will not walk is not the belief that was counted.

Revelation joins the two things the church split apart: "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12). The commandments and the faith stand together in the last days, just as they did in Abraham's tent.
