## The Old Path

Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That belief was credited at the door, and the credit was real. Two chapters later Yahuah told him, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (Genesis 17:1), and afterward He said Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Moses said the same to a people already brought "out of Egypt with a mighty hand" (Deuteronomy 6:21): "it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments" (Deuteronomy 6:25). The psalmist said, "all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172), and Ezekiel said, "the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20), upon him and not on another man's record. James joined the two: Abraham's belief "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Belief is credited first. Feet on the road follow.

## What Christianity Teaches

Since the Reformation, Protestant churches have taught imputed righteousness. Martin Luther called it an "alien righteousness," one that comes from outside the believer. Reformed teaching says that Jesus kept the whole Law perfectly in the believer's place, and that His perfect law-keeping is credited to the believer's account. When God looks at the believer, the teaching says, He sees Jesus's record and not the believer's own.

From there, much of the modern church teaches that obedience is good but not required. Good works are called fruit that may or may not appear. Isaiah 64:6, "all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags," is quoted to show that a believer's own obedience counts for nothing. The Law is said to have been kept for the believer, so the believer need not keep it.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the door. It is true that Abraham's belief was credited before he walked a step, "not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision" (Romans 4:10), and that no man enters by his own deeds. The church holds that favor comes first and that the entry is a gift. That is the first half of the Old Path, and Islam, Hinduism, and Buddhism do not have it.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It credits the wrong thing.** Paul did not say that another man's law-keeping was counted to Abraham. He said, "Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness," and "his faith is counted for righteousness" (Romans 4:3, 5). What was counted was Abraham's own belief. Ezekiel said the righteousness of the righteous "shall be upon him" (Ezekiel 18:20). Scripture never moves one man's walk onto another man's record.

**It stops at the door.** Yahuah credited Abraham in Genesis 15 and told him to walk and be perfect in Genesis 17. In Genesis 26 He named what Abraham kept: His charge, commandments, statutes, and laws. James said, "by works a man is justified, and not by faith only" (James 2:24), and John said, "he that doeth righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous" (1 John 3:7). Making obedience optional keeps Genesis 15 and tears out Genesis 17 and 26.

**It misreads the filthy rags.** Isaiah 64:6 is Israel's confession while in sin, and one verse earlier Isaiah says Yahuah "meetest him that rejoiceth and worketh righteousness, those that remember thee in thy ways" (Isaiah 64:5). The prophet was not calling obedience filthy. He was calling sin filthy. Yahuah meets the man who works righteousness.

**It makes the Son do the walking.** The Son opened the way by His blood. He did not walk the road so His people could sit by it. Yahushua said, "If ye love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15). The way was opened for walking.

## The Verdict

The church has the door and has thrown away the road. It is right that belief is credited at the entry, and wrong to say that the credit is someone else's record or that it replaces the walk. Deuteronomy 6:25 still stands for the delivered: it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do. Belief credited, then feet on the road, in that order and both.

Revelation shows what the bride wears. "To her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints" (Revelation 19:8). The Greek *dikaiomata* means righteous acts. The garment was granted, and it is woven from the saints' own deeds.
