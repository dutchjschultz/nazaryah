## The Old Path

Abram "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6), and afterward Yahuah said Abraham "kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (Genesis 26:5). Israel was spared in Egypt by the blood on the door, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13), and only then was led to Sinai. Moses said the commandment "is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). The psalmist said, "I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45), and Solomon called the commandment "a lamp" and the law "light" (Proverbs 6:23). Deliverance comes through the blood Yahuah provided; Torah is what walking looks like on the other side of it. Torah was never the door, and the door was never the end of the road. Every religion's answer is measured against that order.

## What Judaism Teaches

Judaism is built around the Torah. It teaches that Israel stands in covenant with God by descent from Abraham and by the giving of the Law at Sinai, and that keeping the commandments is Israel's response to that covenant. To be fair to it, Judaism does not usually say a Jew earns his place by keeping commandments. It says he already has a place, by birth, and the commandments are how he lives in it.

Judaism counts 613 commandments in the Torah, and it surrounds them with a large body of later tradition that tells exactly how each is to be done. Since the Temple was destroyed in 70 AD there have been no sacrifices. Judaism teaches that repentance, prayer, and charity now stand in their place, and on Yom Kippur those three are offered instead of blood.

## What Judaism Kept

Judaism kept the walk, and it kept the right walk. It never said the Law was nailed to a cross. It kept the seventh day, the feasts of Leviticus 23, clean and unclean food, and the Torah read aloud through the year. It believes what Moses said, that the commandment is not "too hard for thee, neither is it far off" (Deuteronomy 30:11).

Judaism also kept the order every year at Passover. Each spring Jewish families retell how the blood went on the doors before Israel ever saw Sinai.

## Where Judaism Falls

**It refuses the Lamb who is the door.** In Egypt the blood was put on the door itself, "on the two side posts and on the upper door post" (Exodus 12:7), and Yahuah passed over every house where He saw it. Yahushua said, "I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved" (John 10:9). John pointed to Him and said, "Behold the Lamb of God" (John 1:29). Isaiah saw Him "brought as a lamb to the slaughter" (Isaiah 53:7). Judaism tells the story of the blood on the door every year and refuses the Lamb the story was pointing to.

**It made birth the door.** If a Jew is in the covenant by descent, then his door is his bloodline, not the blood. John the Baptist answered that directly: "think not to say within yourselves, We have Abraham to our father" (Matthew 3:9). Paul wrote, "they are not all Israel, which are of Israel" (Romans 9:6). Abraham was counted righteous because he believed (Genesis 15:6), and Yahushua told the leaders of His day, "If ye were Abraham's children, ye would do the works of Abraham" (John 8:39).

**It put prayer where Yahuah put blood.** Repentance and prayer are good, and Scripture commands both. But they were never given as a substitute. "It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). When the Temple fell, Judaism, refusing the blood every altar pointed to, was left with none.

**It fenced the walk with tradition.** Moses said, "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you" (Deuteronomy 4:2). Yahushua told the scribes, "full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition" (Mark 7:9).

## The Verdict

Judaism kept the walk the church threw away, and it walks it with no door. It tells the story of the blood on the doorposts at every Passover and turns away from the Lamb it described. Torah was never the door, and birth was never the door. The blood is the door, and Torah is the walk beyond it.

Revelation joins the two that Judaism holds apart. The overcomers "sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3). Heaven sings both.
