## The Old Path

Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Adam did not receive a soul; he became one, a *nephesh chayyah*, the same words Genesis uses for the "living creature" of the field (Genesis 1:24). The sentence on sin was "unto dust shalt thou return" (Genesis 3:19). Ezekiel says it twice: "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20). When the breath goes, man "returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4), for "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5). Immortality is not something man has. It is something he must "seek for" (Romans 2:7) and will "put on" at the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:53).

## What Christianity Teaches

Most of Christianity teaches that every person has an immortal soul that leaves the body at death and goes straight to heaven or hell. At funerals the preacher says the departed is "in a better place" and is "looking down" on the family. The Roman Church made it binding at the Fifth Lateran Council in 1513, condemning anyone who taught that the soul is mortal. Most Protestant churches kept the teaching. The resurrection is still in the creeds, but in practice the dead are already where they will be.

## What Christianity Kept

The church kept the resurrection in its creeds and in its Bibles, and it kept the hope of eternal life. It is right that the righteous will live forever and right that death is not the end of the story. It also kept, in its own history, men who saw the error. William Tyndale, who gave the English their Bible, argued against Thomas More that putting the dead in heaven already destroys the very arguments Messiah and Paul used to prove the resurrection.

## Where Christianity Falls

**It took its soul from Plato, not Moses.** The Greek philosopher Plato argued that the soul is deathless and that the body is its prison. Genesis says nothing of the kind. Man was dust given breath, and he "became" a living soul (Genesis 2:7). The church read the Greek idea back into the Hebrew word and made *nephesh*, the whole living creature, into a ghost inside the body.

**It preaches the serpent's sermon at every funeral.** Yahuah said, "thou shalt surely die" (Genesis 2:17). The serpent said, "Ye shall not surely die" (Genesis 3:4). When a pulpit tells the family that their loved one is not really dead but alive in another place, it is preaching the second sermon, not the first.

**It makes the resurrection pointless.** Paul staked everything on the resurrection: "if the dead rise not… they also which are fallen asleep in Messiah are perished" (1 Corinthians 15:16, 18). If their souls were already in heaven, they would not have perished without a resurrection. Peter said plainly that "David is not ascended into the heavens" (Acts 2:34). Yahushua called death sleep (John 11:11), and Paul said the dead in Messiah will rise when the Master comes "with the trump of God" (1 Thessalonians 4:16). They rise then because they are in the grave until then.

**It gives every man what belongs to Yahuah alone.** Paul says the One who will show the Son's appearing is "the blessed and only Potentate… Who only hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:15-16). Two things follow. Man does not have immortality by nature, and neither does the Son, for the One who "only hath immortality" is the One who shows the Son's appearing, a different person from the Son. The Son died. He said, "I am he that liveth, and was dead" (Revelation 1:18), and "as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself" (John 5:26). The immortal soul and the Trinity make the same mistake. Both hand out the Father's immortality to those who receive life only from Him.

## The Verdict

Christianity kept the resurrection on paper and buried it under Plato. Scripture says man is a living soul, not a man with a soul, and that when he dies he sleeps in the dust. Immortality is the Father's alone, and He gives it at the resurrection to those who sought it. Until then the dead wait.

Revelation shows where the dead are when the Son returns. "The sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them" (Revelation 20:13). Heaven gives up no one, because no one went there. Those who sleep in Messiah come up out of the grave, and death is "swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54) on the day the trumpet sounds.
