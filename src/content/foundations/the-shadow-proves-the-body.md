## The Claim

Most churches teach that much of the Torah was a picture book, given to Israel until the real thing came. Every sacrifice pointed to the cross. The priesthood pointed to Messiah’s priesthood. The temple pointed to the believer’s body. Pesach pointed to the Lord’s table, circumcision to immersion, the food laws to a clean heart, and the Sabbath to the rest a believer finds in Messiah. Each one was a type. Messiah is the reality each type was drawn from.

Paul, they say, confirms it in so many words:

> Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Messiah.
> 
> — **Colossians 2:16–17**

Hebrews adds that the law had “a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things” (Hebrews 10:1), and that these things were “imposed on them until the time of reformation” (Hebrews 9:10). The time of reformation came at the cross. The shadows were fulfilled in Messiah, and what is fulfilled is no longer binding.

Most teachers then divide the Torah in two. The moral law, which they find in the Ten Commandments, still stands. The ceremonial law, the feasts, sacrifices, food laws, and purity rules, was shadow, and it is gone. The fourth commandment is usually split down the middle: rest remains a moral principle, but the seventh day itself is ceremonial, a shadow now fulfilled in Messiah’s rest.

The conclusion follows. Once the reality has come, going back to the shadow is a step backward, and to some, a quiet denial that He has come at all. Keeping a Sabbath or a feast today is like keeping a photograph of a wife while she stands in the room. The body has arrived. The shadow can go.

The picture is vivid. The question is whether it is how a shadow actually works, and how Scripture actually uses the word.

## The Point in One Sentence

Calling something a shadow tells what it points to. It does not say the thing has ended. Whether any shadow has ended has to be shown from a verse that says so, one item at a time.

That is the whole foundation. Everything below either explains what a shadow is or shows Scripture doing exactly that sorting.

## The Short Version

- A shadow is cast by something real. It points to that thing. It does not compete with it.
- When the real thing appears, the shadow is explained, not erased.
- Scripture does say that some shadows were set aside: the animal offering and the Levitical priesthood.
- Scripture never says that about the Sabbath, the appointed times, or the table. The prophets show them still kept in the age to come.
- So “it was a shadow” never settles the question by itself. Only a verse that ends a particular thing can do that.

## What a Shadow Is

The Greek word is skia (G4639). Its partner in Paul’s sentence is soma (G4983), body. Three things are true of every shadow, and none of them needs a lexicon.

- A shadow requires a body. Wherever a shadow lies, something real is standing.
- A shadow has the body’s shape. It tells the watcher what is coming.
- A shadow does not disappear when the body arrives. It disappears only when the light goes out.

The modern picture turns all three upside down. No shadow in the natural world behaves the way the argument needs it to.

## Scripture’s Shadows Mean Presence

- “He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty” (Psalm 91:1).
- “Hide me under the shadow of thy wings” (Psalm 17:8).

In both, the shadow is a place to live, close to the One who casts it. It is a sign of presence, not absence.

## Moses Built a Copy of Something Real

Yahuah did not ask Moses to invent the tabernacle. He showed him a pattern (Exodus 25:40). Hebrews quotes that verse and calls the earthly service a shadow because of it:

> Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of Yahuah when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.
> 
> — **Hebrews 8:5**

This is Scripture’s own definition of the word. The earthly tabernacle was a shadow because it was a copy of something real in heaven. The shadow was never the problem. It was the evidence.

## The Original Is Still Standing

Hebrews speaks of “the true tabernacle, which Yahuah pitched, and not man” (Hebrews 8:2). And John was shown it at the end of the age:

> And the temple of Yahuah was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament.
> 
> — **Revelation 11:19**

The earthly ark was made to hold the testimony, the tablets of the commandments (Exodus 25:16). The earthly ark was the shadow. The heavenly one is the body, and it is still there.

## Scripture Never Draws the Moral-Ceremonial Line

The division of the Torah into moral law that stays and ceremonial law that goes is the frame the whole shadow teaching hangs on. Yet the words moral law and ceremonial law appear nowhere in Scripture. Moses sorts Yahuah’s instruction into charges, commandments, statutes, and laws (Genesis 26:5; see F-01), but never into a group that stays and a group that expires.

The Sabbath shows where the frame breaks. It sits inside the Ten Commandments, written “with the finger of Elohim” (Exodus 31:18). And it was blessed and sanctified at creation (Genesis 2:3), before sin entered the world. A day set apart before there was any sin cannot be a shadow of the remedy for sin. How Hebrews speaks of rest is answered in F-12.

## Two Kinds of Shadow

Not every shadow stands in the same place afterward, and Scripture says which is which. Both kinds pointed to Messiah. The difference is what Scripture says happened to each one.

### Shadows Scripture Says Were Set Aside

- The animal sin offering. Messiah “hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust” (1 Peter 3:18). The offering it pictured has been made, once.
- The Levitical priesthood. David foretold “a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek” (Psalm 110:4), a priest of another order who replaced the line of Aaron.

For these, Scripture names the change, and F-06 sets it out. No one has to guess.

### Shadows Scripture Never Sets Aside

- The Sabbath. In the age to come, “from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith Yahuah” (Isaiah 66:23).
- The appointed times. The nations will “go up from year to year to worship the King, Yahuah of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles” (Zechariah 14:16).
- The table. In the same last chapter of Isaiah, those “eating swine’s flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be consumed together” (Isaiah 66:17; see F-10).

For these, Scripture says the opposite of an ending. They are still kept when the King reigns. A shadow still being kept in the age to come was not cancelled when He first came.

### The Tabernacle Shows Both at Once

The earthly tent was a copy. The heavenly tabernacle it copied still stands (Hebrews 8:2; Revelation 11:19). And Paul calls the believer’s own body “the temple of the Holy Ghost” (1 Corinthians 6:19). When the real tabernacle was revealed, the idea of a dwelling place for Yahuah did not disappear. It was explained, and it went on. That is what a shadow does when the body appears.

## The Verdict

The modern teaching treats “it was a shadow” as if it meant “it is over.” Scripture never uses the word that way. A shadow is cast by something real and still standing, and calling something a shadow tells what it points to, not whether it has ended. Scripture names the shadows that were set aside, the altar offering and the Levitical priesthood, and it shows the Sabbath, the appointed times, and the table still kept in the age to come.

The passages that rest here, chiefly Colossians 2:16–17 and Hebrews 10:1, are answered in their own testimonies.

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

The shadow does not compete with the body. It proves Him.
