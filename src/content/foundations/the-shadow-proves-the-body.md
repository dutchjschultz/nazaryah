## The Claim

Most churches teach that the Sabbaths, the feasts, the new moons, and the food instructions were shadows. Paul says they are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Messiah. Hebrews says the law had a shadow of good things to come. A shadow is only an outline. Now that the real thing has come, holding onto the outline is foolish, like a man who keeps a photograph of his wife while she is standing in the room. The body has arrived. The shadow can go.

The picture is vivid, and it sounds reasonable. The question is whether it is how a shadow actually works, and how Scripture actually uses the word.

## The Short Version

- A shadow only exists while a body stands in the light. It is proof the body is real and near.
- A shadow has the shape of the body that casts it. It tells the watcher what is coming.
- When the body arrives, the shadow does not vanish. It is still lying at his feet.
- Scripture says the earthly pattern was a copy of a heavenly original, and the original is still standing.
- Calling something a shadow is a statement about what it points to. It is not a notice of cancellation.

## What a Shadow Is

The Greek word is skia (G4639). Its partner in Paul’s sentence is soma (G4983), body. A shadow is the shape a body casts when it stands in the light.

Three things are true of every shadow, and none of them needs a lexicon.

- A shadow requires a body. There is no such thing as a shadow with nothing casting it. Wherever a shadow lies, something real is standing.
- A shadow has the body’s shape. A man watching a shadow come around a corner can tell whether a man, a horse, or a child is about to appear. The outline is testimony.
- A shadow does not disappear when the body arrives. It disappears when the light goes out. As long as the body stands in the light, the shadow lies right beside it.

The modern picture turns all three upside down. It treats the shadow as something that existed instead of the body, that was not shaped like the body, and that vanished when the body came. No shadow in the natural world behaves that way.

## Scripture’s Other Shadows

Scripture uses the language of shadow in other places, and they show how it thinks about the word.

- “He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty” (Psalm 91:1). The shadow is where a man lives, close to the One who casts it.
- “Hide me under the shadow of thy wings” (Psalm 17:8). The shadow is protection, and it exists because the wings are there.
- “That at the least the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them” (Acts 5:15). People wanted Peter’s shadow because it meant Peter was right there.

In every one of these, the shadow is a sign of presence, not of absence. The person in the shadow is near the one casting it.

## Moses Built a Copy of Something Real

When Yahuah gave Moses the tabernacle, He did not ask Moses to invent a design. He showed him one:

> And look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount.
> 
> — **Exodus 25:40**

Hebrews quotes that verse and calls the earthly service a shadow because of it:

> Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of Yahuah when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.
> 
> — **Hebrews 8:5**

This is Scripture’s own definition of the word. The earthly tabernacle was a shadow because it was a copy of something real in heaven. The shadow was never the problem. It was the evidence. It showed on earth the shape of what stands in heaven.

## The Original Is Still Standing

If the shadow was a copy of a heavenly original, the next question is whether the original still exists. Scripture answers it. Hebrews speaks of Messiah as “a minister of the sanctuary, and of the true tabernacle, which Yahuah pitched, and not man” (Hebrews 8:2). And John was shown it:

> And the temple of Yahuah was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament.
> 
> — **Revelation 11:19**

The ark of the testament. The ark Moses built was made to hold the testimony, the tablets of the commandments (Exodus 25:16). The earthly ark was the shadow. The heavenly one is the body, and it is still there, seen at the end of the age. The shadow pointed to something that has not gone anywhere.

## The Shape Tells Who Is Coming

The appointed times have the shape of the Messiah, because He is the body casting them. Scripture draws the lines itself.

- Pesach: “Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us” (1 Corinthians 5:7).
- Bikkurim: “Now is Messiah risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept” (1 Corinthians 15:20).

Some of the outline has already been filled by what He has done. The rest still points ahead. And the prophets place the appointed times in the age to come: all flesh worshiping “from one sabbath to another” (Isaiah 66:23), and the nations going up “from year to year to worship the King, Yahuah of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles” (Zechariah 14:16). A shadow that is still being kept when the King reigns was not cancelled when He first came.

## What Did Change

Stating the limits keeps the case honest.

- The earthly altar service, the animal offerings, and the Levitical priesthood did change, and Scripture names those changes (F-06). Hebrews applies the shadow language to them, and its testimonies answer those passages one by one.
- Calling something a shadow does not, by itself, settle whether it continues. That has to be shown from what Scripture says of each item.
- What the word shadow cannot do is carry the claim of cancellation on its own. A shadow is evidence of a body, not a substitute for one.

## The Verdict

The modern teaching uses shadow to mean something that existed until the real thing arrived and then vanished. Scripture uses it to mean an outline cast by something real, a copy of a heavenly original, and a place to dwell near the One who casts it. The body has come, and the shadow still lies at His feet, shaped exactly like Him.

The passages that rest on this foundation, chiefly Colossians 2:16–17 and Hebrews 10:1, are answered one at a time in their own testimonies.

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

The shadow does not compete with the body. It proves Him.
