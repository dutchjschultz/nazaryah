## The Claim

Most churches teach that a person is saved by favor through belief, not by works. Paul says so again and again. Keeping the Torah is a work. So anyone who keeps the Torah is trying to earn what can only be received as a gift.

It is the most emotionally powerful argument in the series, made by people who love the gospel and want to protect it. It deserves an answer that protects the gospel too.

## The Short Version

- Scripture describes two stages: coming in, and walking afterward.
- Coming in is by blood, received by belief. Works have no part in it. On this the modern teaching is entirely right.
- Walking afterward is by the word of Yahuah, the bread a man lives on.
- Every “not of works” verse speaks about coming in. Not one of them speaks about the walk.

## Yahuah Always Delivers First, Then Instructs

In Egypt, Israel was not told to keep commandments in order to get out. They were told to put blood on the doorposts:

> And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you.
> 
> — **Exodus 12:13**

That was Pesach. The next morning began Hag HaMatzot, and Israel walked out eating unleavened bread. Only after that, at Sinai, did the commandments come, and they begin with the deliverance already done:

> I am Yahuah thy Elohim, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.
> 
> — **Exodus 20:2**

The Torah was handed to a people already delivered. It was never the way to get delivered. It was the way for the delivered to live. Blood first, then bread, then the walk.

## Paul Kept the Same Order

> For even Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.
> 
> — **1 Corinthians 5:7–8**

Messiah is the Pesach lamb: the blood, the entry. Therefore let us keep the feast: the unleavened bread, the walk. Paul names both, in the same order Moses did.

## Two Stages in One Sentence

> Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to Yahuah by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.
> 
> — **Romans 5:9–10**

Justified by His blood, already done. Saved by His life, still ahead. Paul does not treat these as one event. The first is the doorway; the second is the road beyond it.

Scripture speaks of being saved in more than one tense for the same reason. “We are saved by hope” (Romans 8:24) looks back at the door. “Unto us which are saved” (1 Corinthians 1:18) is a present participle in Greek, those being saved, the walk still underway. “We shall be saved from wrath” (Romans 5:9) looks to the end of the road.

## Entry Is by Blood, and Works Have No Part

This foundation agrees with the modern teaching on the first stage, fully. “All our righteousnesses are as filthy rags” (Isaiah 64:6). Abraham entered the way every man must: “And he believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness” (Genesis 15:6).

Every passage in this series that says “not of works” is guarding this door, and each one is right about the door. None of them is about what a man does after he has walked through it.

## The Walk Is by Bread

Once inside, a man has to eat. Moses told Israel what the food was:

> Man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of Yahuah doth man live.
> 
> — **Deuteronomy 8:3**

Yahushua answered the tempter with this verse (Matthew 4:4). The apostles describe the walk the same way:

- Messiah “gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works” (Titus 2:14). The word for iniquity is anomia (G458): lawlessness. He redeemed His people from lawlessness, not into it.
- “He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked” (1 John 2:6). Yahushua walked in His Father’s commandments.

Abraham’s own life shows both stages, decades apart. He believed in Genesis 15, and that was the door. He offered Isaac in Genesis 22, and that was the walk proving the belief real. Paul points to the first moment and James to the second. They are describing one man’s life, not contradicting each other.

## Where the Collision Comes From

Yahushua told of a son who left his father’s house and came home with nothing. On the road he rehearsed a plan to work his way back: “make me as one of thy hired servants” (Luke 15:19). His father never let him finish. He ran to him, and called for the best robe, a ring for his hand, and shoes for his feet (Luke 15:20–22). Nothing the son did bought his place. He had always been a son, and his father’s welcome brought him back. Then he lived in his father’s house, by his father’s ways. No one would say the son who keeps the ways of the house is trying to earn his way home. He is already home.

The modern teaching takes every verse about the father’s welcome and applies it to the ways of the house. Once that is done, any obedience at all looks like an attempt to earn the way home, and the Torah gets blamed. The Torah was never the price of coming home. It was always the way of the house, given to those already brought back.

## What This Foundation Does Not Claim

- It does not claim that Torah keeping earns anything. Nothing earns the door.
- It claims this: “not of works” is always about entry, and entry was never by Torah. So “not of works” cannot be used against walking in Torah.

## The Verdict

The modern teaching guards the door well, and this foundation stands with it there. Its mistake is carrying the door’s rules down the whole road. Scripture keeps them apart from the first Pesach onward: blood to come in, bread to walk on. Each passage that rests here is answered in its own testimony with one question: is this verse about coming in, or about walking afterward?

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

Belief opens the door. Behind it, the law stands firm, and the delivered walk in it.
