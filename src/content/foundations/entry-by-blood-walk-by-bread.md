## The Claim

Most churches teach that a person is saved by favor through belief, not by works. Paul says so again and again: not by works of righteousness, not of works lest any man should boast, a man is justified by belief without the deeds of the law. Keeping the Torah is a work. So anyone who keeps the Torah is trying to earn what can only be received as a gift. Paul even warns that those who seek to be justified by the law have fallen from favor.

This is the most emotionally powerful argument in the whole series. It is made by people who love the gospel and want to protect it. It deserves an answer that protects the gospel too.

## The Short Version

- Scripture describes two stages: coming in, and walking afterward.
- Coming in is by blood, received by belief. Works have no part in it. On this the modern teaching is entirely right.
- Walking afterward is by the word of Yahuah, the bread a man lives on. Obedience is the whole of it.
- Every “not of works” verse speaks about coming in. Not one of them speaks about the walk.
- The modern teaching takes verses about the door and applies them to the road. Then it blames the Torah for the collision.

## Yahuah Always Delivers First, Then Instructs

The order was set at the very first deliverance. In Egypt, Israel was not told to keep commandments in order to get out. They were told to put blood on the doorposts:

> And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you.
> 
> — **Exodus 12:13**

That was Pesach. The next morning began seven days of unleavened bread, Hag HaMatzot, and Israel walked out of Egypt eating it. Only after that, at Sinai, did the commandments come. And look how they begin:

> I am Yahuah thy Elohim, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.
> 
> — **Exodus 20:2**

The deliverance is stated before the first commandment is given. The Torah was handed to a people already delivered. It was never offered as the way to get delivered. It was the way for the delivered to live.

Blood first, then bread, then the walk. That is the pattern of the Torah itself.

## Paul Kept the Same Order

Paul did not invent a new pattern. He pointed believers back to the old one:

> For even Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.
> 
> — **1 Corinthians 5:7–8**

Messiah is the Pesach lamb: the blood, the entry. Therefore let us keep the feast: the unleavened bread, the walk. Paul names both, in the same order Moses did.

## Two Stages in One Sentence

Paul also states the two stages outright:

> Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to Yahuah by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.
> 
> — **Romans 5:9–10**

Justified by His blood, already done. Saved by His life, still ahead. Reconciled by His death, then kept by His life. Paul does not treat these as one event. He treats the first as the doorway and the second as the road beyond it.

### Deliverance Has Tenses

Scripture speaks of being saved in more than one tense, and each tense has its own work.

- Past: “For we are saved by hope” (Romans 8:24). The door has been entered.
- Present: “unto us which are saved it is the power of Elohim” (1 Corinthians 1:18). The Greek is a present participle: those being saved, an ongoing walk.
- Future: “we shall be saved from wrath through him” (Romans 5:9). The end of the road.

Paul holds the walk and the gift together without strain: “work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is Elohim which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure” (Philippians 2:12–13). The walk is real effort. The power for it comes from Yahuah. Neither half buys the door.

## Entry Is by Blood, and Works Have No Part

This foundation agrees with the modern teaching on the first stage, fully. No one enters by his own record. “All our righteousnesses are as filthy rags” (Isaiah 64:6). Abraham entered the way every man must: “And he believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness” (Genesis 15:6).

Every passage in this series that says “not of works” is guarding this door. Each one is answered in its own testimony, and each one is right about the door. None of them is about what a man does after he has walked through it.

## The Walk Is by Bread

Once inside, a man has to eat. Moses told Israel what the food was:

> And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of Yahuah doth man live.
> 
> — **Deuteronomy 8:3**

Yahushua answered the tempter with this verse (Matthew 4:4). Every word that proceeds from the mouth of Yahuah is what a delivered man lives on. That is the bread of the walk.

The apostles describe the walk the same way:

- “For we are his workmanship, created in Messiah Yahushua unto good works, which Elohim hath before ordained that we should walk in them” (Ephesians 2:10). The verse right after the most-quoted “not of works” passage says what the delivered man is created for.
- Messiah “gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works” (Titus 2:14). The word for iniquity is anomia (G458): lawlessness. He redeemed His people from lawlessness, not into it.
- “He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked” (1 John 2:6). Yahushua walked in His Father’s commandments.

## Abraham Shows Both Stages, Decades Apart

Paul and James are often set against each other, as if one says belief and the other says works. They are describing two different moments in the same man’s life.

- Paul points to Genesis 15. Abraham believed, and it was counted to him for righteousness (Romans 4:3). That is the door.
- James points to Genesis 22. “Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?” (James 2:21). That is the walk, decades later, proving the belief was real.

James even says the second fulfilled the first: “by works was faith made perfect” (James 2:22). The door and the road belong to one life. Neither apostle confuses them.

## Where the Collision Comes From

A child adopted into a family enters by the father’s signature on the papers. Nothing the child does earns the adoption. Then the child lives in the house and keeps the house rules. No one would say the child who makes his bed is trying to get adopted. He already is. He is living like a member of the family.

The modern teaching takes every verse about the adoption papers and applies it to the house rules. Once that is done, any obedience at all looks like an attempt to earn adoption, and the Torah gets blamed. The Torah was never the adoption papers. It was always the house rules, given to those already brought in.

## What This Foundation Does Not Claim

- It does not claim that Torah keeping earns anything. Nothing earns the door.
- It does not claim that every warning in Paul is answered by the two stages alone. Some, like the dispute over circumcision as an entry requirement (Acts 15:1), are answered in their own testimonies.
- It claims this: “not of works” is always about entry, and entry was never by Torah. So “not of works” cannot be used against walking in Torah.

## The Verdict

The modern teaching guards the door well, and this foundation stands with it there. Its mistake is carrying the door’s rules down the whole road. Scripture keeps them apart from the first Pesach onward: blood to come in, bread to walk on.

The passages that rest on this foundation are answered one at a time in their own testimonies. Each one can be met with the same question: is this verse about coming in, or about walking afterward?

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

Belief opens the door. Behind it, the law stands firm, and the delivered walk in it.
