## The Claim

Many churches teach that history is divided into dispensations, ages in which Yahuah deals with mankind under different rules. The age of law ran from Sinai to the cross; the present age is the age of favor. The law was for Israel; favor is for the church. Paul says a dispensation of favor was given to him, and that he preached a mystery hidden from earlier ages. He told Timothy to rightly divide the word of truth, which means sorting out which parts of Scripture belong to Israel and which belong to the church. The law and the prophets were until John; after that, a new administration began.

The system is detailed and confident, and it explains away almost every Torah passage in advance. It rests on two words.

## The Point in One Sentence

Dispensation means household management, and rightly dividing means cutting straight, and neither word divides Scripture into eras with different rules.

## The Short Version

- Dispensation translates oikonomia: the management of a household, a stewardship.
- Rightly dividing translates orthotomeo: to cut a straight path.
- Scripture uses both words in places no one disputes, and neither means an era with its own law.
- Moses and Messiah are described as serving over the same house.
- The system of dispensations was assembled in the 1800s, not taught by the apostles.

## Oikonomia: Running a Household

Oikonomia (G3622) is built from oikos, house, and nomos, law or management. It means the management of a household, and the person who does it is an oikonomos, a steward. Yahushua used the word in a parable no one disputes:

> And he called him, and said unto him, How is it that I hear this of thee? give an account of thy stewardship; for thou mayest be no longer steward.
> 
> — **Luke 16:2**

Stewardship there is oikonomia. The steward lost his job. The household did not change its rules. Paul uses the word the same way of his own calling: “a dispensation of the gospel is committed unto me” (1 Corinthians 9:17), a trust he must carry out. Peter tells every believer to serve “as good stewards of the manifold grace of Yahuah” (1 Peter 4:10).

In every one of these, a dispensation is a responsibility given to a servant inside a household. It is never a new set of house rules.

## Orthotomeo: Cutting Straight

Orthotomeo (G3718) appears only once in the apostles’ writings, in 2 Timothy 2:15. It is built from orthos, straight, and temno, to cut. The Greek Old Testament uses it twice, and both times it means to make a path straight:

> In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.
> 
> — **Proverbs 3:6**

Direct there is orthotomeo: he shall cut your paths straight. Proverbs 11:5 uses it the same way: “The righteousness of the perfect shall direct his way.” The picture is a straight furrow or a straight road, a workman who does not wander off the line. It describes handling the word accurately. It does not describe slicing the word into sections for different peoples. What the verse means in Timothy is answered in its own testimony.

## One House, Not Two

If dispensations were separate households with separate rules, Scripture would describe Moses and Messiah as heads of different houses. It describes them in the same house:

> And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after; But Messiah as a son over his own house; whose house are we.
> 
> — **Hebrews 3:5–6**

His house. Moses served in it as a servant; Messiah rules over it as the Son. The house is the same house, and the believer is part of it. A son taking charge of his Father’s house does not throw out the Father’s ways. Note also who owns the house: the Son is over it, the Father built it (Hebrews 3:4), and the passage keeps the two distinct.

## A System Built Late

History is supporting evidence, not authority, but it is worth knowing. The system of dispensations, with its sharp division between Israel and the church and its separate ages of law and favor, was organized in the 1830s by John Nelson Darby, a teacher among the Plymouth Brethren. It spread widely through the Scofield Reference Bible, first published in 1909, whose notes printed the system beside the text of Scripture. Many readers have since absorbed the notes as if they were the text.

A teaching that the apostles held would not need to wait eighteen centuries to be organized.

## What This Foundation Does Not Claim

- It does not claim that nothing changes across Scripture. Covenants were added, the priesthood changed, and the nations were brought near. F-05 and F-06 name those changes.
- It does not settle Ephesians 1 and 3, Colossians 1, Matthew 11:13, or 2 Timothy 2:15. Each has its own testimony.
- It claims this: the two words the system is built on do not mean what the system needs them to mean.

## The Verdict

The dispensational system divides history into ages with different rules and calls the division rightly dividing the word. But a dispensation is a stewardship in a household, and rightly dividing is cutting a straight path. Moses and Messiah served in one house. Take the two words back to their meaning, and the system loses the ground it was built on. The passages that rest here are answered one at a time in their own testimonies.

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

A household gets new stewards. It does not get new house rules.
