## The Claim

Most churches teach that the Sabbath was always a picture of a deeper rest, and that Yahushua is that rest. He said, “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” He cried from the tree, “It is finished,” and His work was complete. Hebrews says that those who believe “do enter into rest,” and that a believer “that is entered into his rest” has ceased from his own works. The weekly day was the shadow. The rest in Messiah is the reality. A believer rests in Him every day, so no single day is required.

It is a warm teaching, and it speaks of a real rest. The question is whether that rest replaces the day, or whether Scripture holds the two together.

## The Point in One Sentence

There is one Sabbath, the seventh day, and Scripture shows that one day bearing witness to four things at once; the rest Messiah gives is one of the things the day points to, never a replacement for the day.

## The Short Version

- It is one day, the seventh, from Genesis to the age to come.
- It looks back to creation: Yahuah finished His work and rested.
- It looks back to deliverance: Yahuah brought His people out, and Messiah finished His work and rested.
- It is kept now, every week, as the sign between Yahuah and His people.
- It looks ahead to the rest still to come, when all flesh worships before Him.
- Hebrews uses one rare word for what remains for Yahuah’s people: sabbatismos, a Sabbath-keeping.

## It Remembers Creation

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them. And on the seventh day Elohim ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.
> 
> — **Genesis 2:1–2**

Finished, then rested. The fourth commandment rests its whole reason on this day: “for in six days Yahuah made heaven and earth … and rested the seventh day: wherefore Yahuah blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it” (Exodus 20:11). The Sabbath was set apart before there was sin, before Israel, and before any shadow-system (see F-07 and F-11).

## It Remembers Deliverance

When Moses repeated the commandment, he gave a second reason for the same day:

> And remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt, and that Yahuah thy Elohim brought thee out thence through a mighty hand and by a stretched out arm: therefore Yahuah thy Elohim commanded thee to keep the sabbath day.
> 
> — **Deuteronomy 5:15**

The same day remembers deliverance as well as creation. And the greater deliverance followed the same pattern as creation. Messiah finished the work given Him, and was laid in the tomb as “the sabbath drew on” (Luke 23:54). The women who had followed Him “rested the sabbath day according to the commandment” (Luke 23:56). Finished, then rested, just as at the beginning. The finished work did not erase the Sabbath. It was followed by one.

## It Is Kept Now as a Sign

Between His first coming and His return, the weekly Sabbath remains what Yahuah called it at Sinai:

> Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am Yahuah that doth sanctify you.
> 
> — **Exodus 31:13**

Throughout your generations. The apostles kept it after the resurrection, and Paul’s custom was to teach on it (Acts 17:2; see F-11). Each week the same day looks back to creation and to deliverance, and forward to the rest still to come. A believer who keeps it is not denying the finished work. He is remembering it, once a week, the way Yahuah told him to.

## It Looks Ahead to the Rest to Come

The prophets describe a rest that has not yet arrived: “and his rest shall be glorious” (Isaiah 11:10). And they place the weekly Sabbath inside it:

> For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith Yahuah, so shall your seed and your name remain. And it shall come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith Yahuah.
> 
> — **Isaiah 66:22–23**

The rest to come does not replace the Sabbath. The same day is still kept inside it.

## Sabbatismos: A Sabbath-Keeping

Hebrews 3 and 4 speak of rest using the ordinary Greek word katapausis (G2663), a ceasing or a resting place. Then, in one verse, the writer changes words:

> There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of Yahuah.
> 
> — **Hebrews 4:9**

Rest there is sabbatismos (G4520). It appears nowhere else in Scripture. It is built from the verb sabbatizo, which the Greek Old Testament uses for keeping the Sabbath: “So the people rested on the seventh day” (Exodus 16:30), and “even then shall the land rest” (Leviticus 26:34). Greek nouns ending in -ismos usually name an action, the doing of the verb. Sabbatismos is the doing of sabbatizo: a Sabbath-keeping.

Honesty requires a note. Some translators render it as a Sabbath rest, a state rather than a practice. The word allows that reading. But the verb behind it always means to keep the Sabbath, and the writer chose it on purpose after using a different word for rest throughout the passage. At the very least, the one place Hebrews uses Sabbath language for what remains, the Sabbath has not been taken away. How the whole chapter reads is answered in the Hebrews 4 testimony.

## One Day Holding Four Witnesses

The modern teaching takes one thing the Sabbath points to, the rest in Messiah, and lets it swallow the day itself. Scripture never does that. It is the same seventh day that remembers creation, remembers deliverance, stands as the weekly sign, and is still kept in the age to come. The meanings are carried by the day. Take the day away, and every one of its witnesses goes silent.

## What This Foundation Does Not Claim

- It does not deny that Messiah gives rest to the weary now. He does, and that rest is real.
- It does not settle the meaning of every verse in Hebrews 4, Matthew 11, or John 19. Those are answered in their own testimonies.
- It claims this: there is one Sabbath, and none of the things it bears witness to cancels the day that bears witness to them.

## The Verdict

The modern teaching takes a true thing, that Messiah is rest for the weary, and uses it to remove a commandment. Scripture shows one Sabbath carrying four witnesses: it remembers the creation Yahuah finished, remembers the deliverance He accomplished, stands as the sign kept throughout the generations, and is still kept in the rest to come, when all flesh worships from one Sabbath to another. And where Hebrews names what remains for the people of Yahuah, it uses a word built from keeping the Sabbath.

> Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.
> 
> — **Romans 3:31**

The finished work did not end the Sabbath. Twice in Scripture, a finished work is followed by one.
