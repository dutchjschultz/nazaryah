From prison Paul wrote, "I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you" (Philemon 22). He expected his release to come through the prayers of ordinary believers meeting in a house. He even asked Philemon to have a lodging ready.

A popular saying today is that prayer does not change things; it only changes the one who prays. Paul believed those prayers would reach Yahuah and move the outcome.

He said the same to the Philippians: "this shall turn to my salvation through your prayer" (Philippians 1:19). And Scripture records it happening. When Peter was chained in prison, "prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him" (Acts 12:5). That night an angel led him out, and he walked to "the house of Mary … where many were gathered together praying" (Acts 12:12). The prayers in a house opened a prison door.

James gives the rule: "The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much" (James 5:16). Availeth means it accomplishes something. It does not only calm the one who prays.

Prayer is not a mirror. It is a request made to Yahuah, who hears and acts. Revelation shows where such prayers go: "the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God" (Revelation 8:4).
