Jude quotes Enoch, "the seventh from Adam": "Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints, To execute judgment upon all" (Jude 1:14-15). It falls on "murmurers, complainers, walking after their own lusts" (Jude 1:16). The coming and the judgment arrive together. Many were taught that the return opens a thousand-year reign on earth, or that a secret rapture comes first and the judgment years later. Enoch's word knows one coming, and it comes to judge.

Daniel saw the same scene: "ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened" (Daniel 7:10). Zechariah wrote, "Yahuah my God shall come, and all the saints with thee" (Zechariah 14:5).

Yahushua gave the order plainly: "When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory" (Matthew 25:31-32). Coming, then throne, then judgment.

Paul wrote that the Master Yahushua will be "revealed from heaven with his mighty angels, In flaming fire taking vengeance on them that know not God" (2 Thessalonians 1:7-8). The wicked are answered at the revealing, not a thousand years after it.

The usual escape is that the righteous rise first and the wicked long after. Yahushua put both in one hour, when "all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, And shall come forth," the good to life and the evil to damnation (John 5:28-29).

The conclusion is plain. The Master comes once more, and He comes to judge all. Revelation closes the same way: "my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be" (Revelation 22:12).
