Jude warns of "ungodly men, turning the grace of our God into lasciviousness" (Jude 1:4). They did not deny Yahuah's favor. They used it. They turned favor into a reason to live loose. Many were taught that favor means the Law no longer applies. Jude names that as the work of men who "crept in unawares."

The Torah warned of the same man. He hears the curse and still blesses himself in his heart, "saying, I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of mine heart" (Deuteronomy 29:19). It is the same error in older dress.

Yahushua said some who call Him Master will hear, "depart from me, ye that work iniquity" (Matthew 7:23). The word is anomia, lawlessness.

Paul asked the question outright: "Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid" (Romans 6:1-2). John gave the measure of sin: "sin is the transgression of the law" (1 John 3:4).

The usual escape is that belief has set the Law aside. Paul answers it in one verse: "Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law" (Romans 3:31). Belief does not cancel the Law. It sets it on its feet.

The conclusion is plain. Favor is the door, opened by the blood. It is not permission to leave Yahuah's commandments outside. The last chapter of Scripture says who walks through: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life" (Revelation 22:14).
