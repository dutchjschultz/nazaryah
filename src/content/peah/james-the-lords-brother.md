Paul went up to see Peter, and "other of the apostles saw I none, save James the Lord's brother" (Galatians 1:19). Then he adds an oath: "before God, I lie not" (Galatians 1:20). The common teaching in Rome is that Mary stayed a virgin all her life, so James must have been a cousin. Paul calls him the Master's brother, under oath.

The Psalms foretold it. The Messiah says, "I am become a stranger unto my brethren, and an alien unto my mother's children" (Psalm 69:8). The gospels apply the next verse of this same Psalm to Yahushua (John 2:17). His mother had other children.

Matthew names them: "his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas? And his sisters, are they not all with us?" (Matthew 13:55-56). He also says Joseph "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25).

Yahushua spoke of them while they stood outside with His mother: "Who is my mother, or my brethren?" (Mark 3:33).

The usual escape is that "brother" here means cousin. Paul had the word for cousin and used it. He called Mark "sister's son to Barnabas" (Colossians 4:10), the word anepsios. For James he chose adelphos, brother.

The conclusion is plain. Yahushua had brothers and sisters, and James was one of them. Jude opens his letter as "the brother of James" (Jude 1:1), and Matthew names a Judas among the Master's brothers.
