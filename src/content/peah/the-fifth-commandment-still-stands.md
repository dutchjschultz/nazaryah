Paul speaks to the children in the assembly: "Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise" (Ephesians 6:2). These were Gentile believers, about thirty years after the cross. That is worth noticing, since many were taught the Ten Commandments ended there.

Paul is quoting the Ten Commandments: "Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land" (Exodus 20:12). He calls it a commandment, and he says its promise still holds: "That it may be well with thee" (Ephesians 6:3). A law that ended at the cross could not still carry a living promise.

Yahushua defended this same commandment against the religious men of His day: "For God commanded, saying, Honour thy father and mother." Then He charged them: "Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none effect by your tradition" (Matthew 15:4, 6). He did not loosen the fifth commandment. He condemned the men who did.

Paul does not treat it as the only one left. In Romans he lists others, "Thou shalt not commit adultery, Thou shalt not kill, Thou shalt not steal" (Romans 13:9). John adds, "this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous" (1 John 5:3).

If the fifth commandment still stands for Gentile children, the Ten still stand. Revelation describes the saints at the end the same way: "here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12).
