Jude closes his letter with glory "To the only wise God our Saviour" (Jude 1:25). He is the One "able to keep you from falling" (Jude 1:24). Many were taught to call the Son "the Savior." Jude gives the title to God alone, the same God he named the Father in his first line (Jude 1:1).

Isaiah heard it from Yahuah Himself: "I, even I, am Yahuah; and beside me there is no saviour" (Isaiah 43:11). Hosea said the same: "thou shalt know no god but me: for there is no saviour beside me" (Hosea 13:4).

Yahushua said where He stands in this: God sent His Son "that the world through him might be saved" (John 3:17). God saves. The Son is the one through whom it comes.

Paul set the two side by side: "by the commandment of God our Saviour, and Master Yahushua Messiah, which is our hope" (1 Timothy 1:1). God is the Savior. Yahushua is the hope.

The usual escape is that "the only wise God" must include the Son. Paul closed Romans with the same words and answered it: "To God only wise, be glory through Yahushua Messiah for ever" (Romans 16:27). Glory goes to God through the Son. The one it goes through is not the one it goes to.

The conclusion is plain. Yahuah saves, and the Son provided the way. The song of the overcomers gives the glory to Him alone: "Great and marvellous are thy works, Yahuah God Almighty ... for thou only art holy" (Revelation 15:3-4).
