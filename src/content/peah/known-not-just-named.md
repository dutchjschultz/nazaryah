Nahum gives a comfort for hard days: "Yahuah is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble; and he knoweth them that trust in him" (Nahum 1:7). To be known by Yahuah is a great thing. Many have been told that calling on the name once settles it, and that the commandments are optional afterward. Scripture shows what being known looks like.

The first Psalm answers it: "Yahuah knoweth the way of the righteous" (Psalm 1:6). The man whose way He knows is the man whose "delight is in the law of Yahuah" (Psalm 1:2).

Yahushua drew the same line. "Not every one that saith unto me, Master, Master, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father" (Matthew 7:21). To the rest He will say, "I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity" (Matthew 7:23). The word for iniquity is anomia, lawlessness.

John says it plainly: "hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar" (1 John 2:3-4).

Some worry that keeping commandments is trying to earn salvation. But Nahum puts trust first, and the walk follows. Paul sets both on one seal: the Lord "knoweth them that are his," and "Let every one that nameth the name of Messiah depart from iniquity" (2 Timothy 2:19). Deliverance comes first by the blood. Being known shows in the walk after it.

A name spoken once does not prove that Yahuah knows a person. Trust that keeps His commandments does. In Revelation, Yahushua tells Philadelphia, "I know thy works... thou... hast kept my word, and hast not denied my name" (Revelation 3:8).
