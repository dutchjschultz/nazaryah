Paul opens with a warm blessing: "from God our Father and the Master Yahushua Messiah" (Philemon 3). It is easy to read past, but it is worth counting the names. Most were taught one God in three coequal persons. The greeting names two.

Three things stand in that one line. First, the title God belongs to the Father. Second, Yahushua is named beside Him as Master, a distinct title. Third, no third person sends greeting at all.

The pattern does not change from letter to letter. Rome receives favor "from God our Father, and the Master Yahushua Messiah" (Romans 1:7). Thessalonica receives the same, from the same two (1 Thessalonians 1:1). If the holy spirit were a coequal third person, Paul leaves him out of every greeting he writes, and no apostle explains the silence.

Paul states the order plainly when he counts: "to us there is but one God, the Father … and one Master Yahushua Messiah" (1 Corinthians 8:6). And again: "there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). Yahushua counted the same way in His own prayer. He called the Father "the only true God," and Himself the one "whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3).

The Trinity reading needs three names, and the greeting gives two. One God, the Father, and one Master, His Son. The last vision holds the same count. The river flows from "the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Revelation 22:1). Two are named there as well, and no third.
