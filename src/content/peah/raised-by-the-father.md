Paul opens the letter as "an apostle, (not of men, neither by man, but by Yahushua Messiah, and God the Father, who raised him from the dead;)" and sends it with "all the brethren" to "the churches of Galatia" (Galatians 1:1-2). In the first line he names who raised the Son: God the Father. Many were taught that Yahushua raised Himself, and that this proves He is God. Paul gives the raising to the Father.

David foretold it as a prayer to Yahuah: "thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption" (Psalm 16:10). The Holy One is kept by Another.

Peter preached that Psalm and named the One who did it: "This Yahushua hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses" (Acts 2:32).

The usual escape is Yahushua's word, "I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again" (John 10:18). He finished the same sentence: "This commandment have I received of my Father." The power was given, and the Giver is the Father.

Paul says it the same way in every letter. "God hath raised him from the dead" (Romans 10:9). Hebrews names "the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Master Yahushua" (Hebrews 13:20).

The conclusion is plain. The Son died, and the Father raised Him. One who must be raised by God is not that God. In Revelation the risen Son says, "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore" (Revelation 1:18).
