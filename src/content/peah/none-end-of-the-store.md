Nahum calls out, "Take ye the spoil of silver, take the spoil of gold: for there is none end of the store" (Nahum 2:9). In the next breath, "She is empty, and void, and waste: and the heart melteth" (Nahum 2:10). Endless treasure, emptied in a day. Many were taught that riches are the sure sign of Yahuah's favor. Nineveh had more than anyone, and it did not save her.

The Torah warned against that very thought: "thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. But thou shalt remember Yahuah thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth" (Deuteronomy 8:17-18).

Asaph envied the prosperity of the wicked until he "went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end" (Psalm 73:17).

Yahushua told of the rich man who stored up goods: "Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee ... So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God" (Luke 12:20-21).

Paul warned that "they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare" (1 Timothy 6:9).

The conclusion is plain. A full storehouse is not proof of Yahuah's favor. What is gathered without Him is emptied in the end. Revelation says it of Babylon: "in one hour so great riches is come to nought" (Revelation 18:17).
