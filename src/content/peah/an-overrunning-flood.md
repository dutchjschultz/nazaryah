Nahum says how Nineveh will end: "with an overrunning flood he will make an utter end of the place thereof, and darkness shall pursue his enemies" (Nahum 1:8). The next chapter shows it happening: "The gates of the rivers shall be opened" (Nahum 2:6). Yahuah judged Nineveh with water, as He judged the world in Noah's day. Many were taught that Yahuah no longer judges nations, as if judgment belonged only to the Old Testament or to the far future.

The Torah shows the pattern first: "I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh" (Genesis 6:17). Afterward Yahuah promised, "neither shall there any more be a flood to destroy the earth" (Genesis 9:11). Nineveh's flood took one city, not the earth. The promise held, and the judgment still came.

Yahushua used the flood as a warning for His own coming: "as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be." They "knew not until the flood came, and took them all away" (Matthew 24:37, 39).

Peter said the next judgment is already named: the old world "being overflowed with water, perished: But the heavens and the earth, which are now ... reserved unto fire against the day of judgment" (2 Peter 3:6-7).

The conclusion is plain. Yahuah judged by water, He still judges nations, and He has named fire for the last day. Revelation shows that day: "fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them" (Revelation 20:9).
