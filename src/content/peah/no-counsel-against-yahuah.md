Nahum asks Assyria, "What do ye imagine against Yahuah? he will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time" (Nahum 1:9). Its plotters are "folden together as thorns" and will be "devoured as stubble fully dry" (Nahum 1:10). Even "a wicked counsellor" who "imagineth evil against Yahuah" (Nahum 1:11) cannot stand. Many picture the enemy as nearly Yahuah's equal, as if the outcome were in doubt. Nahum shows a contest that was never close.

The Psalms ask the same question: "Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?" Then, "He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh" (Psalm 2:1, 4). Proverbs closes the matter: "There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against Yahuah" (Proverbs 21:30).

Yahushua told Pilate where all such power comes from: "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above" (John 19:11).

Paul wrote that the plotting of men only served Yahuah's plan: "had they known it, they would not have crucified the Master of glory" (1 Corinthians 2:8).

The usual escape is that the enemy is winning in this age, so the end must be uncertain. Nahum says the end is fixed before the battle starts: "he will make an utter end." Affliction does not rise twice.

The conclusion is plain. Every plan made against Yahuah is already answered. Revelation ends the contest in one line: "the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire" (Revelation 20:10).
