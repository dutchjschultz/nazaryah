Jacob was a grasper from the womb, born with his hand on Esau's heel (Genesis 25:26). He took the birthright, put on Esau's clothes, and told his blind father, "I am Esau thy firstborn" (27:15–19). What he took by that deceit was wealth and power: the dew of heaven, the fatness of the earth, corn and wine, and "be lord over thy brethren" (27:28–29).

That was never the blessing Yahuah meant for him. The covenant blessing came openly, when Isaac knew exactly whom he was blessing: "the blessing of Abraham, to thee, and to thy seed with thee; that thou mayest inherit the land" (28:3–4). That same night at Bethel, Yahuah confirmed it Himself: the land, and a seed as the dust of the earth (28:13–14). Jacob did not have to grasp any of it. At the Jabbok he finally stopped reaching for his brother's name, answered "Jacob," and was named Israel (32:27–28).

The next day the stolen blessing came undone. The man told to "be lord over thy brethren" bowed to the ground seven times before Esau and called him "my lord" (33:3, 8). Then he pressed his gifts on him: "Take, I pray thee, my blessing" (33:11). The word is berakah (H1293), the same blessing Esau said had been taken from him (27:35). Jacob gave back the wealth and lordship he had grasped for and kept the land and seed that Yahuah had given him. What was taken had to be returned. What Yahuah gave stayed.
