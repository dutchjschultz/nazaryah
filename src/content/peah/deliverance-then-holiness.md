"But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness" (Obadiah 1:17). Two things are named, and they come in order. First Yahuah delivers. Then His people are holy. Many were taught that holiness is only a status given at the start, with nothing to walk out after. Obadiah puts holiness on the mount where the delivered live.

The Torah set the order at the Exodus. Yahuah spoke the Ten Commandments only after saying He had "brought thee out of the land of Egypt" (Exodus 20:2). Then He gave the reason: "I am Yahuah that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt ... ye shall therefore be holy, for I am holy" (Leviticus 11:45). Deliverance came first, and holiness followed as the response.

Yahushua kept the same order. He sent the woman away uncondemned, then told her, "go, and sin no more" (John 8:11).

Peter quoted Leviticus to believers already redeemed by "the precious blood of Messiah" (1 Peter 1:19). He told them, "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16).

The usual escape is that holiness is only counted to a man, never lived. Hebrews says holiness is something to "follow," and that without it "no man shall see" Him (Hebrews 12:14). A thing a man must follow is a thing he walks.

The conclusion is plain. Deliverance is the door, and holiness is the life lived inside it. Yahuah's order has not changed since Egypt. The last chapter of Scripture keeps the same line: "he that is holy, let him be holy still" (Revelation 22:11).
