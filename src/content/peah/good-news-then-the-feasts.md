Picture a runner on the hills with news of peace: "Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace! O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows" (Nahum 1:15). The first command after the good news is to keep the feasts. That is worth noticing, since most of us were taught that the gospel ended the feasts.

Paul takes these same feet and applies them to the preaching of the gospel (Romans 10:15 · Isaiah 52:7). The good news in Nahum is the gospel.

The Torah set the feasts as "a statute for ever throughout your generations" (Leviticus 23:14). The prophets saw them kept after Yahuah's great day, when the nations "shall even go up from year to year to worship the King, Yahuah of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles" (Zechariah 14:16).

Yahushua kept them. At Sukkot, "then went he also up unto the feast" (John 7:10). Paul told the Gentile assembly at Corinth, "let us keep the feast... with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:8).

Colossians 2:16 is often given as the answer. But that verse says to let no man judge believers "in respect of an holyday." It does not tell them to stop keeping one. The next verse calls these days "a shadow of things to come" (Colossians 2:17). Things to come are still ahead.

The order in Nahum is deliverance first, then the walk. The good news does not cancel the feasts. It frees people to keep them. Revelation shows the multitude with "palms in their hands" (Revelation 7:9), and at the end "the tabernacle of God is with men" (Revelation 21:3).
