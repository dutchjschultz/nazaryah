Yahuah says to Nineveh, "I will shew the nations thy nakedness, and the kingdoms thy shame" (Nahum 3:5). He will "set thee as a gazingstock" (Nahum 3:6). Then the last blow: "all they that look upon thee shall flee from thee, and say, Nineveh is laid waste: who will bemoan her? whence shall I seek comforters for thee?" (Nahum 3:7). The harlot had many lovers. When she fell, not one stayed.

Jeremiah wrote the same of Jerusalem in her fall: "among all her lovers she hath none to comfort her: all her friends have dealt treacherously with her" (Lamentations 1:2). The friends that sin brings do not stay for the judgment.

Yahushua told of the son who spent everything in the far country. When the famine came, he was feeding swine, "and no man gave unto him" (Luke 15:16). The far country's friends had nothing for him. Only the father ran to meet him.

James puts it in one line: "whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" (James 4:4). It is often taught that friendship with the world does no harm. Nahum shows how that friendship ends.

Yahuah alone offers what Nineveh's lovers could not: "I, even I, am he that comforteth you" (Isaiah 51:12).

The conclusion is plain. The world's friendship flees in the day of trouble. Revelation shows Babylon's kings doing just that, "Standing afar off for the fear of her torment" (Revelation 18:10).
