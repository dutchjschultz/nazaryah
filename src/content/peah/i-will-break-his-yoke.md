Yahuah tells Judah, "Though I have afflicted thee, I will afflict thee no more. For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and will burst thy bonds in sunder" (Nahum 1:12-13). The yoke Yahuah breaks is the oppressor's. Many were taught that Yahuah's Law is the yoke believers are freed from. Scripture names a different yoke.

The Torah says it at the Exodus: "I have broken the bands of your yoke, and made you go upright" (Leviticus 26:13). Jeremiah repeats Nahum's words almost exactly: "I will break his yoke from off thy neck, and will burst thy bonds" (Jeremiah 30:8). The yoke was always Egypt, Assyria, or Babylon.

Yahushua did not take His followers out from under a yoke. He gave them His own: "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me ... For my yoke is easy" (Matthew 11:29-30). John says what it weighs: "his commandments are not grievous" (1 John 5:3).

The usual escape is Peter's word about "a yoke ... which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear" (Acts 15:10). The same chapter names that yoke. It was the teaching, "Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved" (Acts 15:1). It was a rule for deliverance laid on men, not the commandments for walking after it.

The conclusion is plain. Yahuah breaks the oppressor's yoke and gives His own, which is light. The saints at the end still wear it gladly, as those who "keep the commandments of God" (Revelation 14:12).
