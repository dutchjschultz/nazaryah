Mainstream Christianity teaches that the Law was a yoke on Israel's neck. Leviticus says the opposite.

Yahuah said, "I have broken the bands of your yoke, and made you go upright" (Leviticus 26:13). The "bands" are *motot*, the wooden bars of the yoke. Egypt's bars were on Israel's neck, and Yahuah broke them. The people who had walked bent over in Egypt walked out standing straight.

The order matters. The Ten Words open with deliverance before a single command is given: "which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage" (Exodus 20:2). Yahuah broke the yoke first and gave the Law second. The Law was not the yoke. It was the road for a people already set free.

The ones who are set free are told to set others free: "undo the heavy burdens" (Isaiah 58:6), in Hebrew the bands of the *motah*, "and that ye break every yoke." Paul uses the same picture: "Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Messiah hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage" (Galatians 5:1).

At the end, the people who were once bent under Egypt's bars "stood before the throne, and before the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9). They are still standing upright.
