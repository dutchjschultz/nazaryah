Paul says Messiah abolished "the law of commandments contained in ordinances" (Ephesians 2:15). This verse is often read as proof that Yahuah's Law ended at the cross. One word decides it. The word for ordinances is dogma, a decree. Luke uses it for "a decree from Caesar Augustus" (Luke 2:1). Elsewhere in the New Testament it means rulings handed down by men.

What decree kept Jew and Gentile apart? Peter names it: "it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation" (Acts 10:28). That rule is not in the Torah. The Torah says the opposite: "the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you" (Leviticus 19:34). The wall was built from men's decrees, not from Yahuah's Law.

Paul uses the same word in Colossians and names the decrees: "Touch not; taste not; handle not," which he calls "the commandments and doctrines of men" (Colossians 2:21-22). Yahushua said the same of the teachers of His day: "laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men" (Mark 7:8).

What came down at the cross was the wall of men's rules. Paul says it plainly: "Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law" (Romans 3:31). The cross took away what divided the two peoples and kept what Yahuah spoke. Revelation still names the saints as those who "keep the commandments of God" (Revelation 12:17).
