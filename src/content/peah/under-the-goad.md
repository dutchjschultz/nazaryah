Mainstream Christianity hears "learn of me" as classroom language: read, listen, take notes. Scripture's own word for learning comes from the field, not the classroom.

The Hebrew word for learning is *lamad*. The tool that drove oxen is a *malmad*, built from the same root. Shamgar slew six hundred Philistines "with an ox goad" (Judges 3:31). In Hebrew, learning began behind a team of oxen.

The letter *lamed*, which carries the root, is commonly read in its oldest form as a staff or goad. The word looks like what it means.

Scripture uses the word for a beast under the yoke. Ephraim confessed he had been chastised "as a bullock unaccustomed to the yoke" (Jeremiah 31:18). "Unaccustomed" is *lo lummad*, which means untaught. An ox that has not learned is an ox that has not worn the yoke.

So when Yahushua said, "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me" (Matthew 11:29), He was using ox language from start to finish. Learning of Him is not collecting information. It is wearing His yoke and walking where the Master drives.

The rest He promised has an old address. "Ye shall find rest unto your souls" is Jeremiah's line. Yahuah told Judah to "ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls." Judah answered, "We will not walk therein" (Jeremiah 6:16). The rest Yahushua offered is rest on Yahuah's old road, the road Judah refused.

The order matters. Rest comes first: "I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). Deliverance comes before the yoke. The yoke follows, and it is not bondage, because "my yoke is easy" (Matthew 11:30). Mainstream teaching says the cross lifted every yoke. The Master says to take His.

The ox that learns its yoke ends up following its driver step for step. The firstfruits in Revelation are the ones who "follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth" (Revelation 14:4).
