Jude sets two kinds of speech side by side. The false men "despise dominion, and speak evil of dignities," and they "speak evil of those things which they know not" (Jude 1:8, 10). Michael the archangel, "contending with the devil," would "not bring against him a railing accusation, but said, The Lord rebuke thee" (Jude 1:9). The archangel left the rebuke to Yahuah. Many were taught that believers should bind, command, and rebuke the devil on their own word. Michael did not dare.

Zechariah saw the same scene. Satan stood at the high priest's right hand to resist him, "And Yahuah said unto Satan, Yahuah rebuke thee, O Satan" (Zechariah 3:1-2). The rebuke came from Yahuah.

Yahushua met the tempter three times with "It is written" (Matthew 4:4, 7, 10). His answer stood on what Yahuah had spoken.

Peter wrote of the same men. They "are not afraid to speak evil of dignities. Whereas angels, which are greater in power and might, bring not railing accusation against them before the Lord" (2 Peter 2:10-11).

The usual escape is that believers were given power over the enemy, so the rebuke is theirs to speak. James gives the order: "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" (James 4:7). The resisting comes after the submitting, and the power stays Yahuah's.

The conclusion is plain. The rebuke belongs to Yahuah, and the believer's part is to submit and stand on what is written. Heaven tells how the accuser is overcome: "by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony" (Revelation 12:11).
