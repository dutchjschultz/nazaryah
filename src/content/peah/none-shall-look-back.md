Nineveh's queen is led away, "and her maids shall lead her as with the voice of doves" (Nahum 2:7). The city empties: "they shall flee away. Stand, stand, shall they cry; but none shall look back" (Nahum 2:8). The captains shout to hold the line, and no one turns. The courage of the city is gone. The Torah had described this very thing.

Yahuah warned what His curse does to a people: "the sound of a shaken leaf shall chase them; and they shall flee, as fleeing from a sword ... and ye shall have no power to stand before your enemies" (Leviticus 26:36-37). Proverbs puts it in one line: "The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion" (Proverbs 28:1).

Yahushua foretold the same fear at the end: "Men's hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things which are coming on the earth" (Luke 21:26).

Paul gave the other side: "God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind" (2 Timothy 1:7).

The common teaching is that the Torah's blessings and curses were for ancient Israel only. Nahum shows the same pattern falling on Nineveh. Yahuah's measure of blessing and curse did not stop at Israel's border.

The conclusion is plain. A people who leave Yahuah lose their courage with Him. Revelation shows great men at the end hiding "in the dens and in the rocks of the mountains" (Revelation 6:15).
