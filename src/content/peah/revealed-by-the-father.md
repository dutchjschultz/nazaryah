Paul tells how it happened: "when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother's womb, and called me by his grace, To reveal his Son in me ... immediately I conferred not with flesh and blood" (Galatians 1:15-16). He went "into Arabia, and returned again unto Damascus" (Galatians 1:17). The Father separated him, the Father called him, and the Father revealed the Son. The Son is the One revealed, and the Father is the Revealer.

Paul's words echo the prophets. Yahuah told Jeremiah, "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee ... I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations" (Jeremiah 1:5). Isaiah wrote, "Yahuah hath called me from the womb" (Isaiah 49:1).

Yahushua told Peter the same thing about his confession: "flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 16:17). Paul uses the same phrase, "flesh and blood," for the same reason.

Yahushua also said, "No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him" (John 6:44). Many were taught that the Son is revealed by His own power as God. Yahushua said the drawing is the Father's work.

The conclusion is plain. The Father reveals the Son, and no man learns Him from flesh and blood. Revelation opens with the same order: "The Revelation of Yahushua Messiah, which God gave unto him" (Revelation 1:1).
