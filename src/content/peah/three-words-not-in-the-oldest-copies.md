In the King James, Ephesians 3:9 speaks of "God, who created all things by Jesus Christ." The verse is often given as proof that the Son is the Creator. But the words "by Jesus Christ" are not in the oldest Greek copies of Ephesians, such as the Sinaiticus and the Vaticanus. Later copyists added them, and most modern translations leave them out.

Without the added words, the verse says only that the mystery was "hid in God, who created all things." That agrees with the rest of Scripture. Yahuah says, "I am Yahuah that maketh all things; that stretcheth forth the heavens alone; that spreadeth abroad the earth by myself" (Isaiah 44:24). "Alone" and "by myself" leave no room for a partner. Malachi asks, "hath not one God created us?" (Malachi 2:10).

Yahushua Himself gave the credit to God: "from the beginning of the creation God made them male and female" (Mark 10:6). The apostles prayed to the One "which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea" (Acts 4:24), and in the same prayer spoke of "thy holy child Yahushua" (Acts 4:27) as a separate one.

Revelation draws the line clearly. The elders worship the One on the throne: "thou hast created all things" (Revelation 4:11). The Lamb comes in the next chapter and takes the book "out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne" (Revelation 5:7). The Creator and the Lamb are two, and the Creator is the One on the throne.
