Paul prays to "the God of our Master Yahushua Messiah, the Father of glory" (Ephesians 1:17). It is a short phrase and easy to pass over. Yahushua has a God, and Paul names Him: the Father. That sits hard against the common teaching that the Son is God, coequal with the Father.

The prophets said it first. Micah foretold that the coming ruler would stand "in the majesty of the name of Yahuah his God" (Micah 5:4). Hebrews quotes the Psalm and applies it to the Son: "God, even thy God, hath anointed thee" (Hebrews 1:9; Psalm 45:7). The one who is anointed has a God who anoints him.

Yahushua said it Himself after He rose: "I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God" (John 20:17).

Some say this was only His human nature speaking. But the words do not stop at the cross. Seated in heaven, Yahushua says "my God" four times in one verse to the assembly at Philadelphia (Revelation 3:12). At the very end, "then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him" (1 Corinthians 15:28). On earth, after the resurrection, and at the end, He has a God.

One who has a God cannot be that God. In His own prayer the Son calls the Father "the only true God" (John 17:3). In Revelation He still stands in "the temple of my God," and He brings the overcomer in with Him.
