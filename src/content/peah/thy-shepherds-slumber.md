Nahum's last word to the king is about his rulers: "Thy shepherds slumber, O king of Assyria: thy nobles shall dwell in the dust: thy people is scattered upon the mountains, and no man gathereth them" (Nahum 3:18). Shepherds are the men set over a people. When they sleep, the flock scatters, and no one gathers it.

Ezekiel charged Israel's shepherds with the same failure: "they were scattered, because there is no shepherd ... and none did search or seek after them" (Ezekiel 34:5-6). Then Yahuah promised, "I will set up one shepherd over them ... even my servant David" (Ezekiel 34:23). The one Shepherd is set up by Yahuah.

Yahushua saw the crowds this way: "he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd" (Matthew 9:36). He said, "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" (John 10:11).

Peter told the elders, "Feed the flock of God which is among you," and promised a crown "when the chief Shepherd shall appear" (1 Peter 5:2, 4).

Many shepherds today keep the flock comfortable rather than awake. Nahum shows where sleeping shepherds lead: to a scattered people.

The conclusion is plain. A shepherd's work is to stay awake and gather. The one Shepherd Yahuah set up does both. Revelation shows Him at the end: "the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters" (Revelation 7:17).
