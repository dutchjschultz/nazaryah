Jude urges the assembly to "earnestly contend for the faith which was once delivered unto the saints" (Jude 1:3). The belief was handed over once. The word is hapax, once for all. Many were taught that doctrine grew over the centuries, and that councils and creeds settled what the apostles left unsettled. Jude says the deposit was already complete in his day.

Moses set the rule at the start: "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). Jeremiah told Israel to "ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein" (Jeremiah 6:16).

Yahushua faced added doctrine in His own day: "in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Matthew 15:9).

Paul closed the door on any later message: "though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed" (Galatians 1:8).

The usual escape is that the church was given authority to define doctrine later, at councils such as Nicaea. But a belief delivered once was not still being finished three hundred years on. What later councils added was not part of what was delivered.

The conclusion is plain. The belief Jude defends came complete from the apostles, and the task is to contend for it, not to improve it. The last book of Scripture seals it: "If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book" (Revelation 22:18).
