Yahuah told Jeremiah to put a wooden yoke bar on his neck as a sign that Judah should serve Babylon and live (Jeremiah 27:2, 12). Babylon was His discipline.

The prophet Hananiah took the bar off Jeremiah's neck, broke it, and promised Babylon would be finished within two years (Jeremiah 28:10-11). It was a comfortable word, and the people wanted to hear it.

Yahuah answered: "Thou hast broken the yokes of wood; but thou shalt make for them yokes of iron" (Jeremiah 28:13). Breaking the yoke Yahuah had placed did not bring freedom. It brought a heavier one. Moses had already named that iron yoke for a people who would not serve Yahuah "with joyfulness": "he shall put a yoke of iron upon thy neck" (Deuteronomy 28:47-48).

Hananiah's sin was making "this people to trust in a lie" (Jeremiah 28:15), and he died that same year, in the seventh month (28:17).

The pulpit still breaks wooden yokes. It tells people that Yahuah's correction is never His hand and that hard times are only the devil. But Scripture says, "whom Yahuah loveth he chasteneth" (Hebrews 12:6, quoting Proverbs 3:12), and afterward that chastening yields "the peaceable fruit of righteousness" (12:11). Refusing the wood only earns the iron.

The Master still speaks this way at the end: "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten: be zealous therefore, and repent" (Revelation 3:19).
