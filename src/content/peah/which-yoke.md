Peter asked, "Why tempt ye Elohim, to put a yoke upon the neck of the disciples, which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?" (Acts 15:10). Mainstream Christianity preaches this verse as proof that the Law was too heavy and has been lifted off believers.

The question in Acts 15 was not about how to walk. It was about what saves. The trouble started when men taught, "Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved" (Acts 15:1).

Peter answered that question. Yahuah purifies hearts "by faith" (15:9), and all are saved "through the favor of the Master Yahushua" (15:11). The yoke Peter refused was salvation earned through the flesh. It was not the commandments as the walk of people who were already delivered.

The council then gave the new believers four starting commands taken from the Law, and added, "For Moses of old time hath in every city them that preach him, being read in the synagogues every sabbath day" (15:20-21). They expected the new believers to keep hearing Moses.

Years later, thousands of believers were still "all zealous of the law," and Paul showed that he walked orderly and kept the Law (Acts 21:20, 24). The yoke was removed. The Law was not.

At the end, the dragon makes war on those who "keep the commandments of Elohim, and have the testimony of Yahushua Messiah" (Revelation 12:17). The commandments are still there at the end.
