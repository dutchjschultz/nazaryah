The word "continually" in Leviticus 24:8 is tamid (H8548), meaning constant, without a break. The KJV also renders it "alway," "always," "perpetual," and "daily." In the tabernacle Yahuah named six things tamid: the showbread (Exodus 25:30), the lamp (Exodus 27:20), the breastplate over Aaron's heart (Exodus 28:29–30), the holy crown on his forehead (Exodus 28:38), the burnt offering of the morning and at even (Exodus 29:38–42), and the incense (Exodus 30:8). Leviticus 24:8 joins the continual to the Sabbath: fresh bread set in order every Sabbath, by an everlasting covenant. When the captives came home, the continual burnt offering was restored together with the new moons, the Sabbaths, and the set feasts (Ezra 3:5; Nehemiah 10:33). The continual was the frame the appointed times hung on.

In Daniel, the little horn takes the continual away (Daniel 8:11–13). The KJV prints "the daily sacrifice," but "sacrifice" is in italics, added by the translators. The Hebrew says only ha-tamid, "the continual." Since Leviticus 24:8 ties tamid to the Sabbath, the word cannot be narrowed to one animal on one altar. It is the continual order Yahuah set, attacked by the same power that thinks "to change times and laws" (Daniel 7:25). Daniel 12:11 counts 1,290 days from the time the continual is taken away, a count read by the rule in [A Day for a Year](/peah/a-day-for-a-year).

The continual things did not end with the tabernacle. They moved to the believer. The outer veil tore at Matthew 27:51, opening the Holy Place to the believer-priest, and each tamid item has its counterpart in the walk. The showbread answers to every word that proceeds out of the mouth of Yahuah (Deuteronomy 8:3). The lamp answers to "a lamp unto my feet" (Psalm 119:105). The breastplate answers to the commandments bound "continually upon thine heart" (Proverbs 6:21), the crown to the words set "as frontlets between thine eyes" (Deuteronomy 6:8), and the incense to prayer "set forth before thee as incense" (Psalm 141:2). The burnt offering becomes "the sacrifice of praise to Yahuah continually, that is, the fruit of our lips" (Hebrews 13:15). David wrote the walk in the same word: "I have set Yahuah always before me" (Psalm 16:8), and "So shall I keep thy law continually for ever and ever" (Psalm 119:44). What stood before Yahuah without a break in the Holy Place is what the believer now carries without a break.
