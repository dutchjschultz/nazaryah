Onesimus ran away. To Philemon that was a plain loss: a servant gone and his labor lost. Paul looks at the same event and writes one careful word, "perhaps." "For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever" (Philemon 15).

Paul does not claim to know Yahuah's mind, but he leaves room for it. A short loss may have bought a lasting gain. The runaway left as a servant and came back "a brother beloved" (Philemon 16), bound to Philemon now in a tie that does not end. So Paul asks, "receive him as myself" (Philemon 17).

Joseph read his own story the same way. His brothers sold him, and years later he told them, "it was not you that sent me hither, but God" (Genesis 45:8). Then he said it plainly: "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good" (Genesis 50:20). The loss of a son for a season became the saving of a whole house.

Paul states the rule behind both stories: "all things work together for good to them that love God" (Romans 8:28). That does not make the runaway's wrong good. It means Yahuah can take a wrong and turn its road toward His purpose.

What a believer loses "for a season" may be the very road by which Yahuah gives something back "for ever." The season ends; the forever does not. At the end, "the former things are passed away" (Revelation 21:4), and only the forever remains.
