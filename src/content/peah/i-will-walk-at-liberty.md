The psalmist sets two things side by side as one: "So shall I keep thy law continually for ever and ever. And I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:44–45). The modern pulpit teaches that these two pull apart. In that teaching, liberty means freedom from the law, and keeping the commandments is a return to bondage.

The word translated "at liberty" is *rachab* (H7342), a wide and roomy place. It does not mean freedom from rules. It means open ground to walk on. Its opposite is the narrow place of distress: "I called upon Yah in distress: Yah answered me, and set me in a large place" (Psalm 118:5). The psalmist names what makes his ground wide: "for I seek thy precepts."

Scripture also names the narrow place. "Sin is the transgression of the law" (1 John 3:4), and "whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin" (John 8:34). The slave's cell is lawbreaking. James calls the law itself "the perfect law of liberty" (James 1:25).

The pulpit has the two places reversed. The law does not deliver; Yahuah delivers through Messiah. The law is the wide road the delivered man walks on afterward. Liberty is not room away from the law. Liberty is the room the law makes.
