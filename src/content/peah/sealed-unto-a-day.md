Paul writes, "grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption" (Ephesians 4:30). The seal comes now. The redemption comes on a day still ahead. That is worth holding onto, since the common teaching is that deliverance is finished the moment a person believes.

Paul already explained the seal. It is "the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession" (Ephesians 1:14). An earnest is a down payment. It is real, but it points to a payment still to come.

Paul says the same in Romans: "now is our salvation nearer than when we believed" (Romans 13:11). If it is nearer now, it was not complete on the day they believed. Yahushua said it plainly: "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13).

That is why Paul warns, "grieve not." A finished deal needs no warning. Israel heard the same warning in the wilderness: "they rebelled, and vexed his holy Spirit: therefore he was turned to be their enemy" (Isaiah 63:10). They came out of Egypt under the blood and still fell in the wilderness.

The seal keeps a person as Yahuah's until the day He comes for His own. Ezekiel saw a mark set "upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry" before judgment fell (Ezekiel 9:4). Revelation shows the same at the end: judgment waits "till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" (Revelation 7:3).
