Babylon reshaped everything it could reach in Daniel. It taught him "the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans" (Daniel 1:4), and it gave him a new name, Belteshazzar (Daniel 1:7). Daniel did not refuse the school, the language, or even the name.

Then came the food: "But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank" (Daniel 1:8). Defile is a Law word. Yahuah's Law was given "to make a difference between the unclean and the clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten" (Leviticus 11:47). Daniel let Babylon teach him. He would not let Babylon make him break the Law.

Yahushua prayed the same pattern over His followers: "I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world" (John 17:15-16).

"In the world but not of it" is usually taught as an attitude. Daniel shows where the line actually sits. A believer can live in Babylon, work in it, and speak its language. The line is the commandment, and where the world asks for that, the answer is no.
