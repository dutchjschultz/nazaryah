Jude writes to "them that are sanctified by God the Father, and preserved in Yahushua Messiah, and called" (Jude 1:1). He greets them with "Mercy unto you, and peace, and love, be multiplied" (Jude 1:2). In his first line he names God, and he names Him the Father. The Son stands beside Him as the One in whom the called are kept. Many were taught that "God" means three persons in one. Jude's greeting knows one God, and He is the Father.

Moses gave Israel the foundation: "Hear, O Israel: Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 6:4). Malachi tied the one God to the Father: "Have we not all one father? hath not one God created us?" (Malachi 2:10).

Yahushua prayed to the Father and called Him "the only true God," then named Himself as the one "whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3).

Paul wrote in the same order as Jude: "to us there is but one God, the Father ... and one Master Yahushua Messiah" (1 Corinthians 8:6). God is the Father. The Son is named next to Him, not inside Him.

The usual escape is that if there is "God the Father," there must also be "God the Son." That phrase is not found in Scripture. Where the apostles write "God the Father," the Son is set beside Him by name, as in "peace from God the Father, and from our Master Yahushua Messiah" (Galatians 1:3).

The conclusion is plain. God is the Father. The Son is the one He sent, and in Him the called are kept. Heaven says the same, where Yahushua made His people "kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6).
