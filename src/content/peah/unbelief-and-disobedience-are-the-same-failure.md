The KJV reads "believed not." The Greek word is apeitheo (G544), which means to refuse to be persuaded, to disobey. Many translations render it "disobeyed." The next verse uses apistia, unbelief, and Hebrews 4:6 and 4:11 return to apeitheia (G543), disobedience. The writer moves back and forth between the two words as if they were one thing. To him they were. The man who will not obey has shown he does not believe.

The wilderness generation proves it. Yahuah asked, "How long will it be ere they believe me?" (Numbers 14:11), and then said they "have not hearkened to my voice" (Numbers 14:22). Same men, same failure, two names for it. Modern teaching splits belief from obedience and calls obedience optional. Hebrews will not allow the split. Belief opens the door, and obedience is the evidence that the belief is real. That is why Hebrews 3:6 and 3:14 both carry an "if": if we hold fast to the end.
