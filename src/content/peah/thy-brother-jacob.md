"For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever" (Obadiah 1:10). Edom was cut off. Jacob was not. Yahuah still guarded Jacob long after Israel's failures, which answers the teaching that the church replaced Israel.

The foundation is the blessing Isaac spoke over Jacob in place of Esau: "cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee" (Genesis 27:29). Esau "lifted up his voice, and wept" (Genesis 27:38), but the blessing stood. Yahuah confirmed it: "I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau" (Malachi 1:2-3).

Jeremiah set the limit on any replacement. Only if the sun, moon, and stars depart "then the seed of Israel also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever" (Jeremiah 31:36).

Yahushua knew who He was sent to: "I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matthew 15:24).

Paul asked the question outright: "Hath Yahuah cast away his people? God forbid" (Romans 11:1). He added, "the gifts and calling of God are without repentance" (Romans 11:29).

The usual escape is that the church is now Israel, so the promises passed to a new people. Paul says the nations were not a replacement. They were grafted "in among them" (Romans 11:17), brought near to "the commonwealth of Israel" (Ephesians 2:12-13).

The conclusion is plain. Yahuah has not traded Jacob for another people. The nations are added to Israel, and those who strike Jacob answer to Yahuah. The holy city bears the proof on its gates, the names of "the twelve tribes of the children of Israel" (Revelation 21:12).
