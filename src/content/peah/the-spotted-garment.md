Jude says, "of some have compassion, making a difference: And others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh" (Jude 1:22-23). Mercy goes to the man. Hatred goes to the stain. Jude draws the picture straight from the Torah.

The Torah taught this picture. A garment with spreading leprosy was not kept: "he shall therefore burn that garment ... for it is a fretting leprosy; it shall be burnt in the fire" (Leviticus 13:52).

Zechariah saw the same picture at a rescue. The high priest was "a brand plucked out of the fire," yet he stood "clothed with filthy garments." The command came, "Take away the filthy garments from him," and he was clothed anew (Zechariah 3:2-4).

Yahushua told of a guest at the wedding "which had not on a wedding garment," and he was cast out (Matthew 22:11-13).

James gave the daily rule: "to keep himself unspotted from the world" (James 1:27).

This verse is often read as if love means accepting the stain with the sinner, and the common teaching holds that the laws of clean and unclean have nothing left to say. Jude uses those very laws to teach the assembly how to rescue. The man is pulled out. The garment is left behind.

The conclusion is plain. Pull the sinner out of the fire, and hate what spotted him. Yahushua said of the faithful few in Sardis that they "have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white" (Revelation 3:4).
