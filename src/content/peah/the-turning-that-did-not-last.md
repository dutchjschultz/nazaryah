Nahum ends on a hard word: "There is no healing of thy bruise; thy wound is grievous" (Nahum 3:19). This was the same city that once repented under Jonah. What happened between Jonah and Nahum speaks to the common teaching that deliverance, once begun, can never be lost.

When Jonah preached, Yahuah "saw their works, that they turned from their evil way," and He spared them (Jonah 3:10). By Nahum's day the city had gone back to being "the bloody city... full of lies and robbery" (Nahum 3:1). The sparing was tied to the turning. When the turning stopped, the sparing stopped.

The prophets apply this to a single person, not only to a city. "When the righteous turneth away from his righteousness, and committeth iniquity... All his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned" (Ezekiel 18:24).

Yahushua said, "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). Hebrews warns, "if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins" (Hebrews 10:26).

Someone may say Nineveh was a pagan city, not a believer. But Peter speaks of people who had truly escaped the world's pollutions and then went back: "the latter end is worse with them than the beginning" (2 Peter 2:20).

A turning that is not walked in does not hold. Entry is by the blood, and the walk must follow. In Revelation, Yahushua tells the believing assembly at Ephesus, "repent, and do the first works; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick" (Revelation 2:5).
