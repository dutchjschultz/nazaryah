Nahum foretells, "The gates of the rivers shall be opened, and the palace shall be dissolved" (Nahum 2:6). He had already named the means: "with an overrunning flood he will make an utter end" (Nahum 1:8). The prophet named the river and the palace before either gave way.

Yahuah tells beforehand on purpose. He is "Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand" (Isaiah 46:10).

Yahushua gave the same reason for His own warnings: "now I have told you before it come to pass, that, when it is come to pass, ye might believe" (John 14:29).

Peter called the written word surer than what he saw on the mount: "We have also a more sure word of prophecy" (2 Peter 1:19).

The usual doubt is that prophecy is vague poetry, made to fit afterward. Nahum named a city, a flood, river gates, and a palace. Words that specific were written to be checked, and they were fulfilled.

The conclusion is plain. The word that foretold Nineveh's fall can be trusted for what it says is still ahead. Revelation closes on the same promise: "These sayings are faithful and true" (Revelation 22:6).
