Paul sets a hard test: "though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed" (Galatians 1:8). Then he says it again (Galatians 1:9). Not even an angel's rank makes a message true. More than one movement has been built on a heavenly visitor bringing a new word. Paul shuts that door before it opens.

The Torah gave the test long before. If a prophet gives "a sign or a wonder, And the sign or the wonder come to pass," yet leads Israel after other gods, "Thou shalt not hearken" (Deuteronomy 13:1-3). Isaiah gave the measure: "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them" (Isaiah 8:20).

Yahushua warned of the same: "there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders" (Matthew 24:24). Signs do not prove a message.

Paul explained why an angel's word cannot be trusted on sight: "Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14).

The conclusion is plain. A message is tested by the written word, not by the one who brings it. Revelation seals the same rule: "If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book" (Revelation 22:18).
