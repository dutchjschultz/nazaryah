Nahum warns Nineveh, "He that dasheth in pieces is come up before thy face" (Nahum 2:1). In the middle of Nineveh's fall, Nahum gives Israel a promise. The KJV reads, "Yahuah hath turned away the excellency of Jacob, as the excellency of Israel" (Nahum 2:2). The Hebrew verb is shuv, to return or bring back. In context, Yahuah is restoring Jacob's glory at the very moment Nineveh falls. Many have heard that the church replaced Israel. Nahum shows Yahuah bringing Israel back.

The prophets said Israel could not be set aside. Yahuah tied Israel's future to the sun and moon: "If those ordinances depart from before me... then the seed of Israel also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever" (Jeremiah 31:36). The sun still rises. Amos heard the same promise: "I will bring again the captivity of my people of Israel" (Amos 9:14).

Yahushua said, "I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matthew 15:24). He promised the twelve they would sit "judging the twelve tribes of Israel" (Matthew 19:28).

Paul asked the question directly: "Hath God cast away his people? God forbid" (Romans 11:1). Then he answered it: "all Israel shall be saved" (Romans 11:26).

Some say "Israel" now simply means the church. But Paul keeps the tree as Israel's. The Gentile is a wild branch "graffed in among them" (Romans 11:17). Even the unbelieving branches remain "beloved for the fathers' sakes" (Romans 11:28). A tree with new branches grafted in is still the same tree.

Israel is restored, and the nations come in with her. They do not replace her. The final city carries "the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel" on its gates (Revelation 21:12).
