Paul writes, "I marvel that ye are so soon removed from him that called you into the grace of Messiah unto another gospel: Which is not another" (Galatians 1:6-7). Many read "another gospel" as Torah-keeping. Galatians itself says what the other gospel was. Men were teaching that circumcision was the way to be made righteous.

Paul names it in the next chapter: "a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Yahushua Messiah" (Galatians 2:16). Later he is plainer still: "whosoever of you are justified by the law; ye are fallen from grace" (Galatians 5:4). The error was seeking righteousness by circumcision, not walking in the commandments after deliverance.

The Torah shows the true order. Abraham "believed in Yahuah; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). That came years before his circumcision in Genesis 17.

Yahushua gave the same order: "This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent" (John 6:29).

The usual escape is that if belief saves, the Law is against the gospel. Paul answers it in this same letter: "Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid" (Galatians 3:21).

The conclusion is plain. The other gospel was a false door, righteousness by circumcision. Belief is the door, and the Law is the walk after it. Revelation calls the true message "the everlasting gospel," and its first word is "Fear God, and give glory to him" (Revelation 14:6-7).
