Paul tells Philemon, "I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers" (Philemon 4). He prays for a man who already believes. He asks that "the communication of thy faith may become effectual" (Philemon 6). Many have been taught that belief is a single decision, finished the moment a person accepts the Messiah. Paul's prayer shows belief still growing.

Philemon's belief was real. Paul had already heard of his "love and faith" (Philemon 5). It was already refreshing others: "the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother" (Philemon 7). Yet Paul prays for that belief to become something it was not yet: effectual, working, producing. Belief that must still become effectual is belief still in motion. Entry was settled; the walk was not finished.

Paul speaks this way to every assembly. He thanks Yahuah for the Thessalonians' "work of faith" (1 Thessalonians 1:3). He says what counts is "faith which worketh by love" (Galatians 5:6). He tells the Philippians, who already believed, to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (Philippians 2:12).

James says the same of Abraham: "faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect" (James 2:22). Abraham believed first. His belief was then made complete by what it did.

Belief is the door, and it is meant to go on working long after the door is passed. A belief that never becomes effectual has stopped short of what Paul prayed for. The Master's words to Thyatira show what He looks for: "the last to be more than the first" (Revelation 2:19).
