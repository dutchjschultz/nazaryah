Nahum tells Nineveh, "All thy strong holds shall be like fig trees with the firstripe figs: if they be shaken, they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater" (Nahum 3:12). "The gates of thy land shall be set wide open unto thine enemies: the fire shall devour thy bars" (Nahum 3:13). The walls that seemed so strong would drop at one shake. Nahum had already named the only strong hold that holds: "Yahuah is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble" (Nahum 1:7).

The Psalms say it plainly: "Except Yahuah keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain" (Psalm 127:1). Proverbs gives the true refuge: "The name of Yahuah is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe" (Proverbs 18:10).

Yahushua told of two houses. The one built on sand looked fine until "the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it" (Matthew 7:27). The house on the rock was the man who "heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them" (Matthew 7:24).

Paul named the believer's walls: "the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds" (2 Corinthians 10:4).

The conclusion is plain. Every wall but Yahuah falls when it is shaken. Revelation uses Nahum's very picture for the last shaking: the stars fall "even as a fig tree casteth her untimely figs, when she is shaken of a mighty wind" (Revelation 6:13).
