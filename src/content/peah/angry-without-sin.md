Paul gives a short rule for a hard feeling: "Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath" (Ephesians 4:26). Many have been taught that anger itself is sin. Paul separates the two. Anger is one thing, and sin is another.

Paul is drawing on Psalm 4:4, which the Greek copies of the Psalms read as "Be ye angry, and sin not." The thought comes from the Hebrew Scriptures.

Yahushua Himself was angry without sinning. In the synagogue "he had looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts" (Mark 3:5). Yahuah is angry too: "God is angry with the wicked every day" (Psalm 7:11). If anger were sin in itself, neither the Father nor the Son could have it.

What turns anger into sin is carrying it. The Torah says, "Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart... Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge" (Leviticus 19:17-18). That is why Paul sets the limit at the end of the day. When the light goes at dusk, the anger goes with it. The next verse gives the reason: "Neither give place to the devil" (Ephesians 4:27). Anger kept overnight becomes a grudge, and a grudge gives the enemy room to work.

Righteous anger is real, and it has a time limit. In Revelation the wrath of Yahuah is never called sin: "true and righteous are thy judgments" (Revelation 16:7).
