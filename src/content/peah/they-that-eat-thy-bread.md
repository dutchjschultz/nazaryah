Edom's allies turned on it: "they that eat thy bread have laid a wound under thee" (Obadiah 1:7). The men at Edom's table were the ones who struck it. The betrayal of Yahushua is often told as a single event with no pattern behind it. Obadiah shows the pattern was already written.

David lived the same wound. "Mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me" (Psalm 41:9). His own counsellor, Ahithophel, joined Absalom's revolt (2 Samuel 15:31).

Yahushua took that psalm as His own. At the table He said, "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" (John 13:18).

Micah warned that it would come into every house: "Trust ye not in a friend," for "a man's enemies are the men of his own house" (Micah 7:5-6). Yahushua quoted Micah and applied it to His followers: "a man's foes shall be they of his own household" (Matthew 10:36).

The usual escape is that this ended with Judas. Paul said otherwise. He told the elders of Ephesus, "of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things" (Acts 20:30).

The conclusion is plain. Shared bread does not make a man faithful. The believer's safety is in Yahuah, not in the company at the table. Micah closed his warning, "I will look unto Yahuah" (Micah 7:7). The assembly at Thyatira learned the same, with a false teacher allowed inside (Revelation 2:20).
