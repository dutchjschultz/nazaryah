Nahum describes Yahuah's power: "He rebuketh the sea, and maketh it dry, and drieth up all the rivers" (Nahum 1:4). "The mountains quake at him, and the hills melt" (Nahum 1:5). These are the marks of the God of the Exodus. He dried the sea for Israel, and He shook Sinai when He gave the Law.

The Psalm names the first: "He rebuked the Red sea also, and it was dried up" (Psalm 106:9). The Torah names the second: "the whole mount quaked greatly" (Exodus 19:18). The God who judges Nineveh is the God who spoke the commandments.

Yahushua also "rebuked the winds and the sea; and there was a great calm" (Matthew 8:26). Many point to this as proof that the Son is Yahuah. Yahushua explained His own works: "The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do" (John 5:19). He said, "the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works" (John 14:10).

The crowds understood it rightly at another miracle. They "glorified God, which had given such power unto men" (Matthew 9:8). Peter preached the same: Yahushua was "a man approved of God among you by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him" (Acts 2:22).

The conclusion is plain. The sea obeys Yahuah alone. When it obeyed the Son, it was the Father's power working through the one He sent. Revelation shows the same power at the end, when "the earth and the heaven fled away" from the face of Him who sat on the throne (Revelation 20:11).
