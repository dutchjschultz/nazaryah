Gethsemane means "oil press." It lay on the Mount of Olives, across the brook Kidron from Jerusalem, and Yahushua went there on the night He was betrayed (John 18:1). The road He walked that night had been walked once before.

David, driven out of Jerusalem by his own son, "passed over the brook Kidron" (2 Samuel 15:23). He "went up by the ascent of mount Olivet, and wept as he went up" (2 Samuel 15:30). On that slope he was told the worst news of all: "Ahithophel is among the conspirators" (2 Samuel 15:31). The betrayer was his own trusted counselor.

Yahushua named the pattern Himself. Speaking of Judas at the supper, He quoted David: "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" (John 13:18; Psalm 41:9).

The two betrayers ended the same way. Ahithophel "put his household in order, and hanged himself" (2 Samuel 17:23). Judas "went and hanged himself" (Matthew 27:5).

Gethsemane was not a new scene. The King was rejected, crossed the Kidron, climbed the Mount of Olives in sorrow, and was betrayed by a man who ate at his table. David walked it as the shadow, and the Son of David walked it as the substance.
