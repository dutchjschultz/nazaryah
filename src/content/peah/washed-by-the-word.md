Paul gives a tender picture of Messiah caring for His bride. He cleanses her "with the washing of water by the word" (Ephesians 5:26). The question is which word. Many were taught that the Old Testament Law was nailed to the cross, but Scripture puts the Law in that water.

Follow the chain. Yahushua prayed, "Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth" (John 17:17). The Psalm says, "thy law is the truth" (Psalm 119:142). If the word is truth, and the Law is truth, then the Law is part of the word that cleanses. The same Psalm describes the washing: "Wherewith shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word" (Psalm 119:9).

When Paul wrote, the Scriptures in the assemblies' hands were the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms. He told Timothy that "from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation" (2 Timothy 3:15). Those were the Old Testament.

The picture comes from the tabernacle. Before the priests could serve, "they shall wash with water, that they die not" (Exodus 30:20). Yahushua told His disciples, "Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you" (John 15:3). The water of the laver points to the word.

The word many have set aside is the very water Messiah uses to wash His bride. In Revelation she stands ready, "arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints" (Revelation 19:8).
