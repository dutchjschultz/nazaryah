Jude recalls the apostles' warning of "mockers in the last time, who should walk after their own ungodly lusts," men "having not the Spirit" (Jude 1:17-19). Then he turns: "But ye, beloved ... Keep yourselves in the love of God, looking for the mercy of our Master Yahushua Messiah unto eternal life" (Jude 1:20-21). The mockers follow their lusts. The beloved keep themselves, and the mercy is still ahead. Many were taught that deliverance was finished the moment a person believed, with nothing left to keep and nothing left to wait for.

The Torah showed whom Yahuah's love holds. He "keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments" (Deuteronomy 7:9).

Yahushua said how a man stays in that love: "If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love" (John 15:10). John gave the meaning of Jude's phrase: "this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments" (1 John 5:3).

Paul prayed for Onesiphorus "that he may find mercy of the Lord in that day" (2 Timothy 1:18). The mercy waits at the end of the walk.

The usual escape is that Jude goes on to say Yahuah "is able to keep you from falling" (Jude 1:24), so the keeping is His alone. Paul holds both together: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you" (Philippians 2:12-13). Yahuah keeps those who keep themselves in His love.

The conclusion is plain. The blood opens the door, and the walk in His commandments keeps the believer in His love until the mercy comes. Heaven names the ones who endure: "here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12).
