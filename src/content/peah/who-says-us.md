Obadiah opens, "Thus saith Adonai Yahuah concerning Edom," then reports a rumour from Yahuah and an ambassador sent among the nations: "Arise ye, and let us rise up against her in battle" (Obadiah 1:1). The "us" is the nations called to war. One speaker sends the word, and many answer it. This is how plural speech works in the prophets, and it answers the claim that every divine "us" means more than one person in Yahuah.

Jeremiah tells the same event and settles who is speaking. "I have heard a rumour from Yahuah," and the ambassador tells the nations, "Gather ye together, and come against her" (Jeremiah 49:14). The plural belongs to the messengers and the armies, not to Yahuah.

At the throne Yahuah asks, "who will go for us?" (Isaiah 6:8) while seraphim stand around Him. Micaiah saw Yahuah on His throne with "all the host of heaven standing by him," and asked them, "Who shall persuade Ahab?" (1 Kings 22:19-20).

The usual escape is Genesis 1:26, "Let us make man." Yahuah answers it Himself: "I am Yahuah that maketh all things; that stretcheth forth the heavens alone; that spreadeth abroad the earth by myself" (Isaiah 44:24). Alone means no partner.

Yahushua quoted the Shema as the first commandment: "Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" (Mark 12:29). Paul said the same: "to us there is but one God, the Father" (1 Corinthians 8:6).

The conclusion is plain. "Us" in Scripture marks Yahuah with His servants, never Yahuah divided into persons. John saw the same pattern at the end: "one sat on the throne," with the elders round about (Revelation 4:2-4).
