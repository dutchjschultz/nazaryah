Yahuah asks Nineveh a pointed question: "Art thou better than populous No, that was situate among the rivers?" (Nahum 3:8). No-Amon was Thebes in Egypt, and Assyria itself had sacked it. Thebes had "Ethiopia and Egypt" for her strength, "yet was she carried away" (Nahum 3:9-10). Yahuah points Nineveh to history it had written with its own sword. Old Testament history is often treated as background with nothing to say to believers, but Yahuah uses it as a lesson.

This is how Yahuah has always taught. The Torah says, "Remember the days of old, consider the years of many generations" (Deuteronomy 32:7). History is meant to be weighed.

Yahushua taught the same way. He said, "Remember Lot's wife" (Luke 17:32). He even used Nineveh itself as a witness: "The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation" (Matthew 12:41).

The apostles did not set the record aside. Paul wrote, "whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning" (Romans 15:4). Peter said Yahuah made Sodom and Gomorrha "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Peter 2:6).

Some say those warnings were for pagan nations, not for believers. But Paul used delivered Israel in the wilderness as his warning to the assembly: "they are written for our admonition." Then he added, "let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall" (1 Corinthians 10:11-12).

The record of the Old Testament is evidence, and Yahuah expects His people to read it. Nineveh thought it was too strong to fall, and it fell like Thebes. Revelation's harlot says the same thing in her heart: "I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow" (Revelation 18:7).
