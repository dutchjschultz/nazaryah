Scripture sets down a plain rule in two places: a day can stand for a year. When the spies searched the land of Canaan for forty days and came back with an evil report, Yahuah answered that Israel would bear their iniquities "after the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year" (Numbers 14:34). Forty days of unbelief became forty years in the wilderness. Later, Ezekiel was told to lie on his left side 390 days for the house of Israel and on his right side 40 days for Judah, and Yahuah said, "I have appointed thee each day for a year" (Ezekiel 4:5–6). Yahuah Himself states the rule, and He states it twice.

The pattern shows up long before either passage. Jacob served Laban seven years for Rachel, was handed Leah instead, and was told, "Fulfil her week, and we will give thee this also for the service which thou shalt serve with me yet seven other years" (Genesis 29:27). The "week" (shabua, H7620) was seven years of work. Torah writes the same pattern into the land itself: "thou shalt number seven sabbaths of years unto thee, seven times seven years" (Leviticus 25:8). Weeks of days and weeks of years run on the same count.

The Hebrew language carries it too. The word for day is yom (H3117), and its plural, yamim, is used again and again where the KJV translators wrote "year." In Exodus 13:10 and 1 Samuel 1:3, "from year to year" and "yearly" are literally "from days to days." In Leviticus 25:29, "a full year" is literally "days." In Judges 17:10, "by the year" is literally "for the days." In 1 Samuel 27:7, David dwelt among the Philistines "a full year and four months," literally "days and four months." To a Hebrew reader, a set of days and a year sat side by side.

This is the ground under every prophecy that counts days as years. The rule is not borrowed from commentators; Yahuah wrote it into Numbers and Ezekiel and wove it through the language. One line must be kept separate from it. "For a thousand years in thy sight are but as yesterday" (Psalm 90:4), repeated in 2 Peter 3:8, is not a conversion rule. It sets Yahuah's time beside man's time and shows He is not bound by man's clock. The day-for-a-year rule counts time on man's calendar; the thousand-years line shows the One who stands above it.
