Paul could command, yet "for love's sake I rather beseech thee," and he pleads "for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds" (Philemon 8-10). Onesimus has one of the quiet great stories in Scripture. His name means profitable. Yet he had been a runaway slave who lived the opposite of his name. Paul says he "in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me" (Philemon 11). The begetting came first, and the change followed.

Some have been told that a person can be truly born again and show no change, a "carnal Christian" whose life stays the same. Scripture expects the change every time. "If any man be in Messiah, he is a new creature: old things are passed away" (2 Corinthians 5:17). John the Baptist told the crowds, "Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance" (Luke 3:8). Yahushua gave the test: "by their fruits ye shall know them" (Matthew 7:20).

Paul offers the change in Onesimus as proof that the begetting was real, and he sends him home to show it. The man who once took from his master now returns to serve him.

The new birth did not leave the man where it found him. It made him what his name had always said. A begetting that produces nothing has nothing to show. The Master says He comes with His reward "to give every man according as his work shall be" (Revelation 22:12).
