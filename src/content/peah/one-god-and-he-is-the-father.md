Paul lists the "ones" of the faith: "One Master, one faith, one baptism" (Ephesians 4:5), and then "One God and Father of all, who is above all" (Ephesians 4:6). The one Master and the one God are two separate entries, and Paul says who the one God is: the Father. Most were taught that the one God is three persons. Paul's list does not read that way.

This is the oldest confession in Scripture: "Hear, O Israel: Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 6:4). Yahushua quoted it as the first commandment (Mark 12:29). When the scribe answered, "there is one God; and there is none other but he," Yahushua told him, "Thou art not far from the kingdom of God" (Mark 12:32, 34). He did not correct him or add two more persons.

Paul counts the same way in his other letters: "to us there is but one God, the Father... and one Master Yahushua Messiah" (1 Corinthians 8:6). And again: "there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). A mediator stands between two parties and cannot be one of them: "a mediator is not a mediator of one, but God is one" (Galatians 3:20).

Every time the apostles count, the count comes out the same: one God, the Father, and one Master, His Son. Revelation opens on the same note: "The Revelation of Yahushua Messiah, which God gave unto him" (Revelation 1:1). God gives, and the Son receives.
