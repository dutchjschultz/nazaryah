Nahum shows two sides of Yahuah in one verse. He is "slow to anger," and yet He "will not at all acquit the wicked" (Nahum 1:3). A common thought today is that because God is love, He simply overlooks sin. Nahum does not leave room for it.

Nahum did not invent this. It is the name Yahuah proclaimed to Moses: "merciful and gracious, longsuffering," and yet He "will by no means clear the guilty" (Exodus 34:6-7).

That leaves a problem, because "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). If Yahuah will not clear the guilty, how is anyone cleared?

The Torah gives the answer in a shadow: "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Leviticus 17:11). Abraham saw who would supply it: "God will provide himself a lamb" (Genesis 22:8).

Paul shows the shadow fulfilled. Yahuah set forth His Son "to be a propitiation through faith in his blood," and Paul names Nahum's patience in the same place: "the forbearance of God." The purpose is that Yahuah "might be just, and the justifier" (Romans 3:25-26). He stays just, and He still clears the one who believes.

Some take His patience to mean He will let sin go. Peter says what the patience is for: He is "longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9). Slowness to anger is time to repent. It is not a pardon.

Yahuah does not acquit by looking away. He acquits by the blood of the Lamb He provided. Revelation sings it: "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood" (Revelation 5:9).
