Demas is named three times in Paul's letters, and the three lines tell a story.

First, in the closing greetings beside Epaphras, "my fellowprisoner," Paul lists him among "my fellowlabourers" (Philemon 23-24). He stands in the same line as Mark and Luke.

Second, in the letter written at the same time, Demas still stands with Paul, sending greeting to the saints: "Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you" (Colossians 4:14).

Third, at the end of Paul's life: "Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world" (2 Timothy 4:10).

A man can be counted a true worker in the gospel and still walk away before the end. Many are taught that a person saved once can never fall away. Demas shows that entry was real, but finishing was not guaranteed.

That is why Yahushua said, "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13). He also warned, "No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God" (Luke 9:62). Hebrews puts a condition on belonging: "we are made partakers of Messiah, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end" (Hebrews 3:14).

Paul closes, "The grace of our Master Yahushua Messiah be with your spirit" (Philemon 25). Favor is given, and it must be held. Deliverance has a beginning and a finish, and Demas had the first without the second. The warning to the assemblies says the same: "hold that fast which thou hast, that no man take thy crown" (Revelation 3:11).
