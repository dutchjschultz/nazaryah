Nahum tells Nineveh, "Thou hast multiplied thy merchants above the stars of heaven: the cankerworm spoileth, and fleeth away" (Nahum 3:16). Its rulers were many, "Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers" (Nahum 3:17), and like locusts they lifted and were gone. The trade looked endless. It vanished in a season.

Ezekiel said the same of Tyre: "by thy traffick hast thou increased thy riches, and thine heart is lifted up because of thy riches" (Ezekiel 28:5). Proverbs gives the rule: "riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven" (Proverbs 23:5).

Yahushua said where not to build: "Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal" (Matthew 6:19).

James spoke to the planners of trade: "ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city ... and buy and sell, and get gain: Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour" (James 4:13-14).

The common thought is that a strong market makes a nation safe. Nineveh had merchants beyond counting. They flew off like locusts when the city fell.

The conclusion is plain. Trade cannot hold up a nation that has left Yahuah. Revelation shows the same end for Babylon's traders: "the merchants of the earth shall weep and mourn over her; for no man buyeth their merchandise any more" (Revelation 18:11).
