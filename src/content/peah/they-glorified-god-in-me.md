Paul went into "Syria and Cilicia," and he was "unknown by face unto the churches of Judaea which were in Messiah." They had only heard, "he which persecuted us in times past now preacheth the faith which once he destroyed. And they glorified God in me" (Galatians 1:21-24). The assemblies in Judea heard the same belief they held. They did not hear a new one.

Who were those assemblies? James told Paul, "Thou seest, brother, how many thousands of Jews there are which believe; and they are all zealous of the law" (Acts 21:20). Believers zealous for the Law glorified Yahuah for Paul's preaching. That sits hard against the teaching that Paul preached against the Law.

Isaiah foretold that Yahuah's people would be "the work of my hands, that I may be glorified" (Isaiah 60:21).

Yahushua said how this works: "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 5:16). The glory went to Yahuah, not to Paul.

Paul told Timothy why his story mattered. He was "before a blasphemer, and a persecutor," and he was shown mercy "for a pattern to them which should hereafter believe" (1 Timothy 1:13, 16).

The conclusion is plain. A changed life gives glory to Yahuah, and the Torah-keeping assemblies of Judea gave it gladly. Revelation asks the same: "Who shall not fear thee, O Yahuah, and glorify thy name?" (Revelation 15:4).
