Paul greets the assemblies from "God the Father, and from our Master Yahushua Messiah, Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world, according to the will of God and our Father" (Galatians 1:3-4). The glory goes to the Father, "To whom be glory for ever and ever" (Galatians 1:5). Galatians is often called the letter of freedom from the Law. Its opening says what believers are freed from: their sins and this evil age.

The prophets named the same rescue: "he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities" (Psalm 130:8). Ezekiel showed what follows it: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:27). The rescue is from sin, and the walk is in the statutes.

Yahushua prayed for the same thing: "I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil" (John 17:15).

Paul says it again to Titus. Yahushua "gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity" (Titus 2:14). The word is anomia, lawlessness. He redeems from lawlessness, not from Law.

The usual escape is that Messiah "redeemed us from the curse of the law" (Galatians 3:13). The curse is what He took away. The Torah names it: "Cursed be he that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them" (Deuteronomy 27:26).

The conclusion is plain. The Son gave Himself, by the Father's will, to free His people from sin and from this evil age. Revelation sings it: He "washed us from our sins in his own blood" (Revelation 1:5).
