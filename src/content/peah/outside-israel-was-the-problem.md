Paul reminds the Gentiles where they once stood: "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise" (Ephesians 2:12).

Look at what made them lost. They were outside Israel. If being outside Israel is the sickness, then the cure is being brought into Israel, not into some other body. That matters, because many have been taught that Yahuah set Israel aside and started a new people called the church. Paul gives the cure seven verses later: "ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:19).

This was never new. The Torah made room for the stranger who joins Israel: "One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you" (Exodus 12:49). Isaiah spoke of "the sons of the stranger, that join themselves to Yahuah," who keep the Sabbath and take hold of His covenant (Isaiah 56:6).

Paul tells it the same way in Romans. The Gentile is a wild branch "graffed in among them" (Romans 11:17), grafted into Israel's tree, not planted as a new tree beside it. Even the new covenant is made "with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah" (Jeremiah 31:31). No covenant in Scripture is made with a church apart from Israel.

The Gentile believer has not replaced Israel. He has been brought into it. The city at the end has "the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel" on its gates (Revelation 21:12). Everyone who enters goes in through Israel's gates.
