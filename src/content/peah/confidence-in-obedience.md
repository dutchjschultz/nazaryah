Paul writes to a man already delivered: "Having confidence in thy obedience I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say" (Philemon 21). Paul does not fear Philemon's obedience. He counts on it, and he expects him to go beyond the request.

Many have learned to treat obedience as a danger to favor, as if any talk of keeping commands slides into legalism. The order answers that. Obedience is not where a believer begins; the blood settles entry. Obedience is what the delivered man does next. Yahushua put it plainly: "If ye love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15). John adds, "this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous" (1 John 5:3).

Obedience was never a rival to belief. Samuel told a king who offered sacrifice in place of obeying, "to obey is better than sacrifice" (1 Samuel 15:22). Peter told the council that Yahuah gives His holy spirit "to them that obey him" (Acts 5:32). The spirit is not given to replace obedience. It is given to the obedient.

Obedience after deliverance is not a threat to favor. It is the fruit favor was looking for. The saints at the end are known by the same two marks: they "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12).
