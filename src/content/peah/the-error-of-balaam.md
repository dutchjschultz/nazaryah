Jude says these men "ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward" (Jude 1:11). Balaam was hired to curse Israel and could not. "How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?" (Numbers 23:8). So he found another way. He taught Israel to sin, and the sin brought the harm the curse could not.

Jude paints such teachers plainly. They are "spots in your feasts of charity," "clouds they are without water," and "wandering stars, to whom is reserved the blackness of darkness for ever" (Jude 1:12-13). They look like rain and bring none.

Moses named the cause. "These caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against Yahuah in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of Yahuah" (Numbers 31:16). Balaam never touched Israel. His teaching did.

Yahushua holds the teacher to account for what he teaches others to break: "Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:19).

The common teaching today is that the commandments ended at the cross. But the error of Balaam was never an open attack on Yahuah's people. It was a teaching that walked them out from under His Law.

The conclusion is plain. A teacher who leads Yahuah's people away from His commandments is doing Balaam's work, whatever he is paid. Yahushua rebuked Pergamos for holding "the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel" (Revelation 2:14).
