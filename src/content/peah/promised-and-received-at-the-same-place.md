When Abram first came into the land, he stopped at Shechem, at the plain of Moreh. There Yahuah appeared to him and said, "Unto thy seed will I give this land" (Genesis 12:7). The seed and the land were promised together, in one sentence, at one spot. Abram built an altar there.

Moses later told Israel where they would stand once they crossed the Jordan: Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, "beside the plains of Moreh" (Deuteronomy 11:29-30). That is the same place. More than four hundred years after the promise, Yahushua the son of Nun led Abram's seed to that spot. He built an altar of whole stones, wrote a copy of the Law upon the stones, and read every word of it before all Israel (Joshua 8:30-35).

The seed Yahuah promised stood on the land Yahuah promised, at the very place the promise was spoken. Abram received the word at Shechem. His children received the land at Shechem, and the first thing they did there was raise an altar and read the Law. Yahuah keeps His word down to the ground it was spoken on, and He will keep the last of it the same way, when the promised inheritance is finished in a new earth and He dwells with His people (Revelation 21:3, 7).
