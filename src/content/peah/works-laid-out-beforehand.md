Ephesians 2:8-9 is well loved: deliverance comes by favor through belief, "not of works." The very next verse finishes the thought. Believers are "created in Messiah Yahushua unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:10). Many stop at verse 9, as though the commandments no longer matter once a person believes.

The order matters. Works do not earn deliverance. That comes by favor through belief (Ephesians 2:8). But a person is delivered for a purpose, and the purpose is a walk. Yahushua gave Himself to "purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" (Titus 2:14).

Which works? Paul says they were "before ordained," laid out ahead of time. Yahuah had already said, "Ye shall walk in all the ways which Yahuah your God hath commanded you" (Deuteronomy 5:33). He also promised what the new covenant would do: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:27). The Spirit does not lead a believer away from the statutes. The Spirit causes him to walk in them.

John gives the pattern: "He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked" (1 John 2:6). Yahushua walked in His Father's commandments.

Favor saves first, and then the believer walks in works Yahuah wrote down long before. Removing the commandments throws away the very works favor was given for. Revelation keeps the same order: "Blessed are they that do his commandments" (Revelation 22:14).
