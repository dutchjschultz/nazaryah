Paul asks, "do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Messiah" (Galatians 1:10). He gives only two choices. A teacher serves Messiah, or he pleases men. The common way today is to shape the message so it draws a crowd and offends no one. Paul says that road leaves the service of Messiah.

The Proverbs warn of it: "The fear of man bringeth a snare" (Proverbs 29:25). Isaiah heard the crowd ask for it: "Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things" (Isaiah 30:10).

Yahushua gave the warning to His own: "Woe unto you, when all men shall speak well of you! for so did their fathers to the false prophets" (Luke 6:26).

Paul said the same to the Thessalonians: "we speak; not as pleasing men, but God, which trieth our hearts" (1 Thessalonians 2:4). Peter told the council, "We ought to obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).

The usual escape is that Paul became "all things to all men" (1 Corinthians 9:22). He changed his manner to reach people, never the message. In the same passage he said he was "not without law to God" (1 Corinthians 9:21).

The conclusion is plain. A servant of Messiah speaks to please Yahuah, and lets men be pleased or not. Yahushua's word to Laodicea shows the other side of love: "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten" (Revelation 3:19).
