"Shall I not in that day, saith Yahuah, even destroy the wise men out of Edom, and understanding out of the mount of Esau?" (Obadiah 1:8). Edom was famous for its wise men. Many believers are taught they need trained scholars to explain Scripture to them. Obadiah shows what Yahuah thinks of borrowed wisdom.

Jeremiah mocked the same reputation: "Is wisdom no more in Teman? is counsel perished from the prudent?" (Jeremiah 49:7). Isaiah said it of Israel's own teachers: "the wisdom of their wise men shall perish" (Isaiah 29:14).

Paul quoted Isaiah to the assembly at Corinth: "I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and will bring to nothing the understanding of the prudent" (1 Corinthians 1:19).

Yahushua thanked the Father for it: "thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes" (Matthew 11:25). The Father, not the schools, gives understanding.

The usual escape is that this means pagan wisdom only, not the church's scholars. Yahushua said it about the scribes of Israel, men who held the Scriptures. They were "teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Matthew 15:9).

The Psalms give the better way. "I have more understanding than all my teachers: for thy testimonies are my meditation" (Psalm 119:99).

The conclusion is plain. Understanding comes from reading Yahuah's word, not from trusting the wise men who explain it. Revelation opens with a promise to that very reader: "Blessed is he that readeth" (Revelation 1:3).
