"In the day that thou stoodest on the other side, in the day that the strangers carried away captive his forces ... even thou wast as one of them" (Obadiah 1:11). Edom did not lead the attack on Jerusalem. It only stood and watched. Yahuah counted the watching as taking part. Many were taught that sin is only what a man does. Obadiah shows that standing aside is also counted.

The Law said it first. "Neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour" (Leviticus 19:16). Proverbs closes the excuse of not knowing: "If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it?" (Proverbs 24:12).

Yahushua told of a priest and a Levite who saw the wounded man and "passed by on the other side" (Luke 10:31-32). Edom's own words come back in the parable. Then He said, "Go, and do thou likewise" (Luke 10:37), pointing to the Samaritan who stopped.

He said the same about the judgment: "Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of the least of these, ye did it not to me" (Matthew 25:45). Those people were judged for what they left undone.

James puts it in one line. "Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin" (James 4:17).

The conclusion is plain. Standing on the other side is not neutral ground. Yahuah counts the watcher with the men who did the harm. The assembly at Laodicea heard the same charge, neither cold nor hot, and was told "I know thy works" (Revelation 3:15).
