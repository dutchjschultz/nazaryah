Yahuah asks, "Where is the dwelling of the lions ... where the lion, even the old lion, walked, and the lion's whelp, and none made them afraid?" (Nahum 2:11). Assyria lived like a lion: it "did tear in pieces enough for his whelps ... and filled his holes with prey" (Nahum 2:12). It ruled by tearing, and no one could stop it. Now the den is empty.

The Psalm gives the end of such power: "The young lions do lack, and suffer hunger: but they that seek Yahuah shall not want any good thing" (Psalm 34:10).

Yahushua told Peter the same rule: "all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword" (Matthew 26:52). Many are taught that Yahuah's kingdom moves forward by force and the power of rulers. Yahushua put the sword away.

Peter later named who walks like Nineveh's lion: "your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8). The devouring lion is the enemy's way.

The conclusion is plain. The kingdom that tears others falls empty. Yahuah's triumph came the other way. In Revelation, John is told to look at "the Lion of the tribe of Juda," and he sees "a Lamb as it had been slain" (Revelation 5:5-6).
