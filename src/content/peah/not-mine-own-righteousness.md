Paul names two kinds of righteousness, and they come from two different sources. One is his own, built by his own keeping. The other is Yahuah’s, received through trust in the Messiah.

If any man could build his own righteousness, Paul could. He was a Hebrew of the Hebrews, a Pharisee, and touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless (Philippians 3:5-6). Yet he counted that record as loss (Philippians 3:7). His keeping was real, and it still could not open the door.

The point is the source, not the law. Paul does not call the law evil; he says his own righteousness cannot deliver him. Entry belongs to Yahuah’s righteousness alone. The walk in His commandments comes after the door is opened, never as the key that opens it.
