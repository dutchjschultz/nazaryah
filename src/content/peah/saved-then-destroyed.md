Jude reminds his readers "how that the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed them that believed not" (Jude 1:5). The same people were saved and then destroyed. Yahuah brought them out under the blood of the lamb. Many were taught "once saved, always saved." Jude sets the whole wilderness generation against it.

Jude gives two more examples in the same breath. The angels "which kept not their first estate" are reserved "in everlasting chains under darkness unto the judgment" (Jude 1:6). Sodom and Gomorrha are "set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 1:7). Each was given a place, and each lost it.

The Torah records the verdict. The men "which have seen my glory, and my miracles ... and have not hearkened to my voice; Surely they shall not see the land" (Numbers 14:22-23).

Yahushua put the deliverance at the end of the walk: "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13).

Paul drew the warning from Israel's story: "let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall" (1 Corinthians 10:11-12).

The usual escape is that Israel only lost the land, not eternal life. Hebrews uses the same story for the believer's rest. "They could not enter in because of unbelief" (Hebrews 3:19). Then it turns to the reader: "Let us therefore fear, lest ... any of you should seem to come short of it" (Hebrews 4:1).

The conclusion is plain. Coming out of Egypt is the entry. Entering the land is the end of the walk. Yahushua's word to Smyrna says the same: "be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life" (Revelation 2:10).
