Nahum asks a question every reader should sit with: "Who can stand before his indignation? and who can abide in the fierceness of his anger?" (Nahum 1:6). Revelation asks it again, and the way it asks names two, not one. That is worth noticing, since many were taught that the Lamb is the second person of one God.

At the sixth seal, men hide "from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb" and cry, "who shall be able to stand?" (Revelation 6:16-17). The one on the throne and the Lamb are named separately.

The Psalms said it first. The kings rise up "against Yahuah, and against his anointed" (Psalm 2:2). The warning follows: "Kiss the Son, lest he be angry" (Psalm 2:12).

Yahushua explained where the Son's wrath comes from: "the Father judgeth no man, but hath committed all judgment unto the Son" (John 5:22). He said the Father "hath given him authority to execute judgment also" (John 5:27). Authority that is given comes from someone greater.

Some say the Lamb shares the throne, so He must be God. But Yahushua said He is "set down with my Father in his throne," and in the same verse He promises the overcomer a seat "with me in my throne" (Revelation 3:21). Sharing a throne does not make anyone God. The Lamb also takes the book "out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne" (Revelation 5:7).

Revelation answers Nahum's question. A great multitude "stood before the throne, and before the Lamb," because they "washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9, 14). Those who stand are those Yahuah washed by the blood He provided.
