## What Came West

Jalal al-Din Rumi was a Muslim teacher and poet who lived in what is now Turkey in the 1200s. He was a Sufi, a member of Islam's mystical branch, which seeks to lose the self in union with Allah through love, poetry, music, and dance. His followers founded the Mevlevi order, known in the West as the whirling dervishes.

In the late twentieth century Rumi became one of the best-selling poets in America. Most of that came through the English versions of Coleman Barks, beginning in the 1970s and peaking with *The Essential Rumi* in 1995. Barks does not read Persian; he reworked earlier scholars' translations into loose American verse, and in doing so most of the Islam was taken out. References to Allah, the Quran, and Muhammad faded into a general language of love and longing. Rumi quotes now appear on wedding invitations, greeting cards, and church social media pages, and most readers have no idea he was an Islamic teacher.

## The Whirling Dance

The Mevlevi ceremony, the *sema*, is a form of *dhikr*, the Sufi practice of remembering Allah through repetition. The dancers turn in circles for long periods, the right palm raised to heaven and the left turned toward the earth, seeking a trance in which the self is lost in Allah. Western workshops and "sacred dance" retreats now teach the turning as a spiritual exercise for anyone.

## What Scripture Says

The danger here is the kind that does not look dangerous. A poem about love, with its source hidden, passes into a church unnoticed. Paul warned that "Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14), and that even "an angel from heaven" bringing another message is to be refused (Galatians 1:8). A beautiful line does not become true because it is beautiful.

The Sufi goal is the opposite of Scripture's. Sufism seeks to dissolve the self into God. Scripture keeps the Creator and the creature apart: man is made "of the dust of the ground" (Genesis 2:7), and Yahuah says, "I am Yahuah, and there is none else" (Isaiah 45:5). The believer is not absorbed into Yahuah; he serves Him and keeps His commandments.

The turning trance is also a form of the repetition Yahushua condemned: "use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do" (Matthew 6:7).

## The Verdict

Rumi in American churches is Islamic mysticism with the label peeled off, and the whirling dance is a trance practice built to lose the self. Both are strange fire — something offered in worship that Yahuah never commanded (Leviticus 10:1). A believer can find every true thing about love in Scripture without borrowing it from a Sufi teacher.

Revelation shows the Son rebuking the assembly at Thyatira for allowing a teacher "to teach and to seduce my servants" (Revelation 2:20). A borrowed teacher with a hidden label is no new danger, and the Son does not overlook it.
