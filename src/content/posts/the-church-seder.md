## What Came Into the Church

Many American churches now hold a Passover Seder each spring, often led by a guest speaker or a Messianic ministry, sometimes under the title "Christ in the Passover." Church members sit at long tables with a printed booklet, the Haggadah, and walk through the evening step by step. Most leave believing they have kept Pesach the way Yahushua and Israel kept it.

## What the Seder Is

The Seder is the rabbinic order of service for the Passover night. Its parts include four cups of wine, parsley dipped in salt water, a roasted egg, a sweet paste called charoset, a hidden piece of matzah called the afikoman that children search for, and a cup set out for Elijah. None of these is commanded in the Torah. The order was written down by the rabbis after the Temple was destroyed in 70 AD, when the Passover lamb could no longer be offered, and the Haggadah grew from there over the following centuries.

The most telling detail is what the Seder lacks. A roasted shank bone sits on the plate as a reminder, but no lamb is eaten. The one thing the Torah requires is the one thing missing.

## What Torah Commands

The Torah's Pesach is short and plain. Israel was to eat the lamb "roast with fire, and unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it" (Exodus 12:8), and to eat it "with your loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand ... in haste" (Exodus 12:11). Lamb, unleavened bread, bitter herbs, readiness to leave. That is the feast.

Yahushua condemned exactly what the Seder does: "laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men" (Mark 7:8), "teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Mark 7:7).

## The Second Error

Churches that hold a Seder usually present it as a reenactment of the Last Supper. That adds a second error to the first. The Last Supper was not the Pesach meal. John places it "before the feast of the passover" (John 13:1), and the next morning the priests still would not enter Pilate's hall "lest they should be defiled; but that they might eat the passover" (John 18:28). Yahushua died at the hour the lambs were slain. He did not eat the Pesach lamb that year; He was the Lamb. As Paul wrote, "Messiah our passover is sacrificed for us" (1 Corinthians 5:7).

## The Verdict

The church Seder is a rabbinic service with no lamb, presented as Torah and as the Last Supper, when it is neither. It is strange fire — a tradition of men laid on Yahuah's altar. A believer who wants to keep Pesach keeps it from Exodus 12, not from the Haggadah, and remembers the Lamb that the Seder leaves off the table.

Revelation centers heaven's worship on that Lamb: "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain" (Revelation 5:12), and "Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9).
