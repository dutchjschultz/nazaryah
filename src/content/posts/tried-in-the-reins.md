Scripture does not place the inner man in the head. It places him in the body: the heart (*lev*), the bowels (*me'im*), the liver (*kabed*), and above all the kidneys (*kilyah*), which the King James Version calls the "reins." These are the parts Yahuah searches and the parts He claimed on His altar. Modern Bibles have quietly moved that inner man up into the "mind," an idea taken from Greek thinking, not from the Hebrew text.

## The Liver and the Life

Solomon describes the young man led away by the strange woman as one who goes on "till a dart strike through his liver," like a bird rushing into a snare, not knowing "it is for his life" (Proverbs 7:23). The two are set side by side: strike the liver and the life is gone. The word for life is *nephesh*, the soul itself. Jeremiah does the same with grief. Over fallen Jerusalem he says "my liver is poured upon the earth" (Lamentations 2:11). His sorrow is not described as a thought. It is an organ emptied out. The word *kabed* comes from a root meaning "heavy," the same root behind *kavod*, "glory." The heaviest organ of the body and the weight of glory are written with the same letters.

## The Kidneys: Where Yahuah Looks

Again and again, the kidneys are the place Yahuah examines. "The righteous Elohim trieth the hearts and reins" (Psalm 7:9). "I Yahuah search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways" (Jeremiah 17:10). David asks for it himself: "try my reins and my heart" (Psalm 26:2).

The kidneys are also where a man feels. David says "my reins also instruct me in the night seasons" (Psalm 16:7). Asaph, envying the wicked, was "pricked in my reins" (Psalm 73:21). A father's joy over a wise son is "my reins shall rejoice" (Proverbs 23:16). Conscience, grief, and joy all live in the reins, and Yahuah formed that place from the womb: "thou hast possessed my reins" (Psalm 139:13).

The body agrees with the picture. The kidneys and the liver are the organs that filter, keeping what is clean and sending out what is waste. That is exactly what Yahuah does when He tries the reins. He separates the true from the false, the way a refiner tries silver. The verb "try" in Jeremiah 17:10 is *bachan*, the word for testing metal.

## On the Altar

The Law gives these same organs a place of their own. In the peace offering the priest takes "the two kidneys" and "the caul above the liver" (*yoteret*, the lobe covering the liver) and burns them on the altar to Yahuah (Leviticus 3:4-5). The same portions are taken from the sin offering (Leviticus 4:9) and the trespass offering (Leviticus 7:4). The worshipper could share in the meat, but these parts were never eaten, for "all the fat is Yahuah's" (Leviticus 3:16).

The burnt offering is different. It went on the altar whole (Leviticus 1:9). But wherever the animal was divided between the altar, the priest, and the worshipper, the kidneys and the liver portion were the part Yahuah kept for Himself. The place He searches is the place He claims. Paul carries the same pattern forward when he urges believers to present their bodies "a living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1).

## How "Mind" Replaced the Reins

Biblical Hebrew has no word for the brain. Greek philosophy drew the map differently, putting reason in the head and ranking the body below it. That way of thinking came into the Scriptures in three steps, and each step can be checked.

First, the Greek translation of the Old Testament. Yahuah promised, "I will put my law in their inward parts" (Jeremiah 31:33). The Hebrew word is *qereb*, the inside of the body. The Greek translators changed it to *dianoia*, "mind," and the letter to the Hebrews quotes that Greek wording: "I will put my laws into their mind" (Hebrews 8:10). The Law that Yahuah placed in the inward parts was moved into the head.

Second, what did not change. Yahushua tells the assembly at Thyatira, "I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts" (Revelation 2:23). The Greek word there is *nephros*, which means kidneys, and it is the same word the Greek translators used for *kilyah* in Jeremiah 17:10. The Greek text kept the kidneys. Any interlinear will show it.

Third, the modern English Bible. The King James keeps "reins" in every one of these verses. Many popular modern versions do not. In Psalm 7:9, Jeremiah 17:10, and Revelation 2:23 they print "mind" or "minds," against both the Hebrew and the Greek. An interlinear does not answer this point. It proves it. The kidneys are in the text. The "mind" is in the translation.

## Why It Matters

If the inner man is only the mind, then belief becomes agreeing with the right ideas. Much of modern Christianity runs on exactly that: a decision made in the head, and nothing more asked of the life. Yahuah asks for far more. He tries the reins, the place of desire, conscience, and longing. He is not checking whether a man knows the right answer. He is checking what the man loves and how he walks, "to give every man according to his ways" (Jeremiah 17:10).

That is why the new covenant writes the Law in the inward parts (Jeremiah 31:33). The Law is not done away with. It is moved inside, written where Yahuah searches. Deliverance comes first through the blood of the Messiah; then the Law, written on the reins, is walked out in the life.

## A Word on Revelation 2:23

Some teach that because the Son searches the reins and Yahuah searches the reins, the Son must be Yahuah. The same chapter answers it. The speaker calls Himself "the Son of Elohim" (Revelation 2:18), and says the authority He gives is "even as I received of my Father" (Revelation 2:27). He searches the reins with authority He was given. Yahuah is the source of it.

## Closing

The liver is bound to the life, and the kidneys are where Yahuah looks. On the altar those organs went up to Him alone. Greek thinking moved the inner man into the head, and modern Bibles finished the move, but the text never followed. In the last book of Scripture the Son still says He searches "the reins and hearts" and will give "unto every one of you according to your works" (Revelation 2:23). The reins are still being tried.
