## Two Treasuries

"Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal" (Matthew 6:19). The argument is not that wealth is wicked. The argument is that earthly treasure is perishable stock, and a man is a fool to store what eats itself. Solomon said it first: "Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven" (Proverbs 23:5).

## The Eye in the Middle

Between the treasure saying and the mammon saying sits a line the modern pulpit cannot place: "The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light. But if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness" (Matthew 6:22-23). It is preached as focus, or as vision, or worse, as something borrowed from eastern mysticism.

It is neither. Good eye and evil eye are Hebrew money idioms, and the Torah and the Proverbs use them exactly that way. "Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart... and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother, and thou givest him nought" (Deuteronomy 15:9). "Eat thou not the bread of him that hath an evil eye" (Proverbs 23:6). "He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye" (Proverbs 28:22). And the reverse: "He that hath a bountiful eye shall be blessed; for he giveth of his bread to the poor" (Proverbs 22:9), where bountiful is literally good of eye.

An evil eye is a stingy eye. A single eye is a generous, undivided one. Read that way the verse stops being an interruption and becomes the hinge: the generous man is full of light, the grasping man is full of darkness, and that is precisely why no man can serve both Yahuah and mammon.

## Two Masters

"No man can serve two masters... Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (Matthew 6:24). This is Sinai language. "Thou shalt fear Yahuah thy God, and serve him... for Yahuah thy God is a jealous God among you" (Deuteronomy 6:13-15). It is Joshua at Shechem: "choose you this day whom ye will serve" (Joshua 24:15). It is Elijah on Carmel: "How long halt ye between two opinions?" (1 Kings 18:21). The mountain repeats the choice the nation had already been given twice.

## Take No Thought, and the Manna

Take no thought translates merimnao, to be pulled apart by care. It is not a command against labor. The Law commands work (Exodus 20:9) and the Proverbs have nothing kind to say about the sluggard. What is forbidden is the anxiety, and Yahuah had already spent forty years training a nation out of it.

In Exodus 16 the bread came down daily. Each man gathered enough for the day and no more; what was hoarded overnight bred worms and stank (Exodus 16:20). On the sixth day a double portion came and it kept, because the seventh was the Sabbath. And Yahuah stated the purpose out loud before the first flake fell: "that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no" (Exodus 16:4). Moses gave the lesson its final form: "he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna... that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 8:3).

That is the passage standing under Matthew 6. Anxiety about provision is the thing that breaks a man's obedience first, and the manna was the test that proved it.

## Seek First

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you" (Matthew 6:33). His righteousness is not left undefined. "My tongue shall speak of thy word: for all thy commandments are righteousness" (Psalm 119:172). The sentence is a command to go after the King and His instructions, with the promise attached that the food and the clothing will follow behind.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

Two readings share this passage and both empty it. One turns it into a promise of increase, where seeking first is a deposit and the added things are the return. The other turns it into a devotional about worry, comforting and vague, with nothing in it a man could obey on a Tuesday.

Neither one keeps the definition the sermon supplies. The remedy offered for a divided heart is not a better attitude and not a bigger barn. It is His righteousness, and Scripture says plainly what that is.
