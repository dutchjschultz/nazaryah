## What Yahuah Is Doing

Most of Jeremiah’s warnings are about idols, false prophets, and a temple the people trusted more than they trusted Yahuah. But in chapter 17, Yahuah sent Jeremiah to stand at the gates of Jerusalem with one simple message. He put the whole future of the city on it. Keep the Sabbath, and the city stands. Break it, and the city burns.

## Go Stand in the Gates

“Go and stand in the gate of the children of the people, whereby the kings of Judah come in, and by the which they go out, and in all the gates of Jerusalem” (17:19). Every gate. The king’s gate and the common people’s gate. Nobody was going to miss this one.

The gates were where business happened. Farmers and traders brought their loads in through the gates to sell. So Yahuah said: “Take heed to yourselves, and bear no burden on the sabbath day, nor bring it in by the gates of Jerusalem; neither carry forth a burden out of your houses on the sabbath day, neither do ye any work, but hallow ye the sabbath day, as I commanded your fathers” (17:21-22).

Then the sad line: “But they obeyed not, neither inclined their ear, but made their neck stiff, that they might not hear” (17:23).

## Two Roads

**If they kept it.** “Then shall there enter into the gates of this city kings and princes sitting upon the throne of David… and this city shall remain for ever” (17:25). People would come from all over Judah bringing offerings and praise to the house of Yahuah (17:26).

**If they broke it.** “Then will I kindle a fire in the gates thereof, and it shall devour the palaces of Jerusalem, and it shall not be quenched” (17:27).

That is a lot riding on one day. Yahuah did not treat the Sabbath as a small thing.

## What Happened

They chose the second road. In 586 BC, Babylon’s captain “burnt the house of Yahuah, and the king’s house; and all the houses of Jerusalem, and all the houses of the great men, burned he with fire” (52:13). The fire started in the gates, just as Yahuah said.

Chronicles even gives the length of the exile in Sabbath terms: seventy years, “until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths” (2 Chronicles 36:21).

## They Did It Again

When the captives came home and rebuilt the walls, Nehemiah walked through Jerusalem and saw men treading winepresses on the Sabbath and bringing loads of grain, wine, grapes, and figs in through the gates. Merchants from Tyre were selling fish (Nehemiah 13:15-16).

Nehemiah confronted the leaders with Jeremiah’s warning: “Did not your fathers thus, and did not our Elohim bring all this evil upon us, and upon this city? yet ye bring more wrath upon Israel by profaning the sabbath” (Nehemiah 13:18). He knew exactly why the city had burned.

## What the Church Says

Many churches today say one of three things about the Sabbath.

**“The Sabbath was only for the Jews.”** The Sabbath was made at creation, long before there was a Jew (Genesis 2:2-3). Yahushua said, “The sabbath was made for man” (Mark 2:27), not for one nation. And Isaiah says the foreigners who join themselves to Yahuah and keep His Sabbath are brought to His holy mountain (Isaiah 56:6-7).

**“The cross did away with it.”** Yahushua kept the Sabbath “as his custom was” (Luke 4:16). He told His followers to pray that their escape from Jerusalem, forty years after the cross, would not fall “on the sabbath day” (Matthew 24:20). He expected them to still be keeping it.

**“Every day is a Sabbath now.”** Hebrews says, “There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of Elohim” (Hebrews 4:9). The word there is *sabbatismos*, a Sabbath-keeping. It remains.

## What We Carry Through the Gates

Judah’s sin was not that they forgot the Sabbath was there. They knew. They just kept business going: carrying loads in, carrying loads out, buying and selling like any other day.

The modern version looks very similar. Shopping, errands, work emails, the to-do list carried right through the gate of a day Yahuah set apart. Isaiah tells what to do instead: “turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of Yahuah, honourable” (Isaiah 58:13). It is not a burden. It is a day off the burden.

## Bringing It Home

Yahuah put the life of His city on whether His people would set down their loads on His Sabbath and honor Him. They would not, and it burned. They came home and started doing it again, and Nehemiah shut it down. The question Jeremiah asked at every gate is the question still standing at the door of every church: will you set down the load on the day Yahuah made holy?

## Where It Lands

Revelation ends at the gates too. The holy city has twelve of them, and “the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day” (Revelation 21:25). And who goes in? “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city” (Revelation 22:14). Jerusalem burned at the gates for breaking His commandments. The new city opens its gates to those who keep them.
