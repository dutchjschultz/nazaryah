## The Charge He Answered First

Before Yahushua expounded a single command, He stopped to deny an accusation that no one in the text had yet spoken aloud: "Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil" (Matthew 5:17). A man does not deny a charge that is not already in the air. Something in His teaching sounded, to the ears of the scribes, like demolition. He named the suspicion and killed it in one sentence, and the modern pulpit has spent centuries reviving the very charge He denied.

The word rendered destroy is kataluo (G2647), to loose down, to pull apart a standing structure. It is the word used of the temple stones thrown down (Matthew 24:2). Set against it is pleroo (G4137), to fill full. A net is filled full of fish. A house is filled full of the smell of ointment (John 12:3). The whole law is filled full by love (Romans 13:8-10). Filling is not finishing. A vessel filled to the brim is more full, not gone.

## The Sentence That Settles It

If fulfil meant abolish, the next verse takes it apart: "For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled" (Matthew 5:18). The word For binds the two verses together. He is not correcting Himself one breath later.

The jot is the yod, the smallest letter in the Hebrew alphabet. The tittle is the pen stroke that separates one letter from its lookalike, a dalet from a resh, a bet from a kaf. The claim is made at the level of the stroke, not at the level of the principle.

And the clock He set is not the tree. It is heaven and earth. Peter wrote of new heavens and a new earth still to come (2 Peter 3:13). John saw them arrive (Revelation 21:1). Isaiah placed the seventh-day Sabbath inside them, kept by all flesh (Isaiah 66:22-23). Whatever was finished at the tree, heaven and earth did not pass.

## Least in the Kingdom

Verse 19 cannot be read as abolition at all: "Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven." A man cannot be demoted for breaking a command that no longer exists. He cannot be demoted for teaching others to break a command that Yahushua had just cancelled two verses earlier. The sentence assumes the commandments are standing, that some are lesser and some are greater, and that a teacher is ranked by how he handles them.

## Exceeding the Pharisees

"For I say unto you, That except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:20). The pulpit reads this as a trap: the standard is impossible, so stop trying and trust. But the three chapters that follow are a detailed description of that exceeding righteousness. Anger underneath murder. Lust underneath adultery. The closet instead of the street corner. Treasure above, mammon below. It is not a door slammed shut. It is the thesis of the sermon, and the sermon is its proof.

The failure of the Pharisees was never that they held too much of the Law. It was that they hung their own rulings on it, adding and taking away, which Yahuah forbade twice (Deuteronomy 4:2; 12:32), and hollowed out the motive underneath the act. "In vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Matthew 15:9). Exceeding them does not mean doing less than they did. It means doing what they only performed.

## Written Long Before

None of this was new on the mountain. "For ever, O Yahuah, thy word is settled in heaven" (Psalm 119:89). "Thy word is true from the beginning: and every one of thy righteous judgments endureth for ever" (Psalm 119:160). "The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever" (Isaiah 40:8). Yahushua opened His sermon by agreeing with the Psalms.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

The modern teaching is that Messiah fulfilled the Law so that no one else has to keep it, as though fulfilling a thing empties it for everybody else. A husband who fulfils his vows has not ended his marriage. He has been faithful inside it. And the man who insists the Law was finished at verse 17 still has to explain why the One who finished it spent the next three chapters teaching it deeper than the crowd had ever heard it taught.

Entry is through the blood, and nothing here disturbs that. A man is brought near by the offering Yahuah provided, not by his record. But the door was never the destination. The sermon on the mountain begins after the door, and it begins with the Law standing, down to the stroke of a pen.
