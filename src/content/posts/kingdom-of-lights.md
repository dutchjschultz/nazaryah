The disciples were standing outside the temple pointing at the stonework when Yahushua told them not one stone would be left on another. Their next question was *when* (Matthew 24:1–3). Everything He says for the next two chapters answers that question, and the parable of the ten virgins is part of the answer. Read on its own it becomes a warning about being on time. Read where it stands, it tells them what happens to the priesthood, the service, and the presence of Yahuah when the building they were pointing at is gone.

## The House Was Never the Building

The purpose behind the tabernacle was stated before it was ever constructed. Yahuah told Moshe to have them build a sanctuary so that He could dwell **among them** — among them, not in it (Exodus 25:8). And before there was any structure at all, He had already named what He was building: a kingdom of priests, an holy nation (Exodus 19:6). **The nation itself was the priesthood.** The building was a model of the thing, not the thing.

The disciples could not yet hold that. They had only ever been shown stone. So Yahushua answers them in parables, and each one takes a piece of the house they knew and shows what it looks like when the house is made of people.

Peter was standing there that day pointing at those stones. Years later he describes the house Yahuah was actually building:

> **1 PETER 2:5 — KJV**
>
> *Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to Elohim by Yahushua Messiah.*

And when he names what that priesthood is, he quotes Sinai back word for word — a royal priesthood, an holy nation (1 Peter 2:9, quoting Exodus 19:6). The definition never changed. Only the material did.

## What the Light Is

This parable turns on a light, and nothing in it can be read until the light is named. Scripture names it with no imagery at all.

> **PROVERBS 6:23 — KJV**
>
> *For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light.*

Two Hebrew words set side by side on purpose. ***Ner*** is the lamp, the vessel that carries. ***Or*** is the light, what actually shines. **The commandment is the lamp. The Torah is the light it gives.** David says the same of Yahuah's word (Psalm 119:105), and Isaiah supplies the test for anyone claiming light without it — if they speak not according to the law and the testimony, there is no light in them (Isaiah 8:20).

This is also why the house was built to shine outward. Israel was told plainly what the watching nations would see: they would hear the statutes and say, surely this is a wise and understanding people (Deuteronomy 4:6). The statutes kept are the light. Isaiah says His law would go out as a light of the people (Isaiah 51:4), and the servant is given as a light of the nations (Isaiah 42:6).

So the priest's assignment was never ceremony for its own sake. Malachi names the duty and the failure in the same breath:

> **MALACHI 2:7–8 — KJV**
>
> *For the priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth: for he is the messenger of Yahuah of hosts. But ye are departed out of the way; ye have caused many to stumble at the law ...*

That is a lamp going out, described by a prophet. Not an army. Not a siege. **The instruction failed at the mouths of the men charged with carrying it, and the house went dark long before the stones came down.**

Yahushua tells the men who are about to become that priesthood exactly the same thing — ye are the light of the world, let your light shine so men see your works — and then, before anyone can mistake what the light is made of: think not that I am come to destroy the law (Matthew 5:14–17).

It needs saying plainly, because a whole branch of modern teaching depends on nobody saying it. A house that teaches Yahuah's instruction was set aside is not a house with dimmer light. It is a house with none, because Scripture never described another kind.

## Who the Priests Are

The ten are called virgins, and that is usually explained by wedding custom, which explains the scenery and nothing else. Ezekiel supplies the real reason. When he describes the Levites who were demoted, he names their disqualification in one clause: they went astray after their idols (Ezekiel 44:10).

And Scripture's standing image for idolatry is not a legal breach. It is marital unfaithfulness. Yahuah puts Israel away with a bill of divorce for committing adultery with stones and with stocks (Jeremiah 3:8–9), and the whole indictment of Ezekiel 16 runs on the same figure.

So the opposite of a priesthood that went after idols is a priesthood described as **virgin**. That is exactly how the word is used of the redeemed:

> **REVELATION 14:4 — KJV**
>
> *These are they which were not defiled with women; for they are virgins. These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth.*

Read against the prophets, that is a statement about idolatry. And it stands in the same book that calls the assemblies lampstands and the redeemed kings and priests (Revelation 1:6, 1:20). **Lampstands, priests, and virgins are three names for the same people.**

Ten virgins holding lamps is therefore not scenery. It is a priesthood named by the one qualification the previous priesthood forfeited.

## What the Oil Is

Torah commands oil to do exactly one thing in a lamp, and it lays the burden of supplying it on the people rather than the priest.

> **EXODUS 27:20 — KJV**
>
> *And thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always.*

Every word of that is doing work. The oil is **brought in by individual households**, not procured by the priesthood. It is **beaten** — first-press oil, which exists only because something was crushed to make it. It is **pure**, unmixed, with nothing added to stretch a small supply into a large one. And the light burns **always**, *tamid*, the same word that governs the daily offering — not an event that happens but an obligation that never lapses.

Read as one sentence: the oil is what each person personally brings so that the light in Yahuah's house never goes out. If the light is His instruction, then the oil is whatever keeps that instruction alive in a man on ordinary days when nothing is happening and nobody is watching.

There are two oils in Torah, and confusing them is where the modern reading fails. The anointing oil is a compound, poured onto a body by someone else, and forbidden to be reproduced or given away (Exodus 30:23–33). The lamp oil is unmixed, carried in by the man himself, poured into a vessel, and it burns until it is gone. **One is a status. The other is a service.**

The parable names vessels, not heads. Every woman in the story already has the status — all ten were called, all ten held the office, and when the cry came all ten performed the priestly work of trimming their lamps correctly (Matthew 25:7). **The motion was never the measure.** Five had something behind it and five did not.

And no one could hand it over. The refusal in verse nine is not stinginess; it is a rule already written. Ezekiel says that though Noah, Daniel, and Job stood in a condemned land, they would deliver neither son nor daughter, but only their own souls by their righteousness (Ezekiel 14:20). No man's standing before Yahuah has ever covered another man.

## What Happened to the Old Priesthood

Scripture records this failure more than once, and never as an invasion. Eli's house is judged on a night when the lamp of Elohim had nearly gone out (1 Samuel 3:3). Under Ahaz the doors were shut and the lamps put out by the very men appointed to tend them (2 Chronicles 29:6–7). **Nobody broke in either time.**

Ezekiel shows the sentence that follows, and it is the detail that decides this parable. Two groups of Levites, both inside the house, both having served. The sons of Zadok kept the charge while the nation went astray, and they come near. The others do not:

> **EZEKIEL 44:11, 13 — KJV**
>
> *Yet they shall be ministers in my sanctuary, having charge at the gates of the house ... And they shall not come near unto me, to do the office of a priest unto me ... but they shall bear their shame.*

They are not expelled. They keep their sanctuary, their office, and their work. They still minister, still slaughter, still stand in the house. **What ends is nearness** — from that day they serve the people and no longer serve Yahuah.

That is the same line the two oils draw. Their status stood. Their service was demoted. And the light itself is not permanent property either: Yahushua tells an assembly that He will remove its lampstand out of its place unless it repents (Revelation 2:5).

## The Door

A door in Torah is not an entrance. **It is where it is settled who belongs to a house.** Blood goes on the door and the house behind it is passed over (Exodus 12:7, 23). The servant who refuses to go free has his ear fixed to the door and belongs to that house permanently (Deuteronomy 15:17). The priests keep their charge at the door of the tent (Leviticus 8:35).

So when the parable says the door was shut, a decision has been made, not an appointment missed. And the answer through it says nothing about the hour:

> **MATTHEW 25:12 — KJV**
>
> *But he answered and said, Verily I say unto you, I know you not.*

To **know**, in Scripture, is to acknowledge a covenant relationship. Yahuah tells Moshe He knows him by name (Exodus 33:17) and says of the whole nation that they only has He known of all the families of the earth (Amos 3:2). What the five hear is a verdict on whether the relationship was ever what it appeared to be.

## The Verdict

The disciples asked when a building would end. Yahushua answered by telling them how a light goes out.

The light in that house was never the stonework and never the ceremony. It was Yahuah's own instruction, carried by men whose assignment was to keep knowledge on their lips so a watching world could see what He is like. Malachi says what became of it — they departed out of the way and caused many to stumble at the law. The lamp went out at the mouths of the men holding it, and the stones came down afterward.

The temple Yahuah is building now is made of people, and its priests are the ones who never went after idols. What is asked of them is what was asked of every household in Israel: bring in what keeps the light burning, day after ordinary day, until He comes. **Yahuah does not dwell in a temple with the light put out.** There is nothing there for Him to dwell in.

Ten held the office. Five kept the light. When the delay ran long enough to show the difference, nothing could be borrowed, because it never could be.
