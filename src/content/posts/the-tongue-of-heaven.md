## The Question

When heaven spoke to men in Scripture, what did men hear? The record is consistent from Genesis to Revelation. Heaven spoke words, the hearers understood them, and the words left untranslated are Hebrew.

## One Lip Before Babel

> **GENESIS 11:1 — KJV**
>
> *And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.*

The Hebrew is *saphah echad* — literally "one lip" (*saphah*, H8193). At Babel Yahuah confounded that lip, and the city was named for the act:

> **GENESIS 11:9 — KJV**
>
> *Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because Yahuah did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did Yahuah scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.*

The name is a Hebrew wordplay: *Babel* sounds out *balal* (H1101), "to confound." The explanation works only in Hebrew. Scripture does not name the first tongue outright, but every name given before the scattering is explained in Hebrew. *Adam* is taken from the *adamah*, the ground (Genesis 2:7). *Ishshah*, woman, is taken from *ish*, man (Genesis 2:23). *Chavah*, Eve, is "the mother of all living," from *chai*, living (Genesis 3:20). *Qayin*, Cain, comes from *qanah*, "I have gotten" (Genesis 4:1). *Sheth*, Seth, from *shith*, "appointed" (Genesis 4:25). *Noach*, Noah, sounds out *nacham*, "this same shall comfort us" (Genesis 5:29).

If the first tongue were anything else, the meanings Scripture attaches to these names would be accidents. The line that kept this speech after the scattering is the line of Eber (Genesis 10:21; 11:14–26), and Abram, Eber's descendant, is the first man Scripture calls *Ivri* — "the Hebrew" (Genesis 14:13; H5680, from *Eber*, H5677).

## Messengers Who Named in Hebrew

> **GENESIS 16:11 — KJV**
>
> *And the angel of Yahuah said unto her, Behold, thou art with child, and shalt bear a son, and shalt call his name Ishmael; because Yahuah hath heard thy affliction.*

*Yishmael* means "El hears" (from *shama*, H8085). The angel gives the name and its reason in one breath, and the reason is the meaning of the Hebrew name. Hagar, an Egyptian, received a Hebrew name from a heavenly messenger. Centuries later Gabriel gave Zacharias the name *Yochanan*, John — "Yahuah has shown favor" (Luke 1:13).

The book of Daniel adds a striking witness. From Daniel 2:4 through the end of chapter 7 the text is written in Aramaic, the language of Babylon. At Daniel 8:1 the book returns to Hebrew, and it is in this Hebrew section that Gabriel appears by name (Daniel 8:16) and delivers the prophecy of the seventy weeks (Daniel 9:21–27). The messenger who stands in the presence of Elohim is recorded speaking Hebrew.

And every messenger who speaks in Scripture is understood. Hagar understood. Daniel understood. Zacharias understood well enough to doubt and be struck silent (Luke 1:20). Mary answered Gabriel in conversation (Luke 1:34). Scripture records no messenger of heaven uttering sounds that no man could follow.

## A Voice from Heaven in the Hebrew Tongue

> **ACTS 26:14 — KJV**
>
> *And when we were all fallen to the earth, I heard a voice speaking unto me, and saying in the Hebrew tongue, Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks.*

Paul names the language heaven used. The voice that stopped him on the road came from heaven, and it spoke Hebrew — the Greek text even keeps the Hebrew form of his name in that voice's mouth (Witness Seven).

## The Pure Lip Restored

The prophets saw the end of the scattering:

> **ZEPHANIAH 3:9 — KJV**
>
> *For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent.*

The word for "language" is again *saphah*, lip — the same word as Genesis 11:1. The one lip confounded at Babel becomes a *saphah berurah* (from *barar*, H1305), a purified lip. Scripture opens with one lip, scatters it in judgment, and promises it back, gathered to call on one Name.

## The Song Before the Throne

When John hears the great multitude in heaven, the word their praise is built on is left untranslated: *Alleluia* (G239), the Hebrew *Hallelu-Yah*, "praise Yah" — the Name itself, preserved inside the Greek (Revelation 19:1, 3, 4, 6). It appears nowhere else in the New Testament. The Greek text could carry heaven's vision, but not heaven's song.

> **REVELATION 19:6 — KJV**
>
> *And I heard as it were the voice of a great multitude, and as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of mighty thunderings, saying, Alleluia: for Yahuah Elohim omnipotent reigneth.*

The last song of heaven is still sung in the tongue of heaven. The modern teaching that the "tongues of angels" (1 Corinthians 13:1) are sounds no man can understand is answered on its own page, [Tongues](/doctrines/tongues/).

*One of eight witnesses in a single case. **The Tongue of Heaven** is the testimony of heaven's own speech — the one lip before Babel, the messengers who named in Hebrew, and the song still sung before the throne.
