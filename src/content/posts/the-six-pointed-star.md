## What Came West

The six-pointed star, two triangles laid over each other, is worn on necklaces and earrings and hung in many American churches, especially those that support the modern state of Israel. Most believe it is an ancient biblical symbol, the shield of King David.

## The Record

Scripture never mentions it. No verse describes David carrying a star on his shield, and no verse names a six-pointed star as a sign of Israel. The name "Shield of David," *Magen David*, comes from much later Jewish writing, not from the Bible.

The hexagram itself is found in many ancient cultures. It was used in Hindu temples, where it is called the *shatkona*; in Islam and in Christian magic books, where it was called the Seal of Solomon and used as a charm; and as a decoration in buildings of many faiths. It became a standard Jewish emblem only gradually. In the fourteenth century the Jewish community of Prague was granted a flag bearing the star, and its use spread through Jewish communities over the following centuries. In 1897 the First Zionist Congress put it on the movement's flag, and in 1948 it became the center of the flag of the modern state of Israel.

Some teachers connect the hexagram with "the star of your god Remphan," which Stephen said Israel carried in the wilderness (Acts 7:43; Amos 5:26). That passage condemns a star carried as an idol, but it does not describe the star's shape, so it cannot be used to prove the hexagram was that star. What can be said is enough: the six-pointed star is not from Scripture, it has a long history as a charm, and it became a Jewish national emblem through a modern political movement.

## Scripture's Star

Scripture does give Israel a star, and it is a person. Balaam prophesied, "there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel" (Numbers 24:17). That Star is the Messiah, not a shape on a flag.

## The Verdict

The six-pointed star is a medieval and modern emblem with a history as a charm, worn by many Christians as though it came from the Bible. It did not. A believer who wants to honor the Star out of Jacob honors the Son, not a symbol of the modern state. The star belongs under the oak.

Revelation names the true Star in the Son's own words: "I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star" (Revelation 22:16).
