## The Double Standard

Hinduism teaches that Vishnu comes down to earth in a human body — as Rama, as Krishna — whenever the world needs rescue. The Sanskrit word is avatara, "descent." Most churchgoers call that paganism without a second thought.

Yet the same churchgoers sing every December, "Veiled in flesh the Godhead see; hail the incarnate Deity" (Charles Wesley, 1739). The Westminster Confession teaches two whole natures, God and man, joined in one person. A god leaves heaven, enters a human body, walks among men, and goes back. The shape is the same. Only the label changed.

If the idea is pagan in a Hindu temple, what makes it holy in a church pew?

## A Word the Bible Never Uses

"Incarnation" does not appear anywhere in the King James Bible. It is Latin — in (into) plus caro (flesh): the "in-fleshing" of a god. It is the creed's word, not Scripture's.

## The Apostles Met This Idea Head-On

At Lystra, after a lame man was healed, the crowd shouted:

> **Acts 14:11**
>
> *The gods are come down to us in the likeness of men.*

They called Barnabas Jupiter and Paul Mercury and brought oxen to sacrifice. Paul and Barnabas tore their clothes and ran in among the people:

> **Acts 14:15**
>
> *Sirs, why do ye these things? We also are men of like passions with you, and preach unto you that ye should turn from these vanities unto the living God.*

The pagan world already believed gods come down in human form. The apostles did not adopt that belief. They called it vanity.

## What the Scriptures Actually Say

Yahuah says it plainly: "I am God, and not man" (Hosea 11:9). Paul says it just as plainly: "there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). One Elohim on one side; the man Yahushua standing between. He was "made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4), and the angel tied his sonship to his birth: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

## "The Word Was Made Flesh"

John 1:1 says, "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God." A man's word is with him the same way — it is his thought, his plan, what comes out of his mouth. The Word (logos, G3056) is the Hebrew dabar (H1697), what Yahuah speaks. "By the word of Yahuah were the heavens made" (Psalm 33:6). "My word… that goeth forth out of my mouth… shall not return unto me void" (Isaiah 55:11).

So when John 1:14 says the Word "was made flesh," the verb is egeneto (G1096) — came to be. What Yahuah had spoken from the beginning, the promised Seed of Genesis 3:15, came to be a born son. That is a promise kept, not a second god putting on a body.

## The Trinity Cannot Survive the Temptation

"God cannot be tempted with evil" (James 1:13). Yet the Son "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15). If he were Elohim in flesh, the temptation was a show. Scripture says it was real, so he was a man.

His death says the same. Yahuah "only hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:16) — if Yahushua were Elohim, he never truly died.

## A Mixing Yahuah Already Judged

Genesis 6 records heavenly beings joining with the daughters of men and producing a mixed race, the Nephilim. Yahuah answered it with the flood. The god-man teaching makes the Messiah that same mixture — half heaven, half earth. Scripture never gives him that shape. It gives a son of Adam, born of a woman by the power of the Most High, as truly a man as the first Adam (1 Corinthians 15:45).

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

The church kept the shape of the pagan god-in-flesh, gave it a Latin name, set it to music, and taught its people to recoil only when they hear it in Sanskrit. The recoil is the right instinct. It needs to be pointed at both.

Revelation keeps the order to the end: it is "the Revelation of Yahushua Messiah, which God gave unto him" (Revelation 1:1), and the risen Messiah still calls Yahuah "my God" four times in one verse (Revelation 3:12).
