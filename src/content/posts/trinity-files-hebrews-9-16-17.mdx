Part One

**The Verse That Trips Everyone Up**

***1.1 — Why This One Is So Confusing***

If you have ever read Hebrews 9:16–17 and felt lost, you are not alone. The chapter is moving along about priests, blood, and the tabernacle — and then two verses show up that sound like a lawyer reading a will. It feels out of place. Many readers slide right past it, sure they have missed something.

By the end of this study you will understand exactly what these two verses are saying, where the idea comes from in the Old Testament, and why it does not say what many have been told it says. Here are the two verses, just as the King James Bible has them.

> **Hebrews 9:16–17**
>
> *For where a testament is, there must also of necessity be the death of the testator. For a testament is of force after men are dead: otherwise it is of no strength at all while the testator liveth.*

***1.2 — The Argument Built On It***

A popular argument is built on these two verses. It runs like this. A testament needs the death of the testator. Yahushua died. So Yahushua must be the testator — and since the testator is the one who made the whole thing, Yahushua must be Yahuah Himself.

It sounds tidy. But it leans its whole weight on one English word the translators chose, and on a courtroom picture that does not belong here. Once you see what the verse is really about, the argument has nothing left to stand on.

***1.3 — The Word in Disguise***

The key word is “testament.” In almost every other place in the Bible, that same word is translated “covenant” — a bond, a solemn promise between two sides. In the book of Hebrews itself it is called a covenant again and again.

A few lines earlier it is “a better covenant” *Hebrews 8:6* and “a new covenant” *Hebrews 8:8*; a few lines later, “the blood of the covenant” *Hebrews 10:29* and “the blood of the everlasting covenant” *Hebrews 13:20*. Same word every time.

So read it as covenant, and the verse says plainly: where there is a covenant, there must be a death. The King James dresses the dying one up as a “testator” — more will-court costume. But a covenant is never sealed by the death of the one who makes it. It is sealed by the death of the sacrifice. Hold that one fact in front of you — the death is the sacrifice’s, never the maker’s — and the whole verse opens up. The rest of this study simply shows it, straight from the Old Testament.

Part Two

**What a Covenant Really Is**

***2.1 — A Covenant Is a Blood-Bond, Not a Will***

To understand the verse, you first have to understand what a covenant is — because it is nothing like a will.

A will is about property. A man writes down who gets his things, and it only takes effect once he dies. A covenant is something else entirely. A covenant is a life-and-death bond between two parties, sealed in blood. In fact the Bible’s own word for making a covenant means to cut one — because a covenant was cut with a knife, over a slain sacrifice.

***2.2 — The Night Yahuah Cut His Covenant With Abraham***

The clearest picture of how a covenant was made is the night Yahuah made His great promise to Abraham. He told Abraham to prepare the animals.

> **Genesis 15:10**
>
> *And he took unto him all these, and divided them in the midst, and laid each piece one against another: but the birds divided he not.*

Abraham cut the animals down the middle and laid the halves in two rows, with a bloody path running between them. Then, after dark, something happened that unlocks our whole verse.

> **Genesis 15:17–18**
>
> *And it came to pass, that, when the sun went down, and it was dark, behold a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces. In the same day the LORD \[Yahuah\] made a covenant with Abram...*

The fire was Yahuah Himself, passing between the pieces. When a covenant was cut, the parties walked the bloody path between the halves, binding themselves to the promise — and the slain animals were its seal. The death that sealed the covenant was the animals’ death. It was never the death of the ones making the promise.

Now notice who walked the path with Abraham: no one. Only Yahuah passed through. Abraham did not walk it at all. Yahuah made the covenant rest on His own word alone — Abraham added nothing to it. And the death that sealed it fell where it always fell: on the sacrifice. Not on Yahuah. He does not die; the sacrifice does.

***2.3 — Sinai Was Sealed the Very Same Way***

Hundreds of years later, when Yahuah made His covenant with the whole nation of Israel at the mountain, it was sealed in exactly the same way — with blood.

> **Exodus 24:8**
>
> *And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD \[Yahuah\] hath made with you concerning all these words.*

Moses did not read a will. He took the blood of slain animals, sprinkled it, and called it “the blood of the covenant.” Hold on to that phrase — it is the very thing the writer of Hebrews is about to point back to.

***2.4 — The Maker Never Died; the Sacrifice Did***

Now notice the one thing every Old Testament covenant has in common. A covenant was never sealed by the death of the one who made it. It was always sealed by the death of a sacrifice that stood in its place.

Abraham did not die to seal his covenant. Israel did not die to seal theirs. Yahuah did not die to seal His. An animal died. The maker gave the promise and lived on; the sacrifice was slain. Yahuah later pointed back to this very ritual to warn covenant-breakers *Jeremiah 34:18* — the men who cut a calf in two and passed between its parts, then broke faith. Keep this firmly in mind, because it is the key that opens Hebrews 9.

***2.5 — So Who Is This Covenant With?***

A covenant always has two sides. One side makes the promise. The other side is the one the promise is made to. So far we have met the Maker — Yahuah — and the sacrifice that seals the bond. But there is a second party we have not yet named. Who is this covenant with?

Every covenant in Scripture names the other side. At the fire and the pieces, it was Yahuah with Abraham and his seed. At the mountain, it was Yahuah with the nation of Israel. And the covenant the book of Hebrews is actually about — the new one — names its other side too.

> **Jeremiah 31:31, 33**
>
> *Behold, the days come, saith the LORD \[Yahuah\], that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah... I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.*

There is the second party: Yahuah’s own people. The new covenant is Yahuah binding Himself to a people and writing His law on their hearts. The whole of Hebrews 8 and 9 is building toward this covenant — Hebrews even quotes these very words of Jeremiah *Hebrews 8:8-10*.

So now the picture is complete. Yahuah is the Maker. His people are the ones He makes the covenant with. And the sacrifice that seals it between them is the Lamb. The Messiah had to be the sacrifice because a covenant between Yahuah and His people could only be sealed in blood — and He said so Himself.

> **Matthew 26:28**
>
> *For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.*

Shed for many. The Lamb’s blood was poured out for the people — to seal the covenant Yahuah was making with them, and to carry away their sins. That is who the covenant is with, and that is why the Messiah had to die: not to prove He was the Maker, but to be the sacrifice that bound a people to their God.

Part Three

**Reading Hebrews 9 With New Eyes**

***3.1 — The Writer Is Talking About Blood, Not Lawyers***

Go back to Hebrews 9 and read what comes right after our two verses. The writer does not mention lawyers, estates, or property. He talks about blood.

> **Hebrews 9:18–20**
>
> *Whereupon neither the first testament was dedicated without blood. For when Moses had spoken every precept to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of calves and of goats... and sprinkled both the book, and all the people, saying, This is the blood of the testament which God hath enjoined unto you.*

Look closely at what he is doing. He is retelling the exact scene from Exodus 24. He is quoting Moses at the mountain — “this is the blood of the covenant.” He has not switched to will-and-testament language at all. He is standing at the altar, describing how a covenant is sealed in blood. That is the whole subject of the passage.

Then he drives it home: “almost all things are by the law purged with blood” (Hebrews 9:22). No will on earth is made good by killing a calf. But every covenant was.

***3.2 — “Over the Dead”: Altar Language***

There is one more quiet clue. In the Greek, verse 17 says a covenant is made sure “over the dead” — over slain victims. That is altar language, not lawyer language. No one makes a will firm “over dead bodies.” A covenant, though, is sealed in exactly that way — over the slain.

***3.3 — Even If You Call It a Will, the Son Is the Heir***

Now step into their make-believe for a moment. Grant them the word “will,” just to be fair, and watch it break in their hands.

A will has a maker, and a will has an heir — and they are never the same person. So who is the heir in this book? The Son. Scripture calls Him “his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things” *Hebrews 1:2* — appointed, by the Father. And we are told we are heirs of Yahuah and joint-heirs with the Messiah *Romans 8:17*, standing in line to receive right beside Him.

So even their own picture puts the Father as the maker and the Son as the heir who receives. That is the exact opposite of what they set out to prove.

Now hold the two pictures side by side, because they do not fight — they agree. In their make-believe will, the Son is the heir, never the one who wrote it. In the Bible’s real covenant, the Son is the sacrifice, never the one who makes it. Heir in the false picture, sacrifice in the true one — and in neither is He the covenant-making God. Both roads end at the very same place.

Part Four

**What It Means**

***4.1 — Yahuah Gives the Promise; Yahushua Is the Sacrifice***

Now set the two where they belong.

Yahuah is the covenant-maker. On that dark night He passed between the pieces alone and made the promise by Himself — the covenant rests on His word. He makes the bond; He does not die to seal it.

Yahushua is the sacrifice that seals it. He is the Lamb. He is the price. He is the death the covenant required — and it was His death, not the Maker’s.

See how the two covenants answer each other. At Abraham’s covenant, animals were slain to seal Yahuah’s promise. At the cross, the true Lamb was slain to seal the new covenant with His people. The pattern never changes: the Maker gives the promise, and a sacrifice dies to seal it. One makes the covenant. One is the sacrifice. They are never the same.

And do not lose the third party. One is the Maker. One is the Lamb. And standing between them is a people — the ones this whole covenant was cut to save, bound to Yahuah by the blood of His Son. That is not one person in two robes. It is a Father, a Lamb, and a people the Lamb was slain for.

***4.2 — Now Read the Verse Again***

With all of that in hand, read the verse one more time. The same words — but now with the covenant meaning poured back into them, the way the Hebrew Scriptures always meant it:

> **Hebrews 9:16–17 — read through covenant eyes**
>
> *For where there is a covenant* \[a blood-bond between Yahuah and His people, cut and sealed over a slain sacrifice\] *there must of necessity also be brought the death of the sacrifice* \[the covenant victim whose blood ratifies the bond — not the death of the Maker, who does not die\]. *For a covenant is made sure over the slain* \[confirmed only once the sacrifice has died\]; *it has no strength at all while the sacrifice is yet unslain* \[until the blood is poured out, the bond does not stand\].

Yahuah is the covenant-maker who gave the promise. Yahushua is the sacrifice whose death sealed it. The verse never folds them into one — it lays them side by side, a Maker and a Lamb.

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

The confusion was never in the verse. It was in one English word that made a blood covenant sound like a lawyer’s will. Put the altar back where it belongs, and the verse reads in a straight line — Yahuah binding a people to Himself, and the Lamb slain to seal the bond.

> **A covenant has a Maker who gives His word, a people He gives it to, and a Lamb who bleeds to bind them — and Hebrews sets them side by side, never one inside the other.**

*And this is where all of Scripture is heading. John sees “a Lamb as it had been slain” standing in the midst of the throne Revelation 5:6, and at the very end, “the throne of God and of the Lamb” Revelation 22:3 — named apart, to the last page. The Maker on the throne, and the Lamb who was slain to seal the covenant: two forever, never one.*
