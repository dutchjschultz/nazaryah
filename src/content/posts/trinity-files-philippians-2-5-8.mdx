**Philippians 2:5-8**

**The Hand That Did Not Reach**

*Two sons in the image of God, and the one who would not grab*

Adam reached to be as God and fell; the Son would not reach, and the Father lifted Him up.

— The Standing Stone —

> **Behind "LORD" in your Bible lies a hidden name — in the Hebrew it is Yahuah** *Psalm 83:18*; **Yahuah is the Father** *Isaiah 63:16*; **Yahuah is the only God, beside Him there is no other** *Isaiah 45:5*; **therefore Yahuah the Father is the only true God, leaving no room for a second or third person** *1 Corinthians 8:6*.

> **Philippians 2:5–8**
>
> *Let this mind be in you, which was also in Messiah Yahushua: Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.*

Part One

**The Claim**

***1.1 — What Is Claimed***

Many teachers call these verses the strongest proof that Yahushua was God before He was born. The claim rests on three phrases. “Being in the form of God” is said to mean He had the very nature of God from eternity. “Thought it not robbery to be equal with God” is said to mean He already held equality with God by right. “Made himself of no reputation” is said to mean God left His throne in heaven and became a baby. Some Bibles print the teaching right into the text: the New International Version says “in very nature God,” words the Greek does not contain.

***1.2 — The Hidden Premise***

Every part of the claim rests on one idea the verses never state: that the story begins in heaven. Verses 5 through 8 never mention heaven, a throne, a coming down, or a time before the world. Paul’s words speak of a form, a choice not to grab, a servant, death, and a cross. The heaven has to be brought to the passage from the creeds.

Part Two

**Verse by Verse**

***2.1 — Verse 5: “Let This Mind Be in You”***

The passage sits in a lesson on pride. Paul had just told the Philippians to do nothing “through strife or vainglory,” and to “esteem other better than themselves” *Philippians 2:3*. Then he gave them an example to copy. Two of his words tie the example to the lesson. “Vainglory” is kenodoxia, empty glory; verse 7 says the Son “emptied” Himself, ekenōsen. And “esteem” in verse 3 is the same Greek verb as “thought” in verse 6, hēgeomai — to count a thing’s worth. The Philippians were to count others above themselves, as the Son counted equality with God as nothing to grab.

An example has to be one a man can follow. No believer can copy an eternal God leaving a throne in heaven. Every believer can copy a man who refused to grab at more than he was given.

***2.2 — Verse 6: “Being in the Form of God”***

**morphē (μορφή)**

Form or shape — the way a thing appears, what can be seen of it. Used in the New Testament only here (twice) and in Mark 16:12.

The one other use settles the meaning. After the resurrection Yahushua “appeared in another form unto two of them” *Mark 16:12*. They did not see a different nature; they saw a different look. Form is what can be seen.

That rules out the nature reading. “No man hath seen God at any time” *John 1:18*; He is “invisible” *1 Timothy 1:17*. The hidden nature of the invisible God has no shape to see. Paul means something about the Son that people could see.

The verse itself shows what. The Son was in “the form of God” and took “the form of a servant” — the same word twice. The form of a servant is a man doing a servant’s work, as when He washed His disciples’ feet *John 13:4–5*. So the form of God is God’s work seen in a man. The Son said it Himself: “he that hath seen me hath seen the Father,” because “the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works” *John 14:9–10*. Peter called Him “a man approved of God … by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him” *Acts 2:22*.

“Being” does not mean “existing from eternity” either. It is hyparchōn, the word Paul used when he wrote that every man “is the image and glory of God” *1 Corinthians 11:7*. Paul is speaking the language of Adam. Adam was made in God’s image *Genesis 1:26* and called “the son of God” *Luke 3:38*, with no human father. The Son had no human father either, and for that reason was “called the Son of God” *Luke 1:35*. Two sons stood in the image of God. The passage is about what each one did with it.

***2.3 — Verse 6: “Thought It Not Robbery to Be Equal With God”***

**harpagmos (ἁρπαγμός)**

A seizing, a thing to be grabbed. Used only here in the New Testament. From harpazō, to seize or take by force.

The root always means taking hold by force: the violent “take it by force” *Matthew 11:12*; the wolf “catcheth” the sheep *John 10:12*. When the crowd tried to “take him by force, to make him a king” *John 6:15*, that is harpazō — and Yahushua walked away. He would not be crowned by a grab.

The KJV wording sounds as if He held equality and saw no theft in claiming it. But the verb is hēgeomai, the same “esteem” of verse 3: Paul is describing what the Son counted worth reaching for, not a right He held. Even translations made by those who hold to the Trinity, such as the English Standard Version, read “did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped.”

Now set the words beside the garden. “Equal with God” is isa theō, on a level with God. The serpent promised, “ye shall be as gods” *Genesis 3:5*. Adam was already in God’s image and still reached for more, and he took the fruit *Genesis 3:6*. The Son, with God at work in Him as in no man before, had more reason than anyone to think himself on God’s level — and would not grab it. When the enemy later offered Him every kingdom in exchange for worship, He answered from the Law: “him only shalt thou serve” *Matthew 4:10*.

***2.4 — Verse 7: “Emptied Himself … the Form of a Servant”***

Emptied of what? Paul answers in the same breath: by taking a servant’s form, by humbling Himself, by obeying unto death *Philippians 2:7–8*. The emptying is not a trip from heaven. It is a life poured out. Isaiah said the Servant of Yahuah “poured out his soul unto death” *Isaiah 53:12*. Gethsemane shows it: “not as I will, but as thou wilt” *Matthew 26:39* — a man emptying himself of his own will.

Adam ended up a servant too, but by sin: he would eat “in the sweat of thy face” until he returned to dust *Genesis 3:19*. Adam fell into slavery by grabbing. The Son walked into the servant’s form by choosing. “The likeness of men” does not mean He only looked human; He came “in the likeness of sinful flesh” *Romans 8:3*, real flesh in every way but sin.

***2.5 — Verse 8: “Obedient Unto Death”***

“As by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous” *Romans 5:19*. Adam earned his death, for “the wages of sin is death” *Romans 6:23*. The Son “did no sin” *1 Peter 2:22*, and went to death anyway.

Obedience belongs to one who has someone above him. The Son “learned obedience by the things which he suffered” *Hebrews 5:8*; the eternal God has no one to obey. And the next verse gives the result: “Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him” *Philippians 2:9*. God exalts; the Son is exalted, and “wherefore” says why — because He obeyed. An eternal, co-equal God cannot be promoted. An obedient man can.

Part Three

**The Same Words Spoken of Believers**

Every key word in this passage is spoken of believers too. Words that Scripture uses of the apostles and the assembly cannot prove that the Son is God.

***3.1 — Form***

Paul labored “until Messiah be formed in you” *Galatians 4:19* — the verb of morphē. The Son was “transfigured” *Matthew 17:2*, and believers are told, “be ye transformed” *Romans 12:2* — the same word, metamorphoō. The God who worked in the Son works in the believer: “it is God which worketh in you” *Philippians 2:13*.

***3.2 — Seized and Equal***

Paul was “caught up to the third heaven” *2 Corinthians 12:2*; believers “shall be caught up” at the return *1 Thessalonians 4:17*; and the Son “was caught up unto God, and to his throne” *Revelation 12:5*. All three are harpazō. The Son did not seize a place with God — God seized Him up to the throne. Equality is given the same way: the raised “are equal unto the angels” *Luke 20:36*.

***3.3 — Emptied, a Servant, Unto Death***

Paul refused his rights as an apostle rather than let any man “make my glorying void” *1 Corinthians 9:15* — kenoō, “emptied” — and wrote, “I made myself servant unto all” *1 Corinthians 9:19*. This very letter opens with Paul and Timothy as douloi, “servants” *Philippians 1:1*, the word of “the form of a servant.” And in the same chapter Epaphroditus, for the work of Messiah, “was nigh unto death” *Philippians 2:30* — mechri thanatou, the exact words of “obedient unto death” in verse 8. Paul set three men of one mind in one chapter. Only one is ever claimed to be God, and the same words are used of all three.

Part Four

**Reading the Verse Again**

With the Greek drawn out and the garden set beside it, the passage reads:

> *Let this same way of thinking be in you that was in Messiah Yahushua. He was a son in the image of God, and the Father was at work in Him so that men who saw Him saw the form of God. Yet He did not count being on a level with God as a prize to snatch, as Adam did when he reached for the promise “ye shall be as gods.” Instead He poured Himself out. He took the shape of a slave and shared the lot of weak and dying men. And being found as a man, He brought Himself low, and obeyed His God all the way to death — even the shameful death of the tree.*

It needs no heaven before birth, no God laying down His powers, and no second person beside the Father.

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

The verse called the strongest proof of the Trinity is a story of two sons. Form is what can be seen, and the form of God was the Father’s work seen in a man. Robbery is a snatch, and the Son would not snatch. Emptying is a life poured out unto death, not a trip from heaven. And every one of these words is spoken of believers too.

Revelation finishes the story. The Son who would not seize was “caught up unto God, and to his throne” *Revelation 12:5*, and He promises the believer who overcomes a seat with Him, “even as I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne” *Revelation 3:21*. He was set down — placed there by His Father.

> **If “the form of God” proves the Son is God, then “until Messiah be formed in you” proves every believer is God. Adam grabbed and was cast down; the Son would not grab, and God lifted Him up.**
