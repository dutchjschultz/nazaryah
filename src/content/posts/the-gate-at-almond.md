## A Night on the Road

Jacob left Beersheba running from his brother and headed for Haran. At dusk he “lighted upon a certain place,” took one of the stones for a pillow, and lay down to sleep (Genesis 28:10-11).

That night he dreamed. “Behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of Elohim ascending and descending on it” (Genesis 28:12). Yahuah stood above it and spoke the same promise He had given Abraham: the land Jacob lay on would go to his seed, and in his seed all the families of the earth would be blessed (Genesis 28:13-14).

Jacob woke up shaken. “Surely Yahuah is in this place; and I knew it not.” Then he named what he had seen: “this is none other but the house of Elohim, and this is the gate of heaven” (Genesis 28:16-17).

## The Name Under the Name

Jacob called the place Bethel, the house of El. But Moses adds a short note that most readers pass right over: “but the name of that city was called Luz at the first” (Genesis 28:19).

Luz (H3870) comes from luz (H3869), the almond tree. Before it was ever called the house of El, the place was called Almond.

So the first place in Scripture called “the gate of heaven,” the place where a way opened between earth and heaven with messengers going up and down on it, was the almond place. In Eden the way to the tree of life was shut and guarded. At Luz a way to heaven was shown to Jacob, and it stood at Almond.

## Jacob and the Almond

The almond does not leave Jacob’s story. Years later, working for Laban, Jacob “took him rods of green poplar, and of the hazel and chestnut tree; and pilled white strakes in them” (Genesis 30:37). The KJV says hazel, but the word is luz again, the almond.

Jacob, the man who saw the gate at Almond, carried almond rods in his hands. Yahuah used those peeled rods and the white of the almond wood to build Jacob’s flocks, though Jacob later made clear the increase came from Elohim, not from the rods (Genesis 31:9-13). In that same passage the messenger said, “I am the El of Bethel” (Genesis 31:13). Yahuah tied Himself to the almond place by name.

## Back to Almond

When Jacob came home to the land, he settled at Shechem. Then Elohim told him, “Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there” (Genesis 35:1).

Before he went, Jacob gave his household an order: “Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments” (Genesis 35:2). They handed over their idols and earrings, and Jacob buried them under the oak by Shechem (Genesis 35:4). Then they went up “to Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, that is, Bethel” (Genesis 35:6).

No one went up to the almond place carrying other gods. The first commandment was kept on the road to Luz, centuries before Sinai. This is not surprising. Yahuah had already said of Abraham that he “kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws” (Genesis 26:5).

## The Promise Spoken Again

At Luz, Elohim appeared to Jacob a second time. He changed his name to Israel and said, “I am El Shaddai: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee... And the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee I will give it, and to thy seed after thee will I give the land” (Genesis 35:10-12).

Jacob set up a pillar of stone, poured a drink offering on it, and anointed it with oil (Genesis 35:14). Near the end of his life, he told Joseph, “El Shaddai appeared unto me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed me” (Genesis 48:3). When Jacob remembered the place, he used its first name. He called it Almond.

Abraham had been there before him. On his first journey into the land, Abram pitched his tent “having Bethel on the west, and Hai on the east: and there he builded an altar unto Yahuah” (Genesis 12:8), and he came back to that same altar after Egypt (Genesis 13:3-4). The fathers kept returning to the almond place to call on the name of Yahuah.

## The Ladder and the Way

Yahushua took Jacob’s dream and applied it to Himself. He told Nathanael, “Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of Elohim ascending and descending upon the Son of man” (John 1:51).

The way between heaven and earth that Jacob saw at Almond runs through Messiah. Yahushua said plainly, “I am the way” (John 14:6). He is not the One who sits at the top of the ladder. Yahuah stood above it (Genesis 28:13). Yahushua is the way Yahuah provided, the ladder on which heaven comes down and man goes up. The way that was kept shut in Eden (Genesis 3:24) was shown open at Luz, and in Messiah it is opened for good.

## A Warning at the Gate

The almond place did not stay pure. Jeroboam set up a golden calf at Bethel and told Israel, “behold thy gods, O Israel” (1 Kings 12:28-29). Hosea stopped calling it Bethel, the house of El, and called it Beth-aven, the house of vanity (Hosea 4:15). The gate of heaven became the gate of a counterfeit the moment Israel brought other gods back up the road Jacob had cleared.

## Right There All Along

Bethel is a familiar Sunday school name. Luz is almost never mentioned. Yet Moses made a point of recording it twice (Genesis 28:19; 35:6), and Jacob used it on his deathbed (Genesis 48:3). Under the house of El lies the name Almond, and it was there in the text the whole time.

## Where It Ends

Jacob called Almond the house of Elohim and the gate of heaven. At the end, the house and the gate come down together: “Behold, the tabernacle of Elohim is with men, and he will dwell with them” (Revelation 21:3), and the gates of the city “shall not be shut at all by day” (Revelation 21:25).
