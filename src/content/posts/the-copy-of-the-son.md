## A Counterfeit, Not a Monster

Revelation 13 is commonly read as a portrait of horror: a creature of heads, horns, and crowns rising from the sea to terrorize the earth. Read beside the gospels, the chapter shows something far more deliberate. The sea beast is built point by point on the life of Yahushua. It rises out of the water, bears the likeness of the one who sends it, receives its power and throne from another, is slain and lives again, holds its office for a measured half-week of time, and is worshipped by the world. Each feature is a copy of something already fulfilled in the Son.

The dragon creates nothing. He can only imitate what Yahuah has already done, and the imitation is his signature. The title *antichristos* (G500) carries this sense in its prefix, for *anti* (G473) means not only "against" but "in the place of." The beast does not merely oppose the Messiah. It stands in His place and wears His pattern.

## Out of Babylon

Daniel received the matching vision in Babylon itself, while Israel sat in captivity:

> *In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed* (Daniel 7:1)

He saw four great beasts come up from the sea (Daniel 7:3): a lion, a bear, a leopard, and a fourth beast with ten horns (Daniel 7:4-7). These are the powers that rose out of the captivity of Israel and ruled over her. John's beast gathers all of them into a single body:

> *And the beast which I saw was like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion* (Revelation 13:2)

The order is reversed. Daniel looked forward across the kingdoms; John looks back across them, from leopard to bear to lion. The beast is the sum of the empires that followed the captivity, carrying Babylon's lion mouth on the front of a ten-horned body. Revelation names the end of that line plainly as Mystery Babylon (Revelation 17:5).

The sea is not open water. The angel interprets it for John:

> *The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues.* (Revelation 17:15)

The beast rises out of the nations of men. Everything that follows is a man-made, dragon-empowered imitation of the Son whom Yahuah raised up from among His own people, as Moses foretold: "Yahuah thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me" (Deuteronomy 18:15; Acts 3:22, 26).

## Seven Marks of the Copy

### 1. Out of the Water

> *And I stood upon the sand of the sea, and saw a beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns, and upon his horns ten crowns, and upon his heads the name of blasphemy.* (Revelation 13:1)

> *And Yahushua, when he was baptized, went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him* (Matthew 3:16)

The public work of Yahushua began as He came up out of the waters of the Jordan. The heavens opened, the Spirit descended, and the Father's voice named Him the beloved Son (Matthew 3:17). The public work of the beast begins the same way, rising up out of the waters of the peoples. One is anointed by the Father from heaven as He comes up; the other is crowned from below, ten crowns on its horns and the name of blasphemy on its heads.

### 2. The Likeness of the One Who Sends Him

The dragon of Revelation 12:3 has seven heads and ten horns. The beast of Revelation 13:1 has seven heads and ten horns. The beast is the visible likeness of an unseen dragon, standing on the sand while the dragon works behind it.

The original stands in the Son. He is "the express image of his person" (Hebrews 1:3), where *charakter* (G5481) is the stamped impression left by a seal. He is "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15), where *eikon* (G1504) is a likeness drawn from another. He said plainly, "he that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9).

Resemblance is not identity, and the copy proves it. The beast resembles the dragon and is never the dragon; the two are named separately, act separately, and are judged separately. The Son is the image of Yahuah and is never Yahuah. An image is always the image of another. The seal and the impression are two things, not one being in two forms.

### 3. Power, Throne, and Authority Given

> *and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority.* (Revelation 13:2)

> *All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth.* (Matthew 28:18)

The beast receives power (*dunamis*, G1411), a throne (*thronos*, G2362), and authority (*exousia*, G1849). The Son likewise receives His *exousia*: "The Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand" (John 3:35), and "The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do" (John 5:19). In both cases there is a giver and a receiver. Neither the beast nor the Son originates the authority he holds.

The dragon had already offered this exact arrangement to Yahushua in the wilderness:

> *All this power will I give thee, and the glory of them: for that is delivered unto me; and to whomsoever I will I give it.* (Luke 4:6)

Yahushua refused and answered from the Torah: "Thou shalt worship Yahuah thy Elohim, and him only shalt thou serve" (Luke 4:8; Deuteronomy 6:13). The beast is the one who said yes. What the Son would receive only from His Father, the beast takes from the dragon.

### 4. Slain and Living Again

> *And I saw one of his heads as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast.* (Revelation 13:3)

The phrase "wounded to death" is built on *sphazo* (G4969), "to slay, to slaughter." It is the Lamb's own word. John uses it for "a Lamb as it had been slain" (Revelation 5:6), for the Lamb who was slain and redeemed men by His blood (Revelation 5:9, 12), and for "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" in this very chapter (Revelation 13:8). The beast is also said to have "had the wound by a sword, and did live" (Revelation 13:14). That verb, *zao* (G2198), is the same word Messiah uses of Himself to the assembly at Smyrna: "which was dead, and is alive" (Revelation 2:8).

The beast is handed a death and a return to life written in the vocabulary of the crucifixion and resurrection. The difference lies in the fruit. Messiah's resurrection is witnessed and testified (1 Corinthians 15:3-8; Revelation 1:18). The beast's healing produces wonder, and the wonder turns the whole world after it.

### 5. Forty-Two Months and the Midst of the Week

> *and power was given unto him to continue forty and two months.* (Revelation 13:5)

Forty-two months is the same span Revelation also gives as 1,260 days and as "a time, and times, and half a time" (Revelation 11:2-3; 12:6, 14). It is three and a half years, half of a week of years.

The Gospel of John carries the measure of Messiah's ministry through the feasts it records: a Pesach (John 2:13), an unnamed feast (John 5:1), a second Pesach (John 6:4), Sukkot (John 7:2), and the final Pesach (John 11:55). With the feast of John 5:1 standing as the Pesach of the intervening year, the record holds four Pesachs. Counted from the baptism in the autumn, when Yahushua "began to be about thirty years of age" (Luke 3:23), to His death at the final Pesach, the ministry runs three and a half years, from 27 AD to 31 AD.

Daniel had already set that half-week in place:

> *And he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease* (Daniel 9:27)

Modern futurist teaching cuts the seventieth week loose from the Messiah, pushes it into a future seven-year tribulation, and hands the covenant of Daniel 9:27 to the antichrist. The text runs the other way. The one who confirms the covenant and causes the sacrifice to cease is the Messiah, who ended the sacrifice by becoming it (Hebrews 10:12, 18). The half-week belongs to the Son and is finished. The beast receives only its copy. Whether the forty-two months are read as days or under the year-day principle (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6), the number was chosen to mirror a ministry already completed.

### 6. Worship After the Healing

> *And they worshipped the dragon which gave power unto the beast: and they worshipped the beast* (Revelation 13:4)

The order in Revelation 13:3-4 is precise: the wound is healed, the world wonders, and then the world worships. After the resurrection the women "held him by the feet, and worshipped him" (Matthew 28:9), and the eleven worshipped Him on the mountain in Galilee (Matthew 28:17).

This verse answers one of the most common Trinitarian arguments, the claim that because the Son receives worship He must be Yahuah. The word is *proskuneo* (G4352). The beast receives *proskuneo*, and the beast is not the dragon. The worship given to the beast passes through to the dragon "which gave power unto the beast." The original works the same way. The angels worship the Son because the Father commands it (Hebrews 1:6), and every tongue confesses Him "to the glory of God the Father" (Philippians 2:11). Honor given to the sent one rises to the Sender. Receiving worship never made the beast the dragon, and it never made the Son the Father.

### 7. Who Is Like Unto the Beast?

> *saying, Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?* (Revelation 13:4)

> *Who is like unto thee, O Yahuah, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?* (Exodus 15:11)

This is the deepest mark of the copy. Exodus 15:11 is the song Israel sang at the sea after Pharaoh's army went under the water. The Hebrew is *mi kamokah*, from *mi* (H4310, "who") and *kemo* (H3644, "like"). The Greek Old Testament renders it *tis homoios soi*, and Revelation 13:4 uses the same construction, *tis homoios to therio*. The worshippers of the beast take the song Israel sang to Yahuah at the sea and sing it to a beast that came up out of the sea.

The name of the angel who casts the dragon out of heaven carries the same question. *Mikael* (H4317) means "who is like El" (Revelation 12:7-9). The dragon's answer to his defeat is to raise a beast and have the whole world ask Michael's question of it. Yahuah settles the matter Himself: "I am God, and there is none else; I am God, and there is none like me" (Isaiah 46:9). The speaker is one, and the answer is singular.

## The Name and the Forehead

> *I am come in my Father's name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive.* (John 5:43)

Yahushua came bearing His Father's name, and His own name declares it: Yahushua, "Yahuah saves." The beast comes in its own name, and that name is blasphemy against Yahuah's name and His tabernacle (Revelation 13:6). Those who follow it receive "the name of the beast, or the number of his name" (Revelation 13:17).

The mark is placed "in their right hand, or in their foreheads" (Revelation 13:16). That location is not random. Torah commanded Israel to bind Yahuah's words "for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes" (Deuteronomy 6:8). The mark of the beast is the counterfeit of the commandments carried in the hand and the mind. The original stands in the next chapter: the company on Mount Zion have "his Father's name written in their foreheads" (Revelation 14:1), and those who endure are described as the ones who keep the commandments of Yahuah and hold the belief of Yahushua (Revelation 14:12). The blood of the Lamb brings a man in; the commandments of the Father mark how he walks once he is in.

## The "Unholy Trinity" Answered

Popular teaching labels the dragon, the beast, and the false prophet (Revelation 16:13) an "unholy trinity," assumed to be a mirror image of a holy one. The text of Revelation 13 does not support the label, and it does not support the original it assumes.

The three are ranked. The dragon gives (Revelation 13:2). The beast receives (Revelation 13:2). The second beast "exerciseth all the power of the first beast before him, and causeth the earth and them which dwell therein to worship the first beast" (Revelation 13:12). There is a giver, a receiver, and a servant who points away from himself. Nothing in the arrangement is co-equal or co-eternal. Even the "life" the second beast gives to the image is *pneuma* (G4151), breath and power exercised, not a third person.

The standard reply is that the third member mirrors the Holy Spirit, since the Spirit "shall glorify me" (John 16:14) and the false prophet glorifies the beast. The reply fails at every point the text can test.

### Scripture Names His Office

Revelation never calls him a spirit. It calls him a prophet, and it does so three times: "the false prophet" (Revelation 16:13; 19:20; 20:10). The word is *pseudoprophetes* (G5578), a false *prophetes* (G4396). The counterfeit is named by the office it copies, and the office it copies is the prophet.

Torah described this man long before John saw him:

> *If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, and the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods* (Deuteronomy 13:1-2)

The second beast does exactly what Deuteronomy 13 warns of. He does great wonders and deceives "by the means of those miracles which he had power to do" (Revelation 13:13-14), and he turns the earth to worship another. The test of a prophet in Torah is the test Revelation applies to him, and he fails it as a prophet.

### His Sign Is a Prophet's Sign

The false prophet "maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men" (Revelation 13:13). That is the sign of Elijah on Carmel, when "the fire of Yahuah fell, and consumed the burnt sacrifice" (1 Kings 18:38), and Elijah called down fire again in 2 Kings 1:10-12. Fire from heaven is the credential of a prophet of Yahuah. The false prophet steals a prophet's sign because he is copying a prophet.

Revelation sets the true prophets in front of him two chapters earlier. The two witnesses "shall prophesy" (Revelation 11:3), are called "these two prophets" (Revelation 11:10), and "fire proceedeth out of their mouth" (Revelation 11:5). The book itself places true prophets with fire against a false prophet with fire. The pairing is prophet against prophet, not Spirit against counterfeit spirit.

### The Spirits Come Out of All Three

> *And I saw three unclean spirits like frogs come out of the mouth of the dragon, and out of the mouth of the beast, and out of the mouth of the false prophet. For they are the spirits of devils, working miracles* (Revelation 16:13-14)

The frog is the plague of Egypt. Frogs came up out of the river into Pharaoh's house and bedchamber (Exodus 8:3), and Pharaoh's magicians "did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs upon the land of Egypt" (Exodus 8:7), making the frog the sign that the deceivers of Egypt were able to copy, which is exactly the work of these spirits that go out to deceive with miracles.

This verse ends the argument. If the false prophet were the counterfeit Holy Spirit, the spirit would be one of the three. Instead the spirits are something that comes out of the mouths of all three. A spirit in Revelation 16 is what is breathed out of a mouth, the power and breath of the one who speaks, not a member of the group. The dragon, the beast, and the false prophet each have a spirit, and none of them is one.

That is exactly how Scripture speaks of the Spirit of Yahuah. It is His breath, *ruach* (H7307): "By the word of Yahuah were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth" (Psalm 33:6). It is "the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you" (Matthew 10:20). The Spirit belongs to the Father and goes out from Him the way the unclean spirits go out from the three mouths. The counterfeit copies the pattern exactly: a spirit is what proceeds from someone, never a third someone.

### He Is Judged as a Man

The false prophet has a body and a body's end. He is "a beast coming up out of the earth" with "two horns like a lamb" and a voice like a dragon (Revelation 13:11). He is taken in battle and "cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone" (Revelation 19:20). No one teaches that the Holy Spirit could be captured, or that its counterfeit is a creature thrown alive into a lake. What is captured and cast in is a man who held a prophet's office and used it for the dragon.

### Pointing to the Son Was the Prophets' Work

The claim rests on one idea: that the Spirit is the one who points to the Son, so whoever points to the beast must be the counterfeit Spirit. Scripture gives that work to the prophets first:

> *To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins.* (Acts 10:43)

Yahushua opened "Moses and all the prophets" and showed the disciples "the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27). He told the leaders of His day that Moses "wrote of me" (John 5:46). Peter writes that the prophets "testified beforehand the sufferings of Messiah, and the glory that should follow" (1 Peter 1:10-11), and that "holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost" (2 Peter 1:21). The prophets did the pointing, and the Spirit was what moved them. The Spirit was never a separate pointer standing beside them.

> *for the testimony of Yahushua is the spirit of prophecy.* (Revelation 19:10)

The angel says this in the same breath as "worship God" and only two chapters after the false prophet's doom is set. Testifying to the Son is the spirit of prophecy. The false prophet takes that office, the prophet's office, and turns its testimony toward the beast.

### What the Copy Actually Mirrors

Laid side by side, the copy shows its original plainly. Yahuah sends; the dragon sends. The Son is sent, receives all things, and comes in His Father's name; the beast is sent, receives the dragon's power, and comes in its own. The prophets testify of the Son and call men to worship the Father; the false prophet testifies of the beast and calls men to worship the dragon. The spirit in each case is the breath that proceeds from the one who sends.

That is the Father, the sent Son, and the prophets, which is the pattern of the whole Bible. Hebrews opens with exactly that order: "God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son" (Hebrews 1:1-2). One God speaking, first through prophets and last through a Son. The dragon copied that, and Christianity looked at the copy and called it a trinity. The copy contains no co-equality and no third person, because the original never had either.

## The Answer at the Sea of Glass

Revelation does not leave the stolen question unanswered. Those who triumphed over the beast, over his image, over his mark, and over the number of his name stand on a sea of glass and sing the song of Moses, the servant of Yahuah, and the song of the Lamb (Revelation 15:2-3). The song begun at the sea of Exodus is finished at the sea of glass:

> *Who shall not fear thee, O Yahuah, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy* (Revelation 15:4)

The beast asked who was like him, and the overcomers answer that Yahuah alone is holy. The Lamb who was truly slain and truly lives is the image of Yahuah that the dragon could only copy, and the copy ends in the lake of fire while the original stands forever (Revelation 19:20; 22:3).
