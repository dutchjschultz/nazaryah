The six-pointed star, called in Hebrew *Magen David*, shield of David, flies on the flag of the modern State of Israel and marks synagogues, gravestones, and ambulances. To the world it means Jewish. Yet for most of Israel's history the symbol of the people was the menorah, the lampstand of pure gold Yahuah commanded Moses to make (Exodus 25:31-40). It is the menorah that Rome carved on the Arch of Titus after the temple fell in AD 70.

## How It Became the Symbol

The hexagram is an ancient shape found in many lands and many religions, used as decoration in churches and mosques as well as synagogues. A six-pointed star appears on a decorated page of the Leningrad Codex, the oldest complete Hebrew Bible, copied about AD 1008. In medieval Christian and Islamic lore the same shape was called the Seal of Solomon and drawn on charms.

The first known use of it as the emblem of a Jewish community came in Prague, where in 1354 the Jews were granted a flag bearing it. Over the next centuries it spread through central Europe as a community mark. In the 1800s, as the churches had the cross, Jewish communities in Europe settled on the six-pointed star as their own sign. The First Zionist Congress adopted it for its flag in 1897, and the State of Israel placed it on its flag in 1948.

## The Reasons Jews Give

The name carries the meaning most give it. David called Yahuah his shield again and again: "Yahuah is my rock... my shield" (Psalm 18:2), and "Yahuah is my strength and my shield" (Psalm 28:7). Yahuah said to Abraham, "I am thy shield" (Genesis 15:1). To many Jews the star stands for divine protection over David's people, and for the hope of a coming king from David's line.

The twentieth century added a second meaning. Under Nazi rule, Jews were forced to wear a yellow six-pointed star as a badge of shame. After the Holocaust the same star became a badge of survival, and placing it on the flag of a new nation was an answer to those who had used it to mark Jews for death.

## What It Means to Those Who Display It
