Ezekiel dates this vision with care: “In the five and twentieth year of our captivity, in the beginning of the year, in the tenth day of the month, in the fourteenth year after that the city was smitten, in the selfsame day” (40:1). That is about 573 BC. Jerusalem has been a ruin for fourteen years. The temple is ashes. The last time Ezekiel saw the glory of Yahuah, it was leaving: it lifted off the house, stopped at the east gate, and went out of the city to the mountain on the east side (10:18-19; 11:23). For fourteen years the captives have lived with that picture. Their house is burned, and the glory is gone. The fourth vision answers the question they could not stop asking: will He ever come back?

## The Vision

Ezekiel is set on a very high mountain, and a man with a measuring reed walks him through a house, measuring every wall and gate. The glory of Yahuah comes from the east and fills the house, and Yahuah names it the place of His throne forever. The east gate is shut behind Him. A river flows from under the threshold and heals the Dead Sea. The stranger inherits among the tribes, and the city is named “Yahuah is there.”

## The Selfsame Day

The date carries a message of its own. “The beginning of the year” and “the tenth day of the month” point back to the very first beginning of Israel’s year. On the tenth day of the first month, every house in Israel was told to take a lamb and set it apart for Pesach (Exodus 12:2-3). The captives are shown the end of their road on the day of the year that once marked its start.

## The Man With the Measuring Reed

The vision opens with a man “whose appearance was like the appearance of brass, with a line of flax in his hand, and a measuring reed” (40:3), and Ezekiel is told, “declare all that thou seest to the house of Israel” (40:4).

A measuring line can measure for ruin, as with “the line of Samaria” stretched over Jerusalem (2 Kings 21:13), or for building. Here it measures for building. Jeremiah had promised that “the measuring line shall yet go forth” over a rebuilt city that “shall not be plucked up, nor thrown down any more for ever” (Jeremiah 31:39-40), and Zechariah would later see “a man with a measuring line in his hand” going out to measure Jerusalem (Zechariah 2:1-2). Whatever Yahuah measures, He is claiming.

## A Pattern, on a Condition

The house is measured down to the cubit, and many readers take that to mean it must one day be built of stone exactly as drawn. But Yahuah tells Ezekiel why he is being shown it: “shew the house to the house of Israel, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities: and let them measure the pattern. And if they be ashamed of all that they have done, shew them the form of the house” (43:10-11).

The house is a pattern, shown on a condition: if they are ashamed. The measurements show a people how far they had fallen, and what Yahuah would build for a people who turned. It was never built of stone; the returning captives built something far smaller (Ezra 3:12). The Ezekiel Quick Scroll, [*Ezekiel: The Glory Leaves and Returns*](/blog/ezekiel-the-glory-leaves-and-returns), explains why this house is not a stone temple for a kingdom of a thousand years on the present earth.

## The Glory Comes Home

Then the center of the whole book. “Behold, the glory of the Elohim of Israel came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters: and the earth shined with his glory” (43:2). Ezekiel makes sure the reader knows it is the same glory: “according to the vision that I saw when I came to destroy the city: and the visions were like the vision that I saw by the river Chebar” (43:3). The throne from the river, the throne that drove away from Jerusalem, is coming back.

“And the glory of Yahuah came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east … and, behold, the glory of Yahuah filled the house” (43:4-5). It left by the east, and it comes back by the east. The road out is the road home.

Scripture has seen this twice before: the glory filled the tabernacle when Moses finished it (Exodus 40:34-35), and filled Solomon’s temple at its dedication (1 Kings 8:10-11). Each time it was Yahuah’s answer that He had come to live among His people. Now the house that was emptied is filled again.

## The Place of My Throne

Yahuah names the house Himself: “Son of man, the place of my throne, and the place of the soles of my feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever, and my holy name, shall the house of Israel no more defile” (43:7). The throne that rolled on wheels in Babylon comes to rest, and this time the word is “for ever.”

Then Ezekiel is shown the east gate shut: “This gate shall be shut, it shall not be opened, and no man shall enter in by it; because Yahuah, the Elohim of Israel, hath entered in by it, therefore it shall be shut” (44:2). The gate the glory left by is closed behind Him. He is not leaving again.

## The Difference Between Holy and Profane

The house comes with a priesthood, and the priests are given a specific charge: “they shall teach my people the difference between the holy and profane, and cause them to discern between the unclean and the clean,” and “they shall keep my laws and my statutes in all mine assemblies; and they shall hallow my sabbaths” (44:23-24).

That charge is the exact reverse of the sin that drove the glory out. In chapter 22 Yahuah had said of Jerusalem: “Her priests have violated my law, and have profaned mine holy things: they have put no difference between the holy and the profane … and have hid their eyes from my sabbaths” (22:26). The glory left a house where no difference was kept. It comes home to a house where the difference is taught. The Law did not end when the glory returned. It was finally kept.

## The Offerings and the Prince

The house has an altar, and offerings are named in it (43:18-27; 45:17-25). A prince offers in the house, and he brings a sin offering “for himself” (45:22), and he has sons who inherit from him (46:16). A man who needs a sin offering for himself and leaves an inheritance to his sons is not the Messiah. The offerings are covenant language, the words of the altar that a captive priest understood, describing a house that lacks nothing the Law required. The sin offering is named because it was never thrown out. It is at rest, because the one offering “hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:14). The Nazaryah study [*Three Verses After the End*](/blog/three-verses-after-the-end) lays this out in full.

## The River From the Threshold

Then Ezekiel is brought back to the door of the house, “and, behold, waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward” (47:1). The man with the reed measures a thousand cubits, and the water is to the ankles. Another thousand, and it is to the knees. Another, and it is to the loins. Another, and it is “waters to swim in, a river that could not be passed over” (47:3-5). It does not get smaller as it goes. It gets deeper.

The river flows down into the desert and into the Dead Sea, “and the waters shall be healed” (47:8). “Every thing shall live whither the river cometh” (47:9). On both banks grow trees whose leaf does not fade, bearing “new fruit according to his months,” and “the fruit thereof shall be for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine” (47:12).

Follow that river back through Scripture. The first river in the Bible is in Eden: “a river went out of Eden to water the garden” (Genesis 2:10). Joel and Zechariah both saw water going out from Yahuah’s house (Joel 3:18; Zechariah 14:8). And Yahushua stood up at the feast and said, “He that believeth on me … out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water,” and John explains, “this spake he of the Spirit” (John 7:38-39). The river that comes out from where Yahuah dwells is His own life, and wherever it reaches, the dead come alive.

## The Stranger Inherits

When the land is divided among the tribes, Yahuah adds a rule: the strangers who live among them “shall be unto you as born in the country among the children of Israel; they shall have inheritance with you among the tribes of Israel” (47:22). He is not given a separate plot; he is written into the house he joined. One people, one Law.

## Yahuah Is There

The book ends with the city’s gates, three on each side, each one named for a tribe of Israel (48:31-34). Then the last sentence of Ezekiel: “and the name of the city from that day shall be, Yahuah is there” (48:35). In Hebrew, *Yahuah shammah*. The book that began with the throne far off in Babylon ends with a city named for His presence.

## Why This Vision, and Why Now

Fourteen years after the fall, the captives believed the story was over. The fourth vision completes what the first one started. In the first, the throne came to the captives in Babylon to show them Yahuah was not trapped in a building. In the second, the same throne left the building because the people had filled it with idols. In the fourth, the same throne comes back by the same gate, fills a house built to His pattern, and stays, in the midst of a people who have learned the difference between holy and profane.

## Bringing It Home

The house Yahuah intended was never a building. Paul tells believers, “ye are the temple of Elohim” (1 Corinthians 3:16), and says the people joined from the nations are “fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of Elohim … an habitation of Elohim through the Spirit” (Ephesians 2:19, 22). The stranger who inherits among the tribes is every believer brought in from the nations.

The measuring line still comes with its condition. The pattern is shown to those who are ashamed of what they have done. A believer who sees how far he is from the pattern is not shut out; he is being shown the plan. And the charge of the priests is the charge of every believer-priest: know the difference between holy and profane, keep His statutes, and hallow His Sabbaths.

And the river is the proof. A house where Yahuah truly dwells does not keep His life to itself. It flows out, gets deeper as it goes, and heals what was dead.

## Where It Lands

John was carried “to a great and high mountain” and shown the holy city, just as Ezekiel was set “upon a very high mountain” (Revelation 21:10; Ezekiel 40:2). An angel measured it with a golden reed (Revelation 21:15). Its twelve gates carried the names of the twelve tribes, three on the east, three on the north, three on the south, three on the west (Revelation 21:12-13). John saw no temple in it, “for Yahuah Elohim Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it” (Revelation 21:22). And “a pure river of water of life” came out from the throne, with the tree of life on either side, bearing “her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations” (Revelation 22:1-2). “Behold, the tabernacle of Elohim is with men, and he will dwell with them” (Revelation 21:3). Yahuah is there.
