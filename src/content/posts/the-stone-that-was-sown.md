## What Is Taught Now

The mustard seed is handed out as a growth promise. Small beginnings, large results. Twelve men in Galilee became a worldwide institution, and the tree that filled the earth is the church itself. The seed becomes the message, the tree becomes the organization, and the birds become converts who found a home.

That reading has no question behind it. Yahushua never told a story to fill time, and He never assigned a meaning out of thin air. Every parable answers something already in the room. The question in the room that day was not whether the movement would grow. It was why the kingdom had not arrived yet.

## What Was Going On

Rome held the country. There was a governor, a tax, and a garrison, and there was one passage every man in that crowd could recite about it.

Daniel had been shown four kingdoms in a single image — a head of gold, breast of silver, belly of brass, legs of iron — and had been told plainly when the next thing would happen.

> And in the days of these kings shall the Elohim of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever. — Daniel 2:44

In the days of these kings. The fourth kingdom was standing on their necks, so the men listening knew they were inside the window. They were not wondering whether the kingdom would come. They were watching for it daily, and they already had a picture in their heads of how it would look when it did, because Daniel had described that too.

> Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and brake them to pieces... and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth. — Daniel 2:34-35

No one was watching the hills for an actual rock. The stone in that dream is what ends the kingdoms — it strikes the image, and gold, silver, brass and iron all break together and blow away like chaff off a threshing floor. To men living under Rome that meant one thing. An overthrow. An army raised, the occupation broken, the country taken back, and the whole of it done out in the open where no one could miss it and no one could argue with it. That was the arrival they expected, and it is why the men around Yahushua kept asking when.

Nothing in front of them looked like that. He had just been accused of casting out devils by the prince of the devils (Mark 3:22), His own family had come to take Him home saying He was beside Himself (Mark 3:21), and the crowd was so heavy on the shore that He had to teach from a boat. No throne, no army, no visible break in Rome's grip.

So He asked the question Himself, out loud, because it was the one they were carrying.

> And he said, Whereunto shall we liken the kingdom of Yahuah? or with what comparison shall we compare it? It is like a grain of mustard seed, which, when it is sown in the earth, is less than all the seeds that be in the earth: But when it is sown, it groweth up, and becometh greater than all herbs, and shooteth out great branches; so that the fowls of the air may lodge under the shadow of it. — Mark 4:30-32

## Why the Two Adjectives Are There

The parable does not simply say a seed grows. It marks both ends. Less than all the seeds that be in the earth. Greater than all herbs. Two superlatives in three lines, and they are not decoration.

They are Daniel 2:35. One picture in the Hebrew Scriptures holds the smallest thing and the greatest thing in the same frame and calls them the same object — a stone small enough to be carried in a hand, and the mountain that filled the whole earth. Daniel gives both ends and no middle. He does not say how the stone became the mountain or how long it took.

That missing middle is what Yahushua supplies. The two ends of His parable are Daniel's two ends. What He puts between them is the part Daniel never showed: it is sown in the earth, and it grows.

## What He Was Correcting

The hearers had the outcome right and the arrival wrong. They were watching for a conquest. Yahushua told them to watch the ground.

An overthrow is sudden, public, and done to a country from the outside. A seed sown is buried, unseen, and works from inside the field it was dropped into. Both end as the largest thing in the picture. They do not start the same way, and they cannot be recognised the same way.

The seed was the thing standing in front of them. A man out of Nazareth, born in Bethlehem with almost no one aware it had happened, no throne behind Him, no army in front of Him, teaching from a boat on a lake in Galilee. Everything about the beginning was quiet, and that is precisely why they could not see it. It had already gone into the ground and was already growing, and it ends as the greatest thing in the field.

That is why the parable was needed at all. A man waiting for an army will look straight past a seed. He will call the thing in front of him too small to be the kingdom, which is exactly what the scribes from Jerusalem had just done out loud.

The breaking of the image is not in the parable. Daniel said the stone would break the kingdoms to pieces, and Yahushua left that clause alone. Where the text is silent nothing is filled in. What He addressed was the beginning, because that was what they had wrong.

The same mistake is taught now. Modern teaching puts Daniel's stone at a second coming, leaves the kingdom unstarted, and then needs a thousand-year reign to hold everything it has pushed into the future — one misreading propped up by the next. It is the crowd on that shore all over again, still watching for the crash, twenty centuries after the seed went into the ground.

## Who Lodges in the Branches

The last line of the parable is the only part that names anyone living in the kingdom, and the Hebrew Scriptures had already used that picture. Ezekiel describes Assyria as a great cedar and says the fowls of heaven nested in his boughs and all great nations dwelt under his shadow (Ezekiel 31:6). Birds in the branches of a kingdom are the peoples who live under it. Daniel uses the same picture of Babylon and says outright that the tree is the king and his dominion (Daniel 4:20-22).

The prophets also use birds for Yahuah's own people, and they use them for the moment those people come back to Him.

> They shall walk after Yahuah: he shall roar like a lion: when he shall roar, then the children shall tremble from the west. They shall tremble as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria: and I will place them in their houses, saith Yahuah. — Hosea 11:10-11

Birds out of Egypt and doves out of Assyria, coming in from the nations, and Yahuah settling them in their houses. Isaiah asks the same thing at the gathering of Zion — who are these that fly as a cloud, and as doves to their windows (Isaiah 60:8). Coming home and being given a place to stay is what a bird does in these passages, and it is exactly what the parable says they do: lodge.

The shadow is settled the same way. To dwell under a shadow in the Hebrew Scriptures is to take refuge with Yahuah — the children of men put their trust under the shadow of His wings (Psalm 36:7), and the man who dwells in the secret place abides under the shadow of the Almighty (Psalm 91:1).

Isaiah names what they come for. When the mountain of Yahuah's house is established and all nations flow to it, the people who come say why they are coming: to be taught His ways and to walk in His paths.

> For out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of Yahuah from Jerusalem. — Isaiah 2:3

That is the whole business of the tree. It grew so that people could come in out of the nations, be taught the law of Yahuah, and live under it.

## The Verdict

The parable answers the question the crowd on that shore was living with. Daniel said it would happen in the days of these kings, the kings were there, so where was it.

It had already started, and it did not start the way they pictured. The kingdom Daniel saw as a stone entered the world as a seed — buried, unnoticed, smaller than anything else in the ground — and it ends as the greatest thing in the field with the nations lodging in it. Daniel also names who does the work. The Elohim of heaven sets the kingdom up, the stone is cut out without hands, and Yahushua speaks of the kingdom as Yahuah's rather than as His own.

What modern teaching does with this parable is stop at the size. But a tree is not measured by how far its branches reach. It is measured by what the people under it are given, and Isaiah said what that is. A house that teaches the law was set aside has grown branches with nothing underneath them.

They were watching for something to be overthrown. It had already been planted.
