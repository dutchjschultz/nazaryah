If the believer is a temple of the renewed covenant, and the kingdom he belongs to is unseen, and the adversary is called the prince of this world, then a fair question follows: does Yahuah concern Himself with the physical world at all? And if He does not, how is a man guided through it? The question is not idle. It assumes the ground underfoot belongs to someone else, that guidance covers spiritual matters only, and that the ordinary decisions of work, money, and neighbor are left to be worked out alone inside enemy territory. Every part of that assumption is answered, and the answer begins with a deed.

## Who Is in Charge

> "The earth is Yahuah's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein." — Psalm 24:1

The deed is stated in the present tense and was never transferred.

> "The most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will." — Daniel 4:17

That is a statement about who holds office on the ground, not about heaven.

Two princes are named in the same Scriptures, and the Greek uses one word for both. The adversary is archon (G758) of this world (John 12:31), also called the god of this age (2 Corinthians 4:4). Yahushua is archon of the kings of the earth (Revelation 1:5). The title settles nothing by itself. What settles it is who granted the authority and for how long.

> "All this power will I give thee, and the glory of them: for that is delivered unto me; and to whomsoever I will I give it." — Luke 4:6

Delivered unto him. Handed over, by permission, for a term.

> "There was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed." — Daniel 7:14

Both authorities were received. Neither prince is the source of his own rule, and the Son says so plainly: "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth" (Matthew 28:18). The difference is the term. One grant runs for a season and ends. The other was given by the Ancient of days and shall not pass away.

The two claims collide in the wilderness account, and the collision is settled in one exchange. The adversary offers all the kingdoms of the world on one condition: worship.

> "If thou therefore wilt worship me, all shall be thine." — Luke 4:7

> "Get thee behind me, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship Yahuah thy God, and him only shalt thou serve." — Luke 4:8

The offer of the world's systems was refused, not negotiated. That is the ruling on the ownership question. One owner, one occupier, and no trade.

## Both Kingdoms Have Laws

Every reign issues statutes. Both of these do, and they are written down.

The law of Yahuah's reign:

> "This is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous." — 1 John 5:3

The law of the system:

> "All that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world." — 1 John 2:16

> "Wherein in time past ye walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air." — Ephesians 2:2

Paul sets the two statute books side by side as lists. The works of the flesh in Galatians 5:19-21, and the fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5:22-23, are the conduct codes of the two reigns.

The ordinary case is submission to the governing authority:

> "Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers." — Romans 13:1

The collision case is stated just as plainly, and it was said to a ruling council:

> "We ought to obey God rather than men." — Acts 5:29

The pattern holds wherever the two laws meet. The midwives refused the king of Egypt and were dealt well with (Exodus 1:17-20). The three refused the image and stated the terms before the fire was lit: "be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods" (Daniel 3:18). Daniel kept his windows open and prayed as before (Daniel 6:10). None of them took up arms, and none of them complied.

Yahushua fixed the boundary with a coin:

> "Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's." — Matthew 22:21

The coin carried Caesar's image, so the coin went to Caesar. Man carries Yahuah's image (Genesis 1:27), so the man goes to Yahuah. The rule is obedience to the system until obedience requires disobedience to Yahuah, then refusal, and then the cost is accepted.

## The Two Commands That Look Opposite

The question offers two options: the believer overtakes the system for good, or Yahuah has written the world off and only strengthens followers.

Commands that send the believer into the physical world:

- "Seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives" — Jeremiah 29:7.
- "As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men" — Galatians 6:10.
- "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works" — Matthew 5:16.
- "Occupy till I come" — Luke 19:13.

Commands that pull the believer out of it:

- "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins" — Revelation 18:4.
- "Come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith Yahuah, and touch not the unclean thing" — 2 Corinthians 6:17.
- "The friendship of the world is enmity with God" — James 4:4.
- "Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world" — 1 John 2:15.

The two sets are reconciled in one sentence, spoken in the same prayer:

> "I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil... As thou hast sent me into the world, even so have I also sent them into the world." — John 17:15, 18

Kept in it and sent into it. The separation commanded is from the system, not from the ground. "Her" in Revelation 18:4 is Babylon, a system with merchants, cargo, and commerce (Revelation 18:11-13), not the earth itself.

## The Parable That Settles It

The exact question — whether servants should move against the corruption in the field — is asked in a parable, and answered.

> "Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up? But he said, Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them. Let both grow together until the harvest." — Matthew 13:28-30

Yahushua gives the key Himself: "The field is the world" (Matthew 13:38), and "the reapers are the angels" (Matthew 13:39).

The purging of the field is scheduled, assigned, and given to someone else. The servants are told not to attempt it, and a reason is given: the attempt damages the wheat. The same order appears in the net, where the sorting happens when the net is full, at the end of the age (Matthew 13:47-50).

The field is not the believer's to clean. The believer's work in the field is stated elsewhere, and it is not idleness.

## The Assignment

> "Occupy till I come." — Luke 19:13

The verb is pragmateuomai (G4231) — to conduct business, to trade, to work capital. It is a commercial word, not a military or a devotional one. The accounting demanded when the nobleman returns confirms it: "that he might know how much every man had gained by trading" (Luke 19:15). Physical means, physical labor, physical results, during the owner's absence.

The effect assigned is preservative and revealing, not restorative.

> "Ye are the salt of the earth." — Matthew 5:13

Salt slows corruption in what is already dying. It does not restore the meat.

> "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven." — Matthew 5:16

The works are seen. Anything seen is physical. The word is erga kala — works that are visibly good, on display. The same standard is applied everywhere the matter is tested: "I will shew thee my faith by my works" (James 2:18); and at the judgment the criterion is food, drink, clothing, and visitation (Matthew 25:35-36). The works are also permanent — "their works do follow them" (Revelation 14:13).

What is not assigned is shown by what Yahushua declined. He refused a crown offered by the crowd: "when Jesus therefore perceived that they would come and take him by force, to make him a king, he departed" (John 6:15). He refused to arbitrate an inheritance: "Man, who made me a judge or a divider over you?" (Luke 12:14). And He barred His servants from fighting for territory:

> "If my kingdom were of this world, then would my servants fight." — John 18:36

> "The weapons of our warfare are not carnal." — 2 Corinthians 10:4

> "No man that warreth entangleth himself with the affairs of this life." — 2 Timothy 2:4

The physical commands that remain are aimed at persons, not institutions: the roof railing so no man falls (Deuteronomy 22:8), the corners of the field left standing (Leviticus 19:9-10), the wage paid before the light goes (Leviticus 19:13), just weights and balances (Leviticus 19:35-36), the enemy's beast lifted from under its load (Exodus 23:5).

## The End Is Physical

The reign does not remain unseen.

- New heavens and a new earth — Isaiah 65:17; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 21:1.
- "From one new moon to another, and from one Sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me" — Isaiah 66:23.

Ground, bodies, a calendar, and worship in the flesh. The reign began physical at Sinai and ends physical on a restored earth.

## The Answer

The kingdom is not where a man lives. It is who rules him, and the King left His statutes in writing.

Yahuah directs the physical walk because He wrote the physical instruction first. He is not restoring the system and He has not written off the ground. He is working a harvest on occupied soil, and every physical command He gives is aimed at people.

Improve the ground: commanded. Reach the people: the entire point. Clean the field: not the servant's to attempt, and already scheduled.
