*Witness Four of Five in the investigation [**The Tree in the Midst**](/investigations/the-tree-in-the-midst) — back to the case.*

## The Question Behind the Rods

Every reader of Genesis 3 is left with a question: once death has entered, can life ever come back? Yahuah answered it in the wilderness, with an almond.

Korah and his company had risen up against Moses and Aaron, claiming that “all the congregation are holy” and that anyone could take the priesthood (Numbers 16:3). The earth opened and swallowed the leaders, and fire consumed the two hundred and fifty men who offered incense (Numbers 16:31-35). Even then the people murmured. Yahuah settled the matter once and for all.

## Twelve Dead Sticks

Moses took a rod from the prince of each tribe, twelve rods in all, and wrote each man’s name on his rod. Aaron’s name was written on the rod of Levi. The rods were laid up “in the tabernacle of the congregation before the testimony” (Numbers 17:2-4).

Yahuah said, “the man’s rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom” (Numbers 17:5). These were not branches cut from a living tree that morning. They were staffs, dead wood, cut off from any root, soil, or water.

## Bud, Blossom, and Almond

“And it came to pass, that on the morrow Moses went into the tabernacle of witness; and, behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds” (Numbers 17:8).

In one night, a dead almond stick ran through every stage of a living tree: bud, blossom, and ripe fruit. These are the same three stages beaten into the gold lampstand, knop, flower, and almond bowl (Exodus 25:33). The tree of gold in the Holy Place showed what Yahuah’s life looks like. Aaron’s rod showed that He can put it into dead wood.

The life did not come from the stick. It came from Yahuah, before whose testimony the stick was laid overnight. The rod was set before the Law, and in the morning it was alive.

## Kept With the Law

Yahuah told Moses, “Bring Aaron’s rod again before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the rebels” (Numbers 17:10). Hebrews names where it was kept: in the ark of the covenant, “wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant” (Hebrews 9:4).

The living almond and the tables of the Law were carried in the same chest.

Scripture adds one more detail that should be faced honestly. When Solomon brought the ark into the temple, “there was nothing in the ark save the two tables of stone” (1 Kings 8:9). By then the rod was gone. Hebrews describes the ark as it was in the wilderness, when the tabernacle stood and Israel was still on the road. The rod was with the Law for the journey, as a token through the wilderness and into the land. When Israel was settled, the Law remained.

## In the Midst at Shechem

When Israel crossed the Jordan, Yahushua the son of Nun led the people to Shechem. He built an altar on Mount Ebal and wrote a copy of the Law on the stones. Then “all Israel, and their elders, and officers, and their judges, stood on this side the ark and on that side,” half of them over against Mount Gerizim and half over against Mount Ebal (Joshua 8:30-33). Blessing on one mountain, curse on the other, and in the midst, the ark.

Moses had already told them what that scene meant: “I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life” (Deuteronomy 30:19). In Eden, the tree of life stood in the midst of the garden beside a tree of death. At Shechem, the ark stood in the midst of the tribes between blessing and curse. In the wilderness that ark had carried the Law and the almond rod that came alive. The choice of Eden was set before Israel again, with life and the Law standing together in the middle.

Shechem lies in the valley between those two mountains, and a tree already stood there. It was the oak of Moreh, where Yahuah first appeared to Abram and promised the land to his seed (Genesis 12:6-7). Moses pointed Israel back to it by name: Gerizim and Ebal stand “beside the plains of Moreh” (Deuteronomy 11:30). Moreh means teacher. It comes from yarah, to point the way, the same root that gives the word torah. Scripture calls it an oak, not an almond. But when the ark came to Shechem, the almond rod and the tables of the Law were carried into the shade of the teacher’s tree. The tree of teaching and the token of life met in the midst.

The oak kept that role. Near the end of his life, Yahushua the son of Nun wrote the words of the covenant “in the book of the law of Elohim, and took a great stone, and set it up there under an oak, that was by the sanctuary of Yahuah” (Joshua 24:26). Generations later, Jotham climbed to the top of Mount Gerizim and called down to the men of Shechem with a parable of the trees: the olive, the fig, the vine, and the bramble (Judges 9:7-15). Even the parable spoken over that valley was about trees.

## The Priest Chosen by Life

Notice how Yahuah chose His priest. Not by birth order, not by vote, not by strength. He chose him by bringing life out of death.

Yahuah chose His great High Priest the same way. Yahushua is called the Branch (Isaiah 11:1; Zechariah 6:12). He was cut off out of the land of the living (Isaiah 53:8), and Yahuah raised Him up, “having loosed the pains of death” (Acts 2:24). Hebrews says He was made priest “not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life” (Hebrews 7:16).

Aaron’s rod was the token. The risen Branch is the fulfillment. In both cases the life came from Yahuah, and in both cases it proved whom He had chosen.

## Right There All Along

Many readers today treat the Law as the dead stick and the resurrection as the living branch, as if one replaced the other. In the ark, the living rod lay beside the tables, not in place of them. Life and Law were carried together into the land and set in the midst of Israel. The almond rod and the tablets were in the same box the whole time.

## Where It Ends

The ark still holds its place. When the temple of Elohim was opened in heaven, “there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament” (Revelation 11:19). The testimony the almond rod once lay beside has never been put away.
