*Moses handled two kinds of stone at Horeb — a rock he struck and tablets he carried. The prophets added a third. All three were written about one Man.*

## Introduction

Yahushua told the leaders of His day, "had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me" (John 5:46). David had said the same thing long before: "in the volume of the book it is written of me" (Psalm 40:7), and Hebrews puts those words in the mouth of Messiah (Hebrews 10:7). So the books of Moses are not only about Messiah in a few scattered prophecies. They are written *of* Him.

### Two Stones, Two Parts of Deliverance

At Horeb, Moses dealt with stone twice, and the order matters. First he struck a rock, and water poured out for people who had just been complaining (Exodus 17). Nobody earned that water. It was pure favor. Only after that did Moses carry down tablets of stone with the commandments written on them (Exodus 24 and 31). The water came first. The Law came second.

That is the whole shape of deliverance in two stones. The first stone is the free gift — Messiah struck, the Spirit poured out, a people delivered by favor before they had done anything. The second stone is the walk — the commandments written for a people already delivered. Yahushua put the two side by side: "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink" (John 7:37), and "If ye love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15). Drink first. Then walk. Modern teaching keeps the first stone and throws away the second. Horeb keeps both, in their order.

## Part I — The Rock That Was Struck

Israel camped at Rephidim with no water. Yahuah told Moses to take the elders and the rod and go on to Horeb. "I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it" (Exodus 17:6). Paul names the Rock plainly: "that Rock was Messiah" (1 Corinthians 10:4). So when Moses lifted the rod, he was acting out, in advance, what would be done to Messiah.

### The Word for Smite

The Hebrew word Moses obeyed is *nakah* — to strike, to wound, often to strike down to death. The prophets use the same word for Messiah. Isaiah says of the Servant, "we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted" (Isaiah 53:4), and "smitten" there comes from *nakah*. Yahuah says through Zechariah, "smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered" (Zechariah 13:7), and Yahushua applied that very verse to Himself the night He was taken (Matthew 26:31). Micah even names the rod: "they shall smite the judge of Israel with a rod upon the cheek" (Micah 5:1). At the cross the soldiers "took the reed, and smote him on the head" (Matthew 27:30). The rock at Horeb was struck by the order of Yahuah, and so was the Servant: "it pleased Yahuah to bruise him" (Isaiah 53:10).

### Living Water From the Struck Rock

Not one drop came out of that rock until it was struck. The same is true of Messiah. On the last day of Sukkot, Yahushua cried, "out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water" (John 7:38). John explains: "this spake he of the Spirit," which "was not yet given; because that Yahushua was not yet glorified" (John 7:39). The living water had to wait for the striking. After the resurrection Peter shows it flowing: Messiah, "having received of the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit, he hath shed forth this, which ye now see and hear" (Acts 2:33).

Notice where the water starts. Yahuah calls Himself "the fountain of living waters" (Jeremiah 2:13), and the Son received the promise "of the Father" before pouring it out. The Father is the fountain. The struck Rock is the opening the water flows through. The source and the channel are never the same.

### The Second Time: Speak, Do Not Strike

Forty years later at Kadesh, Yahuah did not say strike. He said, "speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water" (Numbers 20:8). Even the word for rock changes, from *tsur*, a rock on the ground, to *sela*, a high crag — the Rock lifted up. That is the picture after the cross. The Rock has been struck, and now He is raised and seated at the right hand of the Father. The way to the water is no longer the rod. It is speaking — fellowship "with the Father, and with his Son Yahushua Messiah" (1 John 1:3), and asking "the Father in my name" (John 16:23).

Moses struck it twice anyway (Numbers 20:11). The water still came, but Moses lost the promised land, because "ye believed me not" (Numbers 20:12). Striking the Rock again put Him back under the rod. Hebrews warns that those who fall away "crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh" (Hebrews 6:6). Messiah "was once offered to bear the sins of many" (Hebrews 9:28). The Rock is struck once. After that, His people speak.

## Part II — The Tablets of the Law

After the water came the Law. Yahuah told Moses, "I will give thee tables of stone, and a law, and commandments which I have written" (Exodus 24:12). He gave Moses "two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God" (Exodus 31:18). "The tables were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God" (Exodus 32:16).

### Two Sets of Tablets

Scripture never says where the stone of the first tablets came from. It says only that they were "the work of God." After they were broken at the foot of the mountain (Exodus 32:19), Moses had to hew the second set himself: "Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first" (Exodus 34:1). Moses brought the stone. Yahuah wrote "the words that were in the first tables." The words never changed.

When Moses retells this in Deuteronomy, he tells of hewing the second tablets and putting them in the ark (Deuteronomy 10:1-5), and a few verses later gives the command, "Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart" (Deuteronomy 10:16). Paul says the same: true circumcision "is that of the heart, in the spirit" (Romans 2:29). Man brings a prepared heart. Yahuah does the writing.

## Part III — The Law in a Body

Moses put the tablets "in the ark which I had made" (Deuteronomy 10:5), and it was called the ark of the testimony because they were inside it (Exodus 25:16). Now go back to Psalm 40. The same verse that says "it is written of me" goes on: "I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart" (Psalm 40:8). Hebrews applies that passage to Messiah coming into the world in a body (Hebrews 10:5-7). The ark carried the tablets inside it. Messiah carried the Law inside Him. He was the living ark of the testimony.

### The Goal of the Law

Paul writes, "Messiah is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth" (Romans 10:4). The Greek word for end is *telos*, and it means the goal, the target, the finish line a runner aims at — not the place where something is thrown away. Paul uses the same word when he says "the end of the commandment is charity" (1 Timothy 1:5). Love does not cancel the commandment; it is what the commandment is aiming at. In the same way, Messiah is what the Law was aiming at all along. He is the Law lived out in a body.

John shows this plainly. "Sin is the transgression of the law," and "in him is no sin" (1 John 3:4-5). He never once broke the Law. Yahushua said it in His own words: "I have kept my Father's commandments" (John 15:10). That is why John says anyone who claims to abide in Him "ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked" (1 John 2:6). To see what the tablets look like in a living man, look at Messiah.

This is also why John could call Him the Word made flesh (John 1:14). But He never claimed the words were His own invention. "My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me" (John 7:16). The Law is Yahuah's. The Son carries it and lives it. That is the difference between the source and the bearer, and it is why this picture gives no support to the Trinity.

### From Stone to Heart

The same move from stone to heart is promised to every believer. Yahuah said, "I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh" (Ezekiel 36:26), and "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33). Hebrews repeats that promise for the new covenant (Hebrews 8:10), and Paul calls believers a letter written "not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart" (2 Corinthians 3:3). The tablet changed — stone became flesh. The writing did not change.

## Part IV — The Third Stone: The Cornerstone

There is a third stone. David sang, "The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner" (Psalm 118:22), and Yahushua said that stone was Himself (Matthew 21:42). Peter preached it to the rulers who had crucified Him (Acts 4:11). Isaiah shows who sets this stone in place: "Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone" (Isaiah 28:16). Yahuah lays the stone. Peter quotes that verse and applies it to Messiah (1 Peter 2:6).

A cornerstone is the stone every other stone lines up with. It sets the line for the whole building. Peter says the builders who rejected it "stumble at the word, being disobedient" (1 Peter 2:8). The stumbling is disobedience to the word. Those who throw out the commandments are not honoring the Cornerstone — they are refusing the line He sets. Yahushua said so Himself: "if ye believe not his writings, how shall ye believe my words?" (John 5:47).

Then Peter turns to the believers: "Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house" (1 Peter 2:5). Paul says the house is built "upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Yahushua Messiah himself being the chief corner stone" (Ephesians 2:20). Here the three stones come together. The struck Rock gives the water. The tablets give the writing. The Cornerstone gives the line. And His people become living stones — set true to the Cornerstone, with the Law written on them.

## Conclusion

Put the three stones side by side and they lay out the whole plan of deliverance. The struck Rock is the way in. It poured out water for people who had done nothing to earn it and brought them into His assembly by favor alone. The tablets are the instructions. They tell a people already brought in what Yahuah asks of them and how to come near to Him. The Cornerstone is the example. He is a living Man who walked every one of those words, "leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps" (1 Peter 2:21). First the entrance. Then the way to walk. Then Someone to show how it is done.

All three point to the same Man, who was struck once, who carried His Father's Law in His heart, and who now holds the whole building in line.

Revelation shows the stones still standing at the end. When the temple in heaven is opened, "there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament" (Revelation 11:19) — the ark that holds the commandments. The people Yahuah keeps at the end are those who "keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Yahushua Messiah" (Revelation 12:17). And the water from the struck Rock becomes "a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Revelation 22:1).
