Most of the church world, and a good part of the Hebrew Roots movement with it, counts the day from sundown to sundown. The day, they say, begins when the light goes away. That teaching came out of Babylonian rabbinic tradition, not out of Torah. Scripture tells a different story, and the sun itself backs Scripture up every morning and every evening. One group of people proves it without ever opening a Bible: the men and women who make their living with a camera.

## What the Photographer Knows

Ask any landscape or portrait photographer when to shoot, and the answer will never be noon. They will name two short windows at the edges of the day: the *blue hour* and the *golden hour*. Those windows are the reason people get up in the dark and stay out after supper.

The reason is simple. When the sun sits low, its light has to pass through far more of the air on its way to the ground. Much of the harsh blue is scattered off along the way, and what arrives is warm, soft, and golden, with long gentle shadows. At midday the light comes straight down, flat and hard, and the same scene looks washed out.

Here is the part that matters. Photographers do not start their clock at the moment the sun's edge clears the horizon. The sky starts working long before that. The blue hour comes while the sun is still roughly four to six degrees below the horizon, in the stretch astronomers call civil twilight. Some of the most widely used guides go further and place the start of the golden hour itself at civil dawn, and its end at civil dusk.

To be fair to the record, photographers do not all agree on exact degrees. Some apps draw the lines a little differently, and plenty of people still loosely speak of “the hour around” the sun's appearing and going. But every one of them works the same window. The light worth having is the light at the edges, and it begins before the disc shows and lingers after the disc is gone. The working day of the camera is framed by dawn and dusk.

## What the Word Already Said

Long before anyone built a camera, Yahuah defined the day by its light. On the first day of creation, “Elohim called the light Day” (Genesis 1:5). The day, *yom*, is the light, *or*. It is not a block of hours that happens to contain some light. It is the light itself.

Then He set the lights in the heavens “for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years” (Genesis 1:14), and gave the greater light “to rule the day” (Genesis 1:16). The sun was put in place to rule the day, not to open the night.

Yahushua said the same thing in plain words: “Are there not twelve hours in the day?” (John 11:9). A man who walks in the day does not stumble, He said, because he sees the light of this world. Twelve hours of light. That is a day.

Psalm 104 lays out the whole pattern. When darkness falls, the beasts of the forest creep out. When the light comes, they gather themselves back to their dens, and “man goeth forth unto his work and to his labour until the evening” (Psalm 104:23). The evening, *ereb*, is where man's day ends. It is not where the day begins.

And Psalm 19 says the heavens preach without a voice. The sun comes out “as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber” (Psalm 19:5), and there is no speech or language where its voice is not heard (Psalm 19:3).

## Where the Church Has It Wrong

Sundown-to-sundown reckoning starts the day in the dark. Under that rule, a Sabbath keeper opens the seventh day at the evening of the sixth, before a single ray of the seventh day's light has come. The day Yahuah called “light” is made to begin with no light at all.

When Yahuah wanted a count to run from evening to evening, He said so. For Yom Kippur the command is “from even unto even, shall ye celebrate your sabbath” (Leviticus 23:32). Pesach carries its own stated evening as well. Those are named exceptions written into the text for those appointed times. An exception that has to be spelled out shows what the normal rule is. Those two cannot be stretched to overturn every other day of the year.

The day is light, dawn to dusk. The photographer's charts, the farmer's hours, and the way every living thing rises and rests all line up with that, and none of them line up with a day that starts at dark.

## Witness, Not Evidence

None of this rests on photography. Most photographers have never given a thought to Genesis 1, and that is exactly the point. They found the edges of the day by watching the light, and the light led them right back to dawn and dusk. The sun testified without being asked.

Scripture is the authority. The sun is a witness. Yahuah even calls His lights witnesses: the moon is established “as a faithful witness in heaven” (Psalm 89:37). A witness does not decide the case. A witness only tells what it saw. The Word settled when the day begins, and every morning the sun stands up in court and agrees.

## The Day With No Dusk

The day that begins at dawn now will one day have no dusk at all. In the holy city there is no need of the sun or the moon, “for the glory of Yahuah did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof” (Revelation 21:23). The word for that light is *luchnos*, a lamp: Yahuah is the light, and the Lamb carries it. “There shall be no night there” (Revelation 22:5). The day Yahuah called light will simply never end.
