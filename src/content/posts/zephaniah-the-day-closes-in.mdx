## The Pattern

Zephaniah is three chapters, and the judgment moves like a closing circle. It starts with the whole earth, narrows to Judah, swings around the nations on all four sides, and lands on Jerusalem itself. Then it opens again into mercy. Hold the circle and the whole book is held.

- *The world* (1:2–3): all things swept from the land.
- *Judah* (1:4–2:3): the day of Yahuah is near.
- *The nations, on four sides* (2:4–15): west, east, south, north.
- *Jerusalem* (3:1–8): the city at the center.
- *The remnant* (3:9–20): a pure language, and a people who trust in the name.

One more pattern sits in the prophet's name. Tsephanyah means *Yahuah has hidden* or *Yahuah has treasured*. His central plea, with a different Hebrew word but the same idea, is that the meek *may … be hid in the day of Yahuah's anger* (2:3).

## The Setting

Zephaniah prophesied *in the days of Josiah* (1:1), and he traces his line four generations back to Hizkiah (1:1), most likely King Hezekiah — royal blood. The idolatry he lists — Baal, the host of heaven, Malcham — is what Josiah tore down in his reform (2 Kings 23:4–14), which places Zephaniah just before or early in it. He stands at the start of Jeremiah's ministry, in the last generation before Babylon.

## 1. The World and Judah (1:2–2:3)

> *I will consume man and beast; I will consume the fowls of the heaven, and the fishes of the sea … and I will cut off man from off the land, saith Yahuah.* (Zephaniah 1:3)

**What happens:** the list runs man, beast, birds, fish — creation from Genesis 1 in reverse. Then the sweep narrows to Judah: the remnant of Baal, *the name of the Chemarims*, the rooftop worshippers of the stars, and *them that worship and that swear by Yahuah, and that swear by Malcham* (1:4–5).

**Watch for:** the mixed worshipper. Zephaniah does not condemn only those who left Yahuah; he condemns those who swore by Yahuah *and* by another. Next to them sit the men *settled on their lees* who say *Yahuah will not do good, neither will he do evil* (1:12) — believers in name, who no longer expect Him to act.

> *Seek ye Yahuah, all ye meek of the earth, which have wrought his judgment; seek righteousness, seek meekness: it may be ye shall be hid in the day of Yahuah's anger.* (Zephaniah 2:3)

**And:** the meek who may be hidden are those *which have wrought his judgment* — who have done His mishpat. Seeking Yahuah and keeping His judgments are the same act.

## 2. The Nations on Four Sides (2:4–15)

**What happens:** the day circles Judah. West: Gaza, Ashkelon and the Philistine coast (2:4–7). East: Moab and Ammon, to become *as Sodom … and as Gomorrah* (2:9). South: the Ethiopians (2:12). North: *destroy Assyria; and will make Nineveh a desolation* (2:13).

**Watch for:** Nineveh. It turned under Jonah and was spared. A few generations later it had gone back to boasting *I am, and there is none beside me* (2:15), and Zephaniah names its end. It fell in 612 BC. Mercy received and then abandoned does not hold.

**And:** *he will famish all the gods of the earth; and men shall worship him, every one from his place* (2:11). The gods are starved out, and the nations turn to Yahuah where they are.

## 3. Jerusalem at the Center (3:1–8)

> *Woe to her that is filthy and polluted, to the oppressing city! She obeyed not the voice; she received not correction; she trusted not in Yahuah; she drew not near to her Elohim.* (Zephaniah 3:1–2)

**What happens:** after circling the nations, the day lands on the holy city. Princes are roaring lions, judges are evening wolves, prophets are *light and treacherous* (3:3–4).

**Watch for:** the charge against the priests: they *have polluted the sanctuary, they have done violence to the law* (3:4). The word is chamas, violence. Twisting Torah is not a technicality in Zephaniah; it is violence done to it.

## 4. The Remnant (3:9–20)

> *For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah, to serve him with one consent.* (Zephaniah 3:9)

**What happens:** the word for *language* is safah, lip — the same word as Babel, where Yahuah confused the *lip* of the whole earth (Genesis 11:1, 7, 9). Zephaniah promises Babel reversed, and the purpose is stated: to *call upon the name of Yahuah*. Then a humble remnant who *shall trust in the name of Yahuah* and *shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies* (3:12–13).

**Watch for:** the song at the end. *Yahuah thy Elohim in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save; he will rejoice over thee with joy … he will joy over thee with singing* (3:17). Yahuah is the One who saves, and the book that began with consuming ends with Him singing.

## Three Pulpit Errors Zephaniah Answers

- *The name does not matter.* Zephaniah cuts off *the name of the Chemarims* (1:4) and promises a pure lip whose whole purpose is to *call upon the name of Yahuah* (3:9). The remnant *trust in the name* (3:12).
- *Mixing is harmless if the heart is right.* Those who swore by Yahuah *and* by Malcham are judged with the idolaters (1:5). Worship carried over from other gods' customs does not become clean because Yahuah's name is spoken alongside it.
- *The law is a burden the teachers lifted.* Zephaniah calls the priests' handling of Torah *violence* (3:4), and names the meek as those who *wrought his judgment* (2:3).

## Where Zephaniah Points Next

Zephaniah's remnant trust in the name, speak no lies, and have no deceitful tongue in their mouth (3:12–13). Revelation sees them standing on Mount Zion with *his Father's name written in their foreheads*, and says of them: *in their mouth was found no guile* (Revelation 14:1, 5).
