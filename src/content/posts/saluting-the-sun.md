## What Came West

Yoga is the best-known religious exercise in America. It is taught in gyms, schools, hospitals, and church fellowship halls, and most who practice it think of it as stretching. The word itself says otherwise. *Yoga* comes from the Sanskrit root *yuj*, "to yoke" or "to join." In Hindu teaching the goal of yoga is to yoke the self to the divine — to join the soul to Brahman, the god-force behind all things.

Yoga was brought to the West as a spiritual teaching, not a workout. Swami Vivekananda introduced it to Americans at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago in 1893. In the 1960s Indian gurus carried it into popular culture, and through the twentieth century the poses were slowly separated from the temple and sold as fitness. The poses changed rooms; they did not change meaning.

## The Sun Salutation

The clearest case is the sequence called *Surya Namaskar*, the "Salutation to the Sun." Surya is the Hindu sun god. The sequence is a chain of twelve postures — standing, bowing, kneeling, laying the body down, and rising again — and in its traditional form each posture is paired with a chant naming one of the twelve names of Surya. It was made popular in its modern form in India in the 1920s and is traditionally performed facing the sun at dawn.

Stripped of its chants in an American gym, the sequence is still what its name says it is: a set of bows to the sun.

## What Scripture Says

Ezekiel was shown the worst abomination in Jerusalem. It was not a carved idol. It was twenty-five men in the Temple court "with their backs toward the temple" and "their faces toward the east," and "they worshipped the sun toward the east" (Ezekiel 8:16). Yahuah called it the greater abomination.

Moses warned Israel of the same thing before they entered the land: when they saw the sun, the moon, and the stars, they were not to be "driven to worship them, and serve them" (Deuteronomy 4:19). And he warned them not to study how the nations served their gods, saying, "even so will I do likewise" (Deuteronomy 12:30).

## The Two Yokes

The word *yoga* answers itself. Yahushua also offered a yoke: "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me ... For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light" (Matthew 11:29-30). There are two yokes on offer, and they do not join. One binds a man to the sun god through bows and breath. The other binds him to the Father through the Son and His commandments.

Paul gave the measure for every exercise: "bodily exercise profiteth little: but godliness is profitable unto all things" (1 Timothy 4:8). Stretching is not sin. Bowing to the sun in twelve steps is.

## The Verdict

The sun salutation is a worship sequence, and removing the chant does not remove the bow. A believer can stretch, walk, and strengthen the body without borrowing the postures of a sun god.

Revelation's final call leaves no middle ground: "worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea" (Revelation 14:7) — the Maker of the sun, not the sun.
