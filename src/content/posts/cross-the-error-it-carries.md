Scripture never commands a symbol for the assembly. It commands the opposite: "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing" (Exodus 20:4). The cross was not handed down by the apostles as an emblem. It was raised over the church by an emperor three centuries later.

## Stake and Tree

The Greek word translated "cross" is *stauros*, an upright stake or pole. When Peter and Paul describe the death of Yahushua, they often reach for another word, *xylon*, wood or tree: "whom they slew and hanged on a tree" (Acts 5:30; also Acts 10:39, 13:29, Galatians 3:13, 1 Peter 2:24). The apostles thought of it in Torah terms, the curse of Deuteronomy 21:23: "he that is hanged is accursed of God." The tree was the place of the curse. Paul gloried in what Yahuah accomplished there (Galatians 6:14), not in the shape of the wood. "The preaching of the cross" is a message, not a medallion.

## The Bronze Serpent

Yahushua chose His own picture for His death: "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up" (John 3:14). The bronze serpent of Numbers 21 was made at Yahuah's command, and all who looked on it lived. It was a true instrument of deliverance.

Seven hundred years later Israel was burning incense to it. King Hezekiah "brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made" and called it *Nehushtan*, a mere thing of brass (2 Kings 18:4). Scripture counts that act among the reasons Hezekiah did right in the sight of Yahuah.

The pattern is exact. The instrument Yahuah used became the object the people honored. If the very serpent Yahushua compared to His own lifting up could become an idol worth breaking, the wooden instrument can become one too. It has: kissed, bowed to, carried in procession, signed in the air to ward off evil, hung on walls as a charm, and splintered into relics sold as pieces of the "true cross."

## A Shape the Nations Had First

Crosses in many forms stood long before Golgotha: the sun wheel, the Egyptian ankh, and others across the ancient world. That history alone does not prove the Christian cross pagan, but it shows the shape belonged to the nations first, and Yahuah warned Israel, "Learn not the way of the heathen" (Jeremiah 10:2). Constantine marched under it to win a war, and the Crusaders marched under it to win more. A banner that rallied armies was never the testimony of the Lamb.

## The Verdict

The error is not that Christians remember the death of Messiah. Scripture commands that remembrance and gives the means: the bread and the cup (1 Corinthians 11:26). The error is that the instrument of the curse was made into an object of honor, against the Second Commandment, by the same imperial hand that changed so much else. A believer is marked by what he keeps, not by what he wears.

Revelation shows the true mark of those who follow the Lamb. They stand with Him on Mount Sion, having "his Father's name written in their foreheads" (Revelation 14:1). Not a cross. A Name.
