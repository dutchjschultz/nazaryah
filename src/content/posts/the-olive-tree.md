*Witness Three in the investigation [**The Trees of Judges**](/investigations/the-trees-of-judges) — back to the case.*

## The Man Is the Tree

David did not say Israel was like an olive tree. He said, “I am like a green olive tree in the house of God” (Psalm 52:8). The olive is a man. Of the three good trees, the olive pictures one part of a covenant man’s walk: the anointed walk. The vine shows how he enters. The fig shows how he keeps the Law. The olive shows what Yahuah pours into him. The olive is also the last of the three to bear. As supporting evidence from the land, it is gathered in the fall, after the grapes and figs are in. The anointing reaches its full yield at the end of the walk.

Jotham’s olive named the one thing it would not give up: “my fatness.” Everything in this study turns on that word.

## Fatness and Anointing Are One Word

The word for fatness is *deshen*: richness, abundance, the fat of the oil. Its verb is *dashen*, to make fat. That same verb is the word David used for anointing: “thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over” (Psalm 23:5). In Hebrew, to anoint a man is to make him fat.

So the olive was not guarding a crop. It was guarding the anointing. And Scripture says what the anointing oil is. When Samuel poured oil on David, “the Spirit of Yahuah came upon David from that day forward” (1 Samuel 16:13). The fatness of the olive man is Yahuah’s Spirit filling him.

## Where the Fatness Comes From

No branch makes its own fatness. Paul says the grafted branch shares “the root and fatness of the olive tree” (Romans 11:17). The fatness rises from the root, and the root is Messiah, “the root and the offspring of David” (Revelation 22:16). Cut the branch from the root, and the fatness stops.

The fatness also comes from the place the tree is planted. David’s olive stood “in the house of God” (Psalm 52:8). The psalmist says the same of the righteous man: “Those that be planted in the house of Yahuah shall flourish … They shall still bring forth fruit in old age; they shall be fat and flourishing” (Psalm 92:13-14). Men who stay near Yahuah “shall be abundantly satisfied with the fatness of thy house” (Psalm 36:8). The anointed man is fat because of where he is rooted and where he stays.

## The Fatness Comes Through the Press

An olive on the branch gives no oil. It must be beaten and crushed before the fatness runs out. The anointed man is pressed the same way. Paul wrote, “We are troubled on every side” (2 Corinthians 4:8), and the word is *thlibo*, to press or squeeze. Then he shows what the pressing yields: tribulation, patience, hope, and “the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost” (Romans 5:3-5). The press comes first, then the pouring.

Messiah went first. He prayed in Gethsemane (Matthew 26:36), a name joining *gat*, press, and *shemen*, oil. There His sweat was “as it were great drops of blood” (Luke 22:44). The Anointed One was pressed in the oil press.

The altar shows the same thing with the same word. When the burnt offering was consumed, the priest carried out “the ashes,” and the word is *deshen*, fatness (Leviticus 6:10-11). The fatness of the altar is what remains after the offering is given up. The olive man gets his fatness the same way: by presenting himself “a living sacrifice” (Romans 12:1).

## What the Fatness Does in the Man

Jotham’s olive said its fatness was what “they honour God and man” with. The anointed man’s fatness goes both directions.

*It honors God.* The fatness of the olive fed the lamp, and that oil was commanded: “thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always” (Exodus 27:20). The anointed man is that lamp. “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father” (Matthew 5:16). His fatness shows as good works, and the Father gets the honor.

*It honors man.* The fatness is not hoarded. “The liberal soul shall be made fat” (Proverbs 11:25), and the word is *dashen*. The man who pours out is the man who is filled. The Samaritan found a wounded man and bound up his wounds, “pouring in oil and wine” (Luke 10:34). The anointed man spends his fatness on the wounded beside the road, and Yahuah keeps him full.

*It claims his whole walk.* At the cleansing of the leper, the priest put oil on the right ear, the right thumb, and the right great toe, “upon the blood of the trespass offering” (Leviticus 14:17). What the man hears, what he does, and where he goes are all anointed, and the oil always sits on top of the blood.

## Why the Olive Would Not Trade It

The trees offered the olive a throne, but to take it the olive would have to leave its fatness and go “to be promoted,” *nua*, to sway back and forth over the trees (Judges 9:9). A tree that leaves its fatness for rule has traded what Yahuah poured in for what men can hand out.

The bramble offered its own shade to anyone who would trust it. Proverbs gives the other way: “he that putteth his trust in Yahuah shall be made fat” (Proverbs 28:25). Again the word is *dashen*. The anointed man does not need the throne or the bramble’s shade. His fatness comes from trusting Yahuah, and he will not trade it.

## When the Fatness Is Lost

Fatness can be lost two ways. The first is by dropping the commandments. “If thou wilt not hearken … to observe to do all his commandments” (Deuteronomy 28:15), then “thou shalt not anoint thyself with the oil; for thine olive shall cast his fruit” (28:40). Break the Law, and the oil dries up.

The second is by forgetting where the fatness came from. Moses warned that Israel would eat and “waxen fat; then will they turn unto other gods” (Deuteronomy 31:20). “Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked … then he forsook God which made him” (32:15). Fatness kept by a man for himself turns into pride. Paul gives the grafted branch the same warning: “Be not highminded, but fear” (Romans 11:20).

## The Sign of a True Believer

The olive gives the third sign of a true believer: he is full, he gives light, and he pours out. And the oil always moves him toward the Law, never away from it. “I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them” (Ezekiel 36:27). The Spirit came so “that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit” (Romans 8:4).

John ties the anointing to the commandments in one verse: “he that keepeth his commandments dwelleth in him, and he in him. And hereby we know that he abideth in us, by the Spirit which he hath given us” (1 John 3:24). A man who claims the oil and walks away from the commandments has the bramble’s shade, not the olive’s fatness. The true anointed man grows richer with the years, still “fat and flourishing” in old age (Psalm 92:14).

## How Messiah Walked It

Messiah is the olive walk in full. His very title, *Mashiach*, means the Anointed. At the Jordan “the Spirit of God descending like a dove” came upon Him (Matthew 3:16), and Peter says Elohim “anointed Yahushua of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power” (Acts 10:38). “God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows” (Psalm 45:7). The One who pours is not the One who receives, and the oil is what is poured, not a third person beside them.

Right after the anointing came the throne offer. The adversary showed Him “all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them,” and said, “All these things will I give thee, if thou wilt fall down and worship me” (Matthew 4:8-9). Like the olive, He would not leave His fatness to sway over the trees: “Thou shalt worship Yahuah thy God, and him only shalt thou serve” (4:10; Deuteronomy 6:13). Then He spent the anointing on the wounded: “he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted” (Luke 4:18). And in Gethsemane He was pressed, so the oil could run to every branch on the root.

## The Olive at the End

Revelation shows two olive trees “standing before the God of the earth” (Revelation 11:4), the same trees Zechariah saw pouring golden oil into the lamp (Zechariah 4:12). They never ran dry, because they stood before the One who fills them. That is the end of the anointed walk: a man still fat and flourishing, still giving light, standing in the house of God.
