## Two Photographs

Two pictures from Thailand tell the whole story. The first shows a whole roasted pig on a counter in a busy Bangkok food hall, golden and crisp, with a crowd lined up for it. The second shows a sign at a restaurant door that reads "Not allowed to bring the following into the restaurant," and at the top of the list is "pork or pork products." Right under it is "alcoholic beverages."

The sign belongs to a Muslim (halal) kitchen. The pig belongs to the world, and to most of the churches in it.

Put the two pictures side by side and they show the two ways a man can break the word of Yahuah. One adds to it. The other takes away from it.

## The Rule Over Both

Before Israel entered the land, Moses gave a rule that sits over every other commandment: "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). He said it again later: "thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it" (Deuteronomy 12:32). Solomon put it plainly: "Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar" (Proverbs 30:6).

Adding and taking away are named together as one sin. Either one turns the word of Yahuah into the word of man.

## What Yahuah Said About the Pig

The law is short and clear. The swine "divideth the hoof, and be clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean to you" (Leviticus 11:7). Moses repeats it: "ye shall not eat of their flesh, nor touch their dead carcase" (Deuteronomy 14:8).

This was not a law for one season. Isaiah, looking ahead to the day Yahuah comes with fire, names those who are "eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse" and says they "shall be consumed together" (Isaiah 66:17). The pork law is still standing at the end of the book of Isaiah, in a prophecy about the coming of Yahuah. A law that is still being judged at His coming has not been done away.

## What Yahuah Said About Wine

Here the Muslim sign goes past the word. The Torah never forbids wine. It forbids drunkenness.

Yahuah told Israel to spend their tithe at the feast on "oxen, or for sheep, or for wine, or for strong drink" and to rejoice before Him (Deuteronomy 14:26). The psalmist praises Him for "wine that maketh glad the heart of man" (Psalm 104:15). Yahushua's first sign was turning water into wine at a wedding (John 2:9-10).

There are limits, and they are exact. The priests could not drink when they went into the tabernacle (Leviticus 10:9). The Nazarite gave up the vine for the days of his vow (Numbers 6:3). Proverbs warns that wine "is a mocker" to the man who is deceived by it (Proverbs 20:1). Paul says "be not drunk with wine" (Ephesians 5:18). Every one of these is a limit set by Yahuah Himself, for a certain man, a certain time, or a certain abuse.

The Quran sets those limits aside and goes further. It calls strong drink a filthy work of Satan and tells believers to stay away from it altogether (Quran 5:90). That is not keeping the law more carefully. It is writing a new law on top of His.

## Islam Takes Away Too

The halal sign looks strict, but the Islamic food law also lets go of what Yahuah forbade. The same Leviticus chapter that forbids the pig forbids the camel (Leviticus 11:4) and the hare (Leviticus 11:6), and forbids anything in the waters without fins and scales (Leviticus 11:10). Camel meat is halal and is eaten across the Muslim world. Most Islamic schools allow rabbit, and most allow shrimp and shellfish.

So Islam kept one line of Leviticus 11, dropped others, and added a ban the Torah never gave. It both adds and takes away. That is the pattern of every counterfeit: enough truth to look right, and enough change to make it its own.

## How the Church Took the Pig Back

The church went the other way. It kept the wine cup and gave back the pig. Three passages are used to do it, and none of them says what the pulpit says.

**Mark 7.** The argument in Mark 7 is about eating bread "with unwashen hands" (Mark 7:2, 5), a tradition of the elders. Yahushua answers that what goes into a man through the mouth does not defile his heart. It goes into the belly and out into the draught, "purging all meats" (Mark 7:19). The question was dirty hands, not which animals are food. To a man raised on the Torah, pork was never "meat" (food) in the first place. Many modern Bibles add the words "thus he declared all foods clean." Those words are not in the KJV, and they are an addition to the text, made to settle a question the passage never asked.

**Acts 10.** Peter sees a sheet full of unclean animals and hears "kill, and eat." His answer shows what he had been doing for years after the resurrection: "I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean" (Acts 10:14). Peter had walked with Yahushua for more than three years and had never been taught that the pork law was gone. And Peter himself explains what the vision meant: "Yahuah hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean" (Acts 10:28). The vision was about men, the Gentiles, not about the menu.

**1 Timothy 4.** Paul warns of teachers who command men "to abstain from meats, which Yahuah hath created to be received" (1 Timothy 4:3). He then says every creature of Yahuah is good, being "sanctified by the word of Yahuah and prayer" (1 Timothy 4:5). The word of Yahuah is what set apart which creatures are food. Leviticus 11 is that word. Paul is not turning the pig into food; he is pointing back to the very law the church uses this verse to erase.

Read as written, not one of these passages touches the pork law. The church did not find the pig in Scripture. It took the law away and then went looking for verses to cover it.

## Two Errors, One Sin

The Muslim sign and the Bangkok food hall stand on opposite sides of the same line. One added a ban Yahuah never gave and dropped animals He did forbid. The other dropped the ban He did give and calls it freedom. Deuteronomy 4:2 does not grade one as better than the other. Adding and taking away are the same sin facing in two directions.

There is a hard point in this for the church. A Muslim kitchen in Thailand keeps the pork commandment more faithfully than most pulpits in America. The church that claims to hold the whole Bible has let a religion that rejects Yahushua keep a line of the Torah that it threw away.

The answer is not to copy Islam's strictness or the church's freedom. It is to keep the word exactly as Yahuah gave it, no more and no less. The man delivered by the blood of Yahushua does not walk into the law to earn that deliverance. He walks in it afterward, because it is his Father's word, and he does not change his Father's word.

## The Last Warning in the Book

The Bible closes on the same rule Moses gave at the start. At the end of Revelation, Yahushua warns that if any man adds to the words of this book, Yahuah will add to him the plagues written in it, and if any man takes away, Yahuah will take away his part out of the book of life (Revelation 22:18-19). The rule over the whole word is still standing on the last page.
