> **Mark 12:29–30**
>
> *And Yahushua answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel; The Lord [Yahuah] our God is one Lord [Yahuah]: And thou shalt love the Lord [Yahuah] thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment.*

Part One

**The Claim**

***1.1 — What the Argument Says***

A scribe asked Yahushua which commandment came first of all *Mark 12:28*. The Master answered with the Shema of Moses *Deuteronomy 6:4–5*, the oldest confession in Israel. It says the God of Israel is one. To make room for three inside that word, teachers lean on four arguments: the Hebrew word echad, said to be a “compound unity”; the plural ending on Elohim; David calling the Messiah “my Lord” a few verses later *Mark 12:35–37*; and Paul’s “one God, the Father” and “one Lord” *1 Corinthians 8:6*.

The main one is echad. Teachers point to the man and woman who become “one flesh” *Genesis 2:24* and say that if two can be one, three can be one as well.

***1.2 — The Hidden Premise***

Every one of these arguments rests on two unspoken ideas: that the word “one” can quietly mean three, and that the One who recited the Shema is Himself part of the God He was naming. Neither idea comes from the verse. They come from the creeds. The Athanasian Creed says Father, Son and Spirit “are not three Gods, but one God” — words the Master never spoke. He said the words Moses said, and He meant them the way Moses meant them.

Part Two

**The Text**

***2.1 — The Question and the Answer***

The scribe did not ask about the nature of God. He asked about the Law. Yahushua treated the Shema as a command to be obeyed, not a riddle to be unlocked. A command has to be understood by the plain man who hears it, or it cannot be kept. No Israelite standing in the temple that day heard “one” and thought “three.”

***2.2 — “The Lord Our God”***

Look closely at one small word in the Master’s mouth: “our.” He did not say “the Lord your God.” He said “the Lord our God.” Yahushua stood with Israel as one who hears the Shema, not as the One the Shema is about.

This is how He spoke of the Father everywhere. After He rose He was going “to my God, and your God” *John 20:17*. He told the woman at the well, “we know what we worship” *John 4:22*, placing Himself among the worshippers. A worshipper is not the One he worships. When the Son says “our God,” He names someone above Himself — “the head of Messiah is God” *1 Corinthians 11:3*.

***2.3 — The Scribe’s Echo***

The scribe repeated the answer back: “there is one God; and there is none other but he” *Mark 12:32*, echoing Moses, who said of Yahuah, “there is none else beside him” *Deuteronomy 4:35*. His word “he” is a single masculine pronoun in the Greek, autou — “him,” not “them.”

Yahushua “saw that he answered discreetly” and told him, “Thou art not far from the kingdom of God” *Mark 12:34*. The scribe’s confession of one God with none other was praised, not corrected.

Part Three

**The Word Echad**

**echad (אֶחָד)**

The ordinary Hebrew number one. It is the word used to count one day, one witness, one man, one cluster. It answers the question “how many?” and the answer it gives is always one.

***3.1 — What Echad Means***

Echad is simply the number one. The KJV calls the first day of creation “the first day,” but the Hebrew says yom echad, “day one” *Genesis 1:5*. A man could not be put to death “at the mouth of one witness” *Deuteronomy 17:6*. Nobody thinks one witness is a compound of several witnesses.

Some uses are about a single individual and nothing else. Abraham “was one, and he inherited the land” *Ezekiel 33:24*. Solomon wrote of a man who is “one alone, and there is not a second” *Ecclesiastes 4:8*. That verse uses echad and then rules out a second in the same breath — exactly what the Shema does.

***3.2 — Where the “Compound” Idea Comes From***

Echad sometimes sits beside a word that holds many parts. But the many parts belong to the noun, never to the number. In “one cluster of grapes” *Numbers 13:23*, echad counts clusters, not grapes. In “one flesh” *Genesis 2:24*, a husband and wife remain two people; echad counts the union, and there is one.

So the real question in the Shema is this: what noun does echad count? The answer is Yahuah. It counts a name, and one name has one owner. The prophet says the same of the age to come: “there shall be one LORD [Yahuah], and his name one” *Zechariah 14:9*.

***3.3 — The Yachid Objection***

Some teachers say that if Moses meant one in the absolute sense, he would have used yachid instead. But yachid is not a counting word. It means only or lonely — Isaac as Abraham’s “only son” *Genesis 22:2*, David crying that he was “desolate” *Psalm 25:16*. Moses was not describing loneliness. He was counting, and when a Hebrew counts one, the word is echad.

***3.4 — The Same Word in Mark’s Own Book***

**heis (εἷς)**

The Greek number one, written here in the masculine form. Mark uses it when Yahushua says the Lord is “one Lord,” and again when the scribe says “there is one God.”

Mark wrote in Greek, and his word for “one” is heis. He uses the very same phrase, “one God,” two other times. When the scribes grumbled that Yahushua forgave sins, they asked, “who can forgive sins but God only?” *Mark 2:7* — heis ho theos, “the one God.”

Then a rich man called Yahushua “Good Master.” The Master stopped him: “Why callest thou me good? there is none good but one, that is, God” *Mark 10:18*. Again heis ho theos. Here the Son Himself sets Himself apart from “the one God,” refusing a description that belongs to Him and giving it back to the Father. In Mark’s own book, “one God” always means one, and once, in the Master’s mouth, it draws a line between Himself and God.

Part Four

**Three Further Claims**

***4.1 — The Plural Word Elohim***

Elohim has a plural ending, but a plural form in Hebrew is not a headcount of persons; the “Us” Passages Reference Piece above covers that in full. Inside the Shema the word cannot drift in any case: Yahuah, our Elohim, Yahuah is echad. The plural title sits between the single name and the number one, and Mark’s Greek removes even the plural ending — ho theos, “the God,” singular.

***4.2 — “David Called Him Lord”***

Yahushua quotes David: “The LORD [Yahuah] said to my Master, Sit thou on my right hand” *Mark 12:36*, from *Psalm 110:1*. The second word in the Hebrew is adoni, “my lord” — a word Scripture uses for kings and masters, not for Yahuah Himself. One speaks; the other is spoken to. One gives the seat; the other is seated. The verse names two, and only one of them is Yahuah.

***4.3 — “Paul Put the Son Inside the Shema”***

Paul wrote of “one God, the Father, of whom are all things … and one Master Yahushua Messiah, by whom are all things” *1 Corinthians 8:6*. Paul tells the reader exactly who the one God is: “the Father.” The Son gets a different title, one Master, and Scripture says the Father gave it: “God hath made that same Yahushua … both Master and Messiah” *Acts 2:36*.

The claim also turns on itself. The Yahuah of the Shema is the Father: “thou, O LORD [Yahuah], art our father” *Isaiah 63:16*. Place the Son inside the Shema’s one Yahuah, and the Son becomes the Father — something even the creed denies. The One Sent in His Name Reference Piece above shows how a verse about Yahuah can be fulfilled in the Son without making Him Yahuah.

Part Five

**Reading the Verse Again**

***5.1 — The First Commandment Still Stands***

Modern churches commonly teach that the Law ended at the cross. Yet when the Master was asked about the commandments, He named the first one and told the scribe to keep it. The love it commands is shown by keeping His words *Deuteronomy 6:6*; “this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments” *1 John 5:3*. And the Master kept that commandment Himself, worshipping and serving Yahuah alone *Matthew 4:10*. A Son who keeps the first commandment toward His God is not that God.

***5.2 — The Verse Read Plainly***

With the Hebrew and Greek words drawn out, the Master’s answer reads like this:

> *And Yahushua answered the scribe: The first of all the commandments is this — Hear and obey, O Israel: Yahuah, the Father, who is our God — My God and your God — is Yahuah alone, one in number, with no second beside Him. And you shall love Yahuah your God, giving Him all your heart, all your soul, all your mind, and all your strength, by keeping His words. This is the first commandment.*

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

The verse Trinitarians must explain away is the verse the Son of God chose as the first commandment. He said “one,” using the plain number Moses used. He said “our God,” standing with Israel as a worshipper. He praised a scribe who said there is “none other but he.” Echad counts one name, Elohim takes a singular verb, and Mark’s own “one God” is the God the Son set apart from Himself.

The last book of the Bible shows the same picture. John saw one seated on the throne, and the Lamb came and took the book “out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne” *Revelation 5:7*. The One on the throne gives; the Lamb receives. And the glorified Son still calls Him “my God,” four times in a single promise *Revelation 3:12*.

> **The Son recited the Shema and called Yahuah “our God.” The One who confesses the one God is not the one God He confesses.**
