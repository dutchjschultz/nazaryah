This reading companions the breakdown of Mark 4:26–29. Nothing is argued here — the parable runs start to finish, with the meaning of each image carried alongside the words it belongs to. Where the Hebrew Scriptures do not define an image, no meaning is supplied.

> **MARK 4:26–29 — KJV**
>
> And he said, So is the kingdom of Yahuah *[the stone Daniel was shown, cut out of the mountain without hands, which broke the kingdoms of men and became a mountain that filled the whole earth]* as if a man should cast seed *[the word of Yahuah, named by Yahushua fifteen verses earlier in the same sitting, which goes out of His mouth and does not return empty but accomplishes what He pleases]* into the ground; *[the earth that brought forth at the beginning because He had spoken into it, with no man standing in the field when it happened]* And should sleep, and rise night and day, *[time passing, and the sower somewhere else while it passes]* and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how. *[as a man does not know the way of the wind, nor how the bones grow in the womb, nor the works of Yahuah who makes them all]* For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself; *[the growth no man planted — in the law of Moses the crop the owner of the field is forbidden to put a blade to, because it is not his]* first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear. *[the Torah's own harvest words, green ear to full ear — a crop ripening toward a harvest that cannot be brought in early]* But when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle, *[Joel's sickle, maggal, which in the whole of the Hebrew Scriptures goes into a field only in a harvest of judgment]* because the harvest is come. *[the harvest stands ready — Joel's own words, and the line that follows them is the valley of decision]*

Put together, the parable says this. The kingdom of Yahuah is His own planting, the stone no hand cut out of the mountain. The word sown is His word and it performs His purpose, not the purpose of whoever carried it. The ground produces because He spoke into it. The man who sowed sleeps and rises and is not present for the work, and his not knowing how it happens is the condition of the whole business. What comes up, comes up of itself, which in the law of Moses is the crop a man is forbidden to claim. It ripens in its own stages toward a harvest that cannot be hurried.

And the instant it stands ready, the blade goes in — and the blade is the one Joel named. That answers the men standing on that shore, measuring Him by what they could see and finding nothing they recognised as a kingdom. It was already sown, already growing where they could not watch it or stop it, and the day it would be seen by everyone was a day set by Yahuah and not by them. The sentence does not end in the parable either. It is spoken once more at the close of the Revelation, where one like the Son of man sits on a white cloud with a sharp sickle and the word goes out to thrust it in and reap, because the harvest of the earth is ripe.
