Still the same night (1:7). The first four visions promised the house would be built, the horns cast out, the city opened, and the high priest made clean. But the builders still had a problem nobody could argue away. The work had stopped. Their enemies had “weakened the hands of the people of Judah, and troubled them in building,” and the house sat unfinished “unto the second year of the reign of Darius” (Ezra 4:4, 24). That is the very year of this night. The fifth vision is for the man in charge of that stalled building, Zerubbabel the governor.

## The Vision

The angel wakes Zechariah “as a man that is wakened out of his sleep” (4:1) and asks what he sees. He sees a lampstand all of gold, with a bowl on top, seven lamps, and pipes running to the lamps. Beside it stand two olive trees, one on the right of the bowl and one on the left, feeding golden oil into the lamps through golden pipes. Zechariah asks what these are. The answer comes as a word to Zerubbabel: “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith Yahuah of hosts” (4:6). A great mountain will become flat ground before him, and he will bring out the top stone with shouting. His hands laid the foundation, and his hands will finish it. No one should despise the day of small things. Last, Zechariah asks about the two olive trees, and he is told: “These are the two anointed ones, that stand by the Adon of the whole earth” (4:14).

## Woken Up

The vision opens with Zechariah being woken, the way a man is shaken out of sleep (4:1). After four visions in one night, the prophet had sunk down, and the angel has to rouse him for the next one. It is a fitting start. The whole vision is about people who had gone to sleep on the work, and the message that wakes them is that the work does not depend on their strength.

## The Golden Lampstand

The lampstand is the first thing Zechariah sees, and every Israelite knew what it was. Moses was told to make one for the tabernacle: “thou shalt make a candlestick of pure gold,” with branches and “bowls made like unto almonds” (Exodus 25:31, 33). It stood in the holy place and gave the only light in the room.

The almond shape was not decoration. When Yahuah settled who would serve as His priests, He had each tribe lay a rod before Him, and Aaron’s dead rod “brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds” overnight (Numbers 17:8). The almond is the tree that wakes first in the year, and the lampstand was built in its shape. The Nazaryah studies [*The Almond Watch*](/blog/the-almond-watch) and [*The Tree in the Midst*](/blog/the-tree-in-the-midst) trace that almond through the Scriptures. In this vision, the lampstand stands for the light of Yahuah’s house, the light of His people before the world.

## Oil No Man Pours

Here is the strange part. In the tabernacle, the lamps needed men to keep them burning. Israel brought “pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always,” and Aaron and his sons had to tend it “from evening to morning before Yahuah” (Exodus 27:20-21). Every day, a priest filled the lamps by hand.

In Zechariah’s vision, no one fills them. Two olive trees stand on either side, and their branches “through the two golden pipes empty the golden oil out of themselves” (4:12). The oil flows straight from the living trees into the lamps. There is no priest with a jar and no harvest to press. The light never runs dry because its supply does not come from men.

Oil in Scripture is the picture of Yahuah’s spirit. When Samuel poured the horn of oil on David, “the Spirit of Yahuah came upon David from that day forward” (1 Samuel 16:13). That is why the angel explains the vision the way he does.

## Not by Might, Nor by Power

Zechariah asks what the lampstand means, and the answer is a single sentence aimed at one man: “This is the word of Yahuah unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith Yahuah of hosts” (4:6).

Zerubbabel was the governor of Judah and a son of David’s line (Haggai 1:1; Matthew 1:12). He had laid the foundation of the house years before, and then the work stopped. He had no army to clear away the enemies, and no money of his own to finish the walls. The word to him is the meaning of the lampstand: the house will not be finished by the strength of the builders any more than the lamp burns by a priest’s hand. The supply comes from Yahuah’s spirit.

## The Mountain Made Plain

“Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain” (4:7). In the prophets, a mountain can stand for a power that blocks the way. Yahuah called Babylon itself a “destroying mountain” and promised to roll it down (Jeremiah 51:25). The mountain in front of Zerubbabel was the opposition: the enemies who had stopped the work and the letters they had sent to the king of Persia (Ezra 4). Yahuah says it will be leveled flat.

Then Zerubbabel “shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it” (4:7). The headstone is the top stone, the last stone set when a building is done. The word translated grace, *chen*, means favor. The people would shout “Favor, favor!” over the finished house. The psalm had already sung of such a stone: “The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner” (Psalm 118:22), and Peter says that stone is Yahushua (1 Peter 2:6-7).

## His Hands Shall Finish It

Then the plain promise: “The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it” (4:9). And they did. Once the prophets began to speak, the builders rose up again (Ezra 5:1-2), and “they builded, and finished it,” and the house was completed in the sixth year of Darius (Ezra 6:14-15). The man who started the work lived to set the last stone.

## The Day of Small Things

“For who hath despised the day of small things?” (4:10). Someone had. When the foundation was first laid, “many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house,” wept out loud (Ezra 3:12). Solomon’s temple had been grand. This one looked like nothing. Haggai asked the same people, “is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?” (Haggai 2:3).

Yahuah answers that they “shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of Yahuah, which run to and fro through the whole earth” (4:10). A plummet is a builder’s plumb line, the weight on a string that keeps a wall straight. Seeing it in Zerubbabel’s hand means the building is going up again. And the seven eyes, the same eyes as the stone in the fourth vision (3:9), are glad to watch it. The house the old men wept over was being watched with joy by Yahuah Himself.

## The Two Anointed Ones

Twice Zechariah asks about the olive trees before he gets the answer: “These are the two anointed ones, that stand by the Adon of the whole earth” (4:14). In Hebrew they are literally “sons of oil,” the ones filled with it. The vision has already named the two men Yahuah was using that year: Joshua the high priest in the fourth vision and Zerubbabel the governor in this one. One from the priestly line, one from David’s line, standing on either side of the lampstand, both fed by the same oil.

The crowning at the end of the night will bring those two offices together on one head: a priest “upon his throne,” with “the counsel of peace … between them both” (6:13).

## Why This Vision Then

Put it together. The builders were few and poor. Their work had been stopped for years. Their foundation looked small to the men who remembered Solomon’s glory. They had no army and no power, and they had gone to sleep on the house.

The vision answers every one of those fears with one picture. The lampstand burns because the oil flows from living trees, not from human hands. The house would be finished the same way. The mountain would go flat. The man who began it would finish it. And the small beginning they were ashamed of was the very thing Yahuah’s eyes were watching with joy.

## Bringing It Home

Believers are the lamps now. Yahushua said, “Ye are the light of the world” (Matthew 5:14). But no lamp makes its own oil. A believer who tries to shine by might and power, by programs, money, and effort, runs dry. The light that lasts is fed by Yahuah’s spirit, poured from a supply no man controls.

And many believers are standing in front of a mountain: a work that stalled, opposition that will not move, a calling that looks small next to what others have built. The word to Zerubbabel is still the word. Not by might. Not by power. By His spirit. Do not despise the day of small things. The eyes of Yahuah are on the plumb line in your hand, and what He started, He will finish (Philippians 1:6).

## Where It Lands

Revelation picks up this vision almost word for word. Yahushua walks among seven golden lampstands, and He says, “the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven” assemblies (Revelation 1:20). The two witnesses who prophesy through the age are named from this chapter: “These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the Elohim of the earth” (Revelation 11:4). The Law and the Prophets stand as Yahuah’s two witnesses, feeding light to His people the way the olive trees fed the lamp. And at the end, the city needs no lamp at all, “for the glory of Elohim did lighten it,” and the Lamb is its lamp (Revelation 21:23). The Greek word there is *luchnos*, a lamp. The glory is the light; the Lamb carries it.
