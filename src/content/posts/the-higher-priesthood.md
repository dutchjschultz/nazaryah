*Yahuah has always been the King. The question is who serves as His priest.*

## Introduction

Scripture keeps two offices apart: the king who rules and the priest who serves at the altar. Only two men ever held both. One was Melchizedek. The other is Messiah. Behind them both stands Yahuah, the true King from the beginning.

## Part I — Eden: Yahuah the King, Adam the Priest

Eden was laid out as Yahuah’s sanctuary, and Adam was placed in it "to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15). Those two Hebrew words, *avad* (serve) and *shamar* (guard), are the same pair Torah later uses for the Levites’ work in the tabernacle (Numbers 3:7-8). Adam’s job was a priest’s job.

Adam was never called king. He was given dominion, *radah* (H7287), over fish, birds, and beasts (Genesis 1:28), but never over other men. He ruled creation as a steward under the King. The crown belonged to Yahuah alone.

## Part II — Between Eden and Sinai

After Adam fell, Yahuah stayed the King of His people. The nations had kings. Edom had a line of them "before there reigned any king over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). The line of promise had none. At the sea, Israel sang, "Yahuah shall reign for ever and ever" (Exodus 15:18). Centuries later, when Israel demanded a human king, Yahuah said, "they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them" (1 Samuel 8:7).

The priesthood in those years rested with the head of each family. Noah built an altar (Genesis 8:20). Abraham built altars wherever he went. The King was in heaven; the fathers served at the altar.

## Part III — Melchizedek: The Higher Order Shown

Then, in the middle of Genesis, one man appears holding both offices. Abram is coming home from battle against four kings, and Melchizedek meets him. He is "king of Salem" and "the priest of the most high God" (Genesis 14:18). Notice the word *of*. He is priest OF the Most High. He serves Yahuah.

Hebrews says Melchizedek was "without father, without mother, without descent" (Hebrews 7:3). His priesthood has no earthly record because it does not come from the earth. He is Yahuah’s way of showing, before Levi was ever born, that there is a higher priesthood. Hebrews even says Levi paid tithes to Melchizedek while still in Abraham’s body (Hebrews 7:9-10). The earthly priesthood bowed to the heavenly one before it existed.

Melchizedek brought out bread and wine (Genesis 14:18). That is a covenant meal, the same two things Yahushua set before His disciples when He said, "This cup is the new testament in my blood" (Luke 22:20). The covenant of the higher priesthood was shown to Abram long before it was sealed in Messiah.

## Part IV — Levi: The Earthly Shadow

At Sinai, Yahuah gave the priesthood to the tribe of Levi in place of Israel’s firstborn (Numbers 3:12). The crown went later to Judah. The two offices were split, and Yahuah kept them split. When King Uzziah went into the temple to burn incense, he was struck with leprosy on the spot (2 Chronicles 26:18-19). No king of Israel could also be its priest.

Hebrews explains why the Levitical priesthood was never the final one. It came by "a carnal commandment," a rule based on physical birth (Hebrews 7:16), serving only "the example and shadow of heavenly things" (Hebrews 8:5). Levi was the earthly copy standing in until the heavenly priesthood returned.

## Part V — Messiah: The Higher Order Restored

Yahuah swore an oath to His Son: "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4). Yahushua’s priesthood stands "after the power of an endless life" (Hebrews 7:16), not on a family line.

He did not take the office for Himself. Hebrews says He "glorified not himself to be made an high priest," but was appointed by the One who said, "Thou art my Son" (Hebrews 5:5). A priest is appointed by someone greater. The Father appoints; the Son serves. That alone shows the Son is not the Father, and not co-equal with Him.

And He holds both offices. Zechariah saw it ahead of time: "he shall be a priest upon his throne" (Zechariah 6:13). Yahuah tells Him, "Sit thou at my right hand," and "rule thou in the midst of thine enemies," using the same word *radah* given to Adam (Psalm 110:1-2). Yahuah is still the King. The Son sits at His right hand, ruling and serving under Him, "on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens" (Hebrews 8:1).

## Conclusion

Heavenly, then earthly, then heavenly again. Melchizedek showed the higher order, Levi stood in as its shadow, and Messiah brought it back for good under Yahuah, the King who never stopped reigning. Revelation shows where it leads: Yahushua "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6).
