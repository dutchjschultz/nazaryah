*Witness Three of Five in the investigation [**The Tree in the Midst**](/investigations/the-tree-in-the-midst) — back to the case.*

## Eden Built Again

When Yahuah gave Moses the pattern of the tabernacle, He built the garden story back into it, piece by piece.

In Eden the guard stood at the east (Genesis 3:24). The tabernacle’s only entrance faced east (Exodus 27:13-16). In Eden the guards were cherubim. In the tabernacle, cherubim were woven into the curtains and the veil (Exodus 26:1, 31), and two cherubim of gold spread their wings over the ark (Exodus 25:18-20). A man walking into the tabernacle walked west, back toward the place Adam was driven from, past the images of the very guards that kept the way.

And inside the Holy Place, Yahuah placed a tree.

## A Tree of Gold

The lampstand was not a candlestick in the modern sense. It was a tree, hammered out of one talent of pure gold (Exodus 25:39), with a shaft like a trunk and six branches, three on each side (Exodus 25:32).

Along the branches were knops and flowers, buds and blossoms, and bowls “made like unto almonds” (Exodus 25:33-34). Bud, blossom, and fruit appeared on the tree at once. A living tree passes through those stages one season at a time. This tree carried all three together, always in season.

This is the only tree in Scripture that Yahuah designed with His own instructions, down to the shape of every cup. He could have chosen any tree. He chose the almond.

Even the gold points back to the garden. The river out of Eden watered the land of Havilah, “where there is gold; and the gold of that land is good” (Genesis 2:11-12). The first good gold in Scripture lies beside the garden. The tree in the tabernacle was made of it.

## Twenty-Two Almonds

Count the almond cups. Each of the six branches carried three, and the shaft carried four (Exodus 25:33-34). That makes twenty-two.

The text does not explain the number, and this study does not build a code on it. It is worth noticing only as a pattern. The Hebrew alphabet has twenty-two letters, and the longest psalm in Scripture, Psalm 119, is built in twenty-two sections, one for each letter, every one of them about Yahuah’s Law. The Sealed Count investigation follows that number further. Here the point is simpler: the tree of gold carried as many almonds as the Law has letters.

## Set Before the Testimony

The lampstand was not set in a random corner. Its lamps burned “without the vail, which is before the testimony” (Exodus 27:21). On the other side of that veil sat the ark, and inside the ark lay the tables of the Law.

The almond tree of gold stood facing the commandments, day and night. The priests tended it “from evening to morning before Yahuah” (Exodus 27:21), so the light never went out.

Scripture joins the lamp and the Law in so many words: “For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light” (Proverbs 6:23). “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path” (Psalm 119:105). The tree, the lamp, and the Law stood in one room, facing one direction.

## The Lamp and the Light

The lampstand gave light, but it did not make light. It held oil, and the light belonged to Yahuah. “Yahuah is my light” (Psalm 27:1).

Scripture keeps that order when it speaks of Messiah. Yahushua called Himself “the light of the world” (John 8:12), and Revelation calls Him “the bright and morning star” (Revelation 22:16). But in the city to come, Revelation states plainly who is the source and who is the carrier: “the glory of Elohim did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof” (Revelation 21:23). The word for light there is luchnos, a lamp.

The glory of Elohim is the light. The Lamb is the lamp that carries it. The almond tree of gold taught that difference long before Revelation spelled it out. A lamp holds the light and bears it. The light comes from the One who gives the oil.

## The Way Into the Holy Place

In Eden the way to the tree was shut. In the tabernacle the way into the Holy Place, where the tree of gold stood, was open only to the priests. The people never saw the almond tree with their own eyes. They stood outside the door hanging at the east (Exodus 26:36).

When Yahushua died, the veil of the temple was torn (Matthew 27:51). Hebrews calls the way He opened “a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh” (Hebrews 10:20). The believer is made a priest, and the priest’s place is the Holy Place, the room where the lamp of almonds burns.

Hebrews 10:19 speaks of entering “into the holiest,” which in Greek is ta hagia, the holy places. It does not put the believer behind the inner veil. Behind that veil “went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood” (Hebrews 9:7), and Messiah entered there once, by His own blood (Hebrews 9:11-12). The believer-priest enters to stand before the lamp, facing the testimony, and to walk in its light.

## Right There All Along

Most readers know the lampstand as tabernacle furniture and pass over it. Yet the pattern is plain in Exodus: an Eden-shaped house, an east gate, cherubim at the way, and an almond tree of gold set before the Law. The tree of life was built again in front of Israel’s priests, and it was there in the text the whole time.

## Where It Ends

John saw the same tree in heaven. “I saw seven golden candlesticks” (Revelation 1:12), and in the midst of them stood one like the Son of man (Revelation 1:13). The almond lamps still burn, and the One who walks among them still carries His Father’s light.
