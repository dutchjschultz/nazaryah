## What Yahuah Is Doing in Hebrews

Yahuah spoke to the fathers through the prophets, and in these last days He has spoken through a Son He appointed heir of all things (1:1-2). Hebrews shows Yahuah putting that Son in place as High Priest over His house, so the plan He started at Sinai is carried forward and not lost.

The letter was written to Hebrew believers while the temple in Jerusalem was still standing and the priests were still offering there every day (8:4; 10:11), so before the Romans destroyed it in 70 AD. These believers were under pressure to go back to the old priesthood and drop Messiah. The writer does not name himself. He calls his letter a "word of exhortation" (13:22), a sermon sent in writing.

The pattern is simple. Seven times the writer sets Messiah beside something the Hebrews already honored and shows Him better. After most of those, he stops and warns them not to drift. Teaching, then warning; teaching, then warning.

## 1. A Better Messenger Than the Angels — Chapters 1–2

The Law came through angels (2:2; Acts 7:53). The Son was "made so much better than the angels" and "obtained by inheritance" a better name (1:4). He was made and He inherited, so the One who made Him and gave Him the name is greater. Even the verse quoted for "Thy throne, O God" goes on to say "God, even thy God, hath anointed thee" (1:8-9). The Son has a God, and that God is Yahuah.

> **Warning:** *If the word spoken by angels stood firm, there is no escape for those who neglect what was first spoken by the Master (2:1-3).*

## 2. A Better Leader Than Moses and Joshua — Chapters 3–4

Moses was faithful as a servant in the house; the Son is faithful as a Son over it, "whose house are we" (3:5-6). Joshua led Israel into the land but did not give them the final rest (4:8). So "there remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God" (4:9), the *sabbatismos*, a Sabbath-keeping that still stands. Hebrews does not end the Sabbath; it says one remains.

> **Warning:** *The wilderness generation fell in the desert through unbelief (Psalm 95). Today, if you hear His voice, harden not your hearts (3:7-19).*

## 3. A Better Priest Than Aaron — Chapters 5–7

Aaron's line came by birth from Levi. Messiah was called by Yahuah a priest "after the order of Melchisedec" (5:5-6; Psalm 110:4), made "after the power of an endless life" (7:16). Abraham paid tithes to Melchizedek, so Levi, still in Abraham's body, paid them too (7:9-10). The priesthood changed, and with it came "a change also of the law" (7:12), the law of who may serve as priest. The Ten Words did not change.

> **Warning:** *Those who have tasted the heavenly gift and fall away crucify the Son afresh. Be followers of those who through belief and patience inherit the promises (5:11–6:12).*

## 4. A Better Covenant — Chapter 8

Yahuah "found fault with them" (8:8), with the people, not with the Law. The new covenant of Jeremiah 31 does not remove the Law; it moves it: "I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts" (8:10). What was "ready to vanish away" (8:13) was the old arrangement that left the Law on stone and outside the heart.

## 5. A Better Sanctuary — Chapter 9

The earthly tabernacle was a copy of the true one in heaven (8:5; 9:24). Its high priest went into the holiest once a year, never without blood (9:7). Messiah entered the true holy place in heaven "once" by His own blood and obtained eternal redemption (9:12). He will appear a second time "unto salvation" (9:28), so the finish of deliverance is still ahead.

## 6. A Better Sacrifice — Chapter 10

The blood of bulls and goats could never take away sins (10:4). Yahuah prepared a body (10:5), and through that body offered once the believer is set apart (10:10). The veil is opened by "a new and living way" (10:20), so draw near with a true heart (10:22).

> **Warning:** *If we sin willfully after receiving the truth, there remains no more sacrifice for sins. Do not throw away your confidence; the just shall live by belief (10:26-39).*

## 7. A Better Country and City — Chapters 11–13

Chapter 11 lists those who walked by trust and confessed they were "strangers and pilgrims" (11:13), looking for a city whose builder and maker is Yahuah (11:10, 16). The readers have not come to the burning mountain of Sinai but to Mount Sion, the heavenly Jerusalem (12:18-22). The altar still stands: "We have an altar" (13:10). Yahushua suffered outside the gate, so go out to Him outside the camp (13:12-13).

> **Warning:** *See that you refuse not Him that speaks from heaven; our God is a consuming fire (12:25-29).*

## The Sum

The writer gives the sum himself: "We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens" (8:1). Yahuah raised Him from the dead (13:20) and seated Him as Priest over the house, with the Law written on the hearts of those in it. Revelation shows where the road ends, with the Lamb standing on Mount Sion and His Father's name written on the foreheads of the people with Him (Revelation 14:1).
