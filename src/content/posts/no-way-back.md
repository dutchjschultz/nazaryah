Leaving Egypt took one night. Leaving it for good took the sea. The crossing is where the old master was cut off, and where Israel learned that a closed road is not the same as a changed heart.

## Shut In

Yahuah led Israel to camp by the sea, between Migdol and the water (14:2), and Pharaoh read the map: "They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in" (14:3). He sent his chariots after them. With the sea in front and Egypt behind, the people said to Moses, "it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness" (14:12). Their first answer under pressure was to go back to their old master.

## Stand Still

Moses answered, "Fear ye not, stand still," and watch what Yahuah would do, for "the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever" (14:13). Then he added, "Yahuah shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace" (14:14). Israel did not lift a sword. The cloud moved behind them and stood between the two camps, darkness to Egypt and light to Israel, all night (14:19–20). The deliverance was Yahuah's from start to finish.

## Through the Water

The sea opened, and Israel "went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground" (14:22). When Egypt followed, the waters came back and covered every chariot and horseman; "there remained not so much as one of them" (14:28). Paul reads the crossing as an immersion: the fathers "were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea" (1 Corinthians 10:1–2). Immersion is a burial of the old life. Believers are "buried with him by baptism into death," so that they "should walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:4). Micah gives the same picture of sin: Yahuah "wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea" (Micah 7:19). The old master did not follow them out of the water.

## The Heart Turns Back

On the far shore Israel sang, "Yahuah is my strength and song" (15:2). Three days later, at the bitter water of Marah, they were complaining (15:22–24). A month after that they were remembering "the flesh pots" of Egypt (16:3), and later still the fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic they had eaten there for nothing (Numbers 11:5). At Kadesh they said, "Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt" (Numbers 14:4). Stephen sums up the whole generation: "in their hearts turned back again into Egypt" (Acts 7:39).

The sea closed the road. It did not close the heart. That is why the wilderness came next: Yahuah had brought Israel out of Egypt, and now He had to bring Egypt out of Israel. The believer's crossing is real and final, and the old master is buried. The longing for the flesh pots is what the walk after the water is meant to deal with.

## Revelation

John sees the crossing again at the end. Those who overcame stand "on the sea of glass, having the harps of Elohim," and sing "the song of Moses… and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:2–3), the song first sung on the far shore of the sea. In the new earth the barrier is gone altogether: "there was no more sea" (Revelation 21:1).
