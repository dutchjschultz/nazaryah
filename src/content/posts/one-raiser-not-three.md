*Galatians 1:1 · Romans 8:11*

## The Claim

A common argument for the Trinity goes to the empty tomb. The Father raised Yahushua (Galatians 1:1). Yahushua said He would raise Himself (John 2:19). The Spirit raised Him (Romans 8:11). Three raisers, so the argument goes, and only God raises the dead, so all three must be God.

The whole argument leans on Romans 8:11. Read it slowly, and it says the opposite.

## Galatians 1:1 — The Raiser Named

Paul opens his letter by naming two figures: Yahushua Messiah, and the Father, *"who raised him from the dead"*. One raised. One was raised. The Son is the one acted upon, not the one acting. No third figure stands at the tomb.

## Romans 8:11 — The Spirit Belongs to the Raiser

Paul does not write "the Spirit that raised up Yahushua." He writes *"the Spirit of him that raised up Yahushua"*. Notice what the text does NOT say. Three things stand out.

**One Raiser.** The raising is credited to "him" — one person, the one the Spirit belongs to. That is the same Father named in Galatians 1:1.

**The Spirit is His.** Twice in one verse the Spirit is described as owned: *"the Spirit of him"*, then *"his Spirit"*. The Greek word is pneuma (G4151), meaning breath or wind. Something a person owns is not a second person standing beside him. Yahushua called it *"the Spirit of your Father"* (Matthew 10:20).

**The Spirit is the means.** The same Raiser will give life to believers' bodies *"by his Spirit"*. He does the work; the Spirit is how He does it. A man lifts a stone by his own strength, and nobody counts the strength as a second man.

The verse most often used to put a third Person at the tomb is the verse that names one Raiser.

## Every Witness Agrees

Peter told the crowd that Yahuah raised Him up (Acts 2:24, 32). Paul said the same in the synagogue (Acts 13:30). Yahuah *"raised up the Master, and will also raise up us by his own power"* (1 Corinthians 6:14). Messiah was raised *"by the glory of the Father"* (Romans 6:4). The Father of glory *"raised him from the dead"* (Ephesians 1:17, 20).

Not one verse says the Spirit raised Him. Not one verse says the Son raised Himself apart from the Father. 1 Corinthians 6:14 follows the exact pattern of Romans 8:11 — one Raiser, working by His own power.

## 1 Peter 3:18 — Quickened by the Spirit

Peter says Messiah was *"quickened by the Spirit"*. That is the means again, not the doer. Two chapters earlier the same writer names the doer: Yahuah, *"that raised him up from the dead, and gave him glory"* (1 Peter 1:21). Peter does not argue with himself.

## John 2:19 and 10:18 — The Son's Part

Yahushua said He had power to take His life again. The word is exousia (G1849) — authority given by someone else. He names the giver in the same breath: *"This commandment have I received of my Father"* (John 10:18). When He said *"in three days I will raise it up"* (John 2:19), He spoke as a Son carrying out an order. Here is the key: the one who receives the commandment is not the one who gives it. Every apostle afterward credits the raising to the Father.

The same word is handed to the believer. To as many as received Him, He gave *"power"* — exousia again — *"to become the sons of God"* (John 1:12). The believer's body is a temple (1 Corinthians 6:19), and the believer holds the authority to raise it up: trust in Yahuah through the blood, be covered, walk in His commandments, and rise to new life. Romans 6:4 ties the two together — as Messiah was raised by the glory of the Father, *"even so we also should walk in newness of life"*. The choice is free, and it belongs to the believer.

But the authority is received, just as the Son's was. The Father still gives the life — He *"shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit"* (Romans 8:11). The choice belongs to the believer; the power belongs to the Father.

## What the Verses Show

Put Galatians 1:1 and Romans 8:11 side by side and there are not three raisers. There is one. Yahuah the Father raised Yahushua, by His own Spirit, and the Son was the one brought up out of the grave.

Take the Spirit out as a separate Person and nothing in either verse is lost. Put one in, and Romans 8:11 has to be rewritten to say "the Spirit that raised up Yahushua" — words it never says.

The pulpit uses the resurrection to count Persons. The text uses it to name a Father.

## The Close in Revelation

The risen Son says it Himself: *"I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore"* (Revelation 1:18). The same book opens by naming the source of everything He carries — the revelation *"which Yahuah gave unto him"* (Revelation 1:1). Alive forever, and still receiving from the One who raised Him.
