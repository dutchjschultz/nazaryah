Before Moses died he taught Israel a song. It was not a celebration. Yahuah told him to write it so that it would stand as a witness when the people turned to other gods (31:19–21). The heart step of the walk ends with words meant to be remembered when the heart wanders.

## The Rock

The song opens with who Yahuah is: "He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: an El of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he" (32:4). Everything after it is measured against that Rock.

## The Portion

"Remember the days of old" (32:7). When the Most High divided the nations, "Yahuah's portion is his people; Jacob is the lot of his inheritance" (32:8–9). He found Israel "in a desert land," kept him "as the apple of his eye," and carried him the way an eagle stirs her nest and bears her young on her wings (32:10–11).

## The Turning

Then the song tells what Israel would do with that care. "Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked" (32:15). Full and comfortable, Israel forgot the Rock. "They sacrificed unto devils, not to Elohim; to gods whom they knew not" (32:17). The song does not hide it. It was written down beforehand so no one could later claim surprise.

## No God With Him

The answer comes from Yahuah alone: "See now that I, even I, am he, and there is no god with me: I kill, and I make alive; I wound, and I heal" (32:39). The song names one speaker, one Rock, and one judge. No one stands beside Him.

## Rejoice, O Nations

The song ends wider than Israel: "Rejoice, O ye nations, with his people: for he will avenge the blood of his servants" (32:43). Paul quotes the opening of that line to show that the nations were always meant to join Yahuah's people: "Rejoice, ye Gentiles, with his people" (Romans 15:10).

## For the Walk

The song was given for the day the heart turns. A believer who drifts can sing it back to himself and find his way home: the Rock has not moved, His work is still perfect, and the gods of comfort are still devils dressed up.

## Revelation

In Revelation the overcomers stand on the sea of glass and sing "the song of Moses the servant of Elohim, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3). Their words echo Deuteronomy 32:4: "Great and marvellous are thy works… just and true are thy ways" (Revelation 15:3). The vengeance the song promised arrives in heaven's own praise: He "hath avenged the blood of his servants" (Revelation 19:2).
