## The Riddle on Mount Gerizim

Gideon refused the crown. He told Israel plainly, “Yahuah shall rule over you” (Judges 8:23). After Gideon died, his son Abimelech wanted the throne anyway. He took seventy pieces of silver out of the house of Baal-berith and hired “vain and light persons” to follow him (9:4). Then he killed his seventy brothers on one stone (9:5). Only the youngest, Jotham, escaped.

Jotham climbed Mount Gerizim and called a riddle down to the men of Shechem (9:7). The trees went out to anoint a king. They asked the olive, then the fig, then the vine. Each one refused, and each one named the gift it would have to leave behind. Last of all they asked the bramble, and the bramble said yes.

Each good tree asked the same question: should it leave its gift “and go to be promoted over the trees?” (9:9, 11, 13). The word for “promoted” is *nua*. It means to sway, to totter, to wander back and forth. A tree that leaves its fruit in order to rule becomes a tree that sways in the wind and bears nothing. The three good trees served by bearing. Only the tree with nothing to give wanted the throne.

## The Claim

The common reading stops at the surface. The trees are Israel, the bramble is Abimelech, and the lesson is over. That much is true, and Jotham applies it that way himself (9:16-20). But a nation is only many men, and Scripture calls the man himself a tree. “I am like a green olive tree in the house of God,” David said (Psalm 52:8). “I am the vine, ye are the branches,” Yahushua said (John 15:5).

This investigation puts one claim on the stand. The three good trees of Jotham’s riddle describe, in detail, how a believer walks. Each tree is one part of the same walk, and together they are the signs of a true believer. The bramble is the man who has none of the three and still wants the throne.

- The vine is the covenant entry: a man brought in by the blood.
- The fig is the walk in the Law: a man who loves the commandments and keeps them.
- The olive is the anointing: a man filled with Yahuah’s Spirit, giving light and serving.
- The bramble is the counterfeit: a man offering a shelter he does not have.

## Three Parts of One Walk

These are not three different roads, and a man does not get to pick one. They are three parts of one walk, and Scripture keeps them together. The prophets name the vine and the fig side by side, and the vine comes first: “every man under his vine and under his fig tree” (Micah 4:4). Blood first, then the Law. The cleansing of the leper adds the oil. The priest put blood on the man’s right ear, thumb, and great toe, and then put oil “upon the blood” in the same three places (Leviticus 14:14, 17). The Spirit rests on a man who has come in by the blood and walks in the Law.

Remove the vine, and there is no entry. Remove the fig, and there is a profession with no walk. Remove the olive, and there is a lamp with no light. Remove all three, and only the bramble is left.

## The Trees Bear in the Same Order

As supporting evidence from the land itself: the three good trees give their fruit in the same order as the walk. The vine’s first-ripe grapes come in midsummer (Numbers 13:20), and the walk begins at the vine. The fig bears the longest of the three. Its early figs show with the leaves in spring, and its main crop comes in late summer, just as the walk in the Law is the longest part of a believer’s life. The olive is gathered last, in the fall, around the feasts that look forward to the King. The anointing reaches its full yield at the end of the walk, when a man is “fat and flourishing” and still bearing “fruit in old age” (Psalm 92:14).

## The Counterfeit

The bramble also offered a gift: “put your trust in my shadow” (Judges 9:15). But a thorn bush has no shade to give, and the offer was a lie. It even began with the word truth, “If in truth ye anoint me king,” and it was not true. Behind the shade it hid a bought throne and a fire that devoured the men who trusted it (9:49). The counterfeit believer looks the same way. He offers what only Yahuah can give, and he has none of the three.

## The Four Witnesses

*Witness One, The Vine: The Covenant Entry.* How a man gets in at all. The wine is the blood of the covenant, and the entry of one man cheers God and man.

*Witness Two, The Fig Tree: The Walk in the Law.* How a man walks once he is in. The Law is sweet to him, and his life bears its fruit for all to see.

*Witness Three, The Olive Tree: The Anointed Walk.* What Yahuah pours into the man who walks. The oil sets him apart, makes him a light, and is spent on others.

*Witness Four, The Bramble: The Counterfeit.* The man with none of the three. His shelter is a lie, his throne is bought, and his end is fire.

## The Planter Is Not the Plant

Every picture in this investigation keeps two parties apart. Yahuah planted the olive and named it (Jeremiah 11:16). “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman” (John 15:1). The One who plants is not the One who grows, and the One who pours the oil is not the One who receives it. A co-equal Trinity has no room in a picture where One plants and the other grows.

## Where the Trees Stand at the End

Revelation brings every tree back. The two olive trees stand before the God of the earth (Revelation 11:4). The untimely figs fall in the storm (6:13). The vine of the earth goes into the winepress (14:19). And the men who walked the whole walk are named: “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life” (22:14). That last tree bears twelve manner of fruits, yields “her fruit every month,” and its leaves are “for the healing of the nations” (22:2).
