## The Problem That Trips People Up

Two numbers sit side by side in Scripture and seem to argue with each other. In Genesis, Yahuah tells Abraham his seed will be afflicted 400 years. In Exodus, the text says Israel’s sojourning lasted 430 years. Skeptics point to it as a mistake. Most Bible teachers try to round it off or blend the two together as if they were one prophecy.

They are not one prophecy. They are two different clocks, counting two different things, that happen to stop at the same finish line. The gap between them is exactly 30 years, and Scripture shows where those 30 years went.

## Clock One: The 430 Years of Sojourning

> *Now the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years. And it came to pass at the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day it came to pass, that all the hosts of Yahuah went out from the land of Egypt.* (Exodus 12:40-41)

This clock measures sojourning, which simply means living as a guest in someone else’s land. Paul says plainly where it started:

> *And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of Elohim in Messiah, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect.* (Galatians 3:17)

So the 430-year clock starts the day the promise was given to Abraham and stops when the Law was given after the Exodus. Abraham received that promise when he left Haran at 75 years old (Genesis 12:4). From that day on, he and his family lived as guests, first in Canaan and then in Egypt.

A simple family-tree check proves the 430 years cannot all be Egypt. Yahuah said Israel would come out “in the fourth generation” (Genesis 15:16). Those four generations are Levi, Kohath, Amram, and Moses (Exodus 6:16-20). Kohath went down into Egypt as a boy (Genesis 46:11). Kohath lived 133 years (Exodus 6:18), Amram lived 137 (Exodus 6:20), and Moses was 80 at the Exodus (Exodus 7:7). Even pretending Kohath was a newborn when he arrived and that each father had his son in the very last year of his life, the total is 350 years, still 80 short of 430. Real fathers have their sons long before they die, so the true figure is far lower. The clock had to start back in Canaan, with Abraham.

## Two Older Witnesses Put Canaan Back In

The Hebrew text behind most English Bibles is the Masoretic Text, and in Exodus 12:40 it names only Egypt. Two older witnesses read the verse differently, and both say the same thing.

- **The Septuagint,** the Greek translation made centuries before the Masoretic text was finalized, says the children of Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan for 430 years.

- **The Samaritan Pentateuch,** an independent copy of the five books of Moses, also names both lands, Canaan and Egypt.

Paul agrees with them. His 430 years in Galatians 3:17 run from the promise to Abraham in Canaan, not from Jacob’s arrival in Egypt. The Masoretic copy leaves Canaan out of the verse, and that shorter wording is the open door the “430 years in Egypt” teaching walked through. Two ancient witnesses, Paul’s own count, and the ages written in Genesis all put Canaan back in. When copies disagree, the copy that matches the rest of Scripture is the one to trust, and here the arithmetic leaves no room for doubt.

## Splitting the 430: Half in Canaan, Half in Egypt

The ages Scripture gives make the split easy to see. Think of it as a family timeline on the refrigerator door:

- **25 years:** from the promise at Abraham’s 75 to Isaac’s birth at Abraham’s 100 (Genesis 12:4; 21:5).

- **60 years:** Isaac was 60 when Jacob was born (Genesis 25:26).

- **130 years:** Jacob told Pharaoh he was 130 when he arrived in Egypt (Genesis 47:9).

Add 25, 60, and 130, and the total is 215 years in Canaan. That leaves 215 years for Egypt. The 430 years are cut right down the middle: half as guests in Canaan, half as guests in Egypt.

## The 71 Years of Favor

Israel did not walk into Egypt and straight into chains. Joseph was running the country.

- **Joseph was 30** when he stood before Pharaoh and took charge (Genesis 41:46).

- **Seven years of plenty and two of famine** passed before Jacob came down (Genesis 45:6), which made Joseph 39 when his father arrived.

- **Joseph died at 110** (Genesis 50:22).

From 39 to 110 is 71 years. For those 71 years the family lived in the best of the land under the protection of the second most powerful man in Egypt. That is not slavery. That is a family living off a very well-placed son.

## How Long Was the Slavery, Really?

Scripture says the trouble only started after a change in leadership:

> *And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation. … Now there arose up a new king over Egypt, which knew not Joseph.* (Exodus 1:6, 8)

Take the 215 years in Egypt and subtract Joseph’s 71 years of favor. That leaves 144 years at the very most. Moses was 80 when he stood before Pharaoh (Exodus 7:7), so the 144 breaks down as 64 years before Moses was born and his 80 years of life. And 144 is the ceiling, not the real figure. The new king did not arise until all Joseph’s brothers had died too, and Levi lived to 137 (Exodus 6:16), outliving Joseph by roughly twenty years. The actual bondage was most likely closer to 120 years.

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

Ask almost any congregation how long Israel was enslaved in Egypt, and the answer comes back without hesitation: four hundred years. It is preached from pulpits, printed in Sunday school curriculum, and repeated in every Exodus film. It is wrong, and it is not a small slip. Scripture’s own numbers show Egypt held Israel for 215 years in total, and of those, at most 144 were spent in bondage.

The error comes from reading Genesis 15:13 carelessly and then never checking it against the ages written in plain sight in the same book. The 400 years were never a prophecy about Egyptian slave camps. They were the affliction of the promised seed, and that affliction began with a mocking half-brother at a family party in Canaan, generations before anyone set foot in Egypt.

This matters beyond the arithmetic. A teaching that cannot add up the ages printed in Genesis has not been studying the text. It has been repeating what it was handed. When the same institutions hand down that the Law was a burden, that it was nailed to the cross, or that the Sabbath moved, the reader has every reason to open the book and check the numbers for himself. Scripture keeps its own books balanced. Tradition does not.

## Clock Two: The 400 Years of Affliction

> *Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them, and they shall afflict them four hundred years.* (Genesis 15:13)

This clock measures something different. It is not counting years of living as a guest. It is counting years of the seed being afflicted, picked on, mistreated. And a seed cannot be picked on until the seed is born.

Yahuah made clear which child was the promised seed: “in Isaac shall thy seed be called” (Genesis 21:12). So the affliction clock cannot start with Abraham at 75. It has to start with Isaac.

## Where the Affliction Started

> *And the child grew, and was weaned: and Abraham made a great feast the same day that Isaac was weaned. And Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, which she had born unto Abraham, mocking.* (Genesis 21:8-9)

Picture a family party for a little boy, and an older half-brother standing off to the side making fun of him. That is the first recorded mistreatment of the promised seed. It looks like a small thing, but Paul did not treat it as small. He calls it persecution:

> *But as then he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit, even so it is now.* (Galatians 4:29)

Paul, writing by the Spirit, marks the weaning feast as the day the affliction began. That is the starting gun for the 400-year clock.

## The 30 Years Accounted For

The timeline is easy to follow:

- **Abraham is 75:** the promise is given. The 430-year clock starts (Genesis 12:4).

- **Abraham is 100:** Isaac is born (Genesis 21:5).

- **Abraham is 105:** Isaac, about five years old, is weaned and Ishmael mocks him. The 400-year clock starts (Genesis 21:8-9; Galatians 4:29).

- **400 years later:** both clocks stop together at the Exodus and the giving of the Law.

From the promise to the mocking is 30 years. Take 30 from 430, and the remainder is 400. The two numbers are not a contradiction. They are a starting line 30 years apart and a finish line shared by both. Scripture does not state Isaac’s exact age at weaning, but the two fixed numbers place the mocking 30 years after the promise, and a boy of about five fits a weaning feast naturally. Stephen repeats the 400-year count in Acts 7:6, confirming that the affliction prophecy stood on its own.

## Why the Finish Line Matters

Both clocks ran out at the same place: Sinai, where Yahuah gave His Law. Yahuah later describes that covenant as a marriage, saying He was “an husband unto them” (Jeremiah 31:32). Sinai was the wedding, and the Law was the wedding vows.

That order is the point Paul makes in Galatians 3:17. Many modern teachers use that verse to argue the Law was a temporary add-on that has now been thrown away. Paul says the opposite of throwing anything away. His point is that the Law, coming 430 years after the promise, does not cancel the promise. The promise came first. The vows came after. A bride does not make vows in order to become engaged; she makes them because she already belongs to the groom. In the same way, deliverance comes first through the blood of Yahushua the Messiah, and walking in Yahuah’s commandments follows as the life of those who already belong to Him.

## Summary

- **The 430 years** count the sojourning, from the promise to Abraham at 75 until the Law.

- **Those 430 years** split evenly: 215 in Canaan and 215 in Egypt.

- **The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch** both name Canaan and Egypt in Exodus 12:40, matching Paul’s count.

- **The first 71 years in Egypt** were years of favor under Joseph, not bondage.

- **The slavery** lasted 144 years at the most, and likely closer to 120, never 400.

- **The 400 years** count the affliction of the seed, from Ishmael mocking Isaac until the Law.

- **The 30-year gap** is the time between the promise and the first persecution of the promised child.

- **Both clocks end** at Sinai, where the promise was followed by the wedding vows.

## Looking Ahead to Revelation

The pattern of promise first, vows second, and a wedding at the end has not finished playing out. The bride of the Lamb is still sojourning and still facing the mockery that began at Isaac’s weaning feast. Revelation shows how that story closes: “Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready” (Revelation 19:7), clothed in fine linen, which is the righteousness of the saints.
