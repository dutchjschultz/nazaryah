Still the same night (1:7). Five visions have passed. The city has been promised, the horns cast out, the wall of fire set around Jerusalem, the high priest cleaned, and the lampstand shown burning with oil that no man pours. Every one of them has been about Yahuah’s house going up. Now Zechariah turns and lifts his eyes again, and what he sees is not a builder or a lamp. It is the Law, flying.

## The Vision

Zechariah sees a roll, a scroll, flying through the air. He is asked what he sees and gives its size: twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide (5:2). The angel tells him it is the curse going out over the face of the whole land. Every thief will be cut off according to what is written on one side, and every man who swears falsely according to what is written on the other. Yahuah says He will send it out, and it will enter the house of the thief and the house of the man who swears falsely by His name. It will stay in the middle of that house until it has eaten up the timber and the stones (5:3-4).

## A Scroll the Size of the Porch

Twenty cubits by ten is roughly thirty feet by fifteen, far too big to read in anyone’s hand. It is meant to be seen. And the measure is not random. When Solomon built the temple, he built a porch across its front: “twenty cubits was the length thereof, according to the breadth of the house; and ten cubits was the breadth thereof” (1 Kings 6:3). The flying scroll is exactly the size of the entrance to Yahuah’s house.

That tells the reader what the scroll is measuring. The porch was the way in. Everyone who came to the house of Yahuah passed through that space. Now a scroll the same size goes out over the land, and the question it carries is the question of the porch: who is fit to come in? The people were rebuilding the house. The scroll went out to measure the people who would stand in front of it.

## Written on Both Sides

The angel says the curse is written “on this side” and “on that side” (5:3). The scroll is written front and back. Only one other writing in Scripture is described that way. When Moses came down Sinai, “the tables were written on both their sides; on the one side and on the other were they written” (Exodus 32:15). The tablets were the testimony, the ten words written by Yahuah’s own finger.

The flying scroll is the Law, written the way the tablets were written. It is not a new rule for a new age. It is the same covenant Israel agreed to at Sinai, with the same blessings and the same curses, now flying over the land they had come home to.

## The Thief and the False Swearer

Out of everything the Law forbids, the scroll names two: “every one that stealeth” and “every one that sweareth” falsely “by my name” (5:3-4). Those two are not picked at random either. They come from the two halves of the ten words. “Thou shalt not take the name of Yahuah thy Elohim in vain” (Exodus 20:7) stands among the commandments toward Yahuah. “Thou shalt not steal” (Exodus 20:15) stands among the commandments toward a man’s neighbor. One commandment from each table, one on each side of the scroll.

The Law had already put these two side by side: “Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another. And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy Elohim” (Leviticus 19:11-12). Stealing from a neighbor and lying in Yahuah’s name are two sides of one sin. The man who robs his neighbor and then swears by the Name that he did nothing has broken both tables at once. The scroll names the whole Law by naming one sin from each side of it.

## The Curse That Goes Forth

“This is the curse that goeth forth over the face of the whole earth” (5:3). The word for earth here can also mean land, and it is the land the captives had just come back to. The curse is not a new idea. Moses set it before Israel before they ever crossed the Jordan: “if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of Yahuah thy Elohim, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes … all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee” (Deuteronomy 28:15). And again: “Cursed be he that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them” (Deuteronomy 27:26).

Overtake is the right word for a scroll that flies. The curses of the covenant were never meant to sit on a shelf. They go out and find the people who break it.

## Into the House, Timber and Stones

Then the strangest part of the vision. The scroll does not just pass overhead. It goes into the house of the thief and the false swearer, “and it shall remain in the midst of his house, and shall consume it with the timber thereof and the stones thereof” (5:4).

The Law had a word for a house that had to be eaten down to timber and stones. If a plague of leprosy spread in the walls of a house and could not be cleaned, the priest declared it “a fretting leprosy in the house: it is unclean. And he shall break down the house, the stones of it, and the timber thereof, and all the morter of the house” (Leviticus 14:44-45). A house rotten all the way through could not be patched. It had to come down.

That is what the scroll does to the house of the thief and the liar. It moves in, stays, and takes the house apart. The sin is not on the surface. It is in the walls.

## Why This Vision, and Why Now

Every vision that night answers the same question: Yahuah’s house is going up, so what will He do about the land around it? The first five gave comfort. The house will be built, the enemies cast out, the city protected, the priest cleansed, and the lamp kept burning by His spirit. The sixth vision turns to the people themselves. A holy house cannot stand in the middle of a neighborhood of thieves and liars.

The returned captives knew that problem well. Some of them had spent years building their own houses while His lay waste (Haggai 1:4, 9). Two years after this night, Yahuah sent them the same two commands the scroll carries: “Speak ye every man the truth to his neighbour … And let none of you imagine evil in your hearts against his neighbour; and love no false oath” (Zechariah 8:16-17). The temple would be finished. The question the scroll put to them was whether they would be fit to stand in its porch.

So the sixth vision says what the Law does when Yahuah moves back in. It does not disappear. It goes out ahead of Him over the land, finds the houses built on stealing and lying, and takes them down. The house He is building is protected. The houses that broke His covenant are not.

## Bringing It Home

Most believers were taught that the Law was the curse, and that the Messiah came to remove it. Read the verse again: “Messiah hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us” (Galatians 3:13). From the curse of the Law. Not from the Law. The scroll in Zechariah is that curse, flying over every house that breaks the covenant. The blood of the Lamb Yahuah provided is how a man’s house is taken out from under it. Nothing is added to that, and no one earns it.

But a house that has been spared still has to be built, and Yahushua said how. “Whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock.” The other man heard and did not do, and his house fell, “and great was the fall of it” (Matthew 7:24-27). The difference between the two houses was not the hearing. It was the doing.

The flying scroll still asks what it asked the captives. Is anything in the walls of this house taken from someone else? Is the Name sworn by in this house with a lie? A believer delivered by the blood does not keep the thief’s house or the false swearer’s house. He keeps the commandments the scroll was written with, and he builds on the rock.

## Where It Lands

The scroll flies all the way to the end of the Book. When the dead stand before the great white throne, “the books were opened … and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works” (Revelation 20:12). Outside the holy city are the same kinds of people the scroll went after: “dogs, and sorcerers, and whoremongers, and murderers, and idolaters, and whosoever loveth and maketh a lie” (Revelation 22:15). And inside, the scroll has finished its flight. “And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of Elohim and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him” (Revelation 22:3).
