## When, Not If

Matthew 6 handles three practices in a row, and the structure is identical in all three: when thou doest alms, when thou prayest, when thou fastest. Not if. Yahushua never argues that His hearers should give, pray, or fast, because the Scriptures had settled all three long before, and He is speaking to people who already did them.

Giving: "thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land" (Deuteronomy 15:11; also Leviticus 19:9-10; Deuteronomy 24:19-21).

Prayer: "if from thence thou shalt seek Yahuah thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart" (Deuteronomy 4:29); "Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray" (Psalm 55:17).

Fasting: Yom Kippur, where the command is to afflict your souls, a statute for ever (Leviticus 16:29-31); "turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting" (Joel 2:12).

## The Problem Was the Audience

The hypocrites sound a trumpet, stand praying in the synagogues and on the street corners, and disfigure their faces so their fasting will register. The word is hypokrites, a stage actor. Nothing is said against the alms, the prayer, or the fast. Every correction lands on who the performance was aimed at.

"They have their reward" translates a commercial term, apecho, used on receipts: paid in full. The transaction closed the moment the crowd noticed. There is nothing outstanding to collect.

## Seen in Secret

Against the street corner Yahushua sets the closet, the shut door, the washed face, the hand that does not tell the other hand. The reason is stated in the Scriptures He was raised on: "man looketh on the outward appearance, but Yahuah looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7). "I Yahuah search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways" (Jeremiah 17:10).

## The Prayer, Line by Line

The model prayer at the center of the chapter (Matthew 6:9-13) is assembled from what was already written:

Hallowed be thy name. "And I will sanctify my great name, which was profaned among the heathen" (Ezekiel 36:23; also Leviticus 22:32).

Thy kingdom come. "And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed" (Daniel 2:44).

Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. "I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart" (Psalm 40:8).

Give us this day our daily bread. The manna, gathered a day at a time (Exodus 16:4); "feed me with food convenient for me" (Proverbs 30:8).

Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. The release: "Every creditor that lendeth ought unto his neighbour shall release it" (Deuteronomy 15:1-2), the seventh-year shemitah standing behind the whole line.

That debt line is worth stopping on. Forgiveness is not framed as sentiment. It is framed in the language of a statute where the books are cleared on a schedule Yahuah set, and the man asking for release is required to grant it.

The chapter then presses that one line and no other: "For if ye forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: but if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses" (Matthew 6:14-15). Of the whole prayer, the debt clause is the one He stops to enforce, and He enforces it with the release standing behind it.

## The Fast That Was Chosen

Anoint thine head, wash thy face, and let the fast go unseen. Isaiah had already said what an accepted fast looks like, and it is not a face: "to loose the bands of wickedness... to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house" (Isaiah 58:6-7). That chapter ends on the Sabbath, calling it a delight and honourable (Isaiah 58:13-14). The fast, the giving, and the day are one subject in Isaiah, and Matthew 6 is standing inside it.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

This chapter is regularly used to argue that outward religious practice is finished, that Messiah moved everything indoors, and that what matters now is the heart alone. The text does not remove one of the three practices. It keeps all three and fixes the direction they face.

The result of the modern reading is visible. Giving is announced from platforms and printed in reports. Fasting has quietly disappeared. And the one fast Scripture actually commands, Yom Kippur, is not kept at all by the people most certain that the heart is what counts.
