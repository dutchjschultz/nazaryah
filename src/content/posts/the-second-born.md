*Yahuah gave the firstborn four gifts. Adam lost them, Israel lost them, and one Son received them all.*

## Introduction

Torah gives the firstborn son a special place, and a father may not take it away and hand it to a favorite (Deuteronomy 21:17). Yet from Adam forward, the firstborn keeps losing it. A son, a family line, even a whole nation receives the firstborn’s place and fails to hold it. That is not a flaw in the plan. It is the plan pointing ahead to the one Son who would hold it. Paul calls Him "the last Adam" and "the second man" (1 Corinthians 15:45, 47). This study follows the firstborn’s gifts from Adam, through Israel, to Yahushua, the Son at the center of it all.

## Part I — The Firstborn Plan

### The Four Gifts of the Firstborn

Four words carry this study. Together they make up what Scripture calls the *birthright*, *bekorah* (H1062), everything that came with being born first.

**The firstborn** — *bekor* (H1060). The first son, "the beginning of my strength" (Genesis 49:3). He stood first among his brothers, led the family after the father, and belonged to Yahuah in a special way (Exodus 13:2).

**The inheritance** — *nachalah* (H5159). The family’s land and goods, passed from father to sons. In Israel the land itself was the inheritance (Numbers 26:53-55).

**The double portion** — *pi shenayim*, "a mouth of two" (Deuteronomy 21:17). The estate was split into one more share than there were sons, and the firstborn took two. A father with three sons divided his estate four ways. The firstborn took two of the four.

**The blessing** — *berakah* (H1293). The father’s spoken word over a son, naming his future. It is separate from the birthright. Esau lost both, one at a time (Genesis 27:36).

### Adam Was the Plan

Adam was not a rough draft. He was Yahuah’s firstborn son, "Adam, which was the son of God" (Luke 3:38), made in His image and given the whole earth to rule (Genesis 1:26-28). Every gift of the firstborn was his: first place, the earth as his inheritance, and Yahuah’s own blessing spoken over him (Genesis 1:28).

Adam lost it by disobedience, and death passed to everyone after him in place of the inheritance (Romans 5:12). But Yahuah did not change His plan. He kept it and gave it to another Son. Hebrews says it plainly. Man was made to have everything put under his feet, "but now we see not yet all things put under him. But we see Yahushua" (Hebrews 2:8-9). What Adam was made to be, the Second Adam is.

### Six Times in Genesis

After Adam, Genesis tells the same story six times. Cain lost to Seth by murder. Ishmael lost to Isaac because the promise was given to Isaac from the start (Genesis 17:21). Esau sold his birthright for a meal (Genesis 25:34). Reuben lost his by defiling his father’s bed (1 Chronicles 5:1). Manasseh kept his land, but Jacob, speaking by prophecy, gave the right hand to Ephraim (Genesis 48:19). Zerah’s hand came out first, but Perez was actually born first (Genesis 38:27-30).

Yahuah never broke His own law in any of these. Each firstborn lost by his own conduct, stood outside the promise, or was never truly the firstborn at all. Not one of them could hold what Adam lost. Genesis ends still waiting for a Firstborn who will.

## Part II — Israel, Yahuah’s Firstborn Nation

### All Four Gifts Given to Israel

Next, Yahuah gave the firstborn’s place to a whole nation. "Israel is my son, even my firstborn" (Exodus 4:22). Israel was young beside the old kingdoms of the earth, yet Yahuah made it first.

**The firstborn’s place.** Israel was to be "a kingdom of priests" (Exodus 19:6), set "high above all nations" (Deuteronomy 26:19), "the head, and not the tail" (Deuteronomy 28:13). **The inheritance.** The land of promise, which Yahuah called His own and shared with them (Leviticus 25:23). Israel itself was "the lot of his inheritance" (Deuteronomy 32:9). **The double portion.** Yahuah promised that in their land "they shall possess the double" (Isaiah 61:7). **The blessing.** Blessings would come on them and overtake them, if they would hearken to His voice (Deuteronomy 28:1-2).

### Israel Lost It the Same Way

Israel lost the firstborn’s place the way Cain, Esau, and Reuben did, by its own conduct. Torah warned that disobedience would bring curses in place of the blessing, and that Israel would be "plucked from off the land" (Deuteronomy 28:15, 63). It happened just as written. The firstborn nation went into exile, and the inheritance sat empty.

### The True Israel

So Yahuah sent one Son to be what Israel was called to be. Hosea wrote of the nation, "Out of Egypt have I called my son" (Hosea 11:1). Matthew applies those very words to Yahushua (Matthew 2:15). Yahushua walked Israel’s road step by step. He came up out of Egypt. He passed through the water at His immersion, as Israel passed through the sea. He spent forty days in the wilderness for Israel’s forty years, and He answered every temptation with Deuteronomy, the book Israel was given in the wilderness (Matthew 4:4; Deuteronomy 8:3). Where Israel failed, He obeyed. Isaiah calls the Servant "O Israel, in whom I will be glorified" (Isaiah 49:3). Yahushua is Israel as Israel was meant to be, Yahuah’s firstborn Son who kept the birthright.

## Part III — All Four Gifts in One Son

### The Firstborn

Yahuah once said of David, the youngest of Jesse’s sons, "I will make him my firstborn" (Psalm 89:27). Firstborn is a rank Yahuah gives. Yahushua holds it as "the firstborn of every creature" and "the firstborn from the dead" (Colossians 1:15, 18). Hebrews says Yahuah "appointed" Him heir, and that He "obtained" His name "by inheritance" (Hebrews 1:2, 4). An heir receives from the owner. An equal, eternal God could not be appointed heir to what was always His. The Trinity cannot stand on this title. The Father owns the estate. The Son is the appointed Firstborn and Heir.

### The Birthright Split Three Ways, and Made Whole

In Israel the firstborn’s birthright was broken into three pieces. The double portion went to Joseph, through his two sons becoming two tribes. The right to rule went to Judah, "for Judah prevailed above his brethren, and of him came the chief ruler" (1 Chronicles 5:1-2). And the firstborn’s service to Yahuah went to Levi.

On the night of Pesach in Egypt, Israel’s firstborn sons lived only because a lamb died and its blood was on the door (Exodus 12:13). From then on every firstborn son belonged to Yahuah (Exodus 13:2). After the golden calf, when the sons of Levi stood with Yahuah (Exodus 32:26), He took the tribe of Levi to serve Him in place of all the firstborn (Numbers 3:12). Every firstborn son after that was bought back, or redeemed, for five shekels (Numbers 18:16). So the priesthood went to Levi, Jacob’s third son, not his first.

King and priest in one man was shown only once, and only as a picture. Melchizedek met Abram as "king of Salem" and "the priest of the most high God" (Genesis 14:18), a sign from Yahuah of a higher, heavenly priesthood still to come. In Israel the two offices were kept apart. When King Uzziah tried to burn incense in the temple, he was struck with leprosy (2 Chronicles 26:18-19). The crown belonged to Judah, the altar to Levi, and no man could hold both.

Until Messiah. Yahushua came from Judah, the tribe of the king (Hebrews 7:14). Yahuah made Him a priest "for ever after the order of Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4). Zechariah saw it ahead of time: "he shall be a priest upon his throne" (Zechariah 6:13). The scepter of Judah, the service of Levi, and the double portion of Joseph are gathered back into one Son. The firstborn’s birthright, broken since Jacob, is whole again in Him.

What Melchizedek only pictured, Yahushua is the first to truly hold, and those who are His follow Him into it. He "hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father" (Revelation 1:6).

### The Double Portion

Scripture never uses the words "double portion" of Messiah, but it gives Him two full shares. Yahuah told the Servant that raising up the tribes of Jacob was "a light thing," and so He would also give Him "for a light to the Gentiles" (Isaiah 49:6). In Psalm 2, Yahuah says to His Son, "I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance" (Psalm 2:8). Israel is one share. The nations are the second. The Firstborn receives both.

### The Inheritance

Israel’s inheritance was one land. The Son’s is the whole earth, "the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession" (Psalm 2:8). The promise to Abraham’s seed was that he should be "heir of the world" (Romans 4:13). What Adam was given in Eden and lost, the Second Adam receives in full.

### The Blessing

Yahuah told Abraham, "in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed" (Genesis 22:18). Paul says that seed is one Person, Messiah (Galatians 3:16), and that the blessing of Abraham comes on the nations through Him (Galatians 3:14). Peter shows who sends it: Yahuah, "having raised up his Son Yahushua, sent him to bless you" (Acts 3:26). The Father is the source of the blessing. The Son is the One through whom it comes.

## Part IV — Heirs Through the Second Birth

### Born the Second Time

Yahushua told Nicodemus that no one sees the kingdom unless he is "born again" (John 3:3). The first birth carries Adam’s loss. The second birth carries the inheritance. Those born again become "joint-heirs with Messiah" (Romans 8:17) and are counted among "the general assembly" of "the firstborn" (Hebrews 12:23). Every gift the Son holds, He shares with His brothers.

### Two Modern Teachings Answered

**Inheritance by birth.** Many churches teach that children of believing parents are "covenant children," brought in by infant baptism or by being raised in church. Many others teach that the Jewish people will inherit the land and the promises by bloodline, whether or not they come through Messiah. Genesis answers both. Ishmael was Abraham’s own son and Esau was Isaac’s own son, and both were left out. Paul says "the children of the flesh, these are not the children of God" (Romans 9:8). Israel itself lost the land by conduct, not by bloodline. Only the second birth carries the inheritance.

**An inheritance that can never be lost.** Many teach "once saved, always saved," that nothing a person does can cost him his place. Hebrews holds Esau up as a warning to believers. He had the birthright, sold it for one meal, and later sought the blessing with tears and was rejected (Hebrews 12:16-17). Entry into the family comes through the blood. Keeping the birthright is walked out in Yahuah’s commandments, for "he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Matthew 24:13).

## Conclusion

Adam was given the firstborn’s place and lost it. The sons of Genesis lost it one after another. Israel, Yahuah’s firstborn nation, lost it too. Yahuah never changed the plan. He kept every gift of the firstborn, the first place, the inheritance, the double portion, and the blessing, and gave them all to one Son who would not lose them. The center of the whole story is Messiah.

Revelation shows the inheritance finally given out: "He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his God, and he shall be my son" (Revelation 21:7). The Firstborn holds it all, and every son born the second time shares in it.
