The heart step of the walk has a problem built into it. A heart is supposed to love Yahuah fully, and the human heart does not. Deuteronomy names the problem and names the cure.

## The Command

"Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked" (10:16). Circumcision of the flesh was the sign of the covenant since Abraham (Genesis 17:10–11). Moses says the sign was always meant to reach further, to the heart that hears and obeys. Jeremiah later gave Judah the same charge: "take away the foreskins of your heart" (Jeremiah 4:4).

## The Promise

Twenty chapters later the command becomes a promise. After exile and return, "Yahuah thy Elohim will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love Yahuah thy Elohim with all thine heart" (30:6). What Israel was told to do, Yahuah says He will do Himself. The cutting is His.

## The Same Law, Moved Inward

The prophets describe the same work in the same words. Jeremiah's new covenant does not bring a new law: "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33). Ezekiel adds the result: "A new heart also will I give you… and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them" (Ezekiel 36:26–27). The circumcised heart is not a heart freed from the Law. It is a heart that walks in it.

## In the Apostles

Paul writes that true circumcision "is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter" (Romans 2:29), and that believers were circumcised "with the circumcision made without hands" (Colossians 2:11). Stephen rebuked the council in Moses' own words: "Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart" (Acts 7:51). The issue was never the knife alone. It was the heart that refused to hear.

## For the Walk

The believer at the border is not asked to carve a new heart by his own effort. Yahuah promised to do it, and He does it through His spirit. What remains for the believer is what Moses asked from the start: to stop being stiffnecked, and to let the heart that has been cut walk in what it now carries.

## Revelation

Revelation shows the One who looks at hearts: "I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts: and I will give unto every one of you according to your works" (Revelation 2:23). The circumcised heart is known by what it does, and the end of the walk is the same blessing: "Blessed are they that do his commandments" (Revelation 22:14).
