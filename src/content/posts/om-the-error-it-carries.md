Hinduism, like Buddhism, holds much of the second tablet in its moral teaching. Its error lies in the first. The Om stands for a god with no name, a god who is everything, and in the end a god who is man himself.

## A Sound in Place of the Name

Yahuah did not leave Himself a hum. He gave Moses a Name: "this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations" (Exodus 3:15). The prophets told Israel to call upon it, with a promise: "whosoever shall call on the name of Yahuah shall be delivered" (Joel 2:32), which Paul repeated (Romans 10:13).

The Bhagavad Gita promises the highest goal to the one who utters Om at the hour of death. Scripture promises deliverance to the one who calls on the Name. Both cannot be true. The Om is a counterfeit of the Name, a sacred sound with no revealed meaning set in the place the revealed Name belongs. Yahushua also warned against the very practice the Om is built on: "use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking" (Matthew 6:7).

## The Word That Made the Heavens

Some teachers in the West claim the Om is "the Word" of John 1:1. Scripture's word is not a vibration. "By the word of Yahuah were the heavens made" (Psalm 33:6). "He spake, and it was done" (Psalm 33:9). The word is Yahuah speaking, with meaning, carrying His will, to be heard, understood, and obeyed. A syllable without meaning is the opposite of that word.

## Ye Shall Be as Gods

Hindu teaching says the deepest self is one with Brahman. That is the oldest lie in Scripture. The serpent told Eve, "ye shall be as gods" (Genesis 3:5). Yahuah answers plainly: "I am Yahuah, and there is none else, there is no God beside me" (Isaiah 45:5). Man was formed "of the dust of the ground" (Genesis 2:7). He is not the ground of all being.

## Three in One Sound

The Hindu reading that gathers creator, preserver, and destroyer into one syllable deserves notice. A divine three held within one is not unique to the church's creeds. The nations had their threes long before, and Hinduism has its own. Scripture knows no such three. "Hear, O Israel: Yahuah our God is one Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 6:4). Yahuah made all things "alone" and spread abroad the earth "by myself" (Isaiah 44:24). One Creator, one Name, no partners.

## The Verdict

The Om sets a meaningless sound where the Name belongs, teaches that man is one with God, and gathers three gods into one syllable. It counterfeits the Name, the word, and the oneness of Yahuah at once.

Revelation answers it with a Name. Those who triumph over the beast sing, "Who shall not fear thee, O Yahuah, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy" (Revelation 15:4). Not a sound. A Name, and He alone is holy.
