Genesis is the first step of the walk, and it is where the word "walk" first appears with Elohim. Enoch "walked with Elohim" (5:24). Noah "walked with Elohim" (6:9). Abram is told, "walk before me, and be thou perfect" (17:1). The walk does not begin with the walker. It begins when Yahuah calls, and the one called answers in belief and gets up to follow.

## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah creates a world, watches it turn from Him, and begins again through one family. Generation by generation He chooses the line that will carry His promise, until He calls Abram out of his father's house and swears that through his seed all nations will be blessed. The book ends with that family living in Egypt, holding a promise that Yahuah will come back for them.

## The Name

The title "Genesis" comes from the Greek translators, who rendered the book's refrain as *biblos geneseos*, "the book of the generations" (2:4; 5:1). Unlike most book titles, this one grows out of the text itself. Matthew opens the New Testament with the same two Greek words: "The book of the generation of Yahushua Messiah" (Matthew 1:1). The first book traces the generations toward the seed, and the first gospel opens with the seed.

## The Pattern

The phrase "These are the generations of" (*toledot*) divides the book into ten sections, with one repeat for Esau at 36:9. Each section moves the story one step further, and each time Yahuah narrows the line.

- **The heavens and the earth** (2:4) → **Adam** (5:1) → **Noah** (6:9) → **the sons of Noah** (10:1) → **Shem** (11:10) → **Terah** (11:27) → **Ishmael** (25:12) → **Isaac** (25:19) → **Esau** (36:1) → **Jacob** (37:2).

- **The set-aside line is told short.** Cain's line gets eight verses (4:17–24), Ishmael's seven (25:12–18), and Esau's one chapter (36). The chosen line gets the long story.

- **The choice goes by promise, not by birth order.** Seth instead of Cain, Isaac instead of Ishmael, Jacob instead of Esau, Ephraim crossed over Manasseh (48:14). Paul names the rule: "the children of the promise are counted for the seed" (Romans 9:8).

## The Beginning (Chapters 1–11)

From creation to the birth of Abram, about two thousand years by the ages given in the genealogies of chapters 5 and 11.

- **Creation** (1–2). Light first, then six days of work. Elohim "blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it" (2:3).

- **The fall** (3). Sin enters, and with it the first promise of the seed: the woman's seed "shall bruise thy head" (3:15). Man is barred from the tree of life (3:24).

- **Cain and Seth** (4–5). Abel's offering is accepted, Cain kills him, and the line passes to Seth. Enoch walks with Elohim and is taken (5:24).

- **The flood** (6–9). The earth fills with violence, and "Noah found favor in the eyes of Yahuah" (6:8). One family is carried through the water.

- **Noah's covenant** (9). Yahuah promises never again to destroy the earth by flood and sets the rainbow as the sign (9:13). Noah curses Canaan, Ham's son (9:25).

- **The nations** (10). Seventy families spread out from Noah's three sons, Japheth, Ham, and Shem.

- **Babel** (11:1–9). Men build a tower to "make us a name" (11:4). One chapter later, Yahuah tells Abram, "I will… make thy name great" (12:2). The name men grab for is the name Yahuah gives.

- **For the walk:** every new start in these chapters is Yahuah's doing, and each is carried by the few who walk with Him: Enoch, Noah, and the line of Seth.

## The Fathers (Chapters 12–36)

Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, about two hundred years from Abram's call at seventy-five (12:4) to the day Joseph is sold.

- **The call** (12:1–3). "Get thee out of thy country": Abram leaves Ur and Haran without knowing where he is going (Hebrews 11:8).

- **Abram in Canaan** (12–14). Famine drives him to Egypt and back; he parts from Lot, who chooses the plain toward Sodom (13:10–12); and after rescuing Lot from four kings he is blessed by Melchizedek, priest of the Most High, who brings out bread and wine (14:18–20).

- **Belief counted** (15:6). Abram believes Yahuah, and it is counted to him for righteousness, before circumcision and centuries before Sinai.

- **Hagar and Ishmael** (16). Sarai gives her maid Hagar to Abram, and Ishmael is born, the son of the flesh rather than the son of promise (Galatians 4:22–23).

- **The covenant of circumcision** (17). Abram becomes Abraham, Sarai becomes Sarah, and every male is circumcised as the sign of the covenant (17:5, 10–11, 15).

- **Sodom** (18–19). Yahuah visits Abraham at Mamre, Abraham pleads for the city, and Lot is pulled out before the fire falls. Lot's wife looks back (19:26).

- **Isaac born** (21). The son of promise arrives when Abraham is one hundred (21:5), and Hagar and Ishmael are sent away.

- **The binding of Isaac** (22). "Elohim will provide himself a lamb" (22:8), and the promise is sealed: "in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed" (22:18). Paul says the seed is one, "which is Messiah" (Galatians 3:16), and all who belong to Messiah are "Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:29).

- **A bride for Isaac** (23–24). Sarah dies and is buried at Machpelah, and Abraham's servant brings Rebekah from Abraham's kindred to be Isaac's wife.

- **Jacob** (25–35). He takes the birthright, flees, sees the ladder with angels ascending and descending (28:12), the picture Yahushua applies to Himself (John 1:51), and is renamed Israel (32:28).

- **Jacob's household** (29–34). Jacob serves Laban fourteen years for Leah and Rachel, eleven of his twelve sons are born in Haran (29–30), he makes peace with Esau (33), and his sons take revenge on Shechem for their sister Dinah (34).

- **For the walk:** Abraham is called, believes, and then obeys, the same order every believer follows (15:6; 26:5).

## Joseph (Chapters 37–50)

About ninety years, from Joseph sold at seventeen (37:2) to his death at one hundred ten (50:26).

- **Sold and raised** (37–41). Betrayed by his brothers, sold into Egypt, imprisoned, then set over all the land.

- **Judah and Tamar** (38). Judah's line, the line of the sceptre, continues through Tamar's son Perez (38:29; Ruth 4:18–22).

- **Dreams in Egypt** (39–41). Falsely accused by Potiphar's wife, Joseph reads the dreams of the butler and baker in prison, then Pharaoh's: seven years of plenty and seven of famine. Pharaoh sets him over Egypt at thirty (41:46).

- **The brothers bow** (42–45). The dreams come true, and Joseph forgives: "ye thought evil against me; but Elohim meant it unto good… to save much people alive" (50:20).

- **Israel goes down** (46–47). Jacob brings seventy souls into Egypt (46:27) and settles in Goshen.

- **Jacob's blessing** (49). "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah… until Shiloh come" (49:10).

- **The promise** (50:24–25). Joseph dies saying "Elohim will surely visit you," and makes Israel swear to carry his bones out.

- **For the walk:** the brother who was sold and raised becomes the one through whom his family is kept alive, and the called family ends Genesis waiting on Yahuah's promised visit.

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

- **"The day begins at sundown."** Genesis defines the day before it numbers it: "Elohim called the light Day" (1:5), and the sun is the light that rules it (1:16). The day is the light, dawn to dusk; "evening and morning" closes one day and opens the count of the next.

- **"Abraham lived before the Law, so the Law is optional."** Yahuah says Abraham "kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (26:5). He believed first (15:6) and obeyed after, the same order the walk follows.

- **"The promise runs through the bloodline."** Genesis itself passes over the firstborn again and again. The seed is Messiah (Galatians 3:16), and the children of the promise, not the children of the flesh, are counted (Romans 9:8).

- **"'Let us make man' proves three persons."** The next verse answers in the singular: Elohim created man "in his own image" (1:27). Yahuah says He stretched out the heavens "alone" and spread out the earth "by myself" (Isaiah 44:24).

## Revelation

Revelation answers Genesis point for point. Genesis opens with the heavens and the earth; Revelation opens a "new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1). Genesis shuts man out from the tree of life; Revelation gives its leaves "for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2). Genesis brings the curse; in the city "there shall be no more curse" (Revelation 22:3). Abraham left Ur looking for a city "whose builder and maker is Elohim" (Hebrews 11:10), and John sees it come down from heaven (Revelation 21:2), with Abraham's seed walking in it.
