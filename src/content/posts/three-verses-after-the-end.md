## The Claim

Teachers of a thousand-year kingdom on the present earth point to three verses. Each one seems to show sin, death, or sacrifice still going on after the Messiah returns. If those things are still happening, they say, there must be a long age between His return and the new creation, an age where people still die, still sin, and still bring offerings.

- Isaiah 65:20: a child dies a hundred years old, and the sinner a hundred years old is accursed.
- Ezekiel 45:22 and 46:20: a sin offering and a trespass offering in the house of the glory’s return.
- Revelation 22:15: dogs, sorcerers, and liars outside the city.

All three are real verses, and none of them should be waved away. But each one is written the same way, and each one sits in a chapter that answers the question for itself.

## The Key: Covenant Words

The prophets spoke to people who knew the Law. When they described a world no one had ever seen, they used the words their hearers already lived by: long days in the land, the altar and its offerings, the camp with the unclean put outside. Those words carried a point the hearer could not miss. The point is what the verse is teaching. The chapter around it shows what the point was.

## Isaiah 65:20 — A Child at a Hundred

The verse sits inside the new heavens and the new earth. Isaiah opens the passage with them (65:17) and closes it with them (66:22). It does not sit in a middle kingdom.

The Law’s blessing for keeping the covenant was long life: that thy days may be prolonged (Deuteronomy 5:33; Exodus 20:12). Isaiah takes that blessing and pushes it past anything the hearer could imagine. No more infant who lives only a few days. A man of a hundred is still called a child.

The sinner in the same verse is not living on in the new earth. “Accursed” is the word for the man cut off from the people. Isaiah is saying the sinner has been judged and is gone, the same thing Revelation says when it puts the sinner outside the city (22:15) and names his part as the second death (21:8). That is how a prophet tells his hearers there is no sin left in the camp.

The point is that death has lost its grip. The same chapter says the voice of weeping shall be no more heard (65:19), and Isaiah had already said Yahuah “will swallow up death in victory” (25:8). Revelation settles the literal question: “there shall be no more death” (Revelation 21:4).

## Ezekiel 45:22 and 46:20 — Offerings in the House

Ezekiel’s last chapters show the glory coming home and the house where Yahuah dwells forever (43:7). In that house the sin offering and the trespass offering are still named (45:17, 22; 46:20).

Ezekiel is speaking to captive priests in the words of their own altar. He tells them the house to come lacks nothing the Law required. The sin offering is named because it was never thrown out. As the Nazaryah page [*The Five Offerings*](/five-offerings) lays out, it is at rest: the one offering “hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:14), and where sins are forgiven, no new offering is needed (Hebrews 10:18).

The offerings also fit what is asked of believers right now. Paul tells them to present their bodies “a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable” (Romans 12:1), and Peter calls them a holy priesthood offering up “spiritual sacrifices” (1 Peter 2:5). The believer is already the offering, and he is called to stop letting sin reign in his body (Romans 6:12-13). Offerings named in the house to come describe a people whose whole life is laid on the altar, not a world still sinning and waiting on a new bull for each sin.

The chapter keeps it from becoming a millennial altar in two ways. The house was shown to the captives as a pattern, on the condition that they be ashamed of their sins (43:10-11). And the prince who brings the sin offering brings it “for himself” and has sons (45:22; 46:16). He is not the Messiah, so the house is not a picture of the Messiah reigning over a sinning world.

## Revelation 22:15 — Outside the City

This verse comes after the new heaven and new earth (21:1). “For without are dogs, and sorcerers, and whoremongers, and murderers, and idolaters” (22:15).

The Law kept the camp clean because Yahuah walked in the midst of it (Deuteronomy 23:14). The leper and the unclean were put outside the camp (Numbers 5:2-3). Revelation describes the city in the same terms: the camp with Yahuah dwelling in the midst. “Without” is camp language. It says who is not inside.

It does not mean the wicked live next door. Revelation lists nearly the same people a chapter earlier and names their place: “the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death” (21:8). Outside the city is their sentence, not a neighborhood.

## What the Three Share

Each verse uses the covenant’s own words: long life, the altar, the camp. Each sits after the end, inside the new creation or the house of the glory’s return. Each chapter answers the literal question within a few verses: death swallowed up, the offering perfected forever, the wicked in the second death.

None of them needs a thousand years between the return and the new creation. They need only a reader who hears the prophets the way their first hearers did.

## Where It Lands

Revelation closes the matter in the voice from the throne. The former things are passed away, and He that sat upon the throne said, “Behold, I make all things new” (Revelation 21:4-5). Every covenant word the prophets used was pointing to that one sentence.
