*Hebrews 4:2 · Genesis 15:6 · Romans 3:25*

*Did they look forward to a cross, or to Yahuah?*

## Introduction

Many pulpits teach that people in the Old Testament were saved by looking forward to the cross, and believers today are saved by looking back at it. It sounds neat. Scripture never says it. Hebrews says the good news was preached to us *"as well as unto them"* (Hebrews 4:2). Same message, same way of being saved: belief in Yahuah, with the blood He provided underneath it.

## In Their Own Words: They Prayed for Salvation

The Hebrew word for salvation is *yeshuah* (H3444), meaning deliverance. It is the same root found inside the name Yahushua. The fathers used it just as a believer does today. Jacob, on his deathbed, said he had waited for *"thy salvation, O Yahuah"* (Genesis 49:18). Moses sang at the sea that Yah *"is become my salvation"* (Exodus 15:2). David, after his sin, begged Yahuah to *"restore unto me the joy of thy salvation"* (Psalm 51:12). Job knew his *"redeemer liveth"* and expected to see Eloah in his own flesh (Job 19:25-26). That is a resurrection hope.

They were not hoping only to get out of Egypt. Abraham looked for a city whose builder is Yahuah, and the fathers called themselves strangers looking for a *"heavenly"* country (Hebrews 11:10, 16). Moses chose *"the reproach of Messiah"* over all the treasure of Egypt (Hebrews 11:26).

## Saved by Belief, Not by Works

The modern objection runs this way: they were saved by keeping the law, and believers today are saved by believing in the Son. Scripture says otherwise. Abraham *"believed Yahuah,"* and it was counted to him for righteousness (Genesis 15:6). That was before circumcision, before Sinai, and before a single Levitical sacrifice. Paul says that line was written *"for us also,"* if we believe on the One who raised Yahushua from the dead (Romans 4:23-24).

Notice who the belief is in. Yahushua said that whoever believes on Him is really believing *"on him that sent me"* (John 12:44; 5:24). Belief in the Son ends at the Father, exactly where Abraham’s belief ended.

Even Paul’s best-known line, *"the just shall live by faith"* (Romans 1:17), comes straight from Habakkuk 2:4. Paul says the gospel was preached *"before"* to Abraham (Galatians 3:8). The doctrine of belief is an Old Testament doctrine.

## Saved by the Blood

Belief never stood alone. *"It is the blood that maketh an atonement"* (Leviticus 17:11). Belief is how a man receives deliverance; the blood is the price Yahuah paid to make it possible. Paul puts both in one phrase: *"faith in his blood"* (Romans 3:25).

The difference between then and now is not the method. It is the blood itself. Animal blood covered sin, but it could never *"take away sins"* (Hebrews 10:4). The blood of Yahuah’s own Lamb reached backward and paid what the animal blood had only covered, for *"sins that are past"* (Romans 3:25) and for sins committed *"under the first testament"* (Hebrews 9:15). They had the shadow. Believers now have the real thing (Hebrews 10:1).

## The Exodus Road: Blood First, Then the Law

Paul says the Exodus happened *"for ensamples"* (1 Corinthians 10:11). The road maps onto the tabernacle and onto the believer’s walk. It began at Pesach, the altar, where Israel believed and put the blood on the door (Exodus 12:13). Next came the Red Sea, the laver, where they were *"baptized unto Moses"* and left Egypt behind (1 Corinthians 10:2). Then came Sinai, laid out like the sanctuary itself: the people at the foot, the priests nearer, and Moses at the top beneath the sapphire pavement of Yahuah’s throne (Exodus 19:12, 22; 24:10). There Yahuah gave His law on two tables of stone, to a people already redeemed, never as the way to be redeemed. Then came forty years in the wilderness, where Yahuah tested them to see whether they would keep His commandments (Deuteronomy 8:2). Only at the end of that walk did they enter the land.

The order matters. Blood came first, then the law. Deliverance began in Egypt, but it was not finished there. Jude warns that Yahuah saved the people out of Egypt and *"afterward destroyed them that believed not"* (Jude 5). Hebrews 3 and 4 give believers today the same warning. Salvation has a door and a finish line, *"ready to be revealed in the last time"* (1 Peter 1:5).

## Conclusion

The fathers were not looking forward to a cross while believers today look back at one. Both look to Yahuah. Both believe His word. Both stand under the blood He provided. Both walk the same road from Egypt to the promised land. One gospel, one blood, one Elohim.

Revelation ends it with a single song. Those standing on the sea of glass sing *"the song of Moses"* and *"the song of the Lamb"* (Revelation 15:3). Not two songs for two salvations, but one song for one deliverance.
