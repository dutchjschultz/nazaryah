## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah means to be known. Not guessed at and not argued over, but known, by every nation on the earth.

His way of doing that has never changed. He puts somebody in place to carry it — somebody who lives it out in the open where the rest of the world can watch.

Isaiah is the record of who was holding that assignment. It changes hands twice before the book is finished, and the whole book is readable once a person knows which hand it is in.

## When This Is Happening

These chapters start inside the third stretch — the age the reader is standing in — and run straight through into the fourth, which is the return, the judgment, and the new earth.

This part crosses that line on purpose. The reason is in the chapters themselves.

## Something Anyone Can Check

From chapter 41 through chapter 53, the word is servant. Singular. Every time, without exception.

From chapter 54 to the end of the book, it is servants. Plural. Every time, without exception — 54:17, 56:6, 63:17, five times in chapter 65, and once more in 66:14.

The word changes right after chapter 53 and never changes back. A person can check it at the kitchen table with a concordance in an afternoon.

## What Joins the Two Halves

Chapter 53 ended with a promise about offspring. Chapter 54 pays it.

> “he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days.”
>
> — Isaiah 53:10

> “This is the heritage of the servants of Yahuah, and their righteousness is of me, saith Yahuah.”
>
> — Isaiah 54:17

One man's seed, one chapter later, is a household of servants. The plural is what the offering bought.

## The House Gets Bigger

Chapter 54 opens with a barren woman told to sing, because she will end up with more children than the married wife (54:1).

Chapter 55 throws the door open to anyone thirsty who has no money to pay.

Chapter 56 brings in the two groups the old order kept outside the gate — the foreigner and the eunuch. And it describes the foreigner by what he keeps.

> “Every one that keepeth the sabbath from polluting it, and taketh hold of my covenant; Even them will I bring to my holy mountain … mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.”
>
> — Isaiah 56:6–7

That is the description of the man being let in, not the rule he was let off.

> “Yet will I gather others to him, beside those that are gathered unto him.”
>
> — Isaiah 56:8

Others, beside the ones already gathered. The same household, with more people in it.

## What the Servants Do

Chapter 65 uses the plural five times in a few verses, and every one of them is about eating, drinking, singing, and being named (65:13–15).

The first servant could not see or hear. These ones are at a table.

## A Second Line of Evidence

The servant thread stopped cleanly at the end of the second stretch, in chapter 48, and the title moved to one person at the start of the third, in chapter 49.

Here it does something different. The plural begins in the third stretch and runs straight across into the fourth without pausing.

That is not a crack in the pattern. It is the pattern saying something. The gathering starts in this present age and is still serving in the new earth, so the thread had no reason to stop.

A thread that knows which seams to stop at and which to cross is better evidence of a book written in order than one that stops at all of them.

## The Blindness Answered

The series started with a servant who could not see. Isaiah's last chapter puts the servants somewhere specific.

> “From one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith Yahuah.”
>
> — Isaiah 66:22–23

In the new heavens and the new earth, on His days, on a calendar.

And the last book finishes the sentence.

> “And his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads.”
>
> — Revelation 22:3–4

Blind servant in Isaiah 42. Servants who see His face on the last page. That is the whole road, and it ran in order the entire way.

## In One Sentence

A nation was given the assignment and went blind, one man took it and was wrecked for it, and out of that one man came a household of servants who finally see.
