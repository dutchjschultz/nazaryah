The same night goes on (1:7). The first three visions were about the city: the house to be built, the horns cast out, the wall of fire. The fourth turns to the man who serves in that house. A rebuilt temple needs a clean priest, and the high priest of the returned captives was anything but clean.

## The Vision

Zechariah sees Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of Yahuah, with the adversary standing at his right hand to accuse him. The adversary is rebuked, and Joshua is called a brand plucked out of the fire. Joshua is wearing filthy garments. The command goes out to take them off him, and he is told that his iniquity has passed from him. He is dressed in clean robes, and at Zechariah’s own request a clean mitre is set on his head. Then Joshua is given a charge: if he will walk in Yahuah’s ways and keep His charge, he will judge His house and keep His courts. Last comes a promise of Yahuah’s servant the Branch, a stone with seven eyes, iniquity removed in one day, and every man at peace under his vine and fig tree.

## Joshua the High Priest

This is a real man. Joshua the son of Josedech was the high priest who came home from Babylon and stood beside Zerubbabel to rebuild the altar and the house (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2, where he is called Jeshua). His name in Hebrew is *Yehoshua*, Yahuah saves, the same name the Messiah would carry. Before this vision ends, his name and office will point past him to someone greater.

## The Accuser at His Right Hand

Standing at Joshua’s right hand is “Satan … to resist him” (3:1). In Hebrew it is *ha-satan*, the adversary, and the verb beside it, “to resist,” is the same word. He is an accuser doing an accuser’s job. Scripture places the accuser at the right hand of the man on trial: “let Satan stand at his right hand” (Psalm 109:6).

This is a courtroom, and the charge is true.

## Who Rebukes the Accuser

The answer comes: “Yahuah rebuke thee, O Satan; even Yahuah that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee” (3:2). Some read this as Yahuah speaking about another Yahuah. The chapter shows who is talking. Joshua stands “before the angel of Yahuah” (3:1, 3), and that messenger answers the accuser, not in his own name, but by calling on Yahuah to do the rebuking. Jude records the archangel doing the same thing over the body of Moses: he “durst not bring against him a railing accusation, but said, Yahuah rebuke thee” (Jude 9).

## A Brand Plucked Out of the Fire

“Is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?” (3:2). A brand is a burning stick snatched out just before it is consumed. Amos used the same picture for Israel: “ye were as a firebrand plucked out of the burning” (Amos 4:11). Joshua and his people had been in the fire of Babylon for seventy years. Yahuah pulled them out.

## The Filthy Garments

“Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments” (3:3). The Hebrew word, *tso’im*, is far stronger than “dirty.” It comes from the word for human waste, the thing Israel had to bury outside the camp (Deuteronomy 23:13). A high priest was supposed to wear “holy garments … for glory and for beauty” (Exodus 28:2). Joshua is standing in the opposite.

The vision says what the filth is: “I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee” (3:4). The clothes are the sin of the priest and the people he stood for. It was not imaginary. Years later, Ezra found that even sons of Jeshua had broken the covenant by marrying foreign wives (Ezra 10:18).

## Clean Robes and a Clean Mitre

Joshua does not wash his own clothes. The command goes to the ones standing by: “Take away the filthy garments from him,” and Yahuah says, “I will clothe thee with change of raiment” (3:4). The covering comes from Yahuah, as it did in Eden when He made coats of skins for Adam and Eve (Genesis 3:21). Isaiah sang about it: “he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness” (Isaiah 61:10).

Then Zechariah speaks up for the first time in any vision: “Let them set a fair mitre upon his head” (3:5). The high priest’s mitre carried a gold plate engraved “HOLINESS TO YAHUAH” (Exodus 28:36-38).

## If Thou Wilt Walk

This is the part most pulpits skip. After the clothes are changed, the angel gives Joshua a charge: “If thou wilt walk in my ways, and if thou wilt keep my charge, then thou shalt also judge my house, and shalt also keep my courts, and I will give thee places to walk among these that stand by” (3:7).

The order matters. First the filth is taken away, freely, by Yahuah. Then comes the walk, and it has a condition: if. The cleansing was not earned. But serving in the house, judging it, keeping its courts, and walking among those who stand before Yahuah all depend on walking in His ways and keeping His charge. Cleansing is the door. Obedience is the walk.

## My Servant the Branch

Joshua and the priests with him are called “men wondered at” (3:8), men who are a sign. They point to something coming: “behold, I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH.” Jeremiah used that title for the Son of David: “I will raise unto David a righteous Branch” (Jeremiah 23:5). Notice the words “my servant.” The Branch is Yahuah’s servant, sent by Him. In chapter 6 a crown will be set on Joshua’s head as a picture of “the man whose name is The BRANCH” (6:12).

## The Stone With Seven Eyes

Then a stone is laid before Joshua, with seven eyes on it, and Yahuah says, “I will engrave the graving thereof” (3:9). Engraved stones were part of the high priest’s own clothing: two onyx stones on his shoulders and twelve on his breastplate, each engraved “like the engravings of a signet” with the names of the tribes (Exodus 28:9-12, 21). The seven eyes are the eyes of Yahuah that “run to and fro through the whole earth” (4:10).

And then the promise: “I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day” (3:9). Once a year, on Yom Kippur, the high priest went in to make atonement (Leviticus 16:30, 34), and next year he had to do it again. Here Yahuah promises one day that does not need repeating. Hebrews names it: by the offering Yahuah provided, “once for all,” He “hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:10, 14).

## Under the Vine and Fig Tree

The vision ends in peace: “shall ye call every man his neighbour under the vine and under the fig tree” (3:10). Micah gave the same promise, “and none shall make them afraid” (Micah 4:4).

These are two of the trees in Jotham’s parable, and there they picture the walk of a man with Yahuah (Judges 9:11-13). The vine is the covenant entry, the man brought in by the blood. Its wine is the wine “which cheereth Elohim and man” (Judges 9:13). The fig is the walk in the Law, sweet, and proven by “my good fruit” (Judges 9:11). That is the same order Joshua just walked through: first his filthy garments taken away, then the charge to walk in Yahuah’s ways (3:4, 7). The vision ends with every man sitting under both trees, cleansed by the blood and walking in the commandments, with no accuser left to make him afraid. The four trees are laid out in full in the Nazaryah investigation [*The Trees of Judges*](/blog/the-trees-of-judges).

## Bringing It Home

Every believer is a priest now. Peter calls the assembly “a royal priesthood” (1 Peter 2:9), and Revelation says Yahushua made us “kings and priests unto Elohim and his Father” (Revelation 1:6). So every believer stands where Joshua stood: before the throne, with an accuser at his right hand, and with garments that the accuser can truthfully point at.

The filth is taken away by Yahuah, not scrubbed off by the man wearing it. Nobody earns the robe.

But the charge is the same too. “If thou wilt walk in my ways, and if thou wilt keep my charge.” The modern pulpit preaches verse 4 and stops. A clean robe is not permission to go back and roll in the same filth. Yahushua told one of the assemblies there were a few who “have not defiled their garments: and they shall walk with me in white” (Revelation 3:4). A cleaned priest keeps his robe clean by walking in the ways of the One who cleaned it, and that walk is how he serves in the house.

## Where It Lands

Revelation finishes Joshua’s courtroom. “The accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our Elohim day and night. And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony” (Revelation 12:10-11). The great multitude stands before the throne in robes they “washed … and made … white in the blood of the Lamb” (Revelation 7:14), and the Lamb in the midst of the throne has seven eyes (Revelation 5:6). The stone of Zechariah’s vision is still watching, and the accuser has nowhere left to stand.
