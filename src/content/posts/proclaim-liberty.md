The walk in Leviticus ends in a kingdom, and the kingdom has a calendar for the land itself. Its highest point is the Jubilee, the year when everything taken was given back.

## Sabbaths of Years

The land kept its own Sabbath. For six years Israel sowed and pruned, "but in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land" (25:2–4). Seven of those Sabbath years made forty-nine years, and the fiftieth was the Jubilee (25:8–10).

## The Trumpet on Yom Kippur

The Jubilee did not open on a feast of joy but on Yom Kippur. On the tenth day of the seventh month the trumpet sounded throughout the land (25:9), and the command went out: "proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof" (25:10). Atonement came first, and liberty followed it. It is the walk's order again: blood first, then freedom.

## What Came Back

In the Jubilee "ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family" (25:10). Land sold under hard times went back to its first owner (25:13). An Israelite sold into service went free with his children and returned to his fathers' land (25:39–41). The reason is Yahuah's ownership: "the land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me" (25:23). Even before the Jubilee, a near kinsman could step in and redeem what his brother had lost (25:25).

## This Day Fulfilled

In the synagogue at Nazareth, Yahushua stood and read Isaiah's words: the spirit of Yahuah had anointed Him "to preach deliverance to the captives… to set at liberty them that are bruised, to preach the acceptable year of Yahuah" (Luke 4:18–19). Then He closed the book and said, "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears" (Luke 4:21). He stopped in the middle of Isaiah 61:2, before "the day of vengeance of our Elohim." The liberty was proclaimed then; the vengeance was left for His return.

## Begun, Not Finished

The Jubilee was opened at Messiah's death and is completed at His return. In the parable of the Samaritan, the rescuer leaves two pence with the innkeeper and says, "when I come again, I will repay thee" (Luke 10:35). Peter writes that "one day is with Yahuah as a thousand years" (2 Peter 3:8). Two days are two thousand years. Two thousand years are forty Jubilees, the number of Israel's years in the wilderness. Counted from His death in 31 AD, they reach about 2031. The debt is paid and the liberty is proclaimed, but the full return of the inheritance waits for the last trumpet.

## Revelation

Revelation describes the Jubilee finished. Yahuah makes "all things new" (Revelation 21:5), there is "no more curse" (Revelation 22:3), and the inheritance comes home for good: "He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his Elohim, and he shall be my son" (Revelation 21:7).
