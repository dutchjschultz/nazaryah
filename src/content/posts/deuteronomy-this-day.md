Deuteronomy is the fifth step of the walk: the heart made ready at the border. Most of the people Moses spoke to had never stood at Sinai. They were children then, or not yet born. Yet he told them, "Yahuah made not this covenant with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day" (5:3). Then he went further: the covenant was also "with him that is not here with us this day" (29:15). The covenant reaches every generation that hears it, and every believer stands at that same border, hearing it "this day."

## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah is renewing His covenant with the generation that will inherit the land. Through Moses He retells what He has done, restates what He commands, and sets before them life and death. His aim is the heart: "O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always" (5:29).

## The Name and the Time

"Deuteronomy" means "second law." The Greek translators took the name from 17:18, where the king is to write "a copy of this law" for himself. It is not a second, different Law. It is the same Law repeated. The whole book takes place in the last weeks of Moses' life, from the first day of the eleventh month of the fortieth year (1:3) to his death on Mount Nebo, followed by thirty days of mourning (34:8).

## The Pattern

Moses gives three speeches, and the book closes with four last things.

- **First speech: looking back** (1–4). **Second speech: the Law again** (5–26). **Third speech: the covenant** (27–30). **The end: Joshua, the song, the blessing, and Moses' death** (31–34).

- **The refrain "this day."** Moses presses the choice into the present again and again: "I call heaven and earth to record this day… therefore choose life" (30:19).

## First Speech: Looking Back (Chapters 1–4)

- **From Horeb to Kadesh** (1). An eleven days' journey (1:2) became forty years when the people refused to go up and take the land.

- **Thirty-eight years** (2–3). The wandering until the first count died (2:14), the lands of Sihon and Og taken east of the Jordan, and Moses' plea to cross refused (3:23–27).

- **Take heed** (4). Do not forget what was seen at Horeb, add nothing to the word and take nothing from it (4:2), and know that "Yahuah he is Elohim… there is none else" (4:39).

- **For the walk:** the believer remembers where Yahuah has brought him from, so that he does not repeat the unbelief that cost a whole generation.

## Second Speech: The Law Again (Chapters 5–26)

- **The Ten Words** (5). Repeated for the new generation. The Sabbath reason this time is deliverance: "remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt" (5:15).

- **The Shema** (6). "Hear, O Israel: Yahuah our Elohim is one Yahuah," loved with all the heart, soul, and might, and taught to the children at home and on the road (6:4–7).

- **Do not forget** (7–11). Israel was chosen not for its size but because Yahuah loved it (7:7–8). "Man doth not live by bread only" (8:3). The golden calf is recalled (9–10), and blessing and curse are set before them (11:26).

- **Worship His way** (12–14). One place where He puts His name (12:5), no copying how the nations served their gods (12:30–31), tests for false prophets (13), and clean and unclean food (14).

- **Life in the land** (15–25). The year of release, the three yearly feasts (16:16), the king who must write his own copy of the Law (17:18–19), the prophet like Moses (18:15–19), cities of refuge (19), and laws for justice, family, the poor, and the neighbor (20–25).

- **The basket** (26). The farmer brings his basket and confesses where he came from: "A Syrian ready to perish was my father… and Yahuah brought us forth out of Egypt" (26:5–8).

- **For the walk:** the same Law, given again to people who will live it out in daily life, in the home, the field, the court, and the marketplace.

## Third Speech: The Covenant (Chapters 27–30)

- **Gerizim and Ebal** (27). The Law is written on plastered stones, and the tribes stand on two mountains for the blessing and the curse (27:2–8, 12–13).

- **Blessing and curse** (28). Blessing in the city and the field for obedience; for rebellion, siege, scattering, and exile among the nations.

- **The covenant at Moab** (29). A covenant "beside the covenant which he made with them in Horeb" (29:1). "The secret things belong unto Yahuah… but those things which are revealed belong unto us… that we may do all the words of this law" (29:29).

- **Return and choose** (30). After exile, Yahuah will gather them and "circumcise thine heart" (30:6). The command "is not hidden from thee, neither is it far off" (30:11). "Choose life" (30:19).

- **For the walk:** the believer is not handed the Law from a distance; it is "very nigh," in the mouth and in the heart, so that it can be done (30:14).

## The End (Chapters 31–34)

- **Joshua** (31). Moses charges Joshua, "Be strong and of a good courage" (31:7). The Law is written down, placed beside the ark (31:26), and read to all Israel every seventh year at Sukkot (31:10–11).

- **The song** (32). A witness against the people for the day they turn away, ending with Yahuah's word: "I, even I, am he, and there is no god with me" (32:39).

- **The blessing** (33). Moses blesses the tribes one by one: "The eternal Elohim is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms" (33:27).

- **Moses' death** (34). He sees the land from Nebo and dies at one hundred twenty, "his eye was not dim" (34:7). Yahuah buries him, and no man knows the place (34:6).

- **For the walk:** Moses brings the people to the border, and Joshua, whose name is the name the Son carries, brings them in.

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

- **"Deuteronomy is a second, different law."** The name comes from "a copy of this law" (17:18), and Moses forbids adding to it or taking from it (4:2).

- **"Echad means a compound unity."** The same word describes one man: "Abraham was one" (Ezekiel 33:24). When a scribe answered the Shema with "there is one Elohim; and there is none other but he," Yahushua said he was "not far from the kingdom" (Mark 12:32–34).

- **"The prophet like Moses is Elohim Himself."** Yahuah said He would raise a prophet "from among their brethren, like unto thee," and "put my words in his mouth" (18:18). Peter applies it to Yahushua (Acts 3:22), who said, "the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say" (John 12:49).

- **"The Law is too hard to keep."** Moses says the command "is not hidden from thee, neither is it far off" (30:11), but "very nigh" (30:14), and John says "his commandments are not grievous" (1 John 5:3).

## Revelation

Revelation closes the Scriptures the way Deuteronomy closes the Torah. Moses warned against adding to or taking from the word (4:2); Revelation ends with the same warning (Revelation 22:18–19). Moses set life before the people and said "choose" (30:19); Revelation ends with the same open door: "whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17). And the song Moses taught Israel at the border is sung by the overcomers at the end: "the song of Moses the servant of Elohim, and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:3).
