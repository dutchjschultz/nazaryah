## What the Pulpit Teaches

Most churches teach that the early and latter rain are the Spirit. The early rain is said to be the outpouring in Acts 2, and the latter rain a great revival still to come before the end. Early Pentecostals called their movement "the latter rain," and in 1948 a revival in North Battleford, Saskatchewan took the name outright. But Scripture tells us what the rain is long before any of that, and it does not say revival.

## Moses Tells Us What the Rain Is

Moses wrote a song for Israel near the end of his life, and he opened it by saying what his words would be like: "My doctrine shall drop as the rain, my speech shall distil as the dew" (Deuteronomy 32:2). The word for doctrine is *leqach*, teaching, something you take in. The rain is the teaching of Yahuah, given through the man who gave the Law.

The Hebrew words say the same thing. The early rain is *yoreh*. A teacher is *moreh*. Both come from *yarah*, which means to throw, to aim, to point the way, and to teach. *Torah* comes from that same root. In Hebrew, the rain, the teacher, and the Law are one family of words. Joel uses *moreh* twice in one verse: once it can only mean rain, and once it stands beside righteousness, where the KJV margin reads it "a teacher of righteousness" (Joel 2:23). The teacher and the rain come together.

The old letter pictures add their witness. *Mem*, the picture of water, stands in front of the teaching root in *moreh*, so the water and the teacher are one word. *Yarah* is spelled with the hand, the head, and behold: the hand of the Head reaching out to point the way. *Lamed*, the shepherd’s staff that begins *lamad*, to learn, stands inside both *leqach*, the doctrine, and *malqosh*, the latter rain.

So the rain is not three different things. Torah means instruction, and the commandments are what that instruction teaches. Teaching and commandments are one rain. Growing is not a third thing the rain stands for. Growing is what the rain does.

## What Rain Does to the Ground

Scripture tells us plainly what rain does to a field, and every step matches what Yahuah’s teaching does to a person.

- It softens hard ground. "Thou makest it soft with showers: thou blessest the springing thereof" (Psalm 65:10). His teaching softens a hard heart.

- It makes the seed sprout. The rain "watereth the earth, and maketh it bring forth and bud" (Isaiah 55:10). The word takes root and comes up.

- It feeds the young plant. Moses’ doctrine falls "as the small rain upon the tender herb" (Deuteronomy 32:2), gentle drops for a new plant, and heavier showers for the grown grass. New believers get the small rain; the grown get the showers.

- It fills the grain for harvest. Without the last rain the heads stay thin. Teaching that keeps coming brings fruit to full ripeness.

Yahuah "prepareth rain for the earth" and "maketh grass to grow" (Psalm 147:8). Nothing in the field grows without it, and nothing in a believer grows without His instruction.

## The Sower and the Rain

Isaiah ties the rain straight to the seed: the rain gives "seed to the sower, and bread to the eater," and in the same way His word "shall not return unto me void" (Isaiah 55:10-11). Yahushua builds His parable on this ground. "The seed is the word of God" (Luke 8:11). The seed on the rock withered "because it lacked moisture" (Luke 8:6). It was heard with joy, but no teaching soaked in, so it had "no root" (Matthew 13:21). The good ground hears the word and will "keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience" (Luke 8:15).

Isaiah puts the teacher and the rain in the same order. "Thine eyes shall see thy teachers," and a voice says, "This is the way, walk ye in it." Then "shall he give the rain of thy seed" (Isaiah 30:20-23). Hebrews shows the same rain falling on two kinds of ground. The ground that drinks it in and brings forth a crop "receiveth blessing," but the ground that grows "thorns and briers is rejected" (Hebrews 6:7-8). The rain is the same. The ground is the difference. This is why the rain was promised to those who keep the commandments, "the first rain and the latter rain" in their season (Deuteronomy 11:13-14).

## The Early Rain and the Latter Rain

In the land of Israel, rain does not fall all year. It comes in two seasons, and the crop depends on both.

- The early rain, *yoreh*, falls in the fall, after the last harvest is gathered. It softens the hard, dry ground so it can be plowed and the seed sown. No early rain, no planting.

- The latter rain, *malqosh*, falls in the spring, just before harvest. Its root, *laqash*, means to gather late. It fills out the barley and wheat. No latter rain, no harvest.

Scripture uses the two rains as a picture of a crop’s whole life, not as a calendar of dates. The early rain starts the crop, the latter rain finishes it, and then comes the harvest. Yahushua gives the same picture: "He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man," and "the harvest is the end of the world" (Matthew 13:37, 39). The seed went into the ground at His first coming. The harvest comes at His return. The rain falls in between.

The early rain is the teaching that gets the seed started. Hosea says the Messiah "shall come unto us as the rain" (Hosea 6:3), and Joel calls the early rain "a teacher of righteousness" (Joel 2:23, margin). He came teaching, and the word He sowed took root. The latter rain is the teaching that keeps falling through the whole age, filling out the grain so the crop is full when the harvest comes. That is why James tells believers to wait like a farmer "until he receive the early and latter rain," and to be patient "unto the coming of the Master" (James 5:7). Both rains come before the harvest, and the harvest is His return. Hosea lived before the first coming, so he waited for both, "the latter and former rain" (Hosea 6:3). We live after it. The early rain has already fallen with the Messiah, and the assembly now lives under the latter rain, waiting for the harvest. Every new believer still gets his own early rain when the word is first planted in him, then grows under the latter rain until that day.

The pulpit keeps the same order but changes what falls. It makes the early rain the outpouring of Acts 2 and the latter rain a great revival of signs and feelings before the end. Scripture never calls the rain a feeling. Moses called it doctrine. The latter rain is not a crowd falling down in a tent meeting. It is the teaching of Yahuah ripening those who keep it, so they are full grain on the day He comes.

## Where the Spirit Fits

The Spirit matters, but it is not the rain. Joel promises the Spirit "afterward" (Joel 2:28), after the rain. Ezekiel says what the Spirit does: Yahuah takes away "the stony heart" and gives "an heart of flesh," then puts His Spirit within "and cause\[s\] you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:26-27). The stony heart is the stony ground of the parable. Yahuah’s Spirit turns rock into soft ground so the rain can soak in. A revival that throws out the Law is not the latter rain. It is a wet field with no seed in it.

## Conclusion

The early and latter rain stand for one thing: the teaching of Yahuah, His Torah. Moses called his doctrine rain. The Hebrew joins the rain, the teacher, and the Law in one root. The rain softens the ground, sprouts the seed, feeds the young plant, and fills the grain, and His instruction does the same in a believer. The early rain starts the seed the Messiah sowed, and the latter rain fills it out before the harvest at His return.

Revelation shows where the rain leads. The two witnesses can "shut heaven, that it rain not" (Revelation 11:6), as Moses warned would happen to those who turn from the commandments. But in the end the water never stops. A "pure river of water of life" flows out of the throne, and beside it the tree of life yields "her fruit every month" (Revelation 22:1-2). No dry season, and no more waiting for the rain.
