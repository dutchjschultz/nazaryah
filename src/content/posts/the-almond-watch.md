*Witness One in the investigation [**The Sealed Count**](/investigations/the-sealed-count) — back to the case.*

## The Lampstand Was a Tree

When Yahuah gave Moses the pattern of the tabernacle, He described the lampstand in more detail than almost any other piece of furniture. It was not a candlestick in the modern sense, a stick holding a candle. It was a tree made of gold.

> **EXODUS 25:31 — KJV**
>
> *And thou shalt make a candlestick of pure gold: of beaten work shall the candlestick be made: his shaft, and his branches, his bowls, his knops, and his flowers, shall be of the same.*

The lampstand had a central shaft like a trunk. Six branches came out of its sides, three on one side and three on the other. Along the shaft and the branches were bowls, knops, and flowers, which is to say cups, buds, and blossoms. The whole piece was hammered out of a single talent of pure gold (Exodus 25:39). Nothing was bolted on. Trunk, branches, buds, and blossoms were one piece, beaten into shape.

Moses was not left to design it. "And look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount" (Exodus 25:40). The craftsmen did not choose how many cups to put on it or what shape those cups would take. Yahuah set every count.

## Count the Almonds

The cups were not a generic shape. They were formed like almonds.

> **EXODUS 25:33-34 — KJV**
>
> *Three bowls made like unto almonds, with a knop and a flower in one branch; and three bowls made like almonds in the other branch, with a knop and a flower: so in the six branches that come out of the candlestick. And in the candlestick shall be four bowls made like unto almonds, with their knops and their flowers.*

Three almond bowls on each branch. Six branches. That is eighteen. Four more on the central shaft. Eighteen and four make twenty-two.

The count is not hidden in a footnote or reached by stretching the text. It sits in plain view in the instructions Yahuah gave, and it is repeated when Bezaleel made the lampstand in Exodus 37:19-20. The same numbers appear twice, once in the command and once in the obedience. Twenty-two almond bowls stood on the lampstand that lit the Holy Place.

The Hebrew word behind "made like almonds" is *meshuqqadim*, from *shaqad* (H8246), meaning shaped like an almond. It comes from the same family as *shaqed* (H8247), the almond tree itself.

## Why Almonds

The almond tree has a name that means something in Hebrew. *Shaqed*, almond, sounds almost exactly like *shoqed*, watching, from the verb *shaqad* (H8245), to be awake, to keep watch. The almond is the tree that wakes first. In the land of Israel it blossoms in late winter, while the other trees are still bare. It is the watchman of the orchard.

Yahuah used that very sound to teach Jeremiah. He showed the prophet a rod of an almond tree, a *shaqed*, and then told him what it meant: "for I will hasten my word to perform it." The word translated "hasten" is *shoqed*. Yahuah was saying, in effect, You see an almond, a watcher, because I am watching over My word to do it.

So the almond is not a random decoration. In the mouth of Yahuah Himself, the almond stands for His watch over His word. When that same almond shape is cast twenty-two times into the lampstand, the number and the meaning sit together. Twenty-two watchers over the word.

## The Rod in the Ark

The almond appears again in the wilderness. When the tribes challenged Aaron's priesthood, Yahuah had each tribe bring a rod and lay it before the testimony overnight. In the morning, "the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds" (Numbers 17:8). One dead stick did in a single night what an almond tree does across a whole season.

Yahuah then told Moses to keep Aaron's rod "before the testimony" (Numbers 17:10). The book of Hebrews records where it ended up: inside the ark of the covenant, alongside the golden pot of manna and "the tables of the covenant" (Hebrews 9:4). The almond rod and the stone tables of the Law were kept in the same box. The almond and the Law were placed side by side by Yahuah's own order.

## Lamp and Law

The lampstand was not set in a random corner of the tabernacle. It stood in the Holy Place, and its lamps were to burn "before the testimony" (Exodus 27:21), facing toward the veil behind which the ark held the tables of the Law. Its light was kept with pure beaten olive oil, tended by the priests, never allowed to go out.

Scripture connects a lamp with the Law in so many words.

> **PSALM 119:105 — KJV**
>
> *Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.*

> **PROVERBS 6:23 — KJV**
>
> *For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light.*

Put the pieces together. The lamp stood before the Law. The lamp was shaped like an almond tree, the tree Yahuah used to say He watches over His word. And the almonds on that tree numbered twenty-two, the same number as the letters in which that word was written and, later, the same count by which the books of that word were kept.

## What the Lampstand Proves and What It Does Not

Honesty about a witness makes the witness stronger. The lampstand does not list the books of the Bible. No passage says the twenty-two almonds stand for twenty-two scrolls, and this study does not claim that it does.

What the lampstand does show is that the number twenty-two was set by Yahuah at Sinai, in the one piece of furniture that carried light toward the Law, in the one shape He used to describe His watch over His word. It was set before Joshua was written, before David sang a psalm, before any prophet picked up a pen. When Israel later counted its Scriptures as twenty-two, it was not inventing a number. It was keeping a number that had stood in gold in the Holy Place for centuries.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

The pulpit treats the lampstand as a decoration, or as a Jewish symbol that has nothing to say to believers, or as a picture of Messiah alone with every detail flattened. The almonds are never counted. Most believers have read Exodus 25 several times without once stopping to add three and three and three.

The text did the counting for them. Yahuah set the number before the scrolls were complete, and He set it in the one object that stood watch before the Law. The council-canon teaching asks believers to imagine that the shape of Scripture was left loose until bishops tied it down. The lampstand says the shape was in gold before the Word was even finished, and Yahuah was watching over it the whole time.

The lampstand does not disappear in Revelation, the prophecy Yahuah gave to John. Revelation opens with seven golden lampstands, which are the seven assemblies (Revelation 1:20), and later names the two witnesses as two lampstands standing before the Lord of the earth (Revelation 11:4). The lamp is still burning at the end, carried by those who keep the commandments of Yahuah (Revelation 12:17).
