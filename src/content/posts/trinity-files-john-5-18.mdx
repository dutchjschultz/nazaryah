> **John 5:18**
>
> *Therefore the Jews sought the more to kill him, because he not only had broken the sabbath, but said also that God was his Father, making himself equal with God.*

This verse is quoted as proof that the Son is God. The argument is simple: John wrote “making himself equal with God,” so the Son is equal with God, and if he is equal with God, he is God. The verse answers that argument itself, in three places.

Part One

**Two Charges in One Breath**

***1.1 — Read It Off the Surface***

Read the verse the way most people read it, and John says two things about Yahushua: he “had broken the sabbath,” and he was “making himself equal with God.” Both sit under the same word, “because.” If the second is John’s own statement of fact, so is the first.

But the first cannot be true. Yahushua “did no sin” *1 Peter 2:22*, and sin is “the transgression of the law” *1 John 3:4*. A Sabbath-breaker could not be the Lamb without blemish. So the first charge is not John’s verdict. It is the leaders’ accusation, and John is reporting why they wanted to kill him.

That settles the second charge too. One “because” cannot hold a false accusation and a true confession. Both are the accusers’ words. “Making himself equal with God” is what his enemies said, not what John taught and not what the Son claimed. The pulpit that reads this verse as proof of the Trinity must also read it as proof that the Messiah broke the Law, and many pulpits do exactly that. Both readings fall together.

***1.2 — Their Own Words Answer Them***

Look at what sits between the two charges. The accusers said he “said also that God was his Father.” Even his enemies understood that he called God his Father. A son is not his own father. If God is his Father, then he is the Son of God, not God. The very charge they brought names two, a Father and a Son, and puts God on the Father’s side.

In the same speech the Son spoke of “the honour that cometh from God only” *John 5:44*. One God, and it is the Father.

Part Two

**“Making Himself”**

***2.1 — Always in the Accusers’ Mouths***

In John, “making himself” is always the language of the men who wanted Yahushua dead: “thou, being a man, makest thyself God” *John 10:33*; “he made himself the Son of God” *John 19:7*; “whosoever maketh himself a king” *John 19:12*. Each time the charge is that he seized a rank on his own say-so. Not once does Yahushua say he made himself anything.

**heauton poiōn — “making himself”**

Setting oneself up in a position on one’s own say-so. It is the language of seizing, not of receiving.

***2.2 — Who Did Make Him***

Scripture names who set the Son in his place. He “glorified not himself to be made an high priest” — the Father did *Hebrews 5:5*. God “hath made” that same Yahushua “both Master and Messiah” *Acts 2:36*. And the Son answered the charge in the very next verse: “The Son can do nothing of himself” *John 5:19*. They said he made himself. He said he could do nothing of himself.

Part Three

**Equal in What?**

***3.1 — Hebrew Thinking: Equal in Authority***

To a Greek mind, “equal with God” sounds like the same nature. To a Hebrew mind, a son who acts for his father stands in his father’s authority. Scripture shows this plainly. Pharaoh set Joseph over all Egypt, and Judah said to him, “thou art even as Pharaoh” *Genesis 44:18*. Yet Pharaoh had said, “only in the throne will I be greater than thou” *Genesis 41:40*. Joseph was “as Pharaoh” in authority, and never Pharaoh himself.

The same chapter of John says exactly this of the Son. The Father “hath given him authority to execute judgment also, because he is the Son of man” *John 5:27*. Authority given is authority received, and the one who gives it is greater.

***3.2 — Whose Word Do You Believe?***

The Son himself said, “my Father is greater than I” *John 14:28*. Not equal. Greater.

Yet many Christians read John 5:18, take hold of “equal with God,” and forget John 14:28 entirely. They believe the Pharisees over the Son. The men who called him a Sabbath-breaker and plotted his death said he made himself equal with God. The Son said his Father is greater than he is. One of those two witnesses is the Son of God, and the other wanted him dead. Building a doctrine on the accusers’ charge while setting aside the Son’s own words is believing his enemies about who he is.

***3.3 — Capital G or Small g?***

The oldest Greek copies of the New Testament were written in all capital letters. There was no “God” and “god” on the page; the capital letter in English Bibles is the translator’s choice. In this verse the Greek says “the God,” and the God the sentence has in view is the one it just named — “his Father.” So the charge was that the Son put himself level with his own Father.

Hebrew uses one word, elohim, for God and for men who carry his authority. Yahuah told Moses, “I have made thee a god to Pharaoh” *Exodus 7:1*. Moses was not Yahuah; he stood in Yahuah’s authority before a king. That is the Hebrew meaning of being set level with God: carrying his authority, given by him.

Part Four

**Reading the Verse Again**

> **John 5:18**
>
> *Therefore the Jews sought the more to kill him, because he not only had broken the sabbath, but said also that God was his Father, making himself equal with God.*

> *Therefore the leaders sought all the more to kill him, giving their two charges: that he had loosed the Sabbath — which he had not, for he did no sin — and that he called God his own Father, which they twisted into a man setting himself up on his own say-so as level with God, when the Son held only the authority his Father had given him.*

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

John 5:18 is a charge sheet. The first charge, Sabbath-breaking, is false, so the second, “equal with God,” is the accusers’ word too. Even their charge calls God his Father. The Son did not make himself anything; the Father made him and gave him his authority, as Pharaoh set Joseph over Egypt and kept the throne. The accusers said “equal”; the Son said “my Father is greater than I.” Believe the Son. That is how it ends. The overcoming Son is “set down with my Father in his throne,” and he promises the same seat to the believer who overcomes *Revelation 3:21*. The throne is the Father’s, and it is shared by gift.

> **Even his accusers said God was his Father. A Son of God is not God.**
