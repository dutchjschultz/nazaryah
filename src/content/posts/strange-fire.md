The walk in Leviticus is worship done Yahuah's way. The first lesson in that walk came on the day the priesthood began, and it came through fire.

## The Best Day and the Worst Day

After seven days of ordination, Aaron offered for the first time. "There came a fire out from before Yahuah, and consumed upon the altar the burnt offering," and when the people saw it, "they shouted, and fell on their faces" (9:24). It was the greatest day the camp had seen since Sinai.

On the same day, Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's two eldest sons, took their censers, "put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before Yahuah, which he commanded them not." Fire went out from Yahuah "and devoured them, and they died before Yahuah" (10:1–2).

## Who They Were

These were not outsiders. Nadab and Abihu had gone up Sinai with Moses and the seventy elders, and they "saw the Elohim of Israel" and ate and drank in His presence (Exodus 24:1, 9–11). They were newly ordained priests, standing as close to Yahuah as any men in Israel. Nearness did not give them the right to choose their own fire.

## What Was Wrong

The text gives one reason, and it is short: "which he commanded them not." The incense was not the problem, and neither were the censers. The fire was. On Yom Kippur the high priest was to take coals "from off the altar before Yahuah" (16:12), the fire Yahuah Himself had lit. Nadab and Abihu brought fire of their own.

Moses gave Aaron Yahuah's word: "I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified" (10:3). Aaron held his peace. The next command Yahuah gave the priests was to drink no wine or strong drink when going into the tabernacle, so they could tell holy from unholy (10:9–10).

## Strange Fire Today

The lesson was not about sincerity. Nadab and Abihu were sincere priests bringing incense to the right Elohim at the right tent. What they brought was not what He had asked for. Moses later wrote it as a rule: "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2). Yahushua said the same of worship built on tradition: "in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Matthew 15:9).

Worship today is full of strange fire: days, customs, and practices Yahuah never commanded, offered sincerely in His name. The question Leviticus asks is not whether the worshipper means well. It is whether Yahuah commanded it. The Father seeks those who worship Him "in spirit and in truth" (John 4:23–24), and truth is His word, not our fire.

## Revelation

Revelation shows the right fire in heaven's own sanctuary. An angel offers incense "with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar," and then takes "the censer, and filled it with fire of the altar" (Revelation 8:3–5). The fire comes from Yahuah's altar, as it always had to.
