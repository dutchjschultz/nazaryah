## Blessed Is a Torah Word

Blessed is makarios in the Greek, and behind it stands the Hebrew ashrei. It is the first word of the Psalter: "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly... But his delight is in the law of Yahuah" (Psalm 1:1-2). In Hebrew Scripture the blessing formula is attached to a man's standing toward the Law. Yahushua opened His sermon in that exact form, to a crowd that had been hearing it sung all their lives. Before He said anything, the shape of the sentence had already told them what kind of teacher had sat down in front of them.

## Line by Line

Eight blessings, and every one of them already written:

Poor in spirit. "To this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit" (Isaiah 66:2; also Psalm 34:18; Isaiah 57:15).

They that mourn shall be comforted. "To comfort all that mourn... beauty for ashes" (Isaiah 61:1-3).

The meek shall inherit the earth. "But the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace" (Psalm 37:11). Nearly word for word.

Hunger and thirst after righteousness. "For he satisfieth the longing soul, and filleth the hungry soul with goodness" (Psalm 107:9; also Isaiah 55:1-2).

The merciful obtain mercy. "With the merciful thou wilt shew thyself merciful" (Psalm 18:25; also Proverbs 11:17; Micah 6:8).

The pure in heart shall see God. "He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart... shall ascend into the hill of Yahuah" (Psalm 24:3-4).

The peacemakers called the children of God. "Seek peace, and pursue it" (Psalm 34:14), joined to "Ye are the sons of the living God" (Hosea 1:10).

Persecuted for righteousness' sake. "Fear ye not the reproach of men" (Isaiah 51:7). Yahushua names the source Himself in the next breath: "for so persecuted they the prophets which were before you" (Matthew 5:12).

Nothing on that list was out of reach of a man with the scrolls. If the Beatitudes were the opening of a new religion, the opening was assembled entirely out of quotations from the old one.

## Psalm 37 and the Inheritance

"The meek shall inherit the earth" is not a proverb about temperament. Psalm 37 is a courtroom psalm with two outcomes: the evildoers cut off, the meek left holding the land. Yahushua lifted the line intact, and Scripture carries that same inheritance forward to the new heavens and new earth wherein righteousness dwells (Isaiah 65:17; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 21:1). The blessing is not a mood. It is a verdict about who is left standing when the cutting off is done.

## Verdicts, Not Virtues

Every source verse names a condition and then a consequence. The contrite are looked upon. The mourners are comforted. The meek inherit. The clean of hand ascend. The merciful are met with mercy. Yahushua stacked eight verdicts, not eight admirable attitudes, and He stacked them in the order the prophets had already used.

## Why He Started Here

The Beatitudes are the credential. Before touching a single command in verse 21, Yahushua established that He speaks the same language as the prophets and rules the same way they ruled. A crowd that has just heard eight familiar lines in a row is a crowd prepared for what comes next. Only then does He say, "Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets" (Matthew 5:17), and that sentence lands very differently after eight quotations than it would have landed cold.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

The Beatitudes are preached as the gentle new ethic, the soft opening of a kinder covenant, set against the hard Law standing behind it. That contrast collapses the moment the sources are written in the margin. There is no distance between the mountain and the Law, because on the mountain the Law's own psalms and prophets are being quoted back to the crowd, one line at a time.

The second common reading turns them into attitudes a believer should cultivate, which quietly removes the Old Testament from the passage altogether. Read with the sources restored, the opening of the sermon says something much simpler and much harder: nothing new was said on the mountain.
