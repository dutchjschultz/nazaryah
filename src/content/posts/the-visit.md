## One Word, Two Sides

The census of Numbers is taken with the Hebrew verb *paqad*. Its root meaning is to visit: to come and attend to someone, take note of him, and set him in his place. The word cuts two ways. Yahuah "visited Sarah as he had said" and she bore Isaac (Genesis 21:1). Naomi heard that Yahuah "had visited his people in giving them bread" (Ruth 1:6). Yet the same word stands in the second commandment, where Yahuah warns that He visits "the iniquity of the fathers upon the children" of those who hate Him (Exodus 20:5). A visit from Yahuah is always a visit. What it brings depends on who He finds.

## The Promise Carried Out of Egypt

Joseph did more than speak the promise. He made Israel swear to carry his bones up when the visit came (Genesis 50:25). Generations later, on the night of the exodus, Moses took the bones with him and repeated Joseph's words: "Elohim will surely visit you" (Exodus 13:19). For forty years that coffin traveled in the camp, a standing witness that the visit had come. It was finally buried at Shechem, in the land (Joshua 24:32).

## A Ransom for Every Name

Counting Yahuah's people was never a light thing. When Moses was told to take the sum, the word again is *paqad*, every man counted had to give half a shekel as "a ransom for his soul," so "that there be no plague among them" (Exodus 30:12). The rich could not give more and the poor could not give less (Exodus 30:15). Every soul cost the same. When David later numbered Israel over Joab's objection (2 Samuel 24:3), he confessed afterward, "I have sinned greatly," and a plague followed (2 Samuel 24:10, 15). The people belong to Yahuah, and a count of them is His business.

Peter carries the ransom forward: believers were redeemed "not with corruptible things, as silver and gold," but with the precious blood of Messiah, "as of a lamb without blemish" (1 Peter 1:18–19). The half shekel was the shadow. The blood of the Lamb Yahuah provided is the price that covers every name He counts.

## The Visit in the Gospels

The Greek translators rendered *paqad* with *episkeptomai*, to look in on, and the apostolic writers use that same word for the coming of Messiah. Zacharias, filled with the spirit, said that Yahuah "hath visited and redeemed his people" (Luke 1:68), and that "the dayspring from on high hath visited us" (Luke 1:78). James told the council at Jerusalem that Elohim had visited the nations "to take out of them a people for his name" (Acts 15:14).

Both sides of the word show up again. Weeping over Jerusalem, Yahushua said the city would fall "because thou knewest not the time of thy visitation" (Luke 19:44). It is Numbers 14 told a second time: Yahuah came, the people would not receive Him, and the visit turned to judgment.

## Revelation

Revelation closes the count. The sealed are numbered tribe by tribe (Revelation 7:4–8), and right after them John sees "a great multitude, which no man could number" (Revelation 7:9). No man could count them, but Yahuah can. He "telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names" (Psalm 147:4). The visit Joseph promised ends with every name accounted for before the throne.
