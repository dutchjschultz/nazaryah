## Same Law, New Place

Many Christians are taught that the new covenant brought a new law. The Ten Commandments on stone, they say, belonged to Israel, and believers now live under a softer "law of love" that replaced them. The prophet who first announced the new covenant said something very different. Yahuah promised, "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33). The word is *torah*, the same instruction given at Sinai. The promise is not a new law. It is the same law written in a new place.

Hebrews quotes that promise and applies it to the covenant Yahushua brought (Hebrews 8:10; 10:16). It also names what went wrong with the first arrangement, and it was not the law. Yahuah was "finding fault with them" (Hebrews 8:8), because they broke His covenant "although I was an husband unto them" (Jeremiah 31:32). The fault lay in the people who broke the vows, not in the vows.

This study follows Given in Love and comes before Examined at the Wedding. It traces the ten from the stone at Sinai to the heart of the believer.

## Written by the Finger of Elohim

The ten were the only words at Sinai that Yahuah wrote Himself, "written with the finger of Elohim" (Exodus 31:18). When Moses broke the first tablets over the golden calf, Yahuah wrote again "the words that were in the first tables" (Exodus 34:1). The stone broke. The words did not change.

Yahushua used the same picture. In Luke He casts out devils "with the finger of Elohim" (Luke 11:20); in Matthew the same saying reads "by the Spirit of Elohim" (Matthew 12:28). The finger and the Spirit are one. The Spirit is Yahuah's own hand at work, not a second or third person beside Him. Paul then calls believers a letter written "with the Spirit of the living Elohim; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart" (2 Corinthians 3:3). The same finger that wrote on stone now writes on hearts. Same Writer, same words, new surface.

## The Heart Was Always the Target

The move inward was not an afterthought. Moses told Israel, "these words... shall be in thine heart" (Deuteronomy 6:6), and said the commandment was "very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart" (Deuteronomy 30:14), a verse Paul quotes as the word he preached (Romans 10:8). Solomon used the tablet word itself: "write them upon the table of thine heart" (Proverbs 7:3). The Hebrew *luach* is the same word used for the stone tablets at Sinai. The heart was called a tablet centuries before the new covenant.

The trouble was what had already been written there. "The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron... upon the table of their heart" (Jeremiah 17:1). That is why Yahuah sighed at Sinai, "O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always" (Deuteronomy 5:29).

## A Heart of Stone Made New

Ezekiel names the real problem. Yahuah did not promise to remove the law; He promised to remove the stone: "I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh" (Ezekiel 36:26). The next verse says why: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:27). The Spirit is given so the law will be walked in, not set aside.

Moses called the same work a circumcised heart (Deuteronomy 30:6), and Paul says true circumcision "is that of the heart" (Romans 2:29). The picture is simple. Hard stone tablets met a hard stone heart, and nothing was written that lasted. A heart of flesh can hold the same words the stone held, and keep them.

## What Faded, and What Did Not

Paul's chapter on this subject leans on a story from Exodus. When Moses came down the mountain with the second set of tablets, his face shone so brightly that the people were afraid to come near him. So he covered his face with a veil when he spoke to them, and took it off when he went in to speak with Yahuah (Exodus 34:29-35).

Paul notices that the shine on Moses' face did not last. It faded (2 Corinthians 3:7, 13). Then he makes his point: if the old arrangement came with that much glory, the new one comes with even more. Some readers take this to mean the commandments faded away. They did not. What faded was the glow on a man's face. The words on the tablets stayed exactly as they were.

Paul calls the old arrangement a "ministration of death" (2 Corinthians 3:7). He is not calling the ten commandments death; elsewhere he calls the law "holy, and just, and good" (Romans 7:12). He means that under the old arrangement, the law stood outside the person on stone, and the one who broke it died. Messiah took that penalty: He "hath redeemed us from the curse of the law" (Galatians 3:13). He freed people from the curse, not from the commandments.

Then Paul brings back the veil. When Moses is read, he says, a veil still lies "upon their heart" (2 Corinthians 3:15), and when the heart turns to Yahuah, the veil comes off (3:16), just as Moses took his veil off before Yahuah. The trouble was never the tablets. It was a covered heart that could not see them clearly.

## Blood First, Then the Writing

The new covenant keeps the same order Sinai kept. Jeremiah's promise rests on its foundation: "I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more" (Jeremiah 31:34). Hebrews sets the pieces side by side, the laws written in hearts (Hebrews 10:16), the sins remembered no more (10:17), and then entry "by the blood of Yahushua" (10:19). The blood comes first. The writing on the heart is what the delivered then walk in.

Paul describes it from the inside. He delights "in the law of Elohim after the inward man" (Romans 7:22), and the offering was given "that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit" (Romans 8:4). A heart that fights the ten has not received the new writing. It is still stone.

## The Heart as the Ark

At Sinai the tablets were laid inside the ark, under the mercy seat where the blood was sprinkled (Deuteronomy 10:5; Exodus 25:22). In the new covenant the believer becomes the dwelling: "ye are the temple of Elohim, and... the Spirit of Elohim dwelleth in you" (1 Corinthians 3:16). The tablets did not disappear. The place that held them moved inside. Messiah walked this first: "thy law is within my heart" (Psalm 40:8), words Hebrews applies to Him (Hebrews 10:7).

## Where the Bridegroom Looks

A law on stone can be read from the outside. A law on the heart can be read only by the One who sees inside. "I Yahuah search the heart" (Jeremiah 17:10), and Yahushua says the same to the assembly at Thyatira: "I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts" (Revelation 2:23). When the heart became the tablet, the heart became the place He examines.

Revelation keeps both copies in view. When the temple in heaven opens, "there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament" (Revelation 11:19), the stone witness still in place. And the saints at the end "keep the commandments of Elohim, and have the testimony of Yahushua Messiah" (Revelation 12:17), the same words now carried in living hearts.
