Ezekiel was a captive priest in Babylon. John was an exile on the island of Patmos “for the word of Elohim, and for the testimony” (Revelation 1:9). Six hundred years apart, both were shown the throne, the judgment, the raising of the dead, and the house where Yahuah dwells. John did not invent new pictures. Again and again he was shown what Ezekiel had already seen, and in the same order.

## Vision One: The Throne

Ezekiel’s first vision opens with the throne by the river Chebar (Ezekiel 1). John’s heavenly vision opens the same way: “a door was opened in heaven … and, behold, a throne was set in heaven” (Revelation 4:1-2).

The pieces match one by one. Ezekiel saw a rainbow around the One on the throne (1:28). John saw “a rainbow round about the throne” (4:3). Ezekiel saw a platform like “terrible crystal” (1:22). John saw “a sea of glass like unto crystal” (4:6). Ezekiel saw four living creatures with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox, and an eagle (1:10). John saw four living creatures, “like a lion … like a calf … a face as a man … like a flying eagle” (4:7). Ezekiel’s wheels were “full of eyes round about” (1:18). John’s creatures were “full of eyes before and behind” (4:6).

Even Ezekiel’s commission returns. Ezekiel was handed a scroll “written within and without” (2:10). John saw “a book written within and on the backside” in the hand of the One on the throne (5:1). Ezekiel ate his scroll, and it was “as honey for sweetness” (3:3). John ate a little book, and it was “in my mouth sweet as honey” (10:10). Both were then sent to prophesy.

## Vision Two: The Mark and the Fire

In Ezekiel’s second vision, before judgment fell on Jerusalem, a man clothed in linen went through the city and set “a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations” (Ezekiel 9:4). The killing began at the sanctuary, and the marked were spared (9:6).

John saw the same order. Before the winds of judgment could blow, an angel cried, “Hurt not the earth … till we have sealed the servants of our Elohim in their foreheads” (Revelation 7:3). When the locusts came, they could touch only “those men which have not the seal of Elohim in their foreheads” (9:4). The angels who poured out the last plagues came out of the temple “clothed in pure and white linen” (15:6), just like Ezekiel’s man in linen.

Then the fire. Ezekiel watched the man in linen fill his hands with coals of fire from between the cherubim and scatter them over the city (Ezekiel 10:2). John watched an angel fill a censer with fire from the altar before the throne and cast it into the earth (Revelation 8:5). Judgment comes from the throne, it begins at the house of Yahuah, and those who grieve over the house’s sin carry His mark.

## Vision Three: The Breath and the Graves

In the valley of dry bones, the breath came into the slain, “and they lived, and stood up upon their feet” (Ezekiel 37:10). John watched the two witnesses lie dead in the street, and then “the Spirit of life from Elohim entered into them, and they stood upon their feet” (Revelation 11:11). The words are nearly the same, because the picture is the same.

Ezekiel was told the bones were the whole house of Israel, and that both houses would be one people under one shepherd (37:22-24). John saw the twelve tribes sealed, Joseph among them (Revelation 7:4-8), and then “a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations” standing before the throne, with the Lamb to “feed them” as their shepherd (7:9, 17). And Ezekiel’s promise, “I will open your graves” (37:12), is fulfilled when “the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them” (Revelation 20:13).

## Between the Visions: Gog and Magog

Between the dry bones and the new house, Ezekiel’s book has the last gathering of the nations, Gog and Magog, ended by fire from heaven, with the birds called to the supper of the slain (Ezekiel 38-39). Revelation has the same thing in the same place, just before the new city: the birds called to “the supper of the great Elohim” (Revelation 19:17-18), then Gog and Magog gathered, and “fire came down from Elohim out of heaven, and devoured them” (20:8-9). The Nazaryah investigation [*Gog and Magog*](/blog/gog-and-magog) covers it in full.

## Vision Four: The House and the City

Ezekiel’s last vision begins on “a very high mountain,” with a man holding a measuring reed (Ezekiel 40:2-3). John’s last vision begins the same way: “he carried me away in the spirit to a great and high mountain” (Revelation 21:10), and an angel “had a golden reed to measure the city” (21:15).

Ezekiel saw the glory come back, and “the earth shined with his glory” (43:2). John saw the city “having the glory of Elohim” (21:11), with no need of the sun, “for the glory of Elohim did lighten it” (21:23). Ezekiel’s city had twelve gates, three on each side, each named for a tribe of Israel (48:31-34). John’s city has twelve gates, three on each side, with “the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel” on them (21:12-13).

Ezekiel watched water come out from under the threshold of the house and grow into a river, with trees on both banks bearing fruit every month and leaves “for medicine” (47:1, 12). John saw “a pure river of water of life … proceeding out of the throne,” and on either side “the tree of life,” bearing “every month,” with leaves “for the healing of the nations” (Revelation 22:1-2).

Ezekiel’s book ends with the name of the city: “Yahuah is there” (48:35). John’s ends with the same truth: “Behold, the tabernacle of Elohim is with men, and he will dwell with them” (21:3). John adds one thing: “I saw no temple therein” (21:22). Ezekiel saw a house inside a city. John saw the whole city become the house.

## Why It Matters

Revelation is not a code to be cracked with today’s newspaper. It is the prophets seen again. The throne, the mark, the fire, the raised dead, the last gathering, the measuring reed, the twelve gates, and the river were all in Ezekiel first, and they come in the same order: throne, judgment on the house, the dead raised and gathered, the last battle, and the house where Yahuah dwells forever.

A reader who knows Ezekiel opens Revelation and recognizes it. John was not shown a different story. He was shown how Ezekiel’s story ends.
