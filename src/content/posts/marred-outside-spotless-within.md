## The Verse

Isaiah 52:14 is among the hardest lines in Scripture to read slowly. It sits in the middle of the fourth Servant song, between the promise that the Servant will be lifted up and the promise that He will sprinkle the nations. The middle verse is the price paid between the two.

> *Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high. As many were astonied at thee; his visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men: So shall he sprinkle many nations.* (Isaiah 52:13-15)

The Gospels record the fulfillment: the spitting and the buffeting (Matthew 26:67), the scourging, the crown of thorns, and the reed struck upon the head (Matthew 27:26-30), and Pilate bringing Him out with the words "Behold the man" (John 19:5). The Prophets saw it beforehand: "I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair" (Isaiah 50:6), and "I may tell all my bones: they look and stare upon me" (Psalm 22:17). Those who stood by were **astonied**, shamem (H8074), a word meaning appalled, stunned into desolation. His **visage**, mar'eh (H4758), was what the eye saw. His **form**, to'ar (H8389), was His outline and shape. Both were ruined beyond any man.

## The Word: Marred

The Hebrew word behind "marred" is **mishchath** (H4893), built on the root **shachath** (H7843), to corrupt, ruin, or destroy. It is the root of the flood account: "The earth also was corrupt before God" (Genesis 6:11). It is the root of the destroyer who passed through Egypt on the night of Pesach (Exodus 12:23). The verse does not simply say He was hurt. It says His appearance was corrupted.

The same noun appears in only one other place, and that place settles what the word means in the mouth of a priest. Leviticus 22 lays down which animals may be brought to the altar.

> *Whatsoever hath a blemish, that shall ye not offer: for it shall not be acceptable for you... it shall be perfect to be accepted; there shall be no blemish therein.* (Leviticus 22:20-21)

> *Because their corruption is in them, and blemishes be in them: they shall not be accepted for you.* (Leviticus 22:25)

"Their corruption" in Leviticus 22:25 is mishchath, the very word of Isaiah 52:14. In the Law it is the mark of the rejected offering: the animal the priest turns away from the altar. Isaiah applies that word to the Servant, and applies it beyond measure, "more than any man." To every eye at Golgotha, the Lamb looked like the one offering the Law would refuse.

## Corrupted Outside, Perfect Inside

Here the study turns. The marring was real, but it was on Him, never in Him. Leviticus 22:25 says of the rejected animal that the corruption is *in them*. Of Yahushua the Scriptures say the opposite.

> *But with the precious blood of Messiah, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.* (1 Peter 1:19)

> *Who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God.* (Hebrews 9:14)

The outside bore the full corruption the Law could describe. The inside remained tamim, perfect, the one condition Leviticus 22:21 requires for acceptance. Psalm 16:10 carries the promise to its end: "neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption" (shachath, H7845, from the same field of meaning). The marring could reach His skin; it could not reach His body in the grave, and it could never reach His heart. Peter (Acts 2:27, 31) and Paul (Acts 13:35-37) both press that verse into the resurrection.

This is why the offering was accepted. Yahuah does not judge by the visage. "Man looketh on the outward appearance, but Yahuah looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7). The crowd judged by mar'eh, the appearance; Yahuah judged by what was within, and the Lamb He had provided (Genesis 22:8) was found without blemish.

## The Pattern: From the Outside to the Inside

The body on the tree pictures the whole work it opened. Under the first arrangement, the things of Yahuah stood outside the worshipper: the Law on stone, the priesthood in a family of Levi, the sanctuary in a tent and then a house of stone. What Yahushua carried in His own flesh, outward ruin over inward perfection, was the shape of what came next. The outward forms were opened, and their substance was carried inside.

**The Law.** It was written by the finger of Yahuah on tables of stone (Exodus 31:18). The promised covenant does not erase that Law; it moves it.

> *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts.* (Jeremiah 31:33)

> *Written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.* (2 Corinthians 3:3)

Modern teaching reads these verses as the Law being retired. The text says the reverse. Jeremiah calls it "my law," the same Law, now written in a new place. Ezekiel removes all doubt about its content: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them" (Ezekiel 36:27). The stone was the outside. The heart is the inside. The commandments did not die at the tree; they changed address.

**The Priesthood.** It once belonged to the sons of Aaron, robed in linen and set apart by ordination (Exodus 28-29). Now the believer is "an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices" (1 Peter 2:5), "a royal priesthood" (1 Peter 2:9), made "kings and priests unto God" (Revelation 1:6). The priestly calling has moved from a family line into every one who is washed in the blood.

**The Sanctuary.** Yahuah commanded, "let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them" (Exodus 25:8). The dwelling was a structure Israel walked toward. Yahushua pointed to His own body as the true house: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up... he spake of the temple of his body" (John 2:19, 21). Then the dwelling was carried into His people: "Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?" (1 Corinthians 3:16; 6:19). The temple that was marred and destroyed on the outside was raised, and it now stands on the inside.

The order matters. The body was marred first, and only then was the way opened. The tearing of the outer veil at His death (Matthew 27:51) opened the Holy Place to the believer-priest, while Yahushua alone entered the Most Holy by His own blood, once for all (Hebrews 9:11-12). Deliverance comes first through the blood. The walk in the Law, now written within, follows.

## The Marred One Becomes the Priest

Isaiah 52:15 completes the turn. "So shall he sprinkle many nations." The word is **nazah** (H5137), the priest's word for sprinkling blood before the veil and upon the mercy seat (Leviticus 4:6; 16:14), and for cleansing the leper (Leviticus 14:7). The one whose appearance was corrupted like a rejected sacrifice is the one who sprinkles the nations clean. Even the sprinkling moves inward. Ezekiel 36:25 sprinkles clean water and in the next breath gives a new heart, and Hebrews 10:22 speaks of "our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience." The old sprinkling touched the altar and the flesh; this one reaches the heart.

A manuscript note supports the reading without carrying it. The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) spells the word of verse 14 with a final yod, mashachti, which may be read "I anointed." On that reading the line runs, "I anointed his appearance beyond any man." Whether the scroll preserves the original or a scribe's spelling, the two readings stand side by side in nearly the same letters: the face corrupted by men was the face anointed by Yahuah. The Hebrew of the Masoretic text, read with Leviticus 22, remains the foundation of this study.

## The Face That Was Marred

Isaiah 52:14 is hard to read because it is meant to be. It shows the cost of the change from outside to inside, carried in one body. The visage that no one could recognize is not the last word Scripture gives about that face. John saw it again: "his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength" (Revelation 1:16). And in the midst of the throne scene he saw "a Lamb as it had been slain" (Revelation 5:6), standing, the marks still on Him and no corruption in Him. What was marred outside and spotless within now stands in triumph, and the Law, the priesthood, and the sanctuary He carried inward will be fully at home when "the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3).
