Hebrews was written to Hebrew believers under pressure to draw back (10:38-39). To hold them, the writer takes them through the tabernacle they already knew and shows who serves in it now. He states his main point himself: "this is the sum" (8:1), a High Priest seated in heaven, a minister of the true tabernacle "which the Lord pitched, and not man" (8:2).

This study walks that tabernacle from the outside in: the gate, the brazen altar, the laver, the holy place, the most holy place, and finally the ground outside the camp. Hebrews does not cover these stations in chapter order, so at each station the verses are gathered from wherever the letter speaks of it. Each station opens with its Torah pattern, then shows what Hebrews says stands there now.

## 1. The Gate

The court had one gate, on the east side (Exodus 27:16). Yahushua said the same of Himself: "I am the door" (John 10:9). In Hebrews, the one at the gate is the Son whom Yahuah "appointed heir of all things" (1:2), and whoever comes to Yahuah comes "by him" (7:25). One way in, one man standing in it.

Hebrews explains how that man was qualified to stand there: "in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest" (2:17). The Greek behind "made like" is *homoiōthēnai*, from *homoioō*, to make like or to liken. The verb is passive. He did not make Himself like His brethren; He was made like them, and the verse gives the reason: the priesthood. The sentence describes what was done to Him and why, not a pre-existent being choosing to descend into flesh. Two verses earlier He "took part of the same" flesh and blood as the children (2:14), and "he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one" (2:11). Hebrews sets a man, made like His brethren and appointed by Yahuah, at the gate.

## 2. The Brazen Altar

The first thing past the gate was the altar of burnt offering (Exodus 27:1-2), where every approach began with blood. Hebrews holds the same order: "without shedding of blood is no remission" (9:22). The blood of bulls and goats could never take sins away (10:4), so Yahuah supplied the offering Himself: "a body hast thou prepared me" (10:5), and believers are sanctified through that body offered "once for all" (10:10). The altar was not removed. "We have an altar" (13:10). Entry into everything that follows begins here, through the blood.

## 3. The Laver

Between the altar and the tent stood the laver, where the priests washed before going in, "that they die not" (Exodus 30:20). Hebrews keeps the washing: draw near "having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water" (10:22). The old order had its "divers washings" (9:10, *baptismois*), and the writer counts "the doctrine of baptisms" among the foundations (6:2). The washing exists because the one washed is being made a priest to serve inside, as Revelation says of those washed in His blood and made "kings and priests" (Revelation 1:5-6).

## 4. The Holy Place

Hebrews names the furniture of the first room itself: "the candlestick, and the table, and the shewbread" (9:2), with the priests going in "always" to do the service (9:6). The room was the house, and Hebrews says plainly who the house is now: "whose house are we" (3:6).

The table. Twelve loaves were set in order before Yahuah "every sabbath" (Leviticus 24:8). Bread on the table, renewed each Sabbath, is the walk of the commandments, and Hebrews carries both forward. The new covenant puts Yahuah's laws "into their mind" and writes them "in their hearts" (8:10), and the Sabbath is not retired: "there remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God" (4:9), the *sabbatismos*, a Sabbath-keeping still standing.

The lampstand. It burned continually in the holy place (Leviticus 24:2-4). Hebrews speaks of those "once enlightened" (6:4) and "illuminated" (10:32), and Revelation names the lampstands as the assemblies (Revelation 1:20). The light is Yahuah's; the house carries it.

The altar of incense. Incense rose before the veil morning and evening (Exodus 30:7-8). In Hebrews the High Priest "ever liveth to make intercession" (7:25), and the believer offers "the sacrifice of praise ... the fruit of our lips" (13:15). Revelation shows the same altar in heaven, its smoke rising with the prayers of the saints (Revelation 8:3-4).

## 5. The Most Holy Place

Behind the veil stood the ark, holding "the tables of the covenant," under the mercy seat (9:4-5). The high priest went in alone, once a year, "not without blood" (9:7), on Yom Kippur (Leviticus 16). Hebrews shows the fulfillment: Messiah entered "once" by His own blood and obtained eternal redemption (9:12), entering "into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us" (9:24). Once, not a later investigative phase.

This is where Melchizedek belongs. The believer's hope is an anchor "which entereth into that within the veil; whither the forerunner is for us entered ... made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec" (6:19-20). Melchizedek's order is the priesthood qualified to pass the veil, made "after the power of an endless life" (7:16). The change of priesthood brings "a change also of the law" (7:12), meaning the law of who serves and from what line. The Ten Words were not changed; Hebrews' own inventory still places the tables of the covenant inside the ark (9:4).

The passing in belongs to the forerunner, who entered "for us." The believer follows by hope anchored within the veil (6:19) and is given boldness to enter *ta hagia*, the sanctuary (10:19). The writer uses the full phrase for the inner room, *hagia hagiōn*, only once, in 9:3.

## 6. Outside the Camp

On Yom Kippur, the bodies of the bull and goat whose blood was carried into the holiest were burned outside the camp (Leviticus 16:27). Hebrews makes the same link: "Yahushua also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate" (13:12). Then comes the call: "Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach" (13:13), because "here have we no continuing city" (13:14).

Chapters 11 and 12 belong here. The faithful of chapter 11 confessed they were "strangers and pilgrims" (11:13), seeking a city whose builder and maker is Yahuah (11:10, 16). Chapter 12 tells where that road ends: not at the trembling mountain of Sinai, but at Mount Sion, the heavenly Jerusalem (12:18-22).

## The Sum

Nothing in the tabernacle was torn down. Gate, altar, laver, table, lamp, incense, and ark each have their fulfillment, moved upward to heaven and inward to the heart, under a better High Priest. The walk ends where Revelation opens its doors: "the temple of God was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament" (Revelation 11:19).
