Numbers is the fourth step of the walk. A people already brought out by the blood and already given the Law are now counted one name at a time, then tested on the road between deliverance and the land. The count is Yahuah's visit, and the road shows who trusts Him. Every believer walks that same road today.

## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah is turning a crowd of freed slaves into a people ordered around His dwelling and made ready to receive the land He swore to Abraham. He visits them, numbers them, sets every tribe in place around the tabernacle, and leads them by the cloud. When the first generation refuses the land, He keeps His promise through their children.

## The Name

The title "Numbers" came from the Greek translators of the Septuagint, who called the book *Arithmoi*, and the Latin carried it on as *Numeri*. They named it for the two counts in chapters 1 and 26. The title says nothing about why the counts were taken. That reason is in the Hebrew word for the count itself.

## The Pattern

Two counts frame the book, and one voice drives it.

- **Two counts.** The first count totals 603,550 men (1:46) and the second totals 601,730 (26:51). Between them, a whole generation dies in the wilderness.

- **One voice.** "Yahuah spake unto Moses, saying" opens instruction after instruction from the first verse to the last chapter. Every command comes from one speaker who calls Himself "I," and He says He speaks with Moses "mouth to mouth" (12:8).

## At Sinai (Chapters 1–10:10)

About twenty days in the second year after leaving Egypt, from the first day of the second month (1:1) until the cloud lifts on the twentieth (10:11).

- **The count** (1–4). Every man twenty and older is counted "with the number of their names" (1:2). The Levites are counted apart, from a month old, taken in place of Israel's firstborn (3:12, 15).

- **The camp** (2). Three tribes on each side and the tabernacle in the middle, with Yahuah's dwelling at the center of the host.

- **The blessing** (6:22–27). Aaron's sons put Yahuah's name on the people: "they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them" (6:27).

- **Camp order and gifts** (5–8). The unclean are put outside the camp (5:2–3), the Nazarite vow is set out (6), the twelve princes bring gifts over twelve days (7), and the Levites are cleansed for service (8).

- **The cloud** (9:15–23). A cloud by day and fire by night. Israel rested and moved "at the commandment of Yahuah" (9:18), a phrase repeated seven times in six verses.

- **The silver trumpets** (10:1–10). Two trumpets call the assembly, sound the march, and mark the appointed times.

- **For the walk:** the delivered are set in order around Yahuah's dwelling, each with a name, a tribe, and a place.

## On the Road (Chapters 10:11–21)

This block covers nearly all of the forty years: the march from Sinai, the refusal at Kadesh, and some thirty-eight years that the text passes over in near silence between chapters 14 and 20.

- **The complaints** (11–12). The people tire of the manna, and even Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses.

- **The seventy and the quail** (11). Yahuah puts His spirit on seventy elders (11:25) and sends quail with a plague (11:31–33). Miriam is struck with leprosy for speaking against Moses, "very meek, above all the men" on the earth (12:3, 10).

- **The spies** (13–14). Moses renames Hoshea as Yehoshua, the name the Son carries (13:16). Twelve men search the land, and ten say it cannot be taken. The people refuse, and Yahuah answers that every numbered man twenty and upward will fall in the wilderness (14:29). Only Caleb and Joshua will enter.

- **Laws for the land** (15). Offerings for when they enter, the man gathering sticks on the Sabbath (15:32–36), and the fringes (15:37–41).

- **The rebellion** (16–17). Korah challenges the priesthood. Aaron's rod buds, blossoms, and bears almonds overnight to show whom Yahuah has chosen (17:8).

- **The priests' portion and the red heifer** (18–19). The Levites receive the tithe in place of land (18:21), and the ashes of a red heifer cleanse those who touch the dead.

- **The rock** (20). Moses strikes the rock he was told to speak to and loses his own entry into the land (20:8–12).

- **Edom and Mount Hor** (20:14–29). Edom refuses Israel passage, and Aaron dies on Mount Hor.

- **The serpent** (21:4–9). The bitten look at the bronze serpent and live, the picture Yahushua applies to Himself (John 3:14–15).

- **Sihon and Og** (21:21–35). Israel takes the lands east of the Jordan.

- **For the walk:** the wilderness tests whether the delivered will trust Yahuah's word, and complaint and unbelief keep a believer out as surely as they kept out the first count.

## On the Plains of Moab (Chapters 22–36)

The fortieth year, camped across the Jordan from Jericho (22:1), with the new generation waiting to cross.

- **Balaam** (22–24). A hired prophet tries to curse Israel and can only bless, because "Elohim is not a man, that he should lie" (23:19). His later counsel leads Israel into sin at Peor (25; 31:16).

- **Peor and Phinehas** (25). Israel joins Moab's feasts, and Phinehas's zeal stops the plague (25:7–8, 11).

- **The second count** (26). The new generation is numbered, and the land is to be divided by the size of each tribe (26:53–54).

- **The daughters of Zelophehad** (27; 36). Five daughters ask for their father's inheritance, and Yahuah grants it.

- **Joshua appointed** (27:18–23). Moses lays his hands on the man who will take the people in.

- **The appointed times** (28–29). The offerings for every Sabbath, new moon, and feast are set out for the generation entering the land.

- **Vows, Midian, and the eastern tribes** (30–32). Rules for vows (30), judgment on Midian (31), and Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh settling east of the Jordan on condition that they cross with their brothers (32).

- **The journey and the borders** (33–35). Every stage from Egypt to Moab is recorded (33), the borders of the land are drawn (34), and forty-eight Levite cities include six cities of refuge (35:6–7).

- **For the walk:** the second generation stands at the border with a new count, a new leader, and the same promise.

## Why Yahuah Counted Them

The text gives the surface reason: these are the men "able to go forth to war" (1:3), each tribe set in its place in Yahuah's host. The deeper reason is in the verb. The word for the count is *paqad*, and its first meaning is to visit: to attend to someone and take note of them.

It is the word of Joseph's dying promise, "Elohim will surely visit you" (Genesis 50:24), and the word that made Israel believe in Egypt when they heard "that Yahuah had visited the children of Israel" (Exodus 4:31). The census is that visit finished. Yahuah brought them out, then stood among them and counted every man by name and family.

The same word turns to judgment when the visit is refused. "All that were numbered of you" fell in the wilderness (14:29), and a second count, of their children, inherited the land.

## Why Numbers Is Torah

Torah means instruction, and Numbers instructs in two ways. It carries commands woven between its stories: the Nazarite vow (6), a second Pesach for anyone unclean at the first (9:10–11), the fringes worn "that ye may remember, and do all my commandments" (15:40), the red heifer (19), and the law of inheritance (27; 36). It also instructs by example. Paul says the wilderness events "happened unto them for ensamples" and were "written for our admonition" (1 Corinthians 10:11).

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

- **"Once saved, always saved."** The first count was delivered by the blood at Pesach and still fell in the wilderness. "They could not enter in because of unbelief" (Hebrews 3:19), and Paul says it was written as a warning (1 Corinthians 10:5, 11).

- **"The priestly blessing proves three persons."** The threefold "Yahuah" of 6:24–26 is read as three persons. Verse 27 closes it as one name put on the people by one speaker: "I will bless them."

- **"The wilderness laws were for Israel only."** The fringes were commanded "throughout their generations" (15:38), and Paul calls the whole account instruction for believers (1 Corinthians 10:11).

## Revelation

Revelation takes the count again. John hears the number of the sealed, twelve thousand from each tribe, named tribe by tribe as at Sinai (Revelation 7:4–8). Balaam appears again in the letter to Pergamos, still teaching Yahuah's people to stumble (Revelation 2:14). And the ones who enter the city are those who "do his commandments" (Revelation 22:14): the numbered who did not fall in the wilderness.
