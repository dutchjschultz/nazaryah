## What Came West

A thin red string tied around the left wrist became a fashion item in America in the late 1990s and 2000s, after celebrities were seen wearing it. It was sold through the Kabbalah Centre in Los Angeles and its branches, and it is still sold online and in shops today. The string is advertised as protection from the evil eye and from bad luck.

The string sold by the Kabbalah Centre is said to have been wound around the tomb of Rachel near Bethlehem before being cut into bracelets. It is a folk charm of Jewish mysticism, not a command of the Torah. Red wrist strings are also tied in Hindu and Buddhist ceremonies, which shows the charm is not unique to any one people.

## What It Is For

Like the hamsa and the blue eye, the red string exists to keep harm away. It is trust placed in a thread.

## Scripture's Scarlet Thread

Scripture does have a scarlet thread, and it is never a charm. It always points to something.

At the birth of Tamar's twins, the midwife "bound upon his hand a scarlet thread" when the first hand came out, to mark the firstborn — and yet Pharez was born first and the marked one came second (Genesis 38:28-30). The thread marked a firstborn who was passed over, a pattern Scripture repeats again and again.

When Rahab hid the spies, she was told to "bind this line of scarlet thread in the window" so her house would be spared when Jericho fell (Joshua 2:18). Like the blood on the doorposts in Egypt, the scarlet line marked a house under Yahuah's covenant promise. It saved no one by itself; the promise behind it did.

In the Law, scarlet wool was used with the blood of the sacrifices. Moses "took the blood of calves and of goats, with water, and scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book, and all the people" (Hebrews 9:19). The scarlet always travels with the blood.

## The Verdict

Every scarlet thread in Scripture points to the blood. The red string on the wrist points to a tomb and a charm. Yahuah spoke of scarlet only to show what He removes: "though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow" (Isaiah 1:18). A thread cannot protect anyone. The string belongs under the oak.

Revelation gives the only scarlet that saves: unto Him "that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood" (Revelation 1:5).
