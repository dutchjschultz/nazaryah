## What Yahuah Is Doing

Jeremiah tore down, but he also gave a date for the building to begin again. Judah would serve Babylon seventy years, and then Yahuah would bring them home (25:11; 29:10). A prophet more than sixty years later held that scroll in his hands, did the math, and prayed. The answer he got reaches all the way to the cross.

## The Seventy Years

Jeremiah said it twice. “These nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years” (25:11). “After seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place” (29:10).

Why seventy? Chronicles gives the reason. The people were carried off “to fulfil the word of Yahuah by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years” (2 Chronicles 36:21).

The Law told Israel to let the land rest every seventh year (Leviticus 25:4). They skipped it, year after year. Moses warned what would happen: “Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate, and ye be in your enemies’ land” (Leviticus 26:34). Seventy years in Babylon was the land getting back the Sabbaths it was owed.

## Daniel Reads Jeremiah

Daniel had been taken to Babylon as a young man in the first wave of captives. Near the end of the seventy years, he was reading. “I Daniel understood by books the number of the years, whereof the word of Yahuah came to Jeremiah the prophet, that he would accomplish seventy years in the desolations of Jerusalem” (Daniel 9:2).

Daniel did not sit back and wait for the date. He prayed, fasted, put on sackcloth, and confessed: “we have sinned… neither have we hearkened unto thy servants the prophets” (Daniel 9:3-6). That is the confession Jeremiah had been begging for the whole time.

## The Answer: Seventy Weeks

While Daniel was still praying, the angel Gabriel came with an answer (Daniel 9:21). Daniel asked about seventy years. Gabriel answered with seventy weeks: “Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city” (9:24).

These are weeks of years, seven years each. Seventy times seven is 490 years. Seventy years of exile for broken Sabbath years, and now seventy Sabbath cycles to finish the work.

Gabriel told Daniel what those 490 years would bring (9:24):

- to finish the transgression,
- to make an end of sins,
- to make reconciliation for iniquity,
- to bring in everlasting righteousness,
- to seal up the vision and prophecy,
- and to anoint the most Holy.

That is not a list about a wall or a city. It is a list about the Messiah.

## Counting It Out

Gabriel gave the starting point: “from the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks” (9:25). That is sixty-nine weeks, or 483 years.

- **457 BC.** King Artaxerxes, in his seventh year, sends Ezra back with full authority to restore Jerusalem under the law of Elohim (Ezra 7:7-26).
- **27 AD.** Count 483 years forward and the count reaches 27 AD, when Yahushua was baptized and anointed, and began to preach, “The time is fulfilled” (Mark 1:15).
- **31 AD.** “In the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease” (9:27). Three and a half years into His ministry, at Pesach, Messiah was “cut off, but not for himself” (9:26). He offered “one sacrifice for sins for ever” (Hebrews 10:12).
- **About 34 AD.** Three and a half more years, and the seventieth week closes. Stephen is stoned, and the good news goes out past Judea (Acts 7–8).

All seventy weeks are finished. Nothing is left over.

## What This Rules Out

Much of the modern church takes the seventieth week, cuts it off from the other sixty-nine, slides it two thousand years into the future, and calls it a seven-year tribulation. Gabriel gave Daniel seventy weeks in one unbroken count, and Daniel 9 never mentions a gap. The week that was supposed to be about the end of the world was about the cross.

## Bringing It Home

It started with a missed Sabbath year. Israel would not let the land rest, so the land got its rest anyway, seventy years of it. A man reading Jeremiah’s scroll confessed the sin, and Yahuah answered with 490 years that ended with the Messiah cut off for His people. Yahuah keeps His calendar. Every number He gives is kept.

## Where It Lands

In Revelation, the Lamb stands in the middle of the throne, looking “as it had been slain,” and the elders sing, “thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to Elohim by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation” (Revelation 5:6, 9). The one cut off in the middle of the seventieth week is the one standing at the center of heaven.
