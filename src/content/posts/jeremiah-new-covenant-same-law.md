## What Yahuah Is Doing

Jeremiah was sent to tear down and then to build (1:10). Most of the book is tearing down, because Judah would not hear. But in the middle of the siege, with Babylon’s army outside the walls, Yahuah gave Jeremiah the best promise in the whole book: a new covenant.

It is the one verse from Jeremiah almost every church knows. It is also the verse most often used to say the Law is gone. Read slowly, it says the opposite.

## The Promise

“Behold, the days come, saith Yahuah, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah: not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith Yahuah: but this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith Yahuah, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim, and they shall be my people” (31:31-33).

Then He adds: “I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more” (31:34).

## Four Things the Promise Says

**1. The problem was the people, not the Law.** Yahuah says why a new covenant was needed: “which my covenant they brake” (31:32). He was a faithful husband. They were the unfaithful wife. Hebrews says the same thing when it quotes this passage: “For finding fault with them, he saith, Behold, the days come…” (Hebrews 8:8). The fault was found with them.

**2. The Law stays. Its address changes.** “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts” (31:33). Not a new law. Not no law. “My law,” the same one, moved from tablets of stone to the human heart. Paul describes it the same way: written “not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart” (2 Corinthians 3:3). Ezekiel says what the new heart will do: “cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them” (Ezekiel 36:26-27).

**3. Forgiveness comes with it.** “I will forgive their iniquity” (31:34). At the last supper, Yahushua lifted the cup and said, “This cup is the new testament in my blood” (Luke 22:20). The blood opens the covenant. The Law written on the heart is how the forgiven walk in it. First the blood, then the walk.

**4. It was made with Israel and Judah.** The promise names “the house of Israel, and the house of Judah” (31:31). Nobody from the nations gets a separate deal. They come in by being joined to Israel, grafted into the same olive tree (Romans 11:17), no longer “aliens from the commonwealth of Israel” (Ephesians 2:12-13).

## The Word “New”

The Hebrew word for new here is *chadash*. It is the same root as the word for the new moon, *chodesh*. Every month the moon is renewed. It is not a different moon. It is the same moon, made bright again.

That is the picture of the new covenant. The same Yahuah, the same people, the same Law, renewed and written where it cannot be broken off a mountain and smashed.

## But Doesn’t Hebrews Say It Vanished?

Right after quoting Jeremiah, Hebrews says, “that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away” (Hebrews 8:13). This is often read as the Law fading out.

Look at what Hebrews just quoted two verses earlier: “I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts” (Hebrews 8:10). The writer cannot be saying the Law vanished in the same breath he says it is being written on hearts. What grew old was the first arrangement: tablets of stone, a people who broke it, and an earthly priesthood serving at a copy of the heavenly tabernacle (Hebrews 8:4-5). The Law did not vanish. It moved inside.

## Bringing It Home

The modern church teaches that the new covenant freed believers from the Law. Jeremiah says the new covenant is the Law written on the heart of a forgiven people. Paul says the same: Messiah came “that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit” (Romans 8:4).

A heart with Yahuah’s Law written on it does not look for ways out of His commandments. It wants to keep them.

## Where It Lands

In Revelation, the temple of Elohim in heaven is opened, “and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament” (Revelation 11:19). The ark held the tablets of the Law. At the end of the story, the covenant box is still there, and what was written on stone is now written on the hearts of His people, “they which keep the commandments of Elohim, and the faith of Yahushua” (Revelation 14:12).
