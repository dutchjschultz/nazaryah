## The Pattern

Malachi is written as a courtroom argument, and every dispute follows the same three steps. Yahuah makes a charge. Israel denies it with a question — usually *Wherein?* Yahuah answers the question with evidence. It happens six times, and then the arguing stops and the book ends with a command. Hold that pattern and the whole book is held.

The setting is after the return from Babylon. The temple is rebuilt and the altar is working, but the worship has gone cold: a correct building full of careless service. The name Malachi means *my messenger*, and the book keeps naming messengers — the priest (2:7), the way-preparer (3:1), the messenger of the covenant (3:1), and Elijah (4:5). One thread runs through all six disputes: *my name* appears ten times in four chapters.

## 1. Love (1:2–5)

**The charge:** *I have loved you.*

**Israel replies:** *Wherein hast thou loved us?*

**The evidence:** Edom. Both nations were laid low; Israel was brought home and Edom was not. *They shall build, but I will throw down* (1:4).

**Watch for:** this is about two nations and their land, not individual souls chosen for damnation. Calvinism takes Paul's quotation of it (Romans 9:13) further than Malachi goes.

## 2. The Name (1:6–2:9)

**The charge:** *O priests, that despise my name.*

**Israel replies:** *Wherein have we despised thy name?*

**The evidence:** what they put on the altar — the blind, the lame, the sick. They would not offer it to the Persian governor. Meanwhile *my name shall be great among the Gentiles* (1:11).

**Watch for:** the priest's job: *the law of truth was in his mouth* (2:6). The charge is that they were *partial in the law* (2:9) — teaching the parts they liked and setting aside the rest.

## 3. Treachery (2:10–16)

**The charge:** *Why do we deal treacherously every man against his brother?*

**Israel replies:** *Wherefore?*

**The evidence:** marriage to *the daughter of a strange el* (2:11) and men putting away *the wife of thy youth* (2:14). Yahuah *hateth putting away* (2:16).

**Watch for:** the opening line: *Have we not all one father? hath not one El created us?* (2:10). One Father, one El, singular — no plurality of persons.

## 4. Weariness (2:17–3:5)

**The charge:** *Ye have wearied Yahuah with your words.*

**Israel replies:** *Wherein have we wearied him?*

**The evidence:** they say *Where is the Elohim of judgment?* The answer: *I will send my messenger … and the Adon, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant* (3:1).

**Watch for:** a Trinity proof text that fails on its own words. A messenger is sent, and the sender is not the sent. The one who comes is *ha-Adon*, not Yahuah. And every gospel reads Yahuah's *before me* as *before thee* (Matthew 11:10; Mark 1:2; Luke 7:27).

## 5. Robbery (3:6–12)

**The charge:** *Ye have robbed me.*

**Israel replies:** *Wherein have we robbed thee?*

**The evidence:** *In tithes and offerings* (3:8). The Levites had not been fed, so they had left the temple for their fields (Nehemiah 13:10).

**Watch for:** the hinge verse: *I am Yahuah, I change not* (3:6), tied directly to *mine ordinances* (3:7). The One who gave them does not change.

## 6. Stout Words (3:13–4:3)

**The charge:** *Your words have been stout against me.*

**Israel replies:** *What have we spoken so much against thee?*

**The evidence:** they said *It is vain to serve Elohim* (3:14). Then a remnant appears — *they that feared Yahuah* — written in *a book of remembrance* and called His *jewels* (3:16–17).

**Watch for:** the day that comes splits two ways: an oven for the wicked, leaving *neither root nor branch* (4:1), and a dawn for those who fear the name (4:2).

## The Last Command (4:4–6)

The disputes end and the prophets close on one instruction:

> *Remember ye the law of Moses my servant, which I commanded unto him in Horeb for all Israel, with the statutes and judgments.* (Malachi 4:4)

Not a new law, and not a notice that the old one would expire — an order to remember all of it. Then Elijah is promised before the great day, to turn the hearts of fathers and children (4:5–6). Yahushua said John was that Elijah (Matthew 11:14).

## Three Pulpit Errors Malachi Answers

- *The law is finished.* The prophets' last instruction is to remember it (4:4), from the One who says *I change not* (3:6).
- *Malachi 3:10 means ten percent of a paycheck to the church.* The Torah tithe was produce, given to Levites, eaten at the feasts, and shared with the stranger, fatherless, and widow (Deuteronomy 14:22–29). The same pulpits that preach 3:10 usually call the rest of Torah finished — the very *partial in the law* charge of 2:9.
- *Malachi 3:1 proves Yahushua is Yahuah.* The verse names a sender and a messenger of the covenant; the gospels confirm two by reading *me* as *thee*.

## Where Malachi Points Next

The last word of Malachi is *curse* — the threat left hanging if the hearts are not turned. Revelation lifts it: *And there shall be no more curse* (Revelation 22:3), and those who *thought upon his name* (3:16) carry *his Father's name written in their foreheads* (Revelation 14:1).
