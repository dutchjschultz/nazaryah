## Two Tablets, Two Halves

Yahuah wrote His Ten Words on two tables of stone (Exodus 31:18). When Yahushua was asked which commandment was greatest, He split the Law the same way. The first is to love Yahuah with all the heart, soul, and mind. The second, "like unto it," is to love thy neighbour as thyself. "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets" (Matthew 22:37-40). The first tablet covers man and Yahuah. The second covers man and his neighbour.

Buddhism keeps a good part of the second tablet and has no first tablet at all.

## What Buddhism Kept

Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, taught in India about five centuries before Messiah. Every Buddhist layman takes the Five Precepts. He vows not to kill, not to take what is not given, not to commit sexual misconduct, not to speak falsely, and not to drink what clouds the mind. The first four are the sixth, eighth, seventh, and ninth commandments almost word for word (Exodus 20:13-16). The fifth echoes Solomon: "Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging" (Proverbs 20:1).

The Buddha also taught that craving is the root of man's misery. That is the tenth commandment, "Thou shalt not covet" (Exodus 20:17), which Paul said first showed him his own sin (Romans 7:7). Buddhism also teaches children to honour and support their parents, which is the fifth.

Buddhists even keep observance days set by the moon. On the new moon, the full moon, and the quarter moons between them, devout laymen take extra vows, fast after noon, and gather to hear teaching. The world has a Buddhist people keeping days by the moon while the church keeps a day set by a Roman emperor.

Paul explained how this happens: "the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law," showing "the work of the law written in their hearts" (Romans 2:14-15). Yahuah wrote the second tablet so deep into man that even a religion with no God could find much of it.

## What Buddhism Lost

**There is no Creator.** The Buddha taught a path, not a God. Buddhism has no Maker at its center, no one to whom man answers. Yet the first words of Scripture are "In the beginning God created" (Genesis 1:1), and the first commandment is "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" (Exodus 20:3). Buddhism did not put another god before Yahuah. It put no one there at all.

**The temples are full of images.** A religion that began without a god ended up bowing before statues of a man. The second commandment forbids making any graven image and bowing to it (Exodus 20:4-5). Paul described this exact turn: men "changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man" (Romans 1:23).

**The deliverance is self-made.** Buddhism teaches karma, the weight of a man's own deeds following him from life to life, and teaches that he escapes by his own effort. Its goal is nirvana, a word that means "blowing out," like a flame. Scripture says the opposite on every point. "It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27), with no second life to try again. A man is not saved by his own works (Ephesians 2:8-9). And the end is not a flame blown out but life: "this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Yahushua Messiah, whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3).

## The Lesson for the Church

Buddhism is the second tablet without the first. The modern church claims the first tablet and lets the second slide. Its people sing about loving God and then lie, covet, and wink at sexual sin, because they are told the Law is gone. John said the two cannot be pulled apart: "he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen?" (1 John 4:20).

A Buddhist with no God refuses to steal, lie, or commit adultery. A churchgoer who claims to know the God who wrote those words often does all three and calls it favor.

## Conclusion

Buddhism proves that the second tablet is written on the human heart. It also proves that the second tablet alone delivers no one. Kindness, honesty, and self-control without the Creator end in a flame blown out. A man needs both tablets, and he needs the blood that opens the way to keep them.

Revelation says the gates of the city open to those who keep both: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city" (Revelation 22:14).
