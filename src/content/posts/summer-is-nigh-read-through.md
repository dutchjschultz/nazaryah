A companion to the breakdown Summer Is Nigh. The parable runs start to finish with the meaning of each image carried beside it. Nothing is argued here.

> **MARK 13:28–31 — KJV**
>
> Now learn a parable of the fig tree *[the tree whose leaves tell the season, a calendar any farmer reads; no fruit sought on it and no curse laid on it]*; When her branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, *[the winter is past, the rain is over and gone; the season turning]* ye know that summer is near *[a basket of summer fruit, the end is come upon my people of Israel; the harvest is past, the summer is ended]*: So ye *[the four on the mount of Olives, who asked when the stones would fall]* in like manner, when ye shall see these things *[deceivers in His name, wars, earthquakes, famines, councils and synagogues, the good news to the nations, and last of all Jerusalem compassed with armies]* come to pass, know that it is nigh, even at the doors. *[the desolation thereof is nigh; the kingdom of God is nigh at hand]* Verily I say unto you, that this generation *[the men standing before Him, on whom all the righteous blood would come]* shall not pass, till all these things be done. *[the city and the sanctuary destroyed, the house left desolate, not one stone left upon another]* Heaven and earth shall pass away: *[the sun darkened and the stars falling, as a falling fig from the fig tree]* but my words shall not pass away. *[every word of the answer kept, in the year the stones came down]*

Put together, the parable answers the disciples’ question about when the temple would fall and how they would know it was close. A fig tree in leaf tells any farmer that summer is near, and in the Prophets summer is the end come upon a people. The things Yahushua listed were the leaves, those men lived through every one of them, and when armies surrounded Jerusalem the end of the house was at the doors.

The fig tree is not a date to calculate. It was a sign to read and an order to act on, given to men in Judaea so that they would leave before the stones came down, and that generation saw every word of it done.
