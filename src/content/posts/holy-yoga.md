## What the Church Did

Christianity did not invent an exercise of its own to send into the culture. It borrowed one. Over the past twenty years "Christian yoga" has spread through American churches. The largest program, Holy Yoga, was founded in 2006 by Brooke Boon in Arizona and has trained thousands of instructors. Holy Yoga keeps the physical poses, the breathing, and the meditation of traditional yoga and adds Bible verses, worship music, and prayer.

The claim behind it is simple: the poses are only stretches, and a Christian can fill them with Christian meaning.

## The Problem with the Claim

The poses are not neutral. *Yoga* means "to yoke" — to join the self to a Hindu god. Its most common sequence, *Surya Namaskar*, is a chain of bows named for the sun god Surya (see *Saluting the Sun*). Laying a Scripture reading over the sequence does not change what the sequence is. It changes only whose name is spoken while it is done.

## Aaron's Feast

Scripture already records this exact move. When Israel made the golden calf, Aaron built an altar before it and announced, "To morrow is a feast to Yahuah" (Exodus 32:5). The calf was Egyptian. The name on the feast was Yahuah's. Aaron did not think he was leaving Yahuah; he thought he was bringing Egypt's worship into Yahuah's camp. Three thousand men died that day.

Moses had been told in advance not to do this. Israel was not to ask how the nations served their gods and say, "even so will I do likewise" (Deuteronomy 12:30). Ezekiel later charged Israel's priests with the same sin: they "put no difference between the holy and profane" (Ezekiel 22:26).

## Separation, Not Blending

The command to the believer is not to clean up the nations' practices and use them. It is to leave them: "come out from among them, and be ye separate ... and touch not the unclean thing" (2 Corinthians 6:17). Paul also gave the right weight to exercise itself: "bodily exercise profiteth little" (1 Timothy 4:8). If the body needs stretching, it can be stretched without borrowing the bows of a sun god.

## The Verdict

Christian yoga is Aaron's feast — a borrowed worship sequence with Yahuah's name written over it. The verse on the screen does not sanctify the bow on the mat.

Revelation repeats the call Paul gave the Corinthians, this time for the last generation: "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins" (Revelation 18:4).
