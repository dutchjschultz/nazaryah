Leviticus is not one long list of rules. Its commands fall into kinds, and each kind serves a different part of the walk. What follows is not a full teaching on any of them, only a sentence or two on how each kind is walked by a believer today.

## Offering Laws (Chapters 1–7; 16)

The offerings teach that no one comes near Yahuah except through the blood He provides. The believer enters "by the blood of Yahushua, by a new and living way" (Hebrews 10:19–20), and then brings himself as "a living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1) and his praise as "the fruit of our lips" (Hebrews 13:15).

## Priesthood Laws (Chapters 8–10; 21–22)

The priests kept the charge of the sanctuary and taught the difference between holy and unholy (10:10–11). Believers are "a royal priesthood" (1 Peter 2:9), and the sanctuary they guard and keep is their own body, the temple of the spirit within them (1 Corinthians 6:19).

## Purity Laws (Chapters 11–15)

The purity laws separate clean from unclean in what is eaten and in the body itself. The believer keeps that line in his own temple, cleansing himself "from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit" (2 Corinthians 7:1) and glorifying Elohim in his body (1 Corinthians 6:20).

## Holiness and Moral Laws (Chapters 17–20)

These chapters cover the family, honest weights, the poor, the stranger, and the neighbor, and they are summed up in "love thy neighbour as thyself" (19:18). Paul says every one of these commandments is "briefly comprehended" in that saying, because "love worketh no ill to his neighbour" (Romans 13:9–10).

## Appointed Times (Chapter 23)

The Sabbath and the feasts are called "the feasts of Yahuah… my feasts" (23:2), not Israel's feasts. Paul tells the assembly at Corinth, "let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:8), and calls the appointed times "a shadow of things to come" (Colossians 2:17), which the believer keeps as rehearsals of what Yahuah is still doing.

## Land Laws (Chapters 19; 23; 25)

The corners of the field were left for the poor and the stranger (19:9–10; 23:22), the land rested every seventh year, and debts and land were released at the Jubilee, because "the land is mine" (25:23). The believer holds what he owns as a steward, leaves his corner for those in need, and forgives debts as he has been forgiven (Matthew 6:12).

## One Walk

Six kinds of law, one direction. The offerings bring the believer near, the priesthood sets him apart, purity keeps his temple clean, holiness shapes his dealings with others, the appointed times set his calendar by Yahuah's, and the land laws teach him that nothing he holds is finally his own.

## Revelation

Revelation gathers the six into one blessing: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city" (Revelation 22:14).
