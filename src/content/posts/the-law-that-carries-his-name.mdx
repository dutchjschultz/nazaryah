Modern Christian teaching honors the Name in prayer and song while setting the Law aside as finished. Scripture never separates the two. The people who fear the Name are the people who keep the Law, and the people who break the Law profane the Name.

## One Act, Not Two Duties

Moses warns Israel to "observe to do all the words of this law … that thou mayest fear this glorious and fearful name" (Deuteronomy 28:58). "That thou mayest fear" is one Hebrew word, *leyir’ah* (from *yare’*, to fear), carrying the prefix *lamed*, "for the purpose of." No *vav* (and) stands between the doing and the fearing, though some renderings read as if there were two obligations. The Law is done in order to fear the Name. Doing the words is what fearing the Name looks like.

> **New Testament witness.** The seventh trumpet rewards "them that fear thy name," and the next verse opens the temple to show "the ark of his testament" (Revelation 11:18-19) — the chest that held the Law.

## Carrying the Name in Emptiness

"Thou shalt not take the name of Yahuah thy Elohim in vain" (Exodus 20:7). The Hebrew word behind "take" is *nasa’*, which means to lift up and carry — the way a wife carries her husband’s name. The word behind "in vain" is *shav’*, which means empty or worthless. Read in Hebrew, the command is not mainly about cursing or careless speech. It says: do not carry Yahuah’s Name on yourself for nothing. A person who is called by His Name but does not live by His Law carries the Name empty — the Name is on the outside, but nothing of what it stands for is on the inside. This command about the Name is written on the tablets of the Law itself, so the Name and the Law were given together from the start.

> **New Testament witness.** Paul says breaking the Law is what causes "the name of God" to be "blasphemed among the Gentiles" (Romans 2:23-24).

## Hallowed by Obedience

"Ye shall keep my commandments, and do them … neither shall ye profane my holy name" (Leviticus 22:31-32). Keeping and not profaning are stated as one command.

> **New Testament witness.** Yahushua’s prayer sets the same pair side by side: "Hallowed be thy name. … Thy will be done" (Matthew 6:9-10) — the Father’s Name and the Father’s will.

## The Name Over the Ark

The ark was "called by the name of Yahuah of hosts" (2 Samuel 6:2), and it held nothing "save the two tables of stone" (1 Kings 8:9). The Name was called over the Law, and the house was built "to the name of Yahuah" to receive it (1 Chronicles 22:19). The psalmist joins them in one line: "I have remembered thy name … and have kept thy law" (Psalm 119:55).

> **New Testament witness.** Yahushua prays to the Father, "I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me … and they have kept thy word" (John 17:6) — the Name made known and the word kept, in one line.

## The Last Word of the Prophets

The Old Testament closes with the same pair: "unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise," then "Remember ye the law of Moses my servant" (Malachi 4:2, 4). That charge was never withdrawn.

> **New Testament witness.** The last call to every nation is "Fear God, and give glory to him," and the saints who answer it are "they that keep the commandments of God" (Revelation 14:7, 12).

## Calling on the Name Without the Law

Many will say, "have we not prophesied in thy name?" and hear, "depart from me, ye that work iniquity" (Matthew 7:22-23). The word is *anomia*, lawlessness — literally, without law. They used the Name constantly and carried it in emptiness. Paul gives the rule: "Let every one that nameth the name of Messiah depart from iniquity" (2 Timothy 2:19).

The order does not change. Entry comes through the blood of the Messiah, the provision of Yahuah, never by law-keeping. Israel was delivered by the lamb’s blood first and then brought to Sinai to receive the Law and the Name. The walk in the Law is the life of one who now carries the Name.

## Closing

Solomon gave the whole of it: "Fear God, and keep his commandments" (Ecclesiastes 12:13). Revelation ends the same way — the redeemed see His face and "his name shall be in their foreheads," and "Blessed are they that do his commandments" (Revelation 22:4, 14).

## Six Further Witnesses

The same pairing runs through the rest of Scripture. Six more passages from the Law, the Writings, and the Prophets set the Name and the Law side by side.

### Called by the Name

#### Deuteronomy 28:9-10

The same chapter that warns Israel to do the Law "that thou mayest fear this glorious and fearful name" (Deuteronomy 28:58) opens with the promise. Yahuah will establish Israel as a holy people "if thou shalt keep the commandments of Yahuah thy Elohim, and walk in his ways," and then "all people of the earth shall see that thou art called by the name of Yahuah" (Deuteronomy 28:9-10). Keep the commandments, and the nations see the Name upon the people. The chapter is bracketed by the Name — called by it at verse 10, warned to fear it at verse 58 — and the hinge in both places is doing the Law.

> **New Testament witness.** James asks, "Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?" and in the next breath turns to "the royal law according to the scripture" (James 2:7-8), warning that whoever stumbles in one point is guilty of all (James 2:10). James moves from the Name the believer is called by straight to the Law the believer must keep, because to him they are one subject.

### Gathered to the Place of the Name

#### Nehemiah 1:9-11

Nehemiah reminds Yahuah of His own promise: "if ye turn unto me, and keep my commandments, and do them," He would gather His scattered people "unto the place that I have chosen to set my name there" (Nehemiah 1:9). He then prays for "thy servants, who desire to fear thy name" (Nehemiah 1:11). Keeping the commandments is the road back to the place of the Name, and the servants of Yahuah are those who fear it.

### The Fearful Name and the Doing

#### Psalm 111:9-10

"Holy and reverend is his name. The fear of Yahuah is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that do his commandments" (Psalm 111:9-10). "Reverend" is *nora’*, the same word Deuteronomy 28:58 uses for the "fearful" Name. In two verses the psalm moves from the fearful Name, to the fear of Yahuah, to the doing of His commandments, with no break between them.

### Walking in the Name

#### Micah 4:2, 5

In the last days the nations say, "he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for the law shall go forth of Zion" (Micah 4:2). Three verses later the remnant answers, "we will walk in the name of Yahuah our Elohim for ever and ever" (Micah 4:5). The paths taught by the Law going out from Zion are the same road as walking in the Name.

### The Name Profaned and Restored

#### Ezekiel 36:20-27

Israel in exile "profaned my holy name" among the nations (Ezekiel 36:20). Yahuah answers, "I will sanctify my great name" (Ezekiel 36:23), and He names the means: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them" (Ezekiel 36:27). The Name was profaned by disobedience and is sanctified again by a people made to walk in the statutes. The cure for a profaned Name is a restored obedience, not an abolished Law.

### The Priest Who Feared the Name

#### Malachi 2:5-6

Of the covenant with Levi, Yahuah says, "he feared me, and was afraid before my name. The law of truth was in his mouth" (Malachi 2:5-6). The priest who stood in awe of the Name was the priest who carried the Law of truth in his mouth. The priesthood was defined by both at once.

## Closing

Solomon gave the whole of it: "Fear God, and keep his commandments" (Ecclesiastes 12:13). Revelation ends the same way — the redeemed see His face and "his name shall be in their foreheads," and "Blessed are they that do his commandments" (Revelation 22:4, 14).

Earlier in the same book, to the assembly at Philadelphia, the Son says, "thou hast kept my word, and hast not denied my name" (Revelation 3:8) — the word kept and the Name held, together, in one of only two assemblies that receive no rebuke.
