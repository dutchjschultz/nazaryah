Leviticus is the third step of the walk. The slaves are out, the sea is behind them, and Yahuah has moved into the camp. Now they learn to walk. Near the end of the book He tells them what He did for them: "I have broken the bands of your yoke, and made you go upright" (26:13). Leviticus is the freed slave learning to stand up straight and walk with his Elohim, who says, "I will walk among you" (26:12).

## What Yahuah Is Doing

At Sinai Yahuah told Israel what He was making of them: "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). Leviticus builds it. Yahuah now lives in the middle of the camp, and the book lays out how a holy Elohim and a people can live together without the people being consumed. It begins with an altar and ends with a nation, its land, its calendar, and its King.

## The Name and the Time

"Leviticus" is the Greek and Latin title, meaning the book of the Levites, after the priestly tribe whose work fills it. The whole book takes place at Sinai in about one month: between the raising of the tabernacle on the first day of the first month of the second year (Exodus 40:17) and the census on the first day of the second month (Numbers 1:1).

## The Pattern

The book moves in six steps, the same order the believer walks in.

- **Blood** (1–7) → **Priest** (8–10) → **Clean** (11–15) → **Atonement** (16) → **Holy People** (17–22) → **Kingdom** (23–27).

Each step stands on the one before it. No one learns clean from unclean before there is a priest to teach it, and there is no priest before there is an altar.

## The Blood (Chapters 1–7)

Five offerings open the book: the burnt offering, burned whole (1); the grain offering (2); the peace offering, shared at a meal (3); the sin offering (4–5:13); and the trespass offering, which repays what was taken (5:14–6:7). Instructions for the priests follow (6:8–7). The reason for the blood is given later in one sentence: "the life of the flesh is in the blood… it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (17:11).

## The Priest (Chapters 8–10)

Aaron and his sons are washed, dressed, anointed, and set apart over seven days (8:33). When Aaron offers for the first time, fire comes out from before Yahuah and consumes the offering, and the people shout and fall on their faces (9:24). The same day, two of Aaron's sons are struck down for bringing fire Yahuah had not commanded (10:1–2). The priests' charge is then stated: to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean," and to teach Israel all the statutes (10:10–11).

## The Clean (Chapters 11–15)

What the priests must teach comes next: clean and unclean animals (11), childbirth (12), skin disease and mildew (13–14), and bodily discharges (15). The food chapter carries the book's first "be holy; for I am holy" (11:44).

## The Atonement (Chapter 16)

Once a year, on the tenth day of the seventh month, the high priest goes behind the veil into the most holy place with blood (16:29). On that day atonement is made "that ye may be clean from all your sins before Yahuah" (16:30), and the sanctuary itself is cleansed (16:33). Hebrews reads it plainly: the high priest went in "once every year, not without blood" (Hebrews 9:7), and Messiah entered "by his own blood… once into the holy place" (Hebrews 9:12).

## The Holy People (Chapters 17–22)

The holiness now spreads from the tabernacle to the whole community: no eating of blood (17), the family and the marriage bed (18; 20), honest dealing, care for the poor, the stranger, and the neighbor (19), and a higher standard for the priests (21–22). In the middle stands the line Yahushua called the second great commandment: "thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself" (19:18; Matthew 22:39).

## The Kingdom (Chapters 23–27)

The last block sets up a nation under its King.

- **Its calendar** (23). The Sabbath and the appointed times, "the feasts of Yahuah… my feasts" (23:2).

- **Its name** (24). The lamp kept burning, the bread set out each Sabbath, and the death of the man who blasphemed the Name (24:11, 16).

- **Its land** (25). The Sabbath year and the Jubilee, because "the land is mine" (25:23).

- **Its covenant** (26). Blessing for obedience, curses for rebellion, and a promise to remember the covenant even in exile (26:42).

- **Its vows and tithe** (27). What is promised to Yahuah, and the tenth that is "holy unto Yahuah" (27:30).

## The Kinds of Law

Leviticus sorts its commands into kinds: offering laws (1–7; 16), priesthood laws (8–10; 21–22), purity laws (11–15), holiness and moral laws (17–20), the appointed times (23), and the laws of the land (25). Each kind serves one step of the walk.

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

- **"Leviticus belongs to the Jews."** Yahushua drew the second great commandment from it (Matthew 22:39), and Peter gave its "be ye holy" to believers scattered among the nations (1 Peter 1:16).

- **"The food laws are gone."** Leviticus calls them the law that makes "a difference between the unclean and the clean" (11:46–47). Isaiah describes those "eating swine's flesh" being consumed when Yahuah comes with fire (Isaiah 66:15–17).

- **"The tithe is a tenth of your paycheck to the church."** Leviticus defines the tithe as the seed and fruit of the land and the increase of the herd and flock (27:30, 32), given to the Levites for their service at the tabernacle (Numbers 18:21).

## Revelation

Revelation finishes what Leviticus began. Messiah has made His people "kings and priests unto Elohim" (Revelation 1:6), the kingdom of priests Yahuah promised at Sinai. The line between clean and unclean holds to the very end: into the city "there shall in no wise enter… any thing that defileth" (Revelation 21:27). And the tabernacle built for one camp is replaced by Yahuah Himself: John "saw no temple therein," for Yahuah and the Lamb "are the temple of it" (Revelation 21:22).
