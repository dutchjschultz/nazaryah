**NUMBERS 6:24–27**

**One Name on the People**

*Three lines of blessing, one name, one voice*

The priestly blessing speaks the Father's name three times and seals it as one name, given by one “I.”

> Behind "LORD" in your Bible lies a hidden name — in the Hebrew it is Yahuah *Psalm 83:18*; Yahuah is the Father *Isaiah 63:16*; Yahuah is the only God, beside Him there is no other *Isaiah 45:5*; therefore Yahuah the Father is the only true God, leaving no room for a second or third person *1 Corinthians 8:6*.
>
> **NUMBERS 6:24–27**
>
> *The LORD \[Yahuah\] bless thee, and keep thee: the LORD \[Yahuah\] make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: the LORD \[Yahuah\] lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace. And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them.*

PART ONE

**The Claim**

***1.1 — The Argument***

Trinitarian teachers point to the priestly blessing as a picture of three persons hidden in the Law. The name Yahuah appears three times, once in each line. Each line, they say, matches one person: the Father blesses and keeps, the Son makes His face shine and shows favor, and the Spirit lifts up the countenance and gives peace. The face that shines is tied to the Son, since the glory of Elohim is seen "in the face of Yahushua Messiah" *2 Corinthians 4:6*, and peace is tied to the Spirit.

***1.2 — The Hidden Premise***

The whole argument rests on one unstated rule: that when a name is spoken three times, three different persons must stand behind it. Nothing in the verse says so. The rule has to be brought to the text. This places the verse among the Trinity Formula texts, where a pattern of three is read as a count of persons.

PART TWO

**The Text**

***2.1 — One Name, Not Three***

The blessing does not end at verse 26. Verse 27 tells what the three lines accomplish: the priests "shall put my name upon the children of Israel." The word is singular. It is not "my names," and not "our name." Three lines place one name.

> ***shem***
>
> Name. In verse 27 it carries the first-person ending, *shemi*, "my name." One owner, one name. It is the same name Yahuah declared at the bush: "this is my name for ever" *Exodus 3:15*.

***2.2 — One Speaker***

The verse closes, "and I will bless them." Aaron's sons speak the words, but the One who blesses is a single "I." Three times the priests say the name, and one speaker claims the whole blessing as His own act. The text says "I."

***2.3 — One Face, One Countenance***

Each line keeps the same subject and the same pronoun. Yahuah "make **his** face shine." Yahuah "lift up **his** countenance." The face in line two and the countenance in line three both belong to the one named in line one. No line hands the blessing to someone else.

***2.4 — How Hebrew Uses Three***

In Hebrew, the lines grow as they go: three words in the first, five in the second, seven in the third. Repetition builds weight; it does not count persons. Jeremiah cries "O earth, earth, earth" *Jeremiah 22:29*, and there is one earth. The false prophets chanted "The temple of the LORD \[Yahuah\]" three times *Jeremiah 7:4*, and there was one temple. Yahuah says "I will overturn, overturn, overturn it" *Ezekiel 21:27*, and it is one overturning. Three in Hebrew is emphasis, and the blessing is the strongest form of one name the priests could speak.

PART THREE

**The Anchoring Witness**

***3.1 — The Name Placed on the People***

Putting Yahuah's name on Israel is a theme of the whole Law. Moses told Israel that all the nations would see they were "called by the name of the LORD \[Yahuah\]" *Deuteronomy 28:10*. The place Yahuah would choose was where He would "put his name" *Deuteronomy 12:5*. In every case it is one name, the Father's name, set on what He owns.

***3.2 — The Son Asks the Father to Keep Them***

The first line asks Yahuah to "keep thee." On the night before His death, Yahushua prayed that very request, and He prayed it to the Father: "Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me" *John 17:11*. He said He had "manifested thy name" to them *John 17:6*. The Son does not stand inside the blessing as one of its three givers. He asks the Father to do the keeping, and He asks it in the Father's name.

***3.3 — Whose Face Shines***

The shining face is the Father's. Psalm 80 prays three times to one Elohim of hosts, "cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved" *Psalm 80:3, 7, 19*. Paul's words in 2 Corinthians 4:6 say the same thing when read in full: it is Elohim, who first commanded light out of darkness, who shines in the heart "to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God" in the face of the Son. The glory is the Father's. The Son's face is where that light is seen. The source is the Father; the Son bears His light.

PART FOUR

**Reading the Verse Again**

Read with its own words given their weight, the blessing says this:

> **NUMBERS 6:24–27, READ AGAIN**
>
> *Yahuah, the Father, bless you and guard you. Yahuah, the Father, turn the light of His own face toward you and show you His favor. Yahuah, the Father, lift His countenance toward you and give you peace. So the priests shall set My one name on the children of Israel, and I, one speaker, will bless them.*

Every line names the same Yahuah. Every pronoun points back to Him. Verse 27 seals the three lines as one name and one blessing from one "I."

CONCLUSION

**The Verdict**

The priestly blessing is not a hidden count of persons. It is the Father's own name, spoken three times for weight and set on His people as a mark of ownership. The Son prays that same Father to keep them in that same name. Revelation shows where the blessing ends: the redeemed stand with the Lamb, having "his Father's name written in their foreheads" *Revelation 14:1*, and in the city they see His face, and "his name shall be in their foreheads" *Revelation 22:4*. One face. One name.

**Three lines, one name, one “I”: the blessing of Numbers 6 is the Father's name and the Father's blessing, placed on His people.**
