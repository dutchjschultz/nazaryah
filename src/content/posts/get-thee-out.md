Every calling has two sides: what a man is called to, and what he is called out of. Genesis puts the second side first.

## Where Abram Stood

Abram was born in "Ur of the Chaldees" (Genesis 11:28, 31). The Chaldeans were the people of the land of Babel, the plain of Shinar where the tower was raised (11:2). Isaiah calls Babylon "the beauty of the Chaldees' excellency" (Isaiah 13:19). Abram's family was not neutral there. Joshua says that Terah and his household "served other gods" on the far side of the river (Joshua 24:2). Yahuah called a man out of the heart of Babylon's land and out of a house of idols.

## Three Steps Out

Stephen says Elohim appeared to Abraham while he was still in Mesopotamia (Acts 7:2–3). The call came in three widening circles: "Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house" (Genesis 12:1). Country, then family, then home, each one closer than the last. Only after the leaving comes the promise: "unto a land that I will shew thee," with a great nation, a great name, and a blessing for all families of the earth (12:1–3).

## Stopping Halfway

Terah set out for Canaan with Abram, but when they reached Haran "they… dwelt there" (11:31). Terah died in Haran (11:32). Leaving Ur was not the same as reaching the land. Stephen notes that Abram moved on only after his father died (Acts 7:4), and then "Abram departed, as Yahuah had spoken unto him" (12:4). Hebrews sums up his belief in one line: he "went out, not knowing whither he went" (Hebrews 11:8).

## Looking Back

Not everyone who left kept leaving. Lot looked at the well-watered plain, chose it, and "pitched his tent toward Sodom" (13:10–12). Years later his wife, pulled out of that city, "looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt" (19:26). Yahushua gave a three-word warning about the last days: "Remember Lot's wife" (Luke 17:32).

## The Same Call Today

The apostles repeat the call in the same shape. "Come out from among them, and be ye separate" (2 Corinthians 6:17). Believers are called "out of darkness into his marvellous light" (1 Peter 2:9). The call is still to leave a country, a culture, and sometimes a household that serves other gods, and to go on without stopping at Haran or looking back at Sodom.

## Revelation

The last call in Scripture comes from heaven over the same city: "Babylon the great is fallen" (Revelation 18:2), and "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins" (Revelation 18:4). Yahuah called Abram out of the land of Babylon at the start of the Scriptures, and He calls His people out of Babylon at the end. It is one call, from Genesis to Revelation.
