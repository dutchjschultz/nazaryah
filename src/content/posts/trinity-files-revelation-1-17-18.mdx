**Revelation 1:17–18**

**Was Dead, and Alive**

*The First and the Last, defined by a grave*

— The Standing Stone —

> **Behind "LORD" in your Bible lies a hidden name — in the Hebrew it is Yahuah** *Psalm 83:18*; **Yahuah is the Father** *Isaiah 63:16*; **Yahuah is the only God, beside Him there is no other** *Isaiah 45:5*; **therefore Yahuah the Father is the only true God, leaving no room for a second or third person** *1 Corinthians 8:6*.

Reference Piece

**The Titles He Was Given**

*A title of Yahuah on the Son is worn by gift — a shared title was never a shared identity*

[Foundation: The Titles He Was Given →](/foundations/the-titles-he-was-given)

***1 — The Claim***

The risen Yahushua calls himself “the first and the last” — the title Yahuah takes for Himself in Isaiah. Since the same words name Yahuah there, the argument runs, the One who speaks them here must be Yahuah.

***2 — Read the Next Clause***

He does not stop at the title. He defines it on the spot:

> **Revelation 1:17–18**
>
> *Fear not; I am the first and the last: I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore…*

The One wearing the title here died — and that is the one thing Yahuah cannot do. On Yahuah's lips “first and last” is fenced by “beside me there is no God” *Isaiah 44:6*: a God who never began and cannot end. Here the very next breath anchors it to a grave. So the title means what the text says it means: the first to rise from the dead and die no more, “the first begotten of the dead” *Revelation 1:5*, the Last Adam who succeeded where the first fell *1 Corinthians 15:45*. Same words as Isaiah; the opposite anchor.

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

> **The title that names a God who cannot die is defined, in the same breath, by a death. That marks the Son off from the Father — it does not make him the Father.**
