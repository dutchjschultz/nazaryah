*Witness One of Five in the investigation [**The Tree in the Midst**](/investigations/the-tree-in-the-midst) — back to the case.*

## Two Trees in the Midst

When Yahuah planted the garden, He filled it with “every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food” (Genesis 2:9). Then Scripture singles out two. “The tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.”

Man was given every tree to eat from except one. “Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die” (Genesis 2:17). The tree of life was not forbidden. It stood in the midst, open, and nothing barred the way to it.

From the very first chapter of man’s life, then, Yahuah set a choice in front of him. One tree meant life. The other, taken against His word, meant death.

## A Tree With No Name

Genesis is careful with its trees, yet it never tells what kind of tree the tree of life was.

It names the fig. After they ate, Adam and Eve “sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons” (Genesis 3:7). It names gold, bdellium, and onyx in the land of Havilah nearby (Genesis 2:11-12). It names the rivers. But the tree in the midst is given a purpose and no species. It is called only by what it gives: life.

That silence is honest, and this investigation keeps it. No verse says the tree of life was an almond. What Scripture does show is something quieter and stronger. When Yahuah later built a picture of that tree for His people to see, He made it an almond, every time. The witnesses that follow trace that pattern.

## Guarded, Not Destroyed

After the fall, Yahuah said, “lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever” (Genesis 3:22). So man was sent out of the garden.

Look closely at what happened next. Yahuah did not cut the tree down. He did not burn it. He placed cherubim at the east of the garden, with a flaming sword that turned every way, “to keep the way of the tree of life” (Genesis 3:24).

The tree was still there. What was shut was the way to it.

Three details matter for everything that follows. The guard stood at the east. The guards were cherubim. And the job they were given was to keep the way. Each of those details comes back when Yahuah builds His house in the wilderness.

## The Word Keep

The word translated “keep” in Genesis 3:24 is shamar (H8104). It means to guard, to watch over, to keep watch.

It is the same word Yahuah used for Adam’s work before the fall. He put the man in the garden “to dress it and to keep it” (Genesis 2:15). Adam was the first keeper of the garden. After he failed, the cherubim took the post.

And shamar is the word Scripture uses again and again for keeping Yahuah’s commandments. “Ye shall therefore keep my statutes, and my judgments: which if a man do, he shall live in them” (Leviticus 18:5). “Keep my commandments, and live” (Proverbs 4:4; 7:2).

The same word that guarded the way to the tree of life is the word for guarding the commandments that lead to life. Keeping the Law and keeping the way to the tree were never two separate ideas in the Hebrew mind.

## A Tree Remembered

The tree of life drops out of the narrative after Genesis 3, but it does not drop out of Scripture. Proverbs keeps it alive in four places.

Wisdom “is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her” (Proverbs 3:18), and that chapter opens, “forget not my law; but let thine heart keep my commandments” (Proverbs 3:1). “The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life” (Proverbs 11:30). “When the desire cometh, it is a tree of life” (Proverbs 13:12). “A wholesome tongue is a tree of life” (Proverbs 15:4).

In each case the tree of life is tied to righteousness, to wisdom, to the words of a man’s mouth, and above all to Yahuah’s Law. The tree was out of reach, but the Law that leads back to it was placed in Israel’s hands.

## The Question This Witness Leaves

So the tree in the midst of Eden was real, it was guarded, and its kind was never named. That leaves a question for every reader: if Yahuah ever showed His people what the tree of life looked like, what tree would He choose?

The next four witnesses answer it. At Luz, in the tabernacle, in Aaron’s hand, and in Jeremiah’s vision, the answer keeps coming back the same: an almond, standing beside the word of Yahuah.

## Right There All Along

Many readers picture the tree of life as gone, lost with Eden and found again only in heaven. Genesis never says it was lost. It says the way was kept. A guarded way is a way that can be opened again, and the rest of Scripture is the story of Yahuah opening it.

## Where It Ends

The guard at the east will not stand forever. The overcomer is promised, “To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of Elohim” (Revelation 2:7). The tree that stood in the midst at the beginning stands in the midst at the end.
