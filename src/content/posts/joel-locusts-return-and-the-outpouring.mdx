## The Pattern

Joel is three chapters, and it moves in three steps. A disaster strikes, the people are called to return, and Yahuah answers with more than He took. Hold the three steps and the whole book is held.

- *The locusts* (1:1–2:11): the land is stripped, and the day of Yahuah is near.
- *The return* (2:12–27): turn with the whole heart, and Yahuah restores the years.
- *The outpouring* (2:28–3:21): the spirit on all flesh, and the nations judged.

One phrase holds it together: *the day of Yahuah* appears five times (1:15; 2:1, 11, 31; 3:14). Each time it is nearer and larger. It begins as a locust plague in Judah and ends as the judgment of every nation.

## The Setting

Joel gives no king and no date. What he gives is a farm emergency: a locust plague so complete that the grain offering and the drink offering are *cut off from the house of Yahuah* (1:9). When the field fails, the altar fails with it. Joel reads the plague as a message and calls it what it is: a forerunner of the day of Yahuah.

## 1. The Locusts (1:1–2:11)

> *That which the palmerworm hath left hath the locust eaten; and that which the locust hath left hath the cankerworm eaten; and that which the cankerworm hath left hath the caterpillar eaten.* (Joel 1:4)

**What happens:** wave after wave strips the land. Vines, fig trees, wheat and barley are gone, the priests mourn, and the cattle groan (1:10–18). In chapter 2 the locusts become an army: *Blow ye the trumpet in Zion … for the day of Yahuah cometh* (2:1).

**Watch for:** *Tell ye your children of it, and let your children tell their children* (1:3). Joel wants the disaster remembered for generations, because it is a lesson about the day still to come: *who can abide it?* (2:11).

## 2. The Return (2:12–27)

> *Turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning: and rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto Yahuah your Elohim: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness.* (Joel 2:12–13)

**What happens:** the trumpet blows again, this time for a fast and a solemn assembly (2:15). Yahuah answers: *I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten* (2:25). The threshing floors fill with wheat and the vats overflow (2:24).

**Watch for:** the rain of 2:23. The Hebrew for *the former rain moderately* is hammoreh litsdaqah, which can be read *the teacher for righteousness* — the KJV margin gives it. The rain that restores the harvest and the teaching that restores the people use the same word.

**And:** *I am Yahuah your Elohim, and none else* (2:27). One, with no second.

## 3. The Outpouring (2:28–3:21)

> *And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh … And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of Yahuah shall be delivered.* (Joel 2:28, 32)

**What happens:** the spirit falls on sons and daughters, old men and young, servants and handmaids (2:28–29). Then the nations are gathered to *the valley of Jehoshaphat* — the name means *Yahuah judges* — and judged (3:2, 12). *Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision* (3:14).

**Watch for:** the fulfillment Peter named. On Shavuot in Jerusalem, he stood up and said, *this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel* (Acts 2:16). Shavuot is the feast of *the firstfruits of wheat harvest* (Exodus 34:22). The spirit fell at the feast of the very wheat harvest Joel had promised to restore.

**And:** Joel reverses Isaiah and Micah. They said *beat their swords into plowshares* (Micah 4:3). Joel tells the nations to *beat your plowshares into swords* (3:10), arming for a battle they will lose.

## Three Pulpit Errors Joel Answers

- *Acts 2 is the birthday of a new religion.* The disciples were in Jerusalem keeping Shavuot, a commanded Torah feast (Leviticus 23:15–21). Peter explained what happened from Joel, a Hebrew prophet. The spirit came to people keeping Yahuah's calendar, on Yahuah's feast.
- *Calling on the name proves Yahushua is Yahuah* (Romans 10:13). Joel names the name: Yahuah. In the same sermon that quotes Joel, Peter says Elohim *hath made* Yahushua both master and Messiah (Acts 2:36). The One who makes is not the one made.
- *Rend your heart, not your garments means outward obedience does not matter.* The same verse commands fasting, weeping, and mourning, and the next commands a trumpet and a solemn assembly (2:12, 15). Joel wants the heart and the walk, not the heart instead of the walk.

## Where Joel Points Next

Joel ends with water: *a fountain shall come forth of the house of Yahuah* (3:18), and Yahuah *dwelleth in Zion* (3:21). Revelation finishes the picture with a pure river of the water of life flowing from the throne (Revelation 22:1), in a city where Yahuah dwells with His people (Revelation 21:3).
