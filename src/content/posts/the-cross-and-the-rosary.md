## Christianity's Own Charms

The other religions in this section sent their charms West. Christianity grew its own at home. The cross necklace is the most common religious jewelry in America, and the rosary is Christianity's string of prayer beads.

## The Cross Necklace

The earliest believers did not wear crosses. Their writings and burial art show the fish, the anchor, the shepherd, and the dove. The cross became a widespread public symbol only after the Roman emperor Constantine took it as his battle standard in the early 300s. The crucifix — the cross with the body still hanging on it — came later still. Today the cross is worn by many who keep none of Yahuah's commandments, and it is often worn the way the hamsa is worn: as protection, as luck, or as a sign of belonging.

The apostles spoke of where Yahushua died not as a symbol to wear but as a place of death. Peter said He was slain and "hanged on a tree" (Acts 5:30), and that He "bare our sins in his own body on the tree" (1 Peter 2:24). The Greek word there, *xulon*, means wood or tree. The other word used for it, *stauros*, means an upright stake. Neither word was ever turned into jewelry by the apostles.

## The Rosary

The rosary is a loop of beads used to count prayers, most of them the Hail Mary, a prayer addressed to Mary. It developed in the Middle Ages, when 150 Hail Marys were counted to match the 150 Psalms, and Pope Pius V fixed its standard form in 1569. It works exactly as the Hindu and Buddhist mala works (see *Beads on the Wrist*): one bead for each repetition.

Yahushua answered both its method and its object. Of the method: "use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do" (Matthew 6:7). Of the object: "there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Messiah Yahushua" (1 Timothy 2:5). Prayer counted on beads and sent through Mary breaks both.

## What Scripture Says of Images

The second commandment forbids making "any graven image, or any likeness" to bow down to (Exodus 20:4-5). A crucifix kissed and held in prayer, and a bead string counted toward heaven, are the same kind of object as the charms of the nations.

## The Verdict

The cross necklace and the rosary are Christianity's mala and hamsa — a symbol worn for protection and a bead string counted for merit. Neither came from the apostles. Both belong under the oak, with Jacob's earrings.

Revelation names who has a right to the tree that matters: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life" (Revelation 22:14).
