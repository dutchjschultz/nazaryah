**Alpha and Omega — Who Speaks?**

*The disputed voice of Revelation's last chapter*

— The Standing Stone —

> **Behind "LORD" in your Bible lies a hidden name — in the Hebrew it is Yahuah** *Psalm 83:18*; **Yahuah is the Father** *Isaiah 63:16*; **Yahuah is the only God, beside Him there is no other** *Isaiah 45:5*; **therefore Yahuah the Father is the only true God, leaving no room for a second or third person** *1 Corinthians 8:6*.

***1 — The Claim***

“I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last” (22:13) is read as Yahushua speaking, claiming the title Yahuah uses of Himself in 1:8 — and so claiming to be Yahuah.

***2 — Whose Voice, and Whose Title***

Three things unsettle the claim. First, the speaker here is disputed: Revelation's last chapter changes voices without warning, the Greek carries no quotation marks, and the Son names himself only three verses on — “I Jesus have sent mine angel” *Revelation 22:16*. Second, where “Alpha and Omega” stands undisputed, it is the Father's: He signs the book with it in 1:8 as “the Almighty,” and the One seated on the throne speaks it again in 21:6 *Revelation 1:8* *Revelation 21:6*. Third, the one verse that seems to weld the title plainly onto the Son — the King James “I am Alpha and Omega” of 1:11 — is a scribal addition absent from the oldest Greek manuscripts, which the modern translations drop back out.

Even granting the Son speaks in 22:13, the title lands as every title lands on him: as the beginning and the end of the new creation Yahuah is making through him — “the beginning, the firstborn from the dead” *Colossians 1:18*.

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

> **The one verse that would put “Alpha and Omega” plainly on the Son is a disputed voice resting on a copyist's line. It proves nothing the Father's own title does not already own.**
