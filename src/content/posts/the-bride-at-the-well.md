A call is not complete until it is answered. Genesis 24, the longest chapter in the book, is the story of a call carried to someone who had never seen the one calling her, and of the answer she gave.

## The Errand

Abraham, now old, sent "his eldest servant of his house, that ruled over all that he had" (24:2) to find a wife for Isaac. She was not to come from the daughters of Canaan, but from Abraham's own kindred far away (24:3–4). Isaac was not to go there himself (24:6). The son stayed home, and the servant went out to bring the bride to him.

## The Servant Who Speaks of the Son

The servant is never named in this chapter. When he finally explains his errand, he speaks only of his master and his master's son: Yahuah "hath blessed my master greatly," and "unto him hath he given all that he hath" (24:35–36). Paul saw his own work the same way. He wrote to the assembly at Corinth, "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Messiah" (2 Corinthians 11:2). Those who carry the call speak of the Son, not of themselves.

## At the Well

The servant asked Yahuah for a sign: the right woman would give him a drink and offer to water his camels too (24:14). Rebekah came, gave him water, and then drew for all ten camels until they were done drinking (24:10, 19–20). Before a word about marriage was spoken, the servant gave her a gold earring and bracelets (24:22), and later "jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment" (24:53). The gifts came before the wedding as a pledge of what was coming. Believers are likewise sealed with the spirit, "the earnest of our inheritance" (Ephesians 1:13–14).

## "I Will Go"

Her brother and mother asked for delay, "a few days, at the least ten" (24:55). The servant answered, "Hinder me not" (24:56). So they put the question to her directly: "Wilt thou go with this man? And she said, I will go" (24:58). No one could answer for her. She left her country and her father's house, as Abraham once had, for a husband she had never seen. Peter says the same of believers: "whom having not seen, ye love" (1 Peter 1:8).

## The Meeting

Isaac was out in the field at evening when the camels came into view (24:63). Rebekah covered herself with her veil, and Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, "and she became his wife; and he loved her" (24:65–67).

## Revelation

Revelation ends at the same wedding. "The marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready," dressed in fine linen, which is "the righteousness of saints" (Revelation 19:7–8). The call still goes out with an open question: "the Spirit and the bride say, Come… And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17). The bride at the well drew water for a stranger and was given a husband. The bride at the end is given the water of life.
