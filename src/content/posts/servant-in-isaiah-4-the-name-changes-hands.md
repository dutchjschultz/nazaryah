## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah means to be known. Not guessed at and not argued over, but known, by every nation on the earth.

His way of doing that has never changed. He puts somebody in place to carry it — somebody who lives it out in the open where the rest of the world can watch.

Isaiah is the record of who was holding that assignment. It changes hands twice before the book is finished, and the whole book is readable once a person knows which hand it is in.

## When This Is Happening

Chapter 49 opens the third of Isaiah's four stretches of time. The Babylon chapters are behind. This stretch runs from the work of one man, around 27 AD, forward into the age the reader is standing in.

Between the two stretches is a long gap the text does not narrate — the captives go home, and then the record goes quiet for about four hundred years.

## A New Voice

Up to this point Yahuah has been doing the talking about the servant. Here somebody else speaks, in the first person, and it is not Isaiah.

> “Listen, O isles, unto me; and hearken, ye people, from far; Yahuah hath called me from the womb; from the bowels of my mother hath he made mention of my name.”
>
> — Isaiah 49:1

Called from the womb. Named before birth. He is addressing the far-off countries, not the people standing in front of him.

## He Is Called Israel

> “And said unto me, Thou art my servant, O Israel, in whom I will be glorified.”
>
> — Isaiah 49:3

The name handed to the nation back in chapter 41 is now spoken over one person.

## And Then the Line That Settles It

> “Now, saith Yahuah that formed me from the womb to be his servant, to bring Jacob again to him … It is a light thing that thou shouldest be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob … I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles.”
>
> — Isaiah 49:5–6

Two verses after being called Israel, He is sent to bring Jacob back and raise up the tribes of Jacob.

A man cannot be his own errand. Whoever this is, He is not the nation, because the nation is His assignment.

So the title has moved. One chapter earlier it belonged to a whole people. Here it belongs to one person who is sent to go get them.

## The Job Gets Bigger, Not Smaller

The wording in verse 6 is worth reading twice. Bringing Israel back is called a light thing — too small a job on its own.

When the assignment was on a whole nation it covered one people. On one man it covers the earth. He is given for a light to the Gentiles, and for deliverance to the end of the earth.

The light is given to Him, the same as in chapter 42. It is not something He showed up holding.

## He Knows What It Looks Like

> “Then I said, I have laboured in vain, I have spent my strength for nought, and in vain: yet surely my judgment is with Yahuah, and my work with my Elohim.”
>
> — Isaiah 49:4

The one taking the assignment says out loud that it looks like nothing came of it. The verse does not hide that. It answers it in the same breath: the verdict belongs to Yahuah and not to the scoreboard.

## A Second Line of Evidence

Isaiah's four stretches of time were worked out from something that has nothing to do with the servant — a sentence repeated at the close of two stretches, and a change of tense between chapters 39 and 40.

The servant is a separate thread. Here is where the two land together.

The second stretch ends at 48:22. The third begins at 49:1. The last time the nation is called His servant is 48:20. The first time one person is called Israel is 49:3.

Two threads, found two different ways, turning at the same chapter. At the mouth of two witnesses a matter is established (Deuteronomy 19:15). The book is witnessing to its own order.

## Where It Lands

> “And the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it.”
>
> — Revelation 21:24

The light given to one man in Isaiah 49 is the light the nations are walking in on the last pages.

## In One Sentence

The name Israel is spoken over one person, and two verses later that person is sent to go bring Israel home.
