Exodus is the second step of the walk: the believer leaving the world. Leaving is not the same as becoming his own master. The slave who walks out of Egypt does not walk into nothing; he walks out of Pharaoh's service and into Yahuah's. Paul says it plainly: those who were "the servants of sin" are "made free from sin" and become "servants to Elohim" (Romans 6:17, 22). Exodus is that change of masters, told in full.

## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah is keeping His word to Abraham. When Israel cried out, "Elohim remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob" (2:24). He breaks Egypt's hold, brings His people out by the blood of a lamb, and carries them to Sinai, "on eagles' wings," to bring them "unto myself" (19:4). Then He gives them His covenant and moves in among them: "I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their Elohim" (29:45).

## The Name

"Exodus" is the Greek translators' title, from *exodos*, "the way out." Luke uses the same word on the mount of transfiguration, where Moses and Elijah spoke with Yahushua about "his decease," his *exodos*, "which he should accomplish at Jerusalem" (Luke 9:31). The book is named for Israel's way out of Egypt, and the Son's death is called by the same word.

## The Pattern

- **One demand, repeated.** "Let my people go, that they may serve me" (7:16; 8:1, 20; 9:1, 13; 10:3). The demand was never freedom for its own sake. It was freedom to serve.

- **One word, two masters.** The Hebrew *avad* means to work, to serve, and to worship. Egypt "made the children of Israel to serve with rigour" (1:13). Yahuah calls them out to serve Him. The word stays the same; the master changes.

- **Two building projects.** The book opens with Israel building the store cities Pithom and Raamses for Pharaoh (1:11) and closes with Israel building the tabernacle, which the glory of Yahuah fills (40:34).

## In Egypt (Chapters 1–12:36)

The years of bondage and the first eighty years of Moses' life (7:7), ending on the night Israel walks out.

- **Bondage** (1). A new Pharaoh fears Israel's numbers, sets taskmasters over them, and orders the Hebrew sons killed.

- **The ark on the river** (2). Moses' mother puts him in a basket the text calls a *tevah*, the same word used for Noah's ark (Genesis 6:14). Both carried a deliverer safely through the water.

- **Moses in Midian** (2:11–25). At forty Moses kills an Egyptian, flees to Midian, marries Zipporah, and keeps Jethro's flock for forty years (Acts 7:23, 30).

- **The bush** (3). Yahuah calls Moses from a fire that does not consume, gives His name, and says, "this is my name for ever" (3:15).

- **Before Pharaoh** (4–6). Aaron is given to speak for Moses (4:14–16). Pharaoh answers the first demand by taking away the straw and keeping the same count of bricks (5:7–8), and the people turn on Moses.

- **The plagues** (7–11). Blood, frogs, lice, flies, cattle disease, boils, hail, locusts, darkness, and the death of the firstborn. From the flies on, Yahuah sets "a division between my people and thy people" (8:23). The plagues judge Egypt's gods: "against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment" (12:12).

- **Pesach** (12). A lamb chosen on the tenth, killed on the fourteenth, its blood on the door. "When I see the blood, I will pass over you" (12:13).

- **For the walk:** the believer's deliverance begins with Yahuah hearing a cry and remembering His covenant (2:23–25), not with the slave's own strength.

## To Sinai (Chapters 12:37–18)

The first three months, from the night of Pesach to the arrival at Sinai in the third month (19:1).

- **The sea** (14–15). Israel walks through on dry ground, Egypt's chariots drown, and Moses and the people sing the song of the sea (15:1–18).

- **The firstborn and the pillar** (12:37–13). About six hundred thousand men go out, with a mixed multitude (12:37–38); every firstborn is set apart to Yahuah (13:2); and He leads them in a pillar of cloud by day and fire by night (13:21).

- **Bread and water** (16–17). Manna each morning, a double portion before the Sabbath, and none on the seventh day (16:22–26), all before Sinai. Water flows from the rock (17:6).

- **Amalek** (17:8–16). While Moses' hands are held up on the hill, Israel prevails, and Moses builds an altar named Yahuah-nissi, "Yahuah my banner" (17:15).

- **Jethro** (18). Moses' father-in-law hears what Yahuah has done and offers sacrifice, a man of the nations joining himself to Israel's Elohim.

- **For the walk:** the first days out of the world are a school of trust: bread each morning, water from the rock, and Yahuah fighting the enemy.

## At Sinai (Chapters 19–40)

Just under a year, until the tabernacle is raised on the first day of the first month of the second year (40:17).

- **The covenant** (19–24). Yahuah speaks the Ten Words, opening with who He is and what He has done: "which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage" (20:2). The people answer, "All that Yahuah hath said will we do" (24:7), and the covenant is sealed with blood (24:8).

- **The mountain** (19–20). Three days of preparation, thunder, and a trumpet growing louder, and a people so afraid they ask Moses to speak to them instead (20:18–19).

- **The judgments** (21–23). Laws for servants, injury, property, the poor, and the stranger, and the three yearly feasts (23:14–17).

- **The pattern** (25–31). Yahuah shows Moses the tabernacle: "let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them" (25:8). The section ends with the Sabbath as "a sign between me and the children of Israel for ever" (31:17).

- **Bezaleel** (31:1–6). The craftsman filled with the spirit of Elohim to build what Yahuah showed Moses.

- **The calf** (32–34). While Moses is on the mountain, Aaron makes a golden calf. The tablets are broken, Moses pleads for the people, and the covenant is renewed with new tablets (34).

- **Moses' face** (34:29–35). Coming down with the new tablets, Moses' face shines, and he covers it with a veil (2 Corinthians 3:13).

- **The dwelling** (35–40). The people give so freely they are told to stop (36:6–7). The tabernacle is raised, and the cloud and glory fill it (40:34).

- **For the walk:** the delivered are given a covenant and a dwelling place: the Law to walk by, and Yahuah living among them.

## What the Pulpit Gets Wrong

- **"The Law is a yoke of bondage."** The Law was given to people already set free, and it opens by saying so (20:2). The psalmist says, "I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts" (Psalm 119:45), and James calls it "the perfect law of liberty" (James 1:25).

- **"Easter is the Christian Passover."** Pesach falls on the fourteenth day of the first month (12:2, 6), "a feast by an ordinance for ever" (12:14). A Sunday festival set by Rome's own reckoning, later fixed as church rule at the Council of Nicaea in 325, is a different feast.

- **"The Sabbath began at Sinai, for Israel only."** The seventh day is kept in chapter 16, before Sinai, and the commandment ties it to creation, not to Israel (20:11).

- **"Pagan customs are fine if done for God."** Aaron built the calf and announced, "To morrow is a feast to Yahuah" (32:5). The name was right and the worship was false. Moses later warns Israel not to ask how the nations served their gods in order to do the same for Yahuah (Deuteronomy 12:30–31).

## Revelation

Revelation tells the exodus again. The last plagues return as sores, waters turned to blood, darkness, and unclean spirits "like frogs" (Revelation 16:2–13). The great city is "spiritually… called Sodom and Egypt" (Revelation 11:8). Those who come through stand by a sea of glass and sing "the song of Moses… and the song of the Lamb" (Revelation 15:2–3). And the book that ends with Yahuah's glory filling the tabernacle is answered at the end of all things: "the tabernacle of Elohim is with men, and he will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3).
