*Deuteronomy 16:19 · Deuteronomy 10:17 · 1 Samuel 8:3*

## Introduction

There is an old saying, often heard in the East, that tips and gifts are corruption. To most Western ears it sounds harsh. A gift is a kind thing. A tip is a thank-you. Where is the harm? Yet Torah draws a hard line around one kind of gift, and it gives the reason in a single verse.

> **Deuteronomy 16:19**
> *Thou shalt not wrest judgment; thou shalt not respect persons, neither take a gift: for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise, and pervert the words of the righteous.*

Three commands sit side by side: do not bend judgment, do not favor one person over another, and do not take a gift. They are not three separate sins. They are one sin seen from three sides. The gift is the tool, the favoritism is the effect, and bent judgment is the result.

## A Gift With a Hook

The Hebrew word here is *shochad* (H7810). It is not a thank-you gift. It is a gift given so that the one who takes it will lean. It does not say "thank you." It says "remember me." That is why the King James renders the same word both "gift" and "bribe."

Notice what the verse does NOT say. It does not say a gift tempts the wicked. It says a gift blinds the wise and twists the words of the righteous. This gift does not need a crooked man to work on. It works on good men. A man can take it with a clean heart and still find, later, that his eyes now look one way more easily than the other.

## Yahuah Takes No Bribe

> **Deuteronomy 10:17**
> *For Yahuah your Elohim is Elohim of elohim, and Lord of lords, a great El, a mighty, and a terrible, which regardeth not persons, nor taketh reward.*

"Reward" is the same word. Yahuah cannot be bought, and He cannot be leaned on. His judges were to be like Him. Proverbs adds the detail that gives the game away: the bribe comes "out of the bosom" (Proverbs 17:23), hidden in the folds of a garment. It lives in the dark.

## What the Gift Did to Israel

> **1 Samuel 8:3**
> *And his sons walked not in his ways, but turned aside after lucre, and took bribes, and perverted judgment.*

"Bribes" is the same word again. Samuel's sons sat as judges in Beersheba, and they did exactly what Deuteronomy 16:19 warned against: they took the gift, and judgment bent. The cost came quickly. The elders asked for a king like all the nations, and Israel set aside Yahuah as King (1 Samuel 8:7). A small corruption at the gate opened the door to a national one.

## The Wage Laws: Pay the Worker Yourself

> **Leviticus 19:13**
> *Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that is hired shall not abide with thee all night until the morning.*

> **Deuteronomy 24:15**
> *At his day thou shalt give him his hire … for he is poor, and setteth his heart upon it: lest he cry against thee unto Yahuah, and it be sin unto thee.*

The full wage, from the employer, the same day. Holding it back is called robbery. James carries the same law straight into the Apostolic writings:

> **James 5:4**
> *Behold, the hire of the labourers who have reaped down your fields, which is of you kept back by fraud, crieth: and the cries of them which have reaped are entered into the ears of Yahuah of hosts.*

## Where the Tip Fits

Here the old saying from the East begins to make sense. To be clear, Deuteronomy 16:19 is about judgment, and it does not name restaurants. But the principle it guards is wider than the courtroom, and the tipping system shows that principle broken in two places at once.

First, the wage. In much of the modern world an owner pays his server a fraction of a living wage and leaves the rest to the customer. The full wage is held back, and the stranger at the table is expected to make up the difference. That is the very thing Leviticus 19:13 calls robbery. The tip covers for it and makes it look normal.

Second, the eyes. Once a worker's living depends on what each table chooses to leave, his eyes are trained to look one way. The generous table gets the warmth; the plain family that tips small gets less. That is respect of persons, the second command in Deuteronomy 16:19, grown into a whole trade. The gift does what Torah said it would do. It blinds, and it bends, even in good people.

Think about that. A practice sold as kindness rests on a withheld wage on one side and a leaning eye on the other. That is why a man in the East could call it corruption without blinking. He was not being cold. He was seeing what Torah saw: when the reward comes from the wrong hand, the work starts to serve the reward instead of the right.

## The Clean Gift

Not every gift is a bribe. The gift Yahuah gives is the opposite in every way. It is given openly, not from the bosom. It asks nothing back, and it does not blind; it opens eyes. A bribe buys favor; Yahuah's favor cannot be bought, so it is given. The line between the two is simple: a clean gift is given to bless; the hooked gift is given to lean.

## Conclusion

Torah's rule is short. Do not bend judgment, do not favor persons, do not take the gift, because the gift blinds the wise. Samuel's sons proved it. The wage laws finish the picture: the worker is paid in full, by the one he works for, on the day he earns it.

The book of Revelation closes on the same note. The reward comes with Him, given "to every man according as his work shall be" (Revelation 22:12). No hidden gift, no favored table, no wage kept back. Every worker paid in full, by the right hand, on the right day.
