## Jeremiah in a Nutshell

Jeremiah was a priest’s son from the village of Anathoth (1:1). Yahuah called him when he was still young, and Jeremiah answered, “I am a child” (1:6). He preached for about forty years, from 627 BC until after Babylon burned Jerusalem in 586 BC, and he ended his life dragged off to Egypt by the people he had warned (43:6-7).

His job was given to him in one verse, with six action words: “to root out, and to pull down, and to destroy, and to throw down, to build, and to plant” (1:10). Four words tear down. Two words build up. That is the whole book: Yahuah tearing down what His people built on lies, and then building and planting again.

Jeremiah is long, and it is not in time order. The reason is in the book itself. The king cut Jeremiah’s scroll apart with a knife and threw it in the fire, so Jeremiah had it written again, “and there were added besides unto them many like words” (36:23, 32). Jeremiah is a collection of scrolls, not a diary.

## Lines You Already Know

Most people have quoted Jeremiah without knowing it.

- **“A den of robbers.”** When Yahushua cleared the temple, He quoted Jeremiah 7:11 (Matthew 21:13).
- **“Ye shall find rest unto your souls.”** Yahushua’s promise in Matthew 11:29 comes from Jeremiah 6:16: “ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls. But they said, We will not walk therein.” The old paths are Yahuah’s ways, His Law. Yahushua offered the same rest on the same road.
- **“Rachel weeping for her children.”** Matthew quotes Jeremiah 31:15 at the killing of the babies in Bethlehem (Matthew 2:17-18).
- **“He that glorieth, let him glory” in knowing Yahuah.** Paul quotes Jeremiah 9:24 (1 Corinthians 1:31).
- **The potter and the clay.** Paul borrows Jeremiah’s picture from the potter’s house (Jeremiah 18:6; Romans 9:21).
- **“I know the thoughts that I think toward you… to give you an expected end.”** Jeremiah 29:11 is on coffee mugs everywhere. It was written in a letter to captives in Babylon, telling them to settle in, build houses, and wait seventy years (29:5, 10). The promise was real, but it came after a long wait, not instead of one.
- **The new covenant.** “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts” (31:33). Hebrews quotes it twice (Hebrews 8:8-12; 10:16).

## What Yahuah Is Doing

One complaint runs through the whole book, and Yahuah says it again and again: “I spake unto you, rising up early and speaking, but ye heard not” (7:13; 25:3; 35:14). He kept sending prophets. They kept not listening.

So He tears down what they were trusting in instead of Him:

- **The temple.** They said, “The temple of Yahuah, the temple of Yahuah” (7:4), as if the building would protect them while they stole, lied, and worshipped Baal (7:9-10).
- **The feel-good prophets.** They cried “Peace, peace; when there is no peace” (6:14).
- **The idols.** Gods made of wood and silver that “cannot speak” and “must needs be borne” (10:5).
- **The Sabbath.** They carried their loads through the gates on the Sabbath day, and Yahuah told them to stop (17:21-22).

Then He builds and plants again. After seventy years, He brings them home (29:10). He raises up a Branch from David (23:5). And He makes a new covenant, not a new law, but the same Law moved from stone to the heart (31:31-33).

## The Seven Parts

The book marks its own breaks. Read in these seven pieces, it is easy to follow.

- **Chapter 1 — The Call.** A young man given six words: four to tear down, two to build.
- **Chapters 2–25 — The Warnings.** Sermon after sermon to Judah: come back before it is too late. This part ends with “all that is written in this book” (25:13) and is dated to the same year as the scroll the king burned (25:1; 36:1). It is most likely that scroll, written again.
- **Chapters 26–29 — The Fight with False Prophets.** Jeremiah is nearly killed for preaching in the temple (26:8). He wears a wooden yoke, and the prophet Hananiah breaks it, saying Babylon will be gone in two years. Hananiah is dead within the year (28:10-17). Jeremiah writes a letter to the captives: settle in, it will be seventy years (29).
- **Chapters 30–33 — The Book of Comfort.** Yahuah says, “Write thee all the words that I have spoken unto thee in a book” (30:2). Here is the building and planting: the people brought home, the new covenant, and Jeremiah buying a field while the city is under siege, as proof that fields would be bought again (32:15).
- **Chapters 34–45 — The Fall.** The story of the last days of Jerusalem: the scroll burned (36), Jeremiah thrown into a muddy pit and pulled out by an Ethiopian servant (38:6-13), the city taken (39), and the survivors running to Egypt against Yahuah’s word, taking Jeremiah with them (43).
- **Chapters 46–51 — The Nations.** Yahuah judges the nations around Judah, and the last and longest word is against Babylon. It closes with “Thus far are the words of Jeremiah” (51:64).
- **Chapter 52 — The Record.** The fall of Jerusalem told once more, as history, the same account found in 2 Kings 25.

## The Scribe

Jeremiah did not write the book alone. His scribe was Baruch, son of Neriah. Baruch wrote down every word as Jeremiah spoke it, read it out loud in the temple, and wrote it all again after the king burned it (36:4, 10, 32).

Baruch gets one short chapter of his own. He was tired and discouraged, and Yahuah told him, “Seekest thou great things for thyself? seek them not” (45:5). The man who wrote down the whole book was told to stay small, and his life would be spared.

There is a book called Baruch in the Greek and Catholic Bibles. It was never part of the Hebrew Scriptures. In the old count of twenty-two books, Lamentations was joined to Jeremiah as one book.

## Beyond His Day

Most of Jeremiah was spoken to Judah in his own lifetime. But a few words reach past him to the Messiah and beyond.

- **The Branch.** “I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper” (23:5).
- **The new covenant.** The Law written on the heart (31:33), which Hebrews ties to Messiah’s priesthood (Hebrews 8:6-10).
- **The nations.** “The Gentiles shall come unto thee from the ends of the earth” (16:19).
- **The ark forgotten.** “They shall say no more, The ark of the covenant of Yahuah: neither shall it come to mind” (3:16). A day was coming when the wooden box would no longer be the center.
- **Babylon’s end.** Chapters 50 and 51 are picked up by Revelation for the final fall of Babylon.

One verse here is often used to teach the Trinity. The Branch is called “YAHUAH OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS” (23:6), and the claim is that the Messiah must be Yahuah. Ten chapters later, Jeremiah gives the same name to the city of Jerusalem: “this is the name wherewith she shall be called, YAHUAH our righteousness” (33:16). The name is something carried, not a claim to be Yahuah. The city was not Yahuah, and neither was the Branch.

## The Whole Book in Seven Sentences

A young priest is sent to tear down and then to build.

He warns Judah for twenty-three years, and they will not hear.

The false prophets promise peace, and the one who broke Jeremiah’s yoke is dead within a year.

In the middle of the siege, Yahuah promises a new covenant with His Law written on the heart.

The scroll is burned, the prophet is thrown in a pit, and the city falls.

The nations are judged, and Babylon last of all.

The history is written down once more, so no one can say it did not happen.

## Where It Lands

At the end of his Babylon scroll, Jeremiah told a man named Seraiah to tie a stone to it and throw it into the Euphrates River, and say, “Thus shall Babylon sink, and shall not rise” (51:63-64).

In Revelation, an angel picks up a stone like a great millstone and throws it into the sea: “Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all” (Revelation 18:21). Just before it, a voice from heaven calls out the same words Jeremiah did: “Come out of her, my people” (Revelation 18:4; Jeremiah 51:45). Jeremiah’s last word is the last word on Babylon.
