The calling in Genesis has a backdrop. Before Yahuah calls one man, He divides the whole world. Who is called only makes sense against what He set aside, and against what He means to bring back.

## The Seventy

Genesis 10 lists the families that came from Noah's three sons, Japheth, Ham, and Shem. The Hebrew list names seventy, and it closes, "by these were the nations divided in the earth after the flood" (10:32). The dividing came at Babel, where Yahuah confused the language and "scattered them abroad upon the face of all the earth" (11:8–9).

## Two Readings of One Verse

Moses tells what happened at that dividing in Deuteronomy 32:8. The verse has come down in two forms. The copy of Deuteronomy found among the Dead Sea Scrolls reads that the Most High set the borders of the nations "according to the number of the sons of Elohim." The Greek translation reads the same idea, "the angels of Elohim." The later printed Hebrew text, which the King James follows, reads "according to the number of the children of Israel."

The very next verse decides between them: "For Yahuah's portion is his people; Jacob is the lot of his inheritance" (32:9). Israel is what Yahuah kept for Himself. Israel cannot be both the portion He held back and the number by which He handed out everyone else. The printed reading also has a problem of time. The nations were divided at Babel, generations before Jacob was born, when there were no children of Israel to number. The older reading fits the verse that follows it: the nations were handed to the sons of Elohim, and Israel was kept by Yahuah.

Moses says the same thing earlier in the book. The sun, moon, and stars are things Yahuah "hath divided unto all nations under the whole heaven," but Israel He "hath taken" out of the iron furnace "to be unto him a people of inheritance" (Deuteronomy 4:19–20).

## The Principalities

The sons of Elohim set over the nations are the principalities. Daniel shows them at work. His messenger was held back twenty-one days by "the prince of the kingdom of Persia," with "the prince of Grecia" coming next, while Michael is called "your prince," the one who stands for Daniel's people (Daniel 10:13, 20–21; 12:1). Moses names what the nations ended up worshipping under them: they "sacrificed unto devils, not to Elohim; to gods whom they knew not" (32:17). Paul calls them "principalities" and "powers" and "rulers of the darkness of this world" (Ephesians 6:12).

This was an assignment, not a council. Yahuah handed the nations over and kept His portion. He gave orders; He did not take a vote.

## The Mirror

Seventy nations went out from Babel under the principalities. Seventy souls of Jacob's house went down into Egypt (Genesis 46:27; Deuteronomy 10:22). The numbers mirror each other: the whole table of nations on one side, and on the other one small family that belonged to Yahuah alone.

## Handed Over, Not Abandoned

The handing over was never meant to be the end. The promise to Abram already reached past his own family: "in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). Paul told the men of Athens that Elohim set the bounds of every nation "that they should seek" Him, "though he be not far from every one of us" (Acts 17:26–27). The Law itself kept a door open. The stranger who joined himself to Yahuah, kept His Sabbath, and held His covenant would be brought to His holy mountain, "for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:6–7). Ezekiel goes further: the strangers living among Israel would receive an inheritance "with you among the tribes of Israel" (Ezekiel 47:22–23).

## Brought Back

Psalm 82 pronounces sentence on the rulers of the nations: "ye shall die like men," and then turns to Yahuah, "thou shalt inherit all nations" (Psalm 82:7–8). At the tree, Messiah spoiled the principalities and powers and made a show of them openly (Colossians 2:15). Then came Babel undone. At Babel one language was broken into many and the nations were scattered. At Shavuot the spirit was poured out, and men "out of every nation under heaven" each heard the apostles "speak in his own language" (Acts 2:5–6). Zephaniah had promised it: Yahuah would "turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of Yahuah" (Zephaniah 3:9).

From then on, the portion is not counted by blood from one family. James told the council at Jerusalem that Elohim visited the nations "to take out of them a people for his name" (Acts 15:14). Those who were once "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" become "fellowcitizens with the saints" (Ephesians 2:12, 19), branches grafted into the same olive tree (Romans 11:17), and "Abraham's seed" through Messiah (Galatians 3:29). The nations handed over at Babel are invited back in, one person at a time, into Yahuah's own portion.

## Revelation

John sees the portion gathered in full: "a great multitude… of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" before the throne (Revelation 7:9). The kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of Yahuah "and of his Messiah" (Revelation 11:15). The leaves of the tree of life are "for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2), and in the city "the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it" (Revelation 21:24).
