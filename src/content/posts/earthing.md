## What It Is

Earthing, also called grounding, is the practice of walking barefoot on grass, dirt, or sand — or sleeping on special mats wired to the ground — to take in what is claimed to be the earth's healing electrical charge. Followers say it reduces pain, inflammation, and stress, and improves sleep. Grounding mats, sheets, patches, and shoes are now sold widely.

## Where It Came From

Earthing does not come from any of the five religions in this section. It is modern and American. In the late 1990s Clint Ober, a retired cable-television executive, sat on a park bench in Sedona, Arizona, and reasoned that because electrical systems are grounded to the earth, the human body should be too. He began testing grounding pads on volunteers and, with cardiologist Stephen Sinatra and writer Martin Zucker, published the book *Earthing* in 2010. The studies behind it have been small and mostly funded by people who sell grounding products.

The idea sits comfortably in the New Age, which speaks of drawing energy from "Mother Earth." Sedona itself is a center of New Age belief in earth "energy vortexes."

## What Scripture Says

Scripture does bind man to the ground, but as his origin, not his power source. Yahuah "formed man of the dust of the ground" (Genesis 2:7), and the life in him came from Yahuah's own breath, not from the soil. When Moses was told to remove his shoes, it was because "the place whereon thou standest is holy ground" (Exodus 3:5) — holy because Yahuah was there, not because the ground gave off a charge.

Walking barefoot is not sin. Looking to the earth as a source of healing energy is the old error of serving "the creature more than the Creator" (Romans 1:25).

## Food and the Ground

Yahuah does put healing in what He made, and Scripture names where He put it. He gave every herb and fruit "for meat" (Genesis 1:29), and in Ezekiel's vision the trees by the river bear fruit "for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine" (Ezekiel 47:12). Food heals by plain means anyone can test: what is eaten builds and repairs the body.

Earthing makes a different claim. It says an unseen charge rises out of the ground and into bare feet. Scripture never says healing comes up from the ground, and the evidence offered for it is thin and mostly paid for by those who sell the mats. Eating fruit is using a gift Yahuah named. Seeking a force from the earth is looking to creation for something Scripture never says creation gives.

## The Verdict

Earthing is a modern New Age health claim, not a religious import from the five. It is recorded here because it shares their root: healing drawn from creation instead of from the Creator.

Revelation places the healing of the nations where it belongs: "the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2) — the tree that grows beside the river flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb (Revelation 22:1).
