## A Word Before the Study

This study covers what the KJV calls the "carnal mind" and the "spiritual mind" (Romans 8:6-7). The author holds that these "mind" words were not Paul's own. The Hebrew Scriptures have no word for a separate mind. A man thinks and chooses in his heart, and Yahuah tests "the hearts and reins," the kidneys (Psalm 7:9; Revelation 2:23).

Paul's main word here, *phronēma* (what one is set on), never appears in the Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures. Where those translators did use a "mind" word, the Hebrew underneath says heart, inward parts, or Spirit (Deuteronomy 6:5; Jeremiah 31:33; Isaiah 40:13). That translation was made in Alexandria, a city steeped in Greek philosophy, and Paul's letters were written down by scribes such as Tertius (Romans 16:22). It is far more likely that Paul spoke of the heart and the reins. This study therefore calls them what Scripture calls them: the carnal heart and the spiritual heart.

## The Test

Paul gives a test, not a feeling. What the flesh is set on "is enmity against God," and the proof is that "it is not subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be" (Romans 8:7). The dividing line between the carnal heart and the spiritual heart is the Law of Yahuah.

## The Words

"Carnal" is *sarx* (flesh). "Enmity" is *echthra* (hostility, hatred). "Subject" is *hupotassō* (to rank under, to submit). The flesh will not take its place beneath Yahuah's commandments, and Paul adds that it cannot.

The Hebrew name for that leaning is the *yetzer* (inclination, leaning) of the heart, "evil from his youth" (Genesis 8:21). Those after the flesh set themselves on the things of the flesh (Romans 8:5), and the enemies of the cross are set on "earthly things" (Philippians 3:19). This is not one bad thought; it is a settled direction of the heart.

## Two Hearts, Two Ends

Paul names where each leaning ends: set on the flesh is "death," set on the Spirit is "life and peace" (Romans 8:6).

The flesh does not merely fall short; "they that are in the flesh cannot please God" (Romans 8:8). The word "cannot" appears twice in two verses. The flesh cannot be subject to the Law, and the man in the flesh cannot please Yahuah. These are one failure seen from two sides.

Moses had set the same two roads before Israel, "life and good, and death and evil," and named the road of life as loving Yahuah and keeping "his commandments and his statutes and his judgments" (Deuteronomy 30:15-16). Paul did not invent a new choice. He repeated the one set before Israel at the Jordan.

## The Serpent's Enmity

The Greek Old Testament uses that same word *echthra* (hostility) at Genesis 3:15, where Yahuah puts "enmity" between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed. The hostility of the flesh is the serpent's old quarrel with Yahuah's word.

James uses the same word: "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (James 4:4). Yahushua showed where it starts: "out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts" (Mark 7:21). The hostility begins in the heart and comes out in the works.

## The Spiritual Heart

The spiritual heart is not a vague inner glow; it has a definite shape. If refusing the Law marks the flesh, then submitting to the Law marks the Spirit.

Paul says so three verses earlier: Messiah came so "that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit" (Romans 8:4). He had already said "the law is spiritual" (Romans 7:14).

## Which Law?

Romans 8:2 speaks of "the law of the Spirit of life" freeing a man "from the law of sin and death." Many readers take this as one law replacing another: Yahuah's Law out, a new spiritual law in. Paul has already defined his terms. The law of sin and death is "another law in my members" (Romans 7:23), the pull of the flesh, not the Torah. Of the Torah itself he wrote that "the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good" (Romans 7:12).

Romans 8:3 puts the fault where it belongs: the Law was "weak through the flesh." The Law did not fail; the flesh failed the Law. Hebrews says the same of the first covenant. Yahuah found fault "with them" (Hebrews 8:8), not with His commandments. The cure was never to remove the Law but to change the heart that receives it.

## The Natural Man

In 1 Corinthians Paul calls the man with a carnal heart the "natural man" (*psuchikos*, soul-led, natural), the man run by his own soul and senses. He "receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God," and he cannot know them (1 Corinthians 2:14), the same "cannot" as Romans 8:7. Isaiah heard Yahuah say it first: "my thoughts are not your thoughts" (Isaiah 55:8).

Paul answers by quoting Isaiah 40:13 (1 Corinthians 2:16), and Isaiah's word there is Spirit: the believer carries the Spirit of Messiah. And Messiah never acted apart from the Father: "as my Father hath taught me, I speak these things" (John 8:28). One source, the Father; one faithful bearer, the Son — not two co-equal persons sharing a throne.

## Flesh and Fruit

Galatians runs the same contrast in daily practice: "the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh" (Galatians 5:17). When Paul adds that those led by the Spirit "are not under the law" (Galatians 5:18), he means what Romans 8:1 means, not under its condemnation. Five verses later he lists the fruit of the Spirit and closes, "against such there is no law" (Galatians 5:23). That is, no commandment forbids love, patience, or self-control, so a man walking that way gives the Law nothing to accuse him of. The fruit and the Law never collide.

The first fruit named is love, and "love is the fulfilling of the law" (Romans 13:10). The Spirit does not produce a life apart from the commandments. It produces the very life the commandments describe.

## What the Pulpit Teaches Instead

Modern Christian teaching reverses the chapter. It calls Law-keeping "the flesh" and calls freedom from the Law "the Spirit." Romans 8:7 states the reverse: the heart hostile to the Law is the carnal heart. A teacher who says the commandments no longer bind a believer is describing the flesh and calling it spiritual.

John settles it for the believer: "this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous" (1 John 5:3). Only the carnal heart finds them grievous.

## The Order Matters

The chapter opens with deliverance, not Law: "no condemnation to them which are in Messiah Yahushua" (Romans 8:1), because Yahuah sent His own Son (Romans 8:3). The blood comes first. Only then does the walk follow (Romans 8:4). Law-keeping never buys entry; it is how the delivered walk.

Ezekiel foretold a new heart and a new spirit, "and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:26-27). Jeremiah says where the Law is written: "in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33; Hebrews 8:10). The promise is a Law-written heart.

## One Spirit, Not a Third Person

Romans 8:9 calls it "the Spirit of God" and "the Spirit of Messiah," and 8:11 calls it the Spirit "of him that raised up Yahushua." One Spirit, belonging to Yahuah, given through His Son. Paul never presents it as a third co-equal person.

## Closing

The spiritual heart speaks with David, "O how love I thy law" (Psalm 119:97), and with Paul, "I delight in the law of God after the inward man" (Romans 7:22).

The Father has committed all judgment to the Son (John 5:22), so in Revelation the Son carries the Father's own search: "I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts" (Revelation 2:23). What He looks for is plain. The saints "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Yahushua" (Revelation 14:12), and the heart that submits to the Law is the heart that enters the city (Revelation 22:14).
