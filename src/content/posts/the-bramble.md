*Witness Four in the investigation [**The Trees of Judges**](/investigations/the-trees-of-judges) — back to the case.*

## The Man With a Gift He Does Not Have

Jotham named the bramble himself. It was Abimelech, a man (Judges 9:16-20). The bramble, *atad*, is a thorn bush. The three good trees picture three parts of one covenant walk: entry by the blood, the walk in the Law, and the anointing of the Spirit. Each had a real gift and would not leave it. The bramble pictures the counterfeit walk. It has no oil, no fruit, and no wine, and it still wants the throne.

So the bramble offers a gift too: “put your trust in my shadow.” But the shadow is a lie. This study follows that lie: what it promises, how it is dressed, what it hides, and Who answered it.

## The Riddle Turns on Truth

Listen to how the bramble speaks: “If *in truth* ye anoint me king” (9:15). The word is *emeth*, truth. The liar opens with the word truth. Jotham throws the same word back at Shechem twice: “if ye have done truly and sincerely, in that ye have made Abimelech king” (9:16), and “If ye then have dealt truly and sincerely … rejoice ye in Abimelech” (9:19). The whole riddle comes down to one question. Was the offer true? It was not, and the next three years proved it (9:22-23).

## A Shadow It Does Not Have

As supporting evidence from the field: a thorn bush gives almost no shade, and anything that crawls under it gets torn. Scripture uses that same picture for the man who trusts men instead of Yahuah. “Cursed be the man that trusteth in man, and maketh flesh his arm … For he shall be like the heath in the desert” (Jeremiah 17:5-6). The heath is a low desert scrub, a bramble.

Isaiah watched Israel crawl under a false shadow. They went “to trust in the shadow of Egypt,” and Yahuah said, “the trust in the shadow of Egypt \[shall be\] your confusion” (Isaiah 30:2-3). He named it plainly: “we have made lies our refuge, and under falsehood have we hid ourselves,” and “the hail shall sweep away the refuge of lies” (Isaiah 28:15, 17). A shadow that is not Yahuah’s is a refuge of lies.

## The True Shadow

Shade belongs to Yahuah alone. “He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty” (Psalm 91:1). “Hide me under the shadow of thy wings” (Psalm 17:8). “Yahuah is thy keeper: Yahuah is thy shade upon thy right hand” (Psalm 121:5). The counterfeit always offers the one thing only Yahuah can give. The bramble promised refuge, and it had none.

## How the Lie Is Dressed

The lie never comes dressed as a lie. Abimelech was Gideon’s own son, a man from inside Israel. Paul warned the same: “Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also be transformed as the ministers of righteousness” (2 Corinthians 11:14-15). Yahushua said they come “in sheep’s clothing” (Matthew 7:15). The bramble does not look like a thorn bush when it makes its offer. It looks like a king who can shelter you.

Yahuah had warned Israel about this inside their own borders. The false gods they left standing would be “as thorns in your sides,” and “a snare unto you” (Judges 2:3).

## What the Shadow Hides: A Bought Throne

Under the shadow was a throne built on money. Abimelech took “threescore and ten pieces of silver out of the house of Baal-berith” and “hired vain and light persons” to follow him (Judges 9:4). Baal-berith means “lord of the covenant”. The bramble’s offer of a covenant was paid for by a counterfeit covenant god.

The Law had already said who may be king: “him … whom Yahuah thy God shall choose,” who must not “greatly multiply to himself silver,” and who must write out the Law and keep “all the words of this law” (Deuteronomy 17:15, 17-19). Abimelech was not chosen, was bought with silver, and kept no Law. The prophets saw the same thing in the pulpit: “the priests thereof teach for hire, and the prophets thereof divine for money” (Micah 3:11).

## What the Shadow Hides: Fire

The bramble’s next sentence gave the lie away. If the trees would not trust it, “let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon” (9:15). Behind the offer of shade was a threat of fire. That came true. Abimelech burned the tower of Shechem with about a thousand people inside (9:49), and the men who trusted his shadow died in his fire. Then a woman dropped a millstone on his head (9:53). “Thus God rendered the wickedness of Abimelech” (9:56).

Paul shows the same end for those who trust the lie. The lawless one comes “with all power and signs and lying wonders,” and those who received not the love of the truth are given over “that they should believe a lie: That they all might be damned” (2 Thessalonians 2:9-12). The false shadow always hides a fire.

## The Lie Grows in a Man’s Own Field

The bramble is not only the man on the throne. It can grow in any heart. In the sower’s field the thorns are “the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches,” and they choke the word until the man “becometh unfruitful” (Matthew 13:22). The word for deceitfulness is *apate*, deceit. Riches promise shelter. They are the bramble’s shadow in a man’s own life.

Yahushua drew the line: “of thorns men do not gather figs, nor of a bramble bush gather they grapes” (Luke 6:44). No grapes, no covenant entry. No figs, no walk in the Law. Only the promise of shade.

## The Sign of the Counterfeit

The bramble gives the sign of the false believer, and it is the reverse of all three good trees. He offers a shelter he does not have. He wants to rule rather than bear. And he talks about truth while he keeps no commandment. John described him exactly: “He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him” (1 John 2:4). The bramble said “If in truth,” and the truth was not in it.

The test is the same three signs. Has he come in under the blood and is he glad in it? Does he love the Law and bear its fruit? Is he full of the oil and spending it on others? “Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them” (Matthew 7:20).

## How Messiah Answered the Bramble

At the end, the people were offered the same choice Shechem had. Before them stood the true King, the one who said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:6), and who came “that I should bear witness unto the truth” (John 18:37). They chose the other shadow. “We have no king but Caesar” (John 19:15). Then the soldiers “platted a crown of thorns” and put it on His head (Matthew 27:29). The men who chose the bramble crowned the true King with it.

He wore it on purpose. Thorns came with the curse (Genesis 3:18), and Messiah was “made a curse for us” (Galatians 3:13). The lie’s crown was pressed onto the Truth, so that the curse the bramble grew from could be lifted. Isaiah saw what follows: “Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree” (Isaiah 55:13). The man who trusted the bramble can still come in under the blood.

## The Bramble at the End

Revelation shows the end of the lie. The false prophet, who “deceived them that had received the mark of the beast,” is “cast alive into a lake of fire” (Revelation 19:20). The bramble that threatened fire ends in fire. Outside the city stands “whosoever loveth and maketh a lie” (Revelation 22:15). Inside, the shadow is Yahuah’s again: “there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it” (Revelation 22:3).
