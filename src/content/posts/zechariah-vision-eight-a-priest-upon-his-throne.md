This is still the same night, the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, in the second year of Darius (1:7), early in 519 BC. It is the last of the eight visions. The night began with riders among the myrtles reporting that the whole earth sat at rest while Yahuah’s house lay waste (1:11). Every vision since has answered one question: who will build the house, and how? The last vision gives the answer a name.

## The Vision

Zechariah sees four chariots coming out from between two mountains of brass. The first chariot has red horses, the second black, the third white, and the fourth grisled and bay. The angel says they are the four spirits of the heavens, going out from standing before the Adon of all the earth. The black horses go to the north country, and Yahuah says they have quieted His spirit there. Then the visions stop, and Zechariah is given a task. He takes silver and gold from three men who have come back from Babylon, makes crowns, and sets them on the head of Joshua the high priest. Over Joshua he speaks: “Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH.” The Branch will build the temple of Yahuah, rule on His throne, and be a priest on His throne. The crowns are kept in the temple as a memorial, and people from far off will come and build.

## The Mountains of Brass

The chariots come “from between two mountains; and the mountains were mountains of brass” (6:1). Zechariah does not name the two mountains. What Scripture does give is the meaning of the metal.

Brass, or bronze, was the metal of the altar. The altar of burnt offering in the court of the tabernacle was overlaid with it (Exodus 27:1-2), and so was the laver where the priests washed (Exodus 30:18). It was the metal that stood in the fire, where sin was judged and dealt with. When Moses warned Israel what would happen if they broke the covenant, he said, “thy heaven that is over thy head shall be brass” (Deuteronomy 28:23). And when John saw the risen Messiah, “his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace” (Revelation 1:15). Brass is judgment, and it holds firm in the fire.

So the chariots come out of the place of judgment. They are not patrolling anymore. They are going out to act.

## The Horses, Again

The colors bring the reader back to the first vision. That night opened with a man on a red horse and red, speckled, and white horses behind him (1:8). Their job was to walk to and fro through the earth and report, and their report was that the whole earth “sitteth still, and is at rest” (1:11). Yahuah answered that He was “very sore displeased with the heathen that are at ease” (1:15).

Now the horses are back, but this time they are harnessed to chariots, and chariots are for war. Red, black, white, grisled and bay (6:2-3). The angel calls them “the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Adon of all the earth” (6:5). The word for spirits is the same word for winds. Four winds, four directions, sent out from the throne. The first vision found a world at ease. The last vision sends judgment into it.

## My Spirit Quieted in the North

The black horses head “into the north country,” the white follow them, the grisled go south, and the bay ask to walk through the whole earth (6:6-7). Then Yahuah says one thing about the north: “these that go toward the north country have quieted my spirit in the north country” (6:8).

The north country is Babylon. Earlier that same night Yahuah had called, “flee from the land of the north” (2:6), and Jeremiah had used the same name for Babylon many times (Jeremiah 6:22; 16:15). Babylon was the power that burned the house. In the first vision, Yahuah’s spirit was stirred against the nations at ease. In the last vision, judgment reaches Babylon and His spirit is at rest. The anger that opened the night is settled before it closes.

## Crowns From the Captives

Then Zechariah is told to do something with his hands. Three men have just come back from Babylon, Heldai, Tobijah, and Jedaiah. He is to take silver and gold from them, “and make crowns, and set them upon the head of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest” (6:10-11).

That is the strange part. Crowns belong on kings, and Joshua is a priest. In Israel the two offices were kept apart. The king came from Judah and the priest from Levi, and when King Uzziah walked into the temple to burn incense himself, he was struck with leprosy on the spot (2 Chronicles 26:16-21). Zerubbabel, the governor of David’s line, was standing right there, and it was his hands that had laid the foundation of the house (4:9). Yet the crown does not go on Zerubbabel. It goes on the priest.

## The Man Whose Name Is the Branch

Then Zechariah explains what the crowned priest stands for: “Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall build the temple of Yahuah” (6:12). The Branch was already named in the fourth vision, where Yahuah said to this same Joshua, “I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH” (3:8). Jeremiah had promised a Branch for David: “I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper” (Jeremiah 23:5). Isaiah pictured Him the same way, growing up “as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground” (Isaiah 53:2).

And look at the name of the man wearing the crown. Joshua in Hebrew is *Yehoshua*, the same name the Messiah carried, Yahushua. A high priest named Yahushua is crowned as a picture of the Branch.

Verse 13 says what the Branch will be: “he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and rule upon his throne; and he shall be a priest upon his throne.” King and priest in one man. Israel had seen that only once. Long before Levi, before there was a king in Israel, “Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high El” (Genesis 14:18). David sang of one who would come in that same order: “Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek” (Psalm 110:4). Hebrews says plainly who that is (Hebrews 7:1-3, 17). The Nazaryah study [*The Higher Priesthood*](/blog/the-higher-priesthood) follows that line from Melchizedek to the Son.

## Between Them Both

The verse ends, “and the counsel of peace shall be between them both” (6:13). Some read “both” as the two offices, king and priest, joined in one man. Others read it as Yahuah and the Branch, the One who sends and the One who is sent, working in full agreement. Either way the verse counts two. Zechariah keeps repeating who sent the messenger: “ye shall know that Yahuah of hosts hath sent me” (6:15; also 2:9, 11; 4:9). The sender and the Branch are at peace, and the Branch builds the house the sender asked for.

## A Memorial in the Temple

The crowns do not stay on Joshua. They are to be kept “for a memorial in the temple of Yahuah” (6:14). A memorial is a reminder of something to come. The returned captives would walk past those crowns every time they came into the house, a standing sign that the real Builder had not arrived yet. The stone temple Zerubbabel finished was a start. The Branch would build the one that lasts.

## They That Are Far Off

The last words of the night: “And they that are far off shall come and build in the temple of Yahuah” (6:15). The third vision had promised that many nations would be joined to Yahuah (2:11). Now the far-off are not only joined. They are given tools.

Paul wrote to believers from the nations: “ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Messiah,” and He “came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh” (Ephesians 2:13, 17). Then Paul uses Zechariah’s picture: they are “built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets,” growing “unto an holy temple,” “builded together for an habitation of Elohim through the Spirit” (Ephesians 2:20-22).

And the night ends with a condition: “And this shall come to pass, if ye will diligently obey the voice of Yahuah your Elohim” (6:15). The same “if” was spoken to Joshua in the fourth vision: “If thou wilt walk in my ways” (3:7). The far-off come in by the blood. They build by obeying.

## Why This Vision, and Why Now

The eight visions all came in one night to men rebuilding a house that had stood half-finished for years. The first vision found the world at ease and the house in ruins. The second cast out the horns that scattered the builders. The third showed a city too large for walls. The fourth cleaned the priest. The fifth showed the lamp fed by Yahuah’s own oil. The sixth and seventh carried the curse and wickedness out of the land and back to Babylon. And the eighth sends the chariots, settles the anger, and names the Builder.

The men holding trowels that night needed to know their work mattered. It did, and the house went up within four years (Ezra 6:15). But the crowns in the temple told them something more. The house they were building was a picture. The Branch, king and priest at once, would build the real one, and the far-off would come and build it with Him.

## Bringing It Home

The Branch is building now. He said, “upon this rock I will build my assembly” (Matthew 16:18), and believers are the “lively stones” going into it (1 Peter 2:5). He is not waiting for a future throne to begin. He already sits as priest on His throne, the order of Melchizedek, making peace for those who come near.

That means every believer from the nations is one of “they that are far off.” The invitation is not to sit in the house as a guest. It is to come and build. And the condition has not changed: “if ye will diligently obey the voice of Yahuah your Elohim.” The blood brings a man near. Obedience is how he builds.

## Where It Lands

Revelation shows the crowns again. The rider on the white horse, called Faithful and True, comes with “many crowns” on His head (Revelation 19:11-12), and the ones He redeemed sing that He has “made us unto our Elohim kings and priests” (Revelation 5:10). The memorial crowns of Zechariah’s night end on the head they were made for. And the house is finally finished: “Behold, the tabernacle of Elohim is with men, and he will dwell with them” (Revelation 21:3).
