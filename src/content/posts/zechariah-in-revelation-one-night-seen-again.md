Zechariah was a priest among the exiles who had come home from Babylon, rebuilding a temple that looked like nothing beside the old one (Haggai 2:3). On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, in the second year of Darius (1:7), about 519 BC, he was shown eight visions in one night. Ezekiel’s visions return in Revelation in order. Zechariah’s return scattered across the whole book, because his one night holds the whole story in small.

## Vision One: The Horses Among the Myrtles

The night opens with horses, red, speckled, and white, sent “to walk to and fro through the earth” (1:10). Their report: “all the earth sitteth still, and is at rest” (1:11). The nations that crushed Judah were at ease. The angel cries, “O Yahuah of hosts, how long wilt thou not have mercy on Jerusalem?” (1:12).

Revelation’s seals open with the same horses, white, red, black, and pale (6:2-8), and the same cry follows them. Under the altar the souls of the slain call out, “How long … holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?” (6:10). Both cries get comfort and a wait. Yahuah answered “with good words and comfortable words” (1:13). The souls were given white robes and told to “rest yet for a little season” (6:11).

## Vision Two: The Horns and the Carpenters

Four horns “have scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem,” and four carpenters come “to cast out the horns of the Gentiles” (1:19-21). A horn in Scripture is a king and his power (Daniel 7:24). Revelation is full of them: “the ten horns which thou sawest are ten kings” (17:12). They “make war with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them” (17:14). Against all those horns stands one Lamb “having seven horns” (5:6), the full power the nations could not break.

## Vision Three: The Measuring Line

A young man goes out “with a measuring line in his hand” to measure Jerusalem (2:1-2). He is stopped, because the city will be too full for walls: “I, saith Yahuah, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her” (2:5). A call follows: “Deliver thyself, O Zion, that dwellest with the daughter of Babylon” (2:7). And a promise: “many nations shall be joined to Yahuah in that day, and shall be my people: and I will dwell in the midst of thee” (2:11).

Every line comes back. A reed measures the temple (11:1), and a golden reed measures the city (21:15). The call out of Babylon rings again: “Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins” (18:4). The glory in the midst means the city needs no sun, “for the glory of Elohim did lighten it” (21:23). And the nations joined to Yahuah “walk in the light of it” (21:24), for “they shall be his people, and Elohim himself shall be with them” (21:3).

## Vision Four: The High Priest’s Garments

Joshua the high priest stands in filthy garments, with the adversary at his right hand “to resist him” (3:1). Yahuah rebukes the accuser, the filthy garments are taken away, and the word comes: “I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment” (3:4). Then: “I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH,” and “upon one stone shall be seven eyes” (3:8-9).

Revelation answers each piece. The accuser falls: “the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our Elohim day and night” (12:10). The garments change: a multitude “have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb” (7:14). The Branch is “the Root of David” (5:5), “the root and the offspring of David” (22:16). And the seven eyes rest on the Lamb, “having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of Elohim sent forth into all the earth” (5:6).

Zechariah says whose eyes they are: “the eyes of Yahuah, which run to and fro through the whole earth” (4:10). The Lamb carries them because they were given to Him: “Elohim giveth not the Spirit by measure unto him” (John 3:34). The eyes are the Father’s. The Son bears them.

## Vision Five: The Lampstand and the Two Olive Trees

A lampstand of gold with seven lamps is fed by two olive trees, the oil flowing with no human hand: “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith Yahuah of hosts” (4:6). The trees are “the two anointed ones, that stand by the Adon of the whole earth” (4:14).

Revelation quotes this one outright. Of the two witnesses: “These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the Elohim of the earth” (11:4). Seven lamps burn “before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of Elohim” (4:5), and the lampstands are the seven assemblies (1:20). The witnesses carry no army, only the word: “fire proceedeth out of their mouth” (11:5).

## Vision Six: The Flying Roll

A scroll twenty cubits by ten flies over the land, “the curse that goeth forth over the face of the whole earth” (5:3). It is written on both sides, like the tablets “written on both their sides” (Exodus 32:15). Its two charges come one from each table: the thief, and the one who swears falsely by Yahuah’s name (5:3-4). It enters the guilty man’s house and consumes it (5:4).

Revelation keeps the same Law over the same earth. Its saints “keep the commandments of Elohim” (12:17; 14:12). And the end is the roll’s own verdict: “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life … For without are … whosoever loveth and maketh a lie” (22:14-15). Then the roll’s work is finished: “there shall be no more curse” (22:3).

## Vision Seven: The Woman in the Ephah

An ephah, the measuring basket of trade, goes out, and inside it sits a woman. “This is wickedness” (5:8). A lead weight shuts her in, and two women with wings like a stork carry her off “to build it an house in the land of Shinar,” to be set “upon her own base” (5:11). Shinar is where Babel was built (Genesis 11:2).

Revelation shows the house she was given. John is carried “into the wilderness: and I saw a woman,” and on her forehead is written “MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT” (17:3, 5). She is a woman of trade, like the basket she rode in: “thy merchants were the great men of the earth” (18:23). Wickedness was moved out of the land and housed in Babylon. Revelation finishes the work: “Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen” (18:2), thrown down like a millstone, “and shall be found no more at all” (18:21).

Then the contrast. John is carried to the wilderness to see the woman of Babylon, and to “a great and high mountain” to see the bride (21:9-10). Two women, two cities.

## Vision Eight: The Chariots and the Crown

The night ends where it began, with horses. Four chariots come out between two mountains of brass, drawn by red, black, white, and grisled horses: “These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Adon of all the earth” (6:5). Then crowns are made and set on Joshua’s head, with the word: “Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH … he shall build the temple of Yahuah … and shall sit and rule upon his throne; and he shall be a priest upon his throne” (6:12-13). And “they that are far off shall come and build in the temple of Yahuah” (6:15).

In Revelation, four angels hold “the four winds of the earth” (7:1), and the Rider who comes out of heaven has “on his head … many crowns” (19:12). The priest on the throne says, “I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne” (3:21): the Son seated in the Father’s throne, given to Him. He shares it in turn, making His people “unto our Elohim kings and priests” (5:10). And the far off come in to build: “the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it” (21:24).

## Why It Matters

Zechariah’s night was given to a few thousand exiles laying the foundation of a small house in a ruined city, and it carried the whole story. Revelation is not a code to be cracked with the newspaper. It is the prophets seen again. A reader who knows Zechariah opens Revelation and finds the night waiting there, and learns how it ends: with the Branch crowned, priest and king on one throne, and the nations walking in the light.
