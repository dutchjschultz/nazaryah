## The Pattern

Micah is seven chapters in three rounds. Each round opens with the same command — *Hear* (shim'u) — and each moves from judgment to hope. Hold the three rounds and the whole book is held.

- *Round one* (chapters 1–2): *Hear, all ye people* (1:2). Judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem; hope in 2:12–13.
- *Round two* (chapters 3–5): *Hear, I pray you, O heads of Jacob* (3:1). Judgment on rulers, priests and prophets; hope in chapters 4–5.
- *Round three* (chapters 6–7): *Hear ye now what Yahuah saith* (6:1). Yahuah takes His people to court; hope in 7:7–20.

The book carries one more pattern in its last lines. Micah's name means *Who is like Yah?* The book ends: *Who is a El like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity?* (7:18). The prophet's name becomes the closing question, and the answer is the whole book.

## The Setting

Micah prophesied under Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah (1:1), the same years as Isaiah. He came from Moresheth, a farm town, and his anger is aimed at the people in power: the rich who seize land, the judges who take bribes, and the prophets who preach for pay. A century later his words saved Jeremiah's life, when elders cited him as a prophet Hezekiah had heeded (Jeremiah 26:17–19).

## Round One: Hear, All Ye People (1–2)

**The charge:** the sin of the capitals. *What is the transgression of Jacob? is it not Samaria?* (1:5). The powerful *covet fields, and take them by violence* (2:2), and tell the true prophets, *Prophesy ye not* (2:6).

**The hope:** *I will surely assemble, O Jacob, all of thee … The breaker is come up before them … and Yahuah on the head of them* (2:12–13). A shepherd gathers the flock, and Yahuah goes at its head.

**Watch for:** the people want a different preacher: *If a man … do lie, saying, I will prophesy unto thee of wine and of strong drink; he shall even be the prophet of this people* (2:11).

## Round Two: Hear, O Heads of Jacob (3–5)

**The charge:** *The heads thereof judge for reward, and the priests thereof teach for hire, and the prophets thereof divine for money: yet will they lean upon Yahuah, and say, Is not Yahuah among us?* (3:11). So *Zion for your sake shall be plowed as a field* (3:12).

**The hope:** *The law shall go forth of Zion, and the word of Yahuah from Jerusalem … they shall beat their swords into plowshares* (4:2–3). Isaiah has the same oracle almost word for word (Isaiah 2:2–4). Then a ruler from Bethlehem (5:2).

> *But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.* (Micah 5:2)

**Watch for:** this verse is a standing Trinity proof text, and Micah answers it himself. The ruler comes forth *unto me* — two parties, Yahuah and the one sent to Him. *From of old, from everlasting* is miqqedem mimei olam, *from ancient days*, and Micah uses the same words in chapter 7 for Israel's past: *as in the days of old* (7:14, yemei olam) and *from the days of old* (7:20, yemei qedem). His origins run back to the ancient promise, in David's town. Two verses later the ruler *shall stand and feed in the strength of Yahuah, in the majesty of the name of Yahuah his Elohim* (5:4). The ruler has an Elohim, so he is not that Elohim.

## Round Three: Hear Ye Now (6–7)

**The charge:** *Yahuah hath a controversy with his people* (6:2), and He opens with a question: *O my people, what have I done unto thee?* (6:3). The people ask what would satisfy Him: thousands of rams, rivers of oil, *my firstborn for my transgression?* (6:7).

> *He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth Yahuah require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy Elohim?* (Micah 6:8)

**The hope:** *I will look unto Yahuah; I will wait for the Elohim of my salvation* (7:7). The book closes on pardon: *he retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy … thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea* (7:18–19).

**Watch for:** *He hath shewed thee* — shown already, and where. Micah 6:8 echoes Deuteronomy 10:12–13: *what doth Yahuah thy Elohim require of thee, but to fear … to walk in all his ways … to keep the commandments of Yahuah.*

## Three Pulpit Errors Micah Answers

- *Micah 5:2 proves an eternal Son.* The ruler comes forth *unto* Yahuah, from *ancient days* as Micah himself uses the words, and serves *Yahuah his Elohim* (5:4).
- *Micah 6:8 replaces the commandments with being kind.* *Do justly* is mishpat, the judgments of Torah, and the verse restates Deuteronomy 10:12–13, which ends in keeping the commandments.
- *The law was only for Israel.* In the last days *the law shall go forth of Zion* and the nations come saying *he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths* (4:2). The apostolic writings place the last days from the first coming onward (Acts 2:17; Hebrews 1:2).

## Where Micah Points Next

Micah ends with sins thrown *into the depths of the sea* (7:19). Revelation ends with the sea itself gone: *and there was no more sea* (Revelation 21:1). And the question in Micah's name gets its answer from the redeemed: *who shall not fear thee, O Yahuah, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy* (Revelation 15:4).
