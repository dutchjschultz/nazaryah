Christianity is the only one of the five major world religions whose symbol is an instrument of execution. To those who carry it, that is the whole point. The cross stands for the event their belief rests on: Yahushua put to death, and raised from the dead on the third day.

## The Reasons Christians Give

Christians point first to the words of Paul. He would glory in nothing "save in the cross" (Galatians 6:14). He called his message "the preaching of the cross" and said it is the power of Yahuah to them that are saved (1 Corinthians 1:18). He resolved to know nothing among the Corinthians but Yahushua the Messiah "and him crucified" (1 Corinthians 2:2). Yahushua told His followers to take up the cross and follow Him (Matthew 16:24). To most Christians that settles the matter: a word the apostles used so often must be the right symbol to carry.

Three meanings are read into it. The first is atonement, the place where sin was dealt with, where the handwriting of ordinances was nailed "to his cross" (Colossians 2:14). The second is love, the length to which the Father went in giving His Son (John 3:16). The third is triumph over death. Protestants prefer the empty cross because, they say, He is no longer on it. Roman Catholics and the Orthodox keep the body on it, the crucifix, so the suffering is never forgotten.

## How It Became the Symbol

The cross was not the first Christian symbol. The earliest believers drew the fish, the anchor, and the shepherd carrying a lamb. Death on a stake was still a living Roman punishment, and the image carried deep shame.

The turn came with the emperor Constantine. His biographer Eusebius reports that before the battle at the Milvian Bridge in AD 312, Constantine saw a sign of light in the sky with the words "conquer by this." His army marched under a standard bearing the Chi-Rho, the first two Greek letters of *Christos*. Once the empire took up the religion, the cross moved onto coins, churches, and banners. The oldest surviving carved scene of the crucifixion, on the wooden doors of the church of Santa Sabina in Rome, dates to about AD 430.

From there it spread everywhere: atop steeples, around necks, traced by hand across the chest in prayer, and carried into battle by the Crusaders, whose very name comes from the Latin *crux*, cross.

## What It Means to Those Who Wear It

For the believing Christian, the cross is a confession worn in public. It says: I belong to the One who died for me. Most who wear it do so sincerely, with no thought of any meaning beyond that, and that sincerity is the reason the symbol has lasted.
