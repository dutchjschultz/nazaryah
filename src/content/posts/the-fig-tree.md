*Witness Two in the investigation [**The Trees of Judges**](/investigations/the-trees-of-judges) — back to the case.*

## The Man Under the Fig Tree

When Yahushua first met Nathanael, He said, “when thou wast under the fig tree, I saw thee,” and called him “an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile” (John 1:47-48). The true covenant man was found under the fig tree. Of the three good trees, the fig pictures one part of his walk: keeping the Law. The vine shows how he enters. The olive shows what Yahuah pours into him. The fig shows what his days produce once he is in. The fig also bears the longest of the three. As supporting evidence from the land, its early figs show with the leaves in spring, and its main crop comes in late summer, after the vine’s first grapes. The walk in the Law is the longest part of a believer’s life.

Jotham’s fig named two things it would not give up: “my sweetness, and my good fruit.” The sweetness is what the Law tastes like to the man who loves it. The fruit is what the Law looks like when he keeps it. This study follows those two gifts through the man’s walk.

## The Sweetness: A Tree That Made the Water Sweet

The word for sweetness is *motheq*, from *mathaq*, to be sweet. Its first appearance in Israel’s walk is at Marah. Three days out of the Red Sea the people found water they could not drink, because it was bitter. “And Yahuah shewed him a tree, which when he had cast into the waters, the waters were made sweet” (Exodus 15:25). In the very same verse: “there he made for them a statute and an ordinance, and there he proved them.”

Then Yahuah said what the sweetness meant: “If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of Yahuah thy God, and wilt do that which is right in his sight, and wilt give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none of these diseases upon thee” (Exodus 15:26). A tree made the bitter sweet, and the first statutes of the wilderness were given at the sweet water. That is the fig’s sweetness: the Law turning a bitter road sweet.

## The Sweetness Is Tasted by the Man Who Loves It

David named every part of the Law and then used the fig’s word for it. “The law of Yahuah is perfect … the testimony … the statutes … the commandment … the judgments of Yahuah are true and righteous altogether … sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb” (Psalm 19:7-10). The word for sweeter is *mathoq*, the same root.

A man only tastes that sweetness if he takes the Law in. Ezekiel ate the scroll, “and it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness” (Ezekiel 3:3). John ate the little book, and “it was in my mouth sweet as honey” (Revelation 10:10). The man under the fig tree says, “O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day” (Psalm 119:97). He does not keep the Law the way a man pays a fine. He keeps it because it is sweet to him.

## When Men Call the Sweet Bitter

Isaiah warned of teachers who swap the taste: “Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil … that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!” (Isaiah 5:20). Much modern teaching does exactly this. It calls Yahuah’s Law a bitter yoke of bondage, and it calls freedom from the commandments sweet. David said the opposite: the commandments are sweeter than honey. The man who has been taught that the Law is bitter has never tasted the fig.

Jeremiah shows where that teaching ends. The people said, “the law of Yahuah is with us,” but “the pen of the scribes is in vain” (Jeremiah 8:8). The sentence followed: “there shall be no grapes on the vine, nor figs on the fig tree” (8:13). Bend the Law, and the figs disappear.

## The Good Fruit: The Law Kept

The second gift is “good fruit,” *tenuvah*, the yield of the tree. Moses said what that yield is: “it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments” (Deuteronomy 6:25). Proverbs puts the fig and the Law under the same verb. “Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof” (Proverbs 27:18), and “Whoso keepeth the law is a wise son” (28:7). The word in both is *natsar*, to guard and keep. Keeping the fig and keeping the Law are one keeping.

Yahushua said the same to the men who had entered: “If ye love me, keep my commandments” (John 14:15), and “Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit” (John 15:8). Sweetness and fruit, love and keeping, belong together.

## Always the Vine First

The fig does not stand alone. Scripture names it with the vine, and the vine comes first. Moses gave the commandments (Deuteronomy 8:1, 6) to a people already brought out by the blood of the lamb, and then promised “a land of … vines, and fig trees” (8:8). Micah says the Law goes out of Zion and men walk in it, and then they sit “every man under his vine and under his fig tree” (Micah 4:2, 4). Zechariah hears that the Branch will “remove the iniquity of that land in one day,” and then men sit “under the vine and under the fig tree” (Zechariah 3:9-10). Blood first, then the Law. The fig walk is the walk of a man already under the blood.

## Why the Fig Would Not Trade

To take the throne, the fig would have to leave its sweetness and fruit and go “to be promoted,” *nua*, to sway over the trees (Judges 9:11). Yahushua named men who made that trade. “The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat … they say, and do not. For they bind heavy burdens … on men’s shoulders” (Matthew 23:2-4). They took the seat of the Law and left the fruit of it. They “omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith” (23:23). They gave up the sweetness too: to them the Law was a burden to lay on others. The fig would not do that. It would rather bear than rule.

## Leaves Without Fruit

In Eden, Adam broke the first commandment and covered himself with fig leaves (Genesis 3:7, 11). Leaves without fruit is the oldest disguise. Yahushua found a fig tree in full leaf with “nothing but leaves” (Mark 11:13) and cursed it. He had already said who that tree is: “Not every one that saith unto me, Master, Master … but he that doeth the will of my Father” (Matthew 7:21). The leafy and fruitless will hear, “depart from me, ye that work iniquity” (7:23), and the word is *anomia*, lawlessness. The man with leaves and no fruit has the look of a believer and none of the Law.

Two modern teachings fall here. One says a man may be saved and bear no fruit at all; the fig tree in Mark 11 was not excused, it was cursed. The other says the fig tree of Matthew 24:32 is the modern state of Israel, budding in 1948. Scripture never judges a fig by its borders. It judges by sweetness and fruit.

## The Sign of a True Believer

The fig gives the second sign of a true believer: the Law is sweet to him, and his life shows its fruit. John says it plainly: “this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous” (1 John 5:3). Not grievous means not bitter. The man who has tasted the fig does not call the Law a burden.

The fruit is the proof. “He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him” (1 John 2:4). Leaves can be grown by anyone. Sweetness for the Law and the fruit of keeping it cannot be faked for long. That is the sign of the fig.

## How Messiah Walked It

Messiah is the fig walk in full. He tasted the sweetness: “I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart” (Psalm 40:8), words Hebrews puts in His mouth (Hebrews 10:7). The Law was food to Him: “My meat is to do the will of him that sent me” (John 4:34). And He bore the fruit with no leaves and no gaps: “I have kept my Father’s commandments” (John 15:10), and “Which of you convinceth me of sin?” (John 8:46).

Like the fig, He would not leave the fruit for a throne. When the crowd was about to “take him by force, to make him a king, he departed again into a mountain himself alone” (John 6:15). The scribes took Moses’ seat and left the fruit. Messiah left the seat and kept the fruit.

## The Fig at the End

Revelation shows the shaking, when untimely figs fall “when she is shaken of a mighty wind” (Revelation 6:13). Unripe fruit does not hold. Then it shows the men whose fruit held: “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life” (Revelation 22:14). The man who kept the Law and found it sweet eats at last from the tree that yields “her fruit every month” (22:2).
