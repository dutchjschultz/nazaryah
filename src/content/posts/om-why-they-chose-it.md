The symbol most often used for Hinduism is the written form of a sound: Om, also spelled Aum. It is drawn in Sanskrit script and appears on temple walls, household shrines, and the opening lines of sacred texts. Unlike the other four symbols, it is not a picture of anything. It is a syllable, spoken aloud at the beginning and end of prayers, recitations, and meditation.

## Where It Comes From

Om appears in the oldest Hindu scriptures, the Vedas, as a sacred utterance chanted at the start of recitation. The later Upanishads made it the center of their teaching. The Mandukya Upanishad, only twelve verses long, opens by declaring that all that was, is, and will be is Om, and whatever lies beyond time is Om as well. It divides the syllable into three sounds, A, U, and M, and ties them to waking, dreaming, and deep sleep, with the silence after the sound standing for a fourth state beyond them all.

In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna says he is the syllable Om in all the Vedas (Gita 7:8). He teaches that whoever leaves this life uttering Om and remembering him reaches the highest goal (Gita 8:13).

## The Reasons Hindus Give

Hindus hold that Om is the first sound, the vibration from which the universe came forth and which still runs beneath all things. It stands for *Brahman*, the ultimate reality that Hindu teaching holds to be the ground of everything. Hindu teaching also holds that the deepest self in every person, the *atman*, is one with that reality. To chant Om is to tune oneself to the sound of creation and to the divine within.

Later Hindu writings tie the three sounds to the three great gods: A to Brahma the creator, U to Vishnu the preserver, and M to Shiva the destroyer. In that reading, one syllable holds all three in a single sound.

## What It Means to Those Who Chant It

For the Hindu, Om is a prayer in a single breath: the opening of worship, the sound of the sacred, the way to quiet the mind. Many in the West know it from yoga classes, where it is chanted with little thought of its meaning. The companion study, The Om: The Error It Carries, weighs the symbol against the Word.
