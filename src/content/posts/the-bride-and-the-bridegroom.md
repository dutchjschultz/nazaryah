## A Marriage Older Than the Gospels

Long before the Gospels were written, the prophets described Yahuah’s covenant with Israel as a marriage. "Thy Maker is thine husband; Yahuah of hosts is his name" (Isaiah 54:5). "Turn, O backsliding children, saith Yahuah; for I am married unto you" (Jeremiah 3:14). Of the covenant at Sinai He said, "I was an husband unto them" (Jeremiah 31:32).

The New Testament speaks the same language — bridegroom, bride, betrothal, wedding feast — and a Hebrew hearer knew every step of it from the Torah. A Greek reader did not. Every custom below is drawn from the Torah and the Prophets themselves.

## Betrothal Is Already Marriage

In Israel, a betrothed woman was already counted a wife. The Torah calls a betrothed virgin "her neighbour’s wife" (Deuteronomy 22:23–24). That is why Matthew calls Joseph Mary’s "husband" before they came together, and why ending the betrothal required a divorce:

> **MATTHEW 1:19 — KJV**
>
> *Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily.*

A Greek reader sees a broken engagement. A Hebrew reader sees a covenant already sealed, waiting only for the bride to be brought home.

## The Bride Price

A bridegroom paid a price for his bride (Genesis 34:12; Exodus 22:16). The apostles reach for exactly that picture:

> **1 CORINTHIANS 6:20 — KJV**
>
> *For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.*

Peter names the price: "not… with corruptible things, as silver and gold… but with the precious blood of Messiah, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot" (1 Peter 1:18–19). Yahuah paid the price; the price was the Lamb He provided.

## "I Go to Prepare a Place"

After the betrothal, the bridegroom went away to prepare a dwelling, then returned to bring the bride into his father’s house. Isaac brought Rebekah into his mother’s tent, and she became his wife (Genesis 24:67). The words Yahushua spoke the night before His death are a bridegroom’s words:

> **JOHN 14:2–3 — KJV**
>
> *In my Father’s house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself.*

## The Friend of the Bridegroom

A bridegroom had a companion who stood with him through the wedding (Judges 14:20). John the Immerser took that role and said so:

> **JOHN 3:29 — KJV**
>
> *He that hath the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, which standeth and heareth him, rejoiceth greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice.*

## The Feast, the Absence, and the Lamps

- **The seven-day feast.** Israel’s weddings lasted a week (Genesis 29:27; Judges 14:12). The first sign at Cana was given at a wedding feast, when the wine ran out (John 2:1–11).

- **The bridegroom taken away.** "Can the children of the bridechamber mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them? but the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken from them, and then shall they fast" (Matthew 9:15).

- **The bridegroom at night.** The ten virgins wait with lamps, and the cry comes at midnight: "Behold, the bridegroom cometh" (Matthew 25:6). Only those with oil go in.

- **The wedding garment.** The guest without one is cast out (Matthew 22:11–13). Isaiah had already named the garment: "he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself… and as a bride adorneth herself" (Isaiah 61:10).

## The Marriage Completed

Paul writes as the friend who arranged the match: "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Messiah" (2 Corinthians 11:2). The betrothal promised in Hosea is the betrothal Paul is guarding.

Revelation finishes the story the prophets began. The bride has made herself ready, and her garment is named:

> **REVELATION 19:7–8 — KJV**
>
> *Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready. And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints.*

Then the bride comes down: "the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" (Revelation 21:2). From Hosea to Revelation, it is one marriage — and only a Hebrew reader hears all of it.

*One of six witnesses in a single case. **The Bride and the Bridegroom** is the testimony of Israel’s marriage — the betrothal, the price, the prepared place, and the feast that run from the prophets to Revelation.
