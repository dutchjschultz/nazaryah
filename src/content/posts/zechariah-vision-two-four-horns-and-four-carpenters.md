The second vision comes on the same night as the first (1:7). Zechariah has just heard Yahuah promise that His house will be built and that He is angry with the nations at ease. Now he lifts up his eyes and sees what stands in the way, and what Yahuah sends against it. The whole vision is four verses long (1:18-21), and every piece of it means something.

## The Vision

Zechariah looks up and sees four horns. He asks what they are, and the angel tells him these are the horns that scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem. Then Yahuah shows him four carpenters. Zechariah asks what they have come to do. The answer: they have come to terrify the horns and cast them out, the same horns that pressed Judah down until no man could lift up his head.

## The Horns

Zechariah sees four horns (1:18). A horn, *qeren* in Hebrew, is the strength of an animal, the part it fights with and lifts up high. In Scripture it stands for power, and most often for the power of a king or a kingdom. Daniel is told plainly that “the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings” (Daniel 7:24), and that the ram’s two horns “are the kings of Media and Persia” (Daniel 8:20).

A horn can be good or evil, and the difference is who lifts it. Yahuah raised up “an horn of salvation” in the house of David (Luke 1:69). But a horn lifted up by a man against Yahuah is pride. “Lift not up your horn on high,” says the psalm, and Yahuah answers, “All the horns of the wicked also will I cut off; but the horns of the righteous shall be exalted” (Psalm 75:5, 10). The horns in this vision are the proud kind. The angel says they are the nations “which lifted up their horn over the land of Judah to scatter it” (1:21).

## Why Four

Zechariah does not name the four. What he gives is the job they did: “These are the horns which have scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem” (1:19). The verb looks back. These are powers that already did the scattering, from the north, the south, the east, and the west.

Four is the number of every direction. A few verses later Yahuah says He spread His people abroad “as the four winds of the heaven” (2:6), and the chariots of the last vision go out as the four spirits of the heavens (6:5). Four horns means the people were pushed from every side until there was nowhere left to stand. Daniel saw the same kind of line, four kingdoms one after another (Daniel 7:3-7). Whichever way the four are counted, the point is the same. No direction was safe.

Notice also who was scattered: Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem. Not one tribe. The whole house. The horns came for all of it.

## No Man Lifted His Head

The angel describes what the horns did to the people: they scattered Judah “so that no man did lift up his head” (1:21). That is a people pressed down so long they stopped looking up. The horns were lifted high, and every head under them was bowed low.

David knew the opposite. “Thou, O Yahuah, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head” (Psalm 3:3). The horns push heads down. Yahuah lifts them up.

## The Carpenters

Then Yahuah shows Zechariah four carpenters (1:20). The Hebrew word is *charash*, a craftsman, a man who works with his hands in wood, stone, or metal. The King James renders it carpenter, smith, engraver, craftsman, and workman, depending on the job. It is a builder’s word.

It is the word for the workers who built Yahuah’s house. Bezaleel and his helpers were filled with wisdom to do “all manner of work, of the engraver” for the tabernacle (Exodus 35:35). Hiram sent “carpenters” to build David’s house (2 Samuel 5:11). And when the captives came home, they “gave money also unto the masons, and to the carpenters” to rebuild the temple (Ezra 3:7).

It is also the word for the men Babylon took. When Nebuchadnezzar emptied Jerusalem, he carried away “all the craftsmen and smiths: none remained, save the poorest sort of the people” (2 Kings 24:14). Babylon took the builders so the city could not rise again. In this vision Yahuah sends builders back.

One more link. When the Greek translators of the Old Testament came to this verse, they rendered the carpenters as *tektones*. That is the same word the people of Nazareth used of Yahushua: “Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?” (Mark 6:3). The One who would build Yahuah’s house came as a builder.

## To Fray Them

Zechariah asks the obvious question: “What come these to do?” The answer: “these are come to fray them, to cast out the horns of the Gentiles” (1:21). To fray, *charad* in Hebrew, is to make tremble, to terrify. The horns that made Judah afraid are now made afraid themselves. They are not just broken. They are cast out.

Yahuah does not answer the horns with bigger horns. He does not send an army. He sends craftsmen. The answer to the powers that tore His house down is men who build it back up.

## It Happened That Year

This was not only a picture. The enemies around Jerusalem had “weakened the hands of the people of Judah, and troubled them in building” (Ezra 4:4), and the work stopped. “Then ceased the work of the house of Elohim … unto the second year of the reign of Darius” (Ezra 4:24). That is the very year of this vision. The next chapter says Haggai and Zechariah began to prophesy, the builders rose up and began again, “and with them were the prophets of Elohim helping them” (Ezra 5:1-2). The horns had stopped the work. The prophets and the craftsmen started it again.

## Bringing It Home

The believer is the house now, as the first vision showed (1 Corinthians 3:16; Hebrews 3:6). And the house still has horns lifted against it. Some come from outside: a world that mocks the commandments, a pulpit that calls the Law a burden, the pressure to keep quiet and fit in. Some come from inside: old habits, fear, and plain discouragement that makes a man stop building and stop looking up.

Yahuah’s answer has not changed. He sends builders. The Messiah is the master craftsman, and He said, “upon this rock I will build my assembly” (Matthew 16:18). Paul called himself “a wise masterbuilder” laying the foundation, and warned every man to “take heed how he buildeth thereupon” (1 Corinthians 3:10). The gifts given to the assembly are “for the edifying,” the building up, “of the body” (Ephesians 4:12). The tools are His commandments, and the building goes on one stone at a time in the daily walk.

So the believer has two things to watch. First, do not let the horns stop the work. The captives let their enemies shut the building down for years, and Yahuah had to send prophets to start them again. Second, do not grow a horn of your own. The horns were cast out because they lifted themselves up. “Humble yourselves in the sight of Yahuah, and he shall lift you up” (James 4:10). The proud horn is cut off. The bowed head is lifted.

Yahushua told His followers what to do when the pressure comes: “look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh” (Luke 21:28). The people under the horns could not lift their heads. The people under the Builder can.

## Where It Lands

Revelation is full of horns: the beast with ten horns rises from the sea (Revelation 13:1), and the ten horns make war with the Lamb. “And the Lamb shall overcome them” (Revelation 17:14). Only one horn is left standing at the end, and it belongs to the Lamb, who stands before the throne “having seven horns” (Revelation 5:6), all the strength that Yahuah gave Him.
