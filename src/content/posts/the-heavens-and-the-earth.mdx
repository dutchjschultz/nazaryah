<div class="cz-intro">

Genesis 1 is not a psalm and not a vision. It is an account of what was made and in what order, written in the same flat voice Scripture uses for the flood, for Sinai, and for the empty tomb. The earth stands on the first day. Grass and fruit tree come on the third. Only on the fourth are the lights made and set in the firmament. A man who decides that account is mistaken has not set aside one chapter. He has established that the plain voice of Scripture can be overruled by another authority, and once that is established there is nothing left to protect the resurrection or the promises from the same treatment.

This section does not teach that a man is lost over his picture of the sky. Deliverance came through the blood of the Messiah, and no passage anywhere conditions it on a model of the heavens. But Scripture does tie a man's standing to two things this subject goes straight at: what he trusts, and what he bows to. Israel was warned by name against being drawn away to serve the sun, the moon, and the whole host of heaven, and Judah burned incense to them anyway and was judged under those same lights. Paul names the exchange in Romans without softening it: the creature worshipped and served in the place of the One who made it.

So the question is not geography. A cosmology that takes the throne out of the heavens and puts man on an accident in an endless void is not a neutral map of anything. It is the groundwork for serving the creation instead of its Maker, which is the oldest worship there is and the one Scripture never treats as harmless. What follows is the text first, in its own order and weight, and then the record of who changed the reading and what reasons they gave. Where Scripture does not settle a thing, this section says so plainly rather than filling the gap.

### What This Page Argues

*One question sits under all of it: whose account of the heavens is true?*

Scripture makes plain statements about the making and ordering of the heavens and the earth. It gives a sequence, a structure, and a set of purposes, and it gives them in the same declarative voice it uses for the flood, for the exodus, and for the resurrection. A man cannot hold those statements as mistaken and still hold Scripture as his authority, because the ground for trusting what it says about anything else is the same ground.

This page does not argue that a man is lost for holding a wrong picture of the heavens. Deliverance came through the blood of the Messiah, and no passage conditions it on a model of the sky. What Scripture does tie to a man's standing is where his trust rests and what he bows to. Israel was warned off the sun, the moon, and the whole host of heaven by name, and the exchange is named plainly in Romans: the creature served in the place of the Creator.

So the lie is not aimed at a reader's geography. It is aimed at his confidence in the word of Yahuah, and at the object of his worship. That is why this page exists, and it is the standard every card below is held to: the text first, the record second, and no claim made that the text does not make.

</StudyCard>

The usual answer to this subject is that it changes nothing. A man's picture of the sky does not feed his family, and it is not what he will be asked about. That answer holds only if the question is geography, and it is not. Three things are actually at stake, and each has a reading here. The first is what governs: if the plainest chapter in Scripture can be overruled, the principle that protected the rest of it is gone. The second is what a man serves, because the host of heaven has been drawing worship since Babel and has never stopped. The third is the language Scripture uses for Yahuah Himself, which assumes a settled above and a settled below.

### The Hinge

*If Genesis 1 is wrong where it is plainest, what is left standing?*

Genesis 1 is not a psalm and not a vision. It is an ordered account: this was made, then this, and the evening and the morning were a numbered day. A reader who decides that the sequence is mistaken has not trimmed one verse. He has established that the plain voice of Scripture can be overruled by another authority, and once that is established there is no principle left that protects the resurrection, the flood, or the promises. This is why the subject cannot be waved off as harmless. It is not a quarrel about the sky. It is a question about what governs.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-host-of-heaven" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Host of Heaven

*Every nation that left Babel carried the same worship with it.*

Israel was warned before entering the land: when a man lifts his eyes and sees the sun, the moon, and the stars, he is not to be drawn away to serve them. The warning was not hypothetical. Judah burned incense to the sun, to the moon, and to the host of heaven, and the judgment fell under those same lights. Paul names the mechanism itself in Romans 1: the creature worshipped and served more than the One who made it. A cosmology that fills the sky with ungoverned powers and empties it of a throne is not neutral ground; it is the old worship with the names changed.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-most-high" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Most High

*A title with no meaning in a void.*

Scripture speaks of Yahuah as the Most High and of His ways as higher than ours, and it does so without apology or metaphorical hedging. Heaven is His throne, the earth His footstool, and Yahushua repeats that language without adjustment. In a model with no fixed above, none of it means anything: there is no high, no footstool, and no direction for the Son to have come down from or returned to. The language of Scripture assumes an ordered creation with a settled above and below, and that assumption runs through the whole of it, from the mount of Sinai to the descent in the clouds.

</StudyCard>

</StudyCardGrid>

More harm has been done to this subject by piling up verses than by opposing them. Gather two hundred passages that mention the earth, hand them to a man who disagrees, and he will find the weakest one, answer that, and walk away believing he has answered all two hundred. So this section sorts before it argues. Plain statements are declarative or sequential and carry the case. Supporting witnesses are real cosmology carried inside an argument about something else; they confirm. The third tier holds the dreams, visions, and idioms that cannot carry weight, and this section names them openly rather than waiting to be caught with them.

<StudyCard anchor="plain-statements" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Plain Statements

*Declarative, sequential, and not figures of speech — the ground the page argues from.*

Genesis 1 runs as a chronology: an act, a result, a numbered day. Job 37:18 puts a question to a man about a sky that is spread out and strong. Joshua records a command given to the sun and the moon and the result that followed. These are statements of what was made and what happened, in the voice Scripture uses for fact. They are the first tier, and the page builds on them alone.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="supporting-witnesses" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Supporting Witnesses

*Real cosmology, carried inside an argument about something else.*

Isaiah 40:22 is making a point about the smallness of nations before Yahuah; the circle and the tent are the furniture of the point, not the point itself. First Samuel 2:8 is about the poor being lifted; the pillars are the ground of the boast. These verses show what the writers took for granted, which is worth a great deal, but they are confirming witnesses rather than the case itself, and the page treats them that way.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="handle-with-care" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Handle With Care

*The verses this page will not argue from, and the reason why.*

Nebuchadnezzar's tree is inside a dream, and the dream is interpreted for us. The four corners of the earth is an idiom for every quarter, used of a land under judgment as easily as of the whole world. Job 38:14 is describing dawn taking hold of the earth, not the shape of its base. Naming these openly costs nothing and buys everything, because a reader who sees the page refuse its weakest material will weigh its strongest material honestly.

</StudyCard>

<BandCard id="the-witnesses" title="The Witnesses" deck="The passages themselves, in the order the account gives them." blurb="The sequence of the days, the light that came before the lights, the day named for the light itself, the firmament beaten out, two great lights, the waters above, the foundations, the settled earth, the sun's circuit, the host of heaven, and how it all ends.">

These are the passages themselves, fourteen readings, each holding one strand of what Scripture says was made. They are set out in the order the account gives them: the sequence of the days, then the light, then the day itself, then the firmament and the lights set in it, then the waters, the foundations, the settled earth, the sun's circuit, the host, and the end of it all. No reading argues from anything but the text and the Hebrew or Greek behind it. Where a passage is commonly pressed further than it will go, the reading says so inside the reading rather than in a footnote, because a caution a reader has to hunt for is no caution at all.

<StudyCard anchor="day-one" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Day One

*The earth was here three days before anything was set in the sky.*

The order is not incidental to Genesis 1; it is the shape of the chapter. The heaven and the earth stand on the first day. Grass, herb, and fruit tree come on the third. Only on the fourth are the lights made and set. Whatever else is said about the account, it cannot be harmonized with a sequence that requires the sun to precede the earth and the earth to precede the plants. The chapter says the reverse, twice over, and closes each stage with a numbered day.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="light-before-the-lights" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Light Before the Lights

*Light was, and was named Day, before any luminary existed to carry it.*

On the first day light is spoken into being, divided from darkness, and named. The sun is three days away. This is the plainest statement in Scripture that light does not originate in the sun: Yahuah is the light and its source, and every luminary made on the fourth day is a bearer of light, not its origin. Daniel says the light dwells with Him. John says He is light, and in Him is no darkness at all. The sun is a lamp hung in a house that was already lit.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="he-called-the-light-day" verses={frontmatter.refs} link={{"label": "Dawn to Dusk: when the day begins and ends", "href": "/calendar/the-day/dawn-to-dusk"}}>

### He Called the Light Day

*The day is defined by light itself, not by a luminary and not by a shadow.*

Genesis 1:5 does not say that the sun rules off a day. It says Yahuah called the light Day and the darkness He called Night. The day is the light, from the breaking of it at dawn to its going at dusk. That definition was set before the fourth day and has never been amended, and it is the reckoning the feasts and the Sabbath are counted on. Reckoning a day from dusk to dusk moves the boundary to the dark, which the text calls Night.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-firmament" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Firmament

*A word that means beaten out, not a word that means empty.*

The Hebrew is raqia (H7549), from raqa (H7554), the verb used for hammering metal into thin plates. The Seventy rendered it stereoma, from stereos, firm. Job is asked whether he spread out a sky that is strong, like a cast mirror. Ezekiel, looking up, calls it crystal; John, looking from before the throne, sees glass like crystal. Amos speaks of a bound structure founded over the earth. Whatever the firmament is made of, Scripture never describes it as open space, and it stood still in David's day, long after the flood.

Two more passages treat the firmament as a thing with properties. David calls men to praise Yahuah in the firmament of His power, tying it to His strength rather than to empty distance. Daniel says the wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament, which assumes a brightness belonging to it. Neither is a plain statement of what it is made of, and neither is offered as one; they are witnesses that the writers thought of it as something rather than nothing.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-heavens-stretched-out" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Heavens Stretched Out

*Stretched as a curtain, spread as a tent — and by one pair of hands.*

Four passages use the same image, and it is not a vague one. Yahuah stretches out the heavens as a curtain and spreads them as a tent to dwell in. David repeats it word for picture in the Psalms. Isaiah says it twice more, and the second time adds what the image is for: I am Yahuah that maketh all things, that stretcheth forth the heavens alone, that spreadeth abroad the earth by myself. Alone, and by Himself. There is no second party in the work, which is why this reading reaches past cosmology into who was and was not present at creation.

A tent is a covering with something over it and something under it; a tent of open space is not a tent but a field. The word does its own work here, and it does the same work in Isaiah 40:22, where the circle below and the tent above are given in a single breath. In the last of the four the host of heaven is named as commanded — stretched out by His hands and under His orders, not loose in a void.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-circle-and-the-face" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Circle and the Face

*A compass set on the face of the deep — and three cautions stated up front.*

Isaiah says Yahuah sits upon the circle of the earth. The word is chug (H2329), a circle or compass, and Isaiah knew another word for a ball: in 22:18 he says a man will be tossed like a duwr (H1754) into a large country. The distinction is real and the reading makes it. The caution is made in the same breath: Strong's gives duwr a range that includes circle and pile as well as ball, so the argument is that Isaiah reached for a different word, not that Hebrew had no word for a sphere.

Proverbs sets a compass on the face of the depth, and Job draws a circular bound on the waters at the limit of light and darkness. The face language starts in Genesis 1:2, on the deep and on the waters, and runs through Scripture. Two more cautions belong here rather than in a footnote. Job 38:30, where the face of the deep is frozen, is describing winter ice in its own context, not the first day. And the breadth of the earth in Revelation 20:9 is platos (G4114), width or extent; it says how far, not how flat.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="two-great-lights" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Two Great Lights

*The moon is named a light and set in the same class as the sun.*

Genesis 1:16 makes two great lights, the greater to rule the day and the lesser to rule the night, and adds the stars as a separate work. The prophets hold to that language: the moon does not give her light, causes her light to shine, or is set alongside the sun as a second light by day and by night. Paul adds that the sun, the moon, and the stars each have their own glory, and that stars differ among themselves. Scripture never calls the lesser light a mirror.

The prophets and the Messiah keep the two lights distinct to the end. Joel says the sun shall be turned into darkness and the moon into blood before the day of Yahuah comes — two lights, two different fates. Yahushua repeats the older language without adjusting it: the sun darkened, and the moon not giving her light, in Matthew and again in Mark. Every one of these would be a strange way to speak of a mirror, which does not withhold anything of its own.

</StudyCard>

### The Waters Above

*Water was divided from water, and water is still up there.*

The firmament's first stated work is to divide the waters from the waters. That division did not end at the flood: the windows of heaven were opened and then stopped, and long after, David calls on the waters above the heavens to praise Yahuah. That single verse ends the canopy theory, which requires the upper waters to have emptied onto the earth and never returned. Whatever remains above, Scripture speaks of it as still there and still under His hand.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="founded-and-bounded" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Founded and Bounded

*Measured, fastened, and given a boundary the sea cannot pass.*

Job 38 is building language from end to end: foundations laid, measures taken, a line stretched, a cornerstone set, doors and bars given to the sea. The Psalms and Proverbs repeat it — a bound the waters may not pass, foundations appointed, a world set upon pillars. Held together with Job 26:7, where the earth hangs upon nothing, the picture is of something deliberately built and hung, not something that fell together. Scripture gives both, and a faithful page keeps both.

The same building language reaches past the earth to the men on it. Paul tells Athens that Yahuah determined the times appointed and the bounds of men's habitation — boundaries set for people as the sea has boundaries set for water. Job speaks of the earth shaken out of her place and her pillars trembling, and David says plainly that when it is shaken it is Yahuah who bears up the pillars of it. Isaiah gathers the dispersed from the four corners, kanaph (H3671), a wing or an extremity — an idiom for every quarter, and named as one here so no weight is hung on it.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="it-shall-not-be-moved" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### It Shall Not Be Moved

*Three times, in three books, in the same words.*

The world is established that it cannot be moved. The statement stands in Chronicles, and twice in the Psalms, each time tied to the reign of Yahuah rather than to a figure of speech about durability. The same books that speak of the sun's going forth and its circuit never once speak of the earth going anywhere. That asymmetry is worth stating plainly: the movement Scripture describes in the heavens is never transferred to the ground underfoot.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="the-suns-circuit" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### The Sun's Circuit

*Scripture gives the sun a tent, a course, and a command to stop.*

The sun is given a tabernacle and runs a circuit from one end of heaven to the other. It rises, goes down, and hastens to the place it rose from. At Gibeon the command is given to the sun and to the moon, and the record says the sun stood still and hasted not to go down about a whole day. In Hezekiah's sign the shadow goes back ten degrees, and the text says the sun returned. In every case the motion described is theirs.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="named-and-numbered" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Named and Numbered

*Named, numbered, and spoken of as an army.*

He tells the number of the stars and calls them all by name. They fought from heaven in their courses against Sisera. The morning stars sang together while the foundations were laid. A horn casts some of the host and of the stars to the ground and tramples them; a dragon's tail draws a third of them down. In Revelation the seven stars are interpreted for us as messengers. Whatever else the host of heaven is, Scripture speaks of it in the language of living beings under command.

Jude gives the word behind our planet: wandering stars, planetes (G4107), for whom the blackness of darkness is reserved. The caution is plain and the reading gives it rather than hiding it. In Jude's own context the phrase is one of five images applied to false teachers, alongside clouds without water and trees whose fruit withers. What is worth noticing is the image he reached for, and that a reserved judgment is a strange thing to speak of over a ball of burning gas.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="seen-from-one-place" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### Seen From One Place

*Four passages that assume a single vantage over the whole of it.*

The devil takes Yahushua up into an exceeding high mountain and shows him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory. Babel's builders set out to raise a tower whose top would reach heaven. John writes that He comes with clouds and every eye shall see Him, and Yahushua Himself says the coming will be as lightning out of the east shining to the west. These are four different kinds of text — a temptation account, a narrative, a vision, and a teaching — and they share an assumption that something can be seen, reached, or shown from one place.

The reading states its own limits. Revelation 1:7 sits inside a vision, and a man may reasonably read every eye as a figure for all mankind rather than a claim about sightlines. Babel says what the builders intended, not whether the thing was possible. What the four do together is show what the writers took for granted, which is worth setting down without pressing it into proof.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard anchor="how-it-ends" verses={frontmatter.refs}>

### How It Ends

*Rolled up like a scroll, and its elements melted.*

Isaiah and Revelation give the same picture: the host dissolved, the heavens rolled together as a scroll, every mountain and island moved. Peter adds that the heavens pass with a great noise and the elements melt with fervent heat. Neither rolling nor melting is something empty space can do; both require a thing. The end of the account matches its beginning, and both describe something made, something real, and something that will be taken down before the new heavens and the new earth.

What comes down at the end is measured. The city lies foursquare, its length as large as its breadth, and the height equal to both — twelve thousand furlongs on every side, measured with a reed in the hand of a messenger. It descends from Yahuah out of heaven to a new earth. The reading does not calculate anything from those figures; it notes that the account ends as it began, with something made, measured, and set in place.

</StudyCard>

Four objections come back every time, and each is stated here in its strongest form before it is answered. A weak version of an objection is worse than none: a reader who already holds it recognizes the caricature and stops trusting everything around it. Two of the four are fair as far as they go, and the answers say so. The point of this band is not to leave an opponent with nothing to say. It is to move the argument onto the ground it actually belongs on, which is what governs a reader's reading and what fills the sky once the throne has been taken out of it.

### “That is only poetry.”

*Sometimes it is — and the page said so first.*

Much of what is cited in this argument is poetry, and this page has already set that material aside in its own band. What cannot be set aside is Genesis 1, which is a chronology, and Joshua 10, which is a battle record. Calling those poetry is not a reading of the text; it is a decision made before reading it. The honest form of the objection would have to show that Moses wrote the six days as verse, and nothing in the Hebrew supports that.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard>

### “They were describing what they saw.”

*Then the Spirit carried them into error, and the problem is much larger than the sky.*

Holy men spoke as they were moved. If that carrying stopped at the sky, a reader is owed an account of where else it stopped, and no such account exists. The objection is not a small concession about ancient perspective; it is a claim that inspiration has gaps whose edges no one can locate. Anyone willing to grant that about Genesis 1 has already granted it about everything, and usually has not noticed.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard>

### “Cosmology is not a salvation issue.”

*Agreed, in the sense meant — and it misses what is actually at stake.*

No man is saved or lost by his picture of the sky, and this page has never said otherwise. What Scripture does put at stake is the authority a man submits to and the thing he serves. Israel was warned off the host of heaven by name; Romans names the exchange. The question is not whether a wrong model damns. It is what a man does with the word of Yahuah once he has decided it can be overruled, and what fills the sky once the throne is taken out of it.

</StudyCard>

### “This was settled centuries ago.”

*It was decided — and the grounds given at the time are on record.*

The reading changed, and the record of who changed it and what reasons they gave is public. Band 7 walks that record in their own published words, with dates and sources. A reader is free to weigh those reasons and find them good. What he is not entitled to do is assume the change was forced by evidence and then decline to look at what was actually said.

### What This Page Does Not Claim to Know

*The text settles less than either side pretends, and saying so costs nothing.*

Scripture calls the firmament beaten out, strong, and like crystal. It does not give its shape, its material, or its height. It calls the sun and the moon great lights and sets them in the firmament; it does not give their size or their distance. It calls the stars a host, names them, and speaks of them falling; it does not say what they are made of. The circle of Isaiah 40:22 is a circle, and the text does not say what lies past its edge.

These are not embarrassments. They are the limits of what was revealed, and a page that respects them is far harder to dismiss than one that claims an answer for everything. Where this page has no answer it says so, and it will keep saying so as the studies go up.

</StudyCard>

This band is history rather than Scripture, and it asks one question of each step: what problem arose, and what was added to answer it. Every claim is dated, sourced, and quoted from what the man published. Read end to end, the record shows a structure built by addition — a centre chosen, a shape exchanged, a force supplied, an explanation for what is not felt, an experiment that found nothing, and an account of why nothing could be found. It does not claim that every addition was invented out of nothing and never confirmed afterward. Whether that is the ordinary refinement of a theory or something else is the reader's to weigh. This band hands him the dates and the words and lets him weigh it.

### The Center Moves — 1543

*The reason given was fitness, and he gave it himself.*

Copernicus places the sun at the center in De Revolutionibus, published the year of his death. In Book 1, chapter 10, he writes that at rest, in the middle of everything, is the sun, and asks who could set this lamp in a better place in so beautiful a temple. The ground offered in that passage is the fitness and beauty of the arrangement, and the language is the language of a temple and a lamp. The card quotes him and lets the reader weigh what kind of reason that is.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard source={"Astronomia Nova, 1609."} sourceLinks={[{"label": "The 1609 edition (Latin)", "href": "https://archive.org/details/Astronomianovaa00Kepl"}]}>

### The Circles Fail — 1609

*One shape exchanged for another to keep the arrangement standing.*

Circular paths would not fit the observations. Working from Tycho Brahe's records, Kepler replaces them with ellipses in Astronomia Nova. The arrangement survives, but only by changing the figure it was built on. Note for accuracy: the ellipses were fitted to Brahe's Mars data and did predict positions better than what came before, which is why the change held. The card states that plainly and still makes its point, which is that the first arrangement did not survive contact with the observations.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard source={"Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, 1687."} sourceLinks={[{"label": "English translation (Motte, 1846 edition)", "href": "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Principles_of_Natural_Philosophy_(1846)"}]}>

### Something Must Hold It — 1687

*With the spheres gone, a single force is introduced to do two jobs.*

The older model had the bodies carried in solid spheres. Once those were discarded, nothing kept the bodies in their paths and nothing held objects to a moving earth. Newton's Principia supplies one force answering both. The card should say what it is, when it appeared, and what it was answering, and should avoid the claim that no evidence was ever offered for it afterward, which is not true and is the easiest sentence on the page to attack.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard>

### Why Is It Not Felt?

*The answer given is that steady motion cannot be felt and the air travels along.*

The objection is as old as the proposal, and the answer offered has not changed: motion at a constant velocity is imperceptible, and the atmosphere moves with the body it rests on. The card states that answer as its defenders state it, without caricature, and lets the reader weigh whether it satisfies him. A page that misstates the other side's answer here loses the reader who already knows it.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard source={"Michelson, American Journal of Science, 1881; Michelson and Morley, American Journal of Science, 1887."} sourceLinks={[{"label": "Michelson 1881, full text", "href": "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Relative_Motion_of_the_Earth_and_the_Luminiferous_Ether"}, {"label": "Michelson and Morley 1887, full text", "href": "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Relative_Motion_of_the_Earth_and_the_Luminiferous_Ether"}]}>

### The Motion Will Not Measure — 1881 and 1887

*The experiments built to find it came back empty.*

Michelson's 1881 experiment returned no displacement of the interference bands. Of that result he wrote that this conclusion directly contradicts the explanation of the phenomenon of aberration then generally accepted. The 1887 run with Morley, at far greater sensitivity, concluded more cautiously that any relative motion between the earth and the ether must be small. Both statements should be quoted as written and dated correctly, because the famous sentence belongs to 1881, and a page that attaches it to 1887 hands away its credibility on a detail it did not need.

</StudyCard>

<StudyCard source={"Lorentz 1892; Einstein, Annalen der Physik, 1905; Kyoto lecture, 14 December 1922, from Ishiwara's notes, translated in Physics Today, August 1982."} sourceLinks={[{"label": "Lorentz 1892, full text", "href": "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:The_Relative_Motion_of_the_Earth_and_the_Aether"}, {"label": "Einstein 1905, full text", "href": "https://www.fourmilab.ch/etexts/einstein/specrel/www/"}, {"label": "Kyoto lecture, Physics Today 1982", "href": "https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/how-i-created-the-theory-of-relativity"}]}>

### The Null Result Needs an Answer — 1892 to 1905

*A contraction is proposed, and then a theory in which the question no longer arises.*

FitzGerald and Lorentz propose that a body contracts along its line of motion, which would conceal the effect the experiment was built to find. Einstein's 1905 paper follows. In his 1922 Kyoto lecture he said that the motion of the earth cannot be detected by any optical experiment, and described Michelson's null result as the first path that led him to the theory.

Handle with care, and the page is stronger for saying it: the Kyoto lecture survives only through a listener's Japanese notes, and historians still dispute how much the experiment actually shaped Einstein's thinking. Quote it, date it, and say where it comes from. A quotation that is honestly framed cannot be taken away from you.

</StudyCard>
