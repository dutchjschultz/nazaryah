## The Test

After Korah's rebellion the people still muttered against the priesthood. Yahuah ended it with a test. Each tribe's prince brought a rod with his name written on it, and Aaron's name was written on the rod of Levi (Numbers 17:2–3). The twelve rods were laid before the testimony overnight. Yahuah had said, "the man's rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom" (Numbers 17:5). In the morning Aaron's rod had "brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds" (Numbers 17:8), all three stages of a year's growth in one night.

## Why the Almond

The Hebrew for almond is *shaqed*, from *shaqad*, to watch or to stay awake. The almond is the first tree to wake at the end of winter, blooming while other trees still look dead. Yahuah used this exact wordplay with Jeremiah. The prophet saw "a rod of an almond tree," and Yahuah answered, "I will hasten my word to perform it" (Jeremiah 1:11–12). The word behind "hasten" is the almond word: Yahuah is awake and watching over what He has said. Aaron's rod carried the same message. Yahuah was watching His priesthood, and He had made His choice plain.

## Almonds in the Lampstand

The almond was already in the sanctuary. The golden lampstand had cups "made like unto almonds": three on each of its six branches and four on the center shaft (Exodus 25:33–34), twenty-two in all. The light of the Holy Place and the rod of the chosen priest bore the same mark. The one who tends the light is the one whose rod bears the almond.

## Dead Wood Made Alive

A cut rod has no root and no sap. Life coming out of it overnight could only be Yahuah's doing, and it proved His choice by bringing life out of death. The apostles read the priesthood of Messiah the same way. No man takes that honor to himself; he must be "called of Elohim, as was Aaron," and even Messiah "glorified not himself to be made an high priest" (Hebrews 5:4–5). The Father chose Him, and the Father proved it: He was declared the Son of Elohim "by the resurrection from the dead" (Romans 1:4), and Yahuah gave assurance to all men "in that he hath raised him from the dead" (Acts 17:31). The Son is the one chosen and raised. The Father is the one who chooses and raises.

The rod was kept "before the testimony" as a token against the rebels, so their murmuring would end (Numbers 17:10). Hebrews places it inside the ark beside the tablets and the manna (Hebrews 9:4).

## Revelation

John turns and sees one like the Son of man walking "in the midst of the seven candlesticks" (Revelation 1:12–13), the chosen priest tending the almond lamps. To the assembly that left its first love He warns that He will "remove thy candlestick out of his place" (Revelation 2:5). The rod still settles the question. Yahuah chose the priest, and that priest tends the light.
