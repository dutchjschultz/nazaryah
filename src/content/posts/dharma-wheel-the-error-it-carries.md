Buddhism holds much of the second tablet. Its Five Precepts forbid killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, and lying, and stand close to the sixth through ninth commandments. What it lacks is the first tablet. There is no Creator at the center of the wheel and no Name to call upon. The hub is a man's own discipline.

## Wheels Under the Throne

Scripture has wheels too. Ezekiel saw wheels beside the living creatures, high and full of eyes, "for the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels" (Ezekiel 1:20). They went wherever the spirit went. Above them stood a throne, and on it the likeness of the glory of Yahuah (Ezekiel 1:26-28). In Scripture the wheels serve the throne. In Buddhism the wheel has replaced the throne. It turns by human effort, and no one sits above it.

## The Circle With Nothing New

Solomon described the world as it looks without a view upward. The wind "whirleth about continually," the rivers run to the sea and return again, and "there is no new thing under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 1:6-9). That is the wheel of samsara: the round of life, death, and return, with no exit except a man's own effort to stop it.

Scripture breaks the circle with a straight line. "It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). Once, not again and again. No rebirth, and no second round to work off a debt.

## Merit as a Bank

The eight spokes are eight efforts, and the path runs as a ledger: merit earned, merit stored, merit paid out. *The 38 Blessings* promises those who keep the First Precept for a day that they will be "protected by gods," have "fine skin," and live longer. It warns that those who break the Fifth Precept will "serve their terms in hell" and return as mentally impaired beings. It urges Thais to make merit on behalf of their families, the nation, and the King.

Torah also ties blessing to obedience (Deuteronomy 28:1-14), so reward itself is not the error. The error is reward as a banked transaction, earned apart from Yahuah and His covenant. Paul warned of men "supposing that gain is godliness" (1 Timothy 6:5). The same counterfeit runs through modern Christianity. The prosperity preacher's "sow a seed, reap a harvest" is the merit ledger with a church sign on it. Merit made on behalf of others is the Roman treasury of merit and its indulgences. A set term in hell before release is purgatory by another name.

No man walks off the wheel by his own path. Yahuah alone saves: "I, even I, am Yahuah; and beside me there is no saviour" (Isaiah 43:11). The way to Him was provided in His Son: "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6). Not eight paths. One way.

## The Verdict

The Dharma wheel carries moral teaching that echoes the second tablet, set on a hub of self-effort with no Creator, no Name, and no Deliverer. It promises escape from a circle Scripture never taught, by a ledger of merit Scripture never honored.

Revelation ends the circle. The dead stand once before the throne and the books are opened (Revelation 20:12), and He that sits upon the throne says, "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5). Something new under the sun after all, made by Yahuah and not earned by man.
