The most influential prophetic timetable in modern Christianity rests on one decision about one verse: whether the seventieth week of Daniel 9 runs where the text puts it, or whether it may be detached from the sixty-nine before it and moved across two thousand years of history. The seven-year tribulation, the secret removal of the assembly, the rebuilt temple with restored sacrifices, the personal antichrist who signs and breaks a treaty with Israel, and the postponed thousand-year kingdom all hang from that detachment. Remove it and the structure has nothing left to hang from.

## The Text Under Examination

**DANIEL 9:24 — KJV**

> *“Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy.”*

**DANIEL 9:25–27 — KJV**

> *“Know therefore and understand, that from the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks... And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself... And he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease.”*

Four verses, one continuous sentence of time, divided by its own author into seven weeks, sixty-two weeks, and one week.

## The Count, From Its Decree to Its Close

The unit is a seven of years. The Hebrew shabua means a seven, not necessarily seven days, and Torah had already established the counting of time in sevens of years: seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years, forty-nine years to the Jubilee (Leviticus 25:8). The day-for-a-year measure is given by Yahuah twice and stated as a principle both times — forty days of spying answered by forty years of wandering, a year for each day (Numbers 14:34), and Ezekiel told to bear iniquity a set number of days at the ratio of a day for a year (Ezekiel 4:6). Daniel was praying about the seventy years of the captivity, a period measured in years (Daniel 9:2), and the answer extends that measure to seventy sevens. Seventy weeks of years is four hundred ninety years; the sixty-nine that reach the Messiah are four hundred eighty-three.

The clock begins at the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem. Several Persian decrees touched the return and they are not interchangeable. Cyrus authorized rebuilding the house (Ezra 1:1–4) and Darius reaffirmed it (Ezra 6:1–12); both concern the temple. The decree that restored Jerusalem as a functioning body — with law, magistrates, judges, and authority to govern its own people — is the one Ezra carried in the seventh year of Artaxerxes (Ezra 7:11–26). A city is restored when its law is restored. That seventh year falls in 457 BC, and Scripture gathers the three decrees into one commandment, the builders finishing according to the commandment of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes (Ezra 6:14), with the last completing what the first two began.

Four hundred eighty-three years from 457 BC, with no year zero standing between BC and AD, arrives at 27 AD. Messiah means Anointed, and the prophecy points to an anointing rather than a birth. Luke fixes the moment by dating the beginning of the ministry to the fifteenth year of Tiberius (Luke 3:1) and immediately describes the anointing at the Jordan. Yahushua then opened His ministry with a statement about the calendar: the time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of Yahuah is at hand (Mark 1:15). The time that was fulfilled was a counted time, and Daniel 9 is the only counted period expiring at that moment. The first seven weeks have their own terminus as well — forty-nine years from 457 BC reaches 408 BC, in the era when the wall and the street were finished in troublous times, exactly as verse 25 said.

The seventieth week runs from 27 AD to about 34 AD, and its midpoint falls three and a half years in. The ministry of Yahushua ran three and a half years and ended at Pesach in 31 AD, when He died at the ninth hour on the fourteenth of Aviv, at the hour the lambs were being killed.

**DANIEL 9:27 — KJV**

> *“...and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease...”*

The Hebrew verb is shabath, the same verb used of Yahuah resting from the finished work of creation. It does not mean abolished, discarded, or annulled. It means brought to rest, entered into Sabbath. The offerings did not fail; they arrived. The thing they had pointed toward for fifteen centuries stood on the hill, and the types laid down because the substance had come. The outer veil tore that afternoon, and the sacrificial function of the earthly sanctuary was finished as a means of atonement decades before Rome burned the building.

Half the week still remained, and the record shows what filled it. The message went to Israel first and exclusively for roughly three and a half more years. Yahushua had said He was sent to the lost sheep of the house of Israel (Matthew 15:24) and had sent the twelve on the same restricted commission (Matthew 10:5–6); after the resurrection the witness began at Jerusalem (Acts 1:8) and stayed there. The week closes near 34 AD with the covenant nation rendering its verdict: Stephen stoned by the council after charging them with resisting the Spirit (Acts 7:51–60), a scattering that carries the message outward (Acts 8:1–4), Peter given the vision that opens the door to the nations and the household of Cornelius receiving the Spirit (Acts 10), and Paul afterward stating the sequence as settled policy — the word spoken first to them, and upon rejection turning to the Gentiles (Acts 13:46). Seventy weeks were determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, and at the end of the seventieth the exclusive determination expired.

Verse 26 places the destruction of the city and the sanctuary after the sixty-two weeks and does not assign it a week. That is deliberate. The desolation of 70 AD is the consequence of what happened inside the seventy weeks, not one of their divisions, and Yahushua described it in the same terms — days of vengeance, the city compassed with armies, not one stone left upon another (Luke 21:20–24; Matthew 24:2). It stands outside the count as the sentence executed on the generation that rejected the One the count led to.

## The Clause That Decides the Case

Verse 24 states what the whole period was appointed to accomplish: to finish the transgression, to make an end of sins, to make reconciliation for iniquity, to bring in everlasting righteousness, to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy. These are not the works of an antichrist. They are the works of the Messiah, and the apostolic writings state every one of them as finished — sin put away once by His sacrifice (Hebrews 9:26), the righteousness of Yahuah manifested and witnessed by the law and the prophets (Romans 3:21–22), the prophets’ word finding its terminus in Him (1 Peter 1:10–11), and the anointing at the Jordan when the Spirit descended (Luke 3:21–22; Acts 10:38).

Verse 24 assigns all six to the seventy weeks, not to the first sixty-nine. If the seventieth week is still future, then transgression is not finished, sin has not been ended, reconciliation for iniquity has not been made, and everlasting righteousness has not been brought in. The futurist reading cannot hold the six purposes and the gap at the same time. It must either move the accomplishment of the atonement into a future age or concede that the seventy weeks are complete.

## The Pronoun in Verse 27

The whole modern system rests on the identity of one word: he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week. Futurism assigns that he to the prince that shall come in verse 26, read as an antichrist signing a seven-year treaty with Israel. The text does not support the transfer. Verse 26 does not make that prince the actor; it says the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary, naming the prince only to identify whose people they are. The acting subject through the whole prophecy is the Anointed One — Messiah the Prince of verse 25, cut off in verse 26, confirming the covenant and ending the sacrifice in verse 27.

The Hebrew for confirm is a strengthening word, not the language of drafting a treaty; it describes making a covenant prevail. Scripture states plainly who did that and for whom. Yahushua was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of Yahuah, to confirm the promises made unto the fathers (Romans 15:8), and at the cup He said the words directly — this is my blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many (Matthew 26:28). The many of Daniel 9:27 and the many of Matthew 26:28 are the same many. No treaty with a future antichrist appears anywhere in Scripture, in any book, in any wording. It exists only as an inference drawn from a pronoun.

## Why the Gap Cannot Be Inserted

Weeks in a count of weeks run consecutively. No language of suspension, postponement, interruption, or resumption appears anywhere in the passage, and nothing marks the sixty-ninth week as terminal or the seventieth as detachable. The interval that must be inserted to make the futurist reading work is now approaching two thousand years — more than four times the length of the entire prophecy it interrupts. A measuring line that stops at ninety-eight percent and resumes two millennia later is not a measurement; its terminus becomes unlocatable by definition. And if a prophecy of seventy consecutive sevens can hold a gap of that size without saying so, then no prophetic period in Scripture can be trusted to mean what it counts.

One word on the starting point, since it is sometimes waved off rather than answered. The decree of Artaxerxes in Ezra 7 is the only one of the three that restores Jerusalem's own law, courts, and magistrates rather than merely funding a building project, and Daniel's wording is restore and build, not rebuild the walls. The date stands on what the decree actually granted. It does not depend on any other prophecy, any other calculation, or any conclusion anyone has ever drawn from a different chapter of Daniel.

## The Futurist Reading, Clause by Clause

The disagreement is not a matter of emphasis. The two readings assign nearly every clause of the prophecy to a different subject and a different century. Set against the count above, the futurist position holds the following.

**The decree.** The count is usually started at the wall commission of Nehemiah 2 in 445 BC rather than the restoration of Jerusalem’s law in Ezra 7, and the years are then recalculated on a three-hundred-sixty-day figure to make the arithmetic land.

**The end of the sixty-ninth week.** It is placed at the triumphal entry in 32 or 33 AD rather than at the anointing, after which the clock is stopped.

**The cutting off of the Messiah.** It falls after the sixty-ninth week and before the seventieth begins — inside the gap, belonging to neither week, which leaves the central event of the prophecy outside the period appointed to accomplish it.

**The covenant.** The confirming is assigned to a future antichrist signing a seven-year treaty with Israel, rather than to the Messiah confirming the promises to the fathers in His own blood.

**The ceasing of the sacrifice.** It becomes a desecration by that antichrist midway through a future week, in a rebuilt temple with restored offerings — which requires the offerings to be reinstituted after the Messiah brought them to rest, and requires the once-for-all entry of Hebrews 9 to be set aside.

**The final half week.** It becomes three and a half years of great tribulation still ahead, rather than the three and a half years of witness to Israel that ran from the tree to the opening of the door to the nations.

**The six purposes.** They are held open, awaiting completion after the week resumes, rather than accomplished by the Messiah within the seventy weeks as verse 24 requires.

**Seventy AD.** It is reduced to a preview or type of a future desolation rather than the desolation the passage itself names.

**The twelve hundred sixty days.** They are collapsed into literal days inside the future seven years, which requires the year-day measure to be abandoned in the very passages the prophets used it for.

Every one of those clauses depends on the gap. Close the gap and they fall together in a single motion — the treaty, the rebuilt temple, the restored offerings, the two three-and-a-half-year halves, the removal of the assembly before them, and the postponed kingdom on the far side.

## Where the Seven-Year Tribulation Came From

No verse in Scripture mentions a seven-year tribulation. The phrase does not occur. No prophet names it, no apostle warns of it, and Yahushua never once mentions a seven-year period of any kind. The number is not read out of a text; it is assembled. The detached week supplies seven years with no fixed location in history, verse 27 is halved at its own midpoint to produce two segments of three and a half years, and every three-and-a-half-year figure elsewhere in prophecy is then gathered into those halves — the time, times, and the dividing of time of Daniel 7:25 and 12:7, the forty-two months of Revelation 11:2 and 13:5, and the twelve hundred sixty days of Revelation 11:3 and 12:6. None of those passages mentions Daniel’s seventieth week. They are attached to it because the detached week created an empty container that needed filling.

The historical record of when this reading entered is supporting evidence only, never authority. It is short, and it is not disputed by historians of any camp.

- For roughly fourteen centuries the seventy weeks were read as fulfilled in the Messiah. That was the universal Protestant position at the Reformation, which also applied the year-day measure to the twelve hundred sixty days and identified the power fulfilling Daniel’s little horn as the papal system.

- In the late sixteenth century, in direct response to that identification, a Spanish Jesuit named Francisco Ribera published a commentary on Revelation relocating the antichrist to a single future individual ruling a literal three and a half years at the end of history. Cardinal Robert Bellarmine defended the same relocation. The effect, and the acknowledged purpose, was to remove the prophecy from the present.

- Protestants would not touch the reading for more than two centuries, regarding it as an argument manufactured to shield the papacy. That changed in 1826, when Samuel Roffey Maitland, librarian to the Archbishop of Canterbury, published in favor of the futurist scheme and reintroduced it into English Protestant circles.

- Within four years John Nelson Darby, among the Plymouth Brethren, built a full system on it. The gap became structural: it supplied the interval in which a separate program for the assembly could run alongside a suspended program for Israel, and it created the future seven years in which a removal of the assembly, a personal antichrist, a rebuilt temple, and a restored sacrificial system could all be placed.

- The Scofield Reference Bible of 1909 printed the system in the notes on the same page as the text. A generation absorbed the framework as though it were the reading of the verses above it, and pulpits, seminaries, and popular books carried it from there into the assumed background of most of Christendom.

The age of a doctrine does not prove it false, and the motives of the men who advanced it do not settle whether it is true. The argument against it is the text. But the history answers a question readers reasonably ask: if this framework is not in Daniel and not in the apostles, why does nearly everyone teach it? Because it is inherited rather than examined.
