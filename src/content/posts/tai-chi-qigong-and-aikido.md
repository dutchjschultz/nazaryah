These three exercises came West from religions outside the five covered in this section. They are recorded here because Americans meet them in parks, senior centers, and gyms, and because all three rest on the same idea.

## Tai Chi

Tai chi is taught in America as slow, gentle movement for balance and health, often to seniors. Its full name, *taijiquan*, means "supreme ultimate fist." *Taiji*, the "supreme ultimate," is the Taoist name for the source of yin and yang, the two opposing forces said to balance all things. The well-known black-and-white circle is the *taiji* symbol. The art traces to the Chen family village in China in the 1600s, and legend credits its invention to Zhang Sanfeng, a Taoist immortal. Each movement is meant to balance yin and yang and move *qi*, the life energy, through the body.

## Qigong

Qigong means "working the *qi*." It is a set of breathing and movement exercises meant to gather, store, and circulate life energy. It comes from Taoist and Chinese Buddhist practice and is now offered in Western wellness centers and hospitals. Falun Gong, a modern Chinese religious movement, grew out of qigong.

## Aikido

Aikido is a Japanese martial art built in the 1920s and 1930s by Morihei Ueshiba. Ueshiba was a devoted follower of Onisaburo Deguchi, leader of Oomoto, a Shinto-based new religion, and Oomoto teaching shaped the art. The name means "the way of harmony with *ki*" — *ki* being the Japanese form of *qi*.

## The Thread That Ties Them

All three rest on the belief that an impersonal life force flows through the body and can be gathered and steered by breath and movement. Hinduism has the same idea under the name *prana*. Scripture gives no such force. Life is the breath Yahuah gave: He "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life" (Genesis 2:7), and "the breath of the Almighty hath given me life" (Job 33:4). Breath is held from the Maker, not mastered by the man.

## The Verdict

Moving slowly is not sin. Moving to balance yin and yang and steer *qi* is a Taoist practice, whatever the senior center calls it. The command stands: "Learn not the way of the heathen" (Jeremiah 10:2). In Revelation the breath of life comes only from Yahuah, as when "the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood upon their feet" (Revelation 11:11).
