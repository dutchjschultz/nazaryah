*Witness Five of Five in the investigation [**The Tree in the Midst**](/investigations/the-tree-in-the-midst) — back to the case.*

## Why the Almond?

The first four witnesses show a pattern. The gate of heaven stood at Almond. The tree of gold in the Holy Place was an almond. The rod that came alive before the testimony was an almond. Every time Yahuah built a picture of the tree of life, He chose the same tree.

That raises the question this witness answers: why the almond? Scripture does not leave it to guesswork. Yahuah explained it Himself, with a play on words that only works in Hebrew.

## The Waking Tree

The almond in Hebrew is shaqed (H8247). It comes from shaqad (H8245), which means to watch, to be awake, to keep watch.

In the land of Israel the almond is the first tree to blossom. While the rest of the orchard still stands bare in late winter, the almond is already awake and white with flowers. It is the tree that wakes first and watches for the spring. Hebrew named it after what it does.

## The Prophet's Rod

When Yahuah called Jeremiah, He showed him a rod of an almond tree and asked what he saw. Jeremiah answered, “I see a rod of an almond tree.” Yahuah said, “Thou hast well seen: for I will hasten my word to perform it” (Jeremiah 1:11-12).

The word translated “hasten” is shoqed, watching. Yahuah was saying: you see a shaqed, because I am shoqed over my word. The almond is the sign that Yahuah is awake over what He has spoken, and He will bring it to pass.

Here is the answer to the whole investigation. The almond stands beside the Law because the almond is the tree that watches over Yahuah’s word. The tree of gold faced the testimony. The living rod lay with the tables. The tree was placed where its name said it belonged, keeping watch over the commandments.

## Watching in Both Directions

Jeremiah uses shaqad again, and the second time shows how far Yahuah’s watching reaches. “And it shall come to pass, that like as I have watched over them, to pluck up, and to break down, and to throw down, and to destroy, and to afflict; so will I watch over them, to build, and to plant, saith Yahuah” (Jeremiah 31:28).

Yahuah watches over His word both ways. He watches over the curse, and He watches over the blessing. That is exactly what was set before Israel at Shechem, with the curse spoken toward Ebal and the blessing toward Gerizim (Deuteronomy 27:12-13; Joshua 8:33-34).

Daniel saw the curse side come true. Confessing Israel’s sin, he prayed, “the curse is poured upon us, and the oath that is written in the law of Moses... Therefore hath Yahuah watched upon the evil, and brought it upon us” (Daniel 9:11, 14). The word for watched is shaqad. The almond’s word was kept to the letter, just as it was written in the Law.

## The Law on the Heart

Look at what comes just three verses after Jeremiah 31:28. Having said He will watch over Israel to build and to plant, Yahuah promises a new covenant: “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim, and they shall be my people” (Jeremiah 31:33).

The watching does not end with the Law set aside. It ends with the Law written inside the man. The almond that once stood before the tablets of stone now watches over tablets of flesh (2 Corinthians 3:3). The same Law is written in a new place, and the same watching tree keeps it.

## The Eyes of the Lampstand

Zechariah saw a lampstand of gold with seven lamps, and he asked what they meant. The answer came back: “those seven; they are the eyes of Yahuah, which run to and fro through the whole earth” (Zechariah 4:2, 10).

The lamps of the almond tree are Yahuah’s eyes. The tree of gold in the Holy Place was not decoration. It was a watching tree with lamps for eyes, set before the Law, burning all night (Exodus 27:21). Moses said of the land itself, “the eyes of Yahuah thy Elohim are always upon it, from the beginning of the year even unto the end of the year” (Deuteronomy 11:12). The almond is the picture of those eyes that never close.

## A Watching Tree Calls for a Watching People

If Yahuah is awake over His word, His people are called to be awake as well. “Except Yahuah keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain” (Psalm 127:1). The watchman there waketh, shaqad, but his watching only counts when Yahuah is the One keeping the city.

Yahushua said the same to His disciples: “Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Master doth come” (Matthew 24:42). The tree of life is a watching tree, and the ones who eat from it are watching people, keeping the word the tree keeps.

## Right There All Along

The almond’s name has sat in the Hebrew text since Jeremiah’s call, plain to anyone who reads the word behind the English. Yahuah told the prophet what the almond means in the same breath He showed it to him. The reason the tree of life was built as an almond was given in Scripture’s own words, right there all along.

## Where It Ends

The last word of the investigation belongs to the last chapters of Scripture. “Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth” (Revelation 16:15). And to those who keep what the almond watched over: “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life” (Revelation 22:14).
