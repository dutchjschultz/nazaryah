## Introduction

Yahuah sent Joseph ahead of Israel to keep a family alive through seven years of famine, and that family carried the promised seed. The Psalm says it plainly: “He sent a man before them” (Psalm 105:17). Joseph said the same to his brothers: “God did send me before you to preserve life” (Genesis 45:5). Yahuah did the saving. Joseph was the man He put in place to carry it out (Genesis 45:7; 50:20).

Genesis stops at the height of the story to record one detail: “Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh” (Genesis 41:46). Luke stops at the Jordan to record the same detail: Yahushua “began to be about thirty years of age” (Luke 3:23). The two scenes match in far more than age. Pharaoh gave Joseph all of Egypt and held back one thing: “only in the throne will I be greater than thou” (Genesis 41:40). Paul places the same limit on the Son (1 Corinthians 15:27-28), and the Trinity cannot live with that limit.

## The Age of Service

The Law counted Levites for the work of the tabernacle “from thirty years old and upward” (Numbers 4:3). David was thirty when he began to reign (2 Samuel 5:4). Thirty marked a man stepping into office before Yahuah, at the tabernacle and on the throne. Numbers 8:24 does start the Levites at twenty-five, so thirty is the age of the count in Numbers 4, not the only age in the Law.

Joseph stood before Pharaoh long before Numbers was written, and he was not a Levite. No law put him in office at thirty. Yahuah’s timing did. He was seventeen when his brothers turned on him (Genesis 37:2), and about thirteen years passed “until the time that his word came” (Psalm 105:19). Then “the king sent and loosed him” (Psalm 105:20).

Yahushua waited as well, and when He came out of the Jordan and the wilderness He announced, “The time is fulfilled” (Mark 1:15). He was not a Levite either. He came from Judah (Hebrews 7:14), and Yahuah made Him a priest after the order of Melchizedek (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5:5-6). His age at the Jordan honored the Law’s pattern without placing Him in Levi’s line.

## From the Pit to the Linen

Joseph’s brothers threw him into a pit (Genesis 37:24). Genesis uses the same Hebrew word, *bor*, for the prison he left at thirty, where the KJV says “dungeon” (Genesis 41:14). The Psalms use *bor* for the grave (Psalm 88:4). Joseph did not simply change jobs. He came up out of the pit.

Before he stood in front of Pharaoh, “he shaved himself, and changed his raiment” (Genesis 41:14). The Levites were set apart with water, a shaved body, and washed clothes (Numbers 8:7). Priests were washed, clothed, and anointed (Exodus 29:4-7). Joseph received no oil, but Pharaoh saw what the oil pictures. He called Joseph “a man in whom the Spirit of God is” (Genesis 41:38), and he dressed him in fine linen, *shesh* (Genesis 41:42). The same word names the coat of the priest (Exodus 28:39).

At the Jordan no one had to guess. Yahushua came up from the water and the Spirit came “lighting upon him” (Matthew 3:16). Peter tells what happened: “God anointed Yahushua of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power,” “for God was with him” (Acts 10:38). Stephen used those same words of Joseph: “God was with him” (Acts 7:9).

Neither man raised himself. When Pharaoh praised Joseph’s gift, Joseph answered, “It is not in me” (Genesis 41:16). Yahushua said, “I can of mine own self do nothing” (John 5:30), and He “glorified not himself to be made an high priest” (Hebrews 5:5). The same book says “God, even thy God, hath anointed thee” (Hebrews 1:9). One who has a God above him and is anointed by that God is not that God.

One scene in Joseph opens two scenes in Yahushua. Joseph’s age points to the Jordan, where the Son’s public work began. Joseph’s rise out of the pit points to the tomb, for Yahushua was raised first and then “by the right hand of God exalted” (Acts 2:32-33).

## All Things, With One Exception

Twice in Egypt Joseph received everything except one thing. Potiphar “hath committed all that he hath to my hand,” Joseph said, and “neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee” (Genesis 39:8-9). Pharaoh did the same on a larger scale. Everything Joseph received, Pharaoh gave, and the Son received the same things from His Father.

- **The house.** “Thou shalt be over my house” (Genesis 41:40). Messiah is “as a son over his own house,” and “faithful to him that appointed him” (Hebrews 3:2, 6).

- **The ring.** Pharaoh took the ring from his own hand and put it on Joseph’s (Genesis 41:42). A king’s ring sealed a command in the king’s name (Esther 8:8). Of the Son it is written, “for him hath God the Father sealed” (John 6:27).

- **The second chariot.** Joseph rode in the chariot of the *mishneh*, the second (Genesis 41:43). The same word says Mordecai was “next unto king Ahasuerus” (Esther 10:3) and names “the second priest” (2 Kings 25:18). The second rank is high, and it is still second. Yahushua is seated “on the right hand of the throne” (Hebrews 8:1), beside the One whose throne it is.

- **The title.** “He made him lord of his house” (Psalm 105:21). The word is *adon*, master. Peter says of the Son, “God hath made that same Yahushua, whom ye have crucified, both Master and Messiah” (Acts 2:36). A title that is made is a title that is given.

- **The knee and the name.** Pharaoh gave Joseph a new name, and Egypt was ordered to “Bow the knee” (Genesis 41:43, 45). Yahuah “hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name,” so that every knee should bow (Philippians 2:9-10).

Then comes the exception. Yahushua said, “All power is given unto me” (Matthew 28:18), and John’s Gospel records, “The Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand” (John 3:35). Paul stops at that word *all* and makes the same point Pharaoh made: “it is manifest that he is excepted, which did put all things under him” (1 Corinthians 15:27). The Giver is not one of the things given. When everything is finished, “then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all” (1 Corinthians 15:28).

Joseph also ended where the Son will end. He “brought the money into Pharaoh’s house” (Genesis 47:14), and “the land became Pharaoh’s” (Genesis 47:20). What he gathered, he gathered for the one on the throne. The Son “shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father” (1 Corinthians 15:24).

## Trinity Arguments Answered

**“He that hath seen me hath seen the Father.”** The church reads John 14:9 as proof that the Son is God. Judah said to Joseph, “thou art even as Pharaoh” (Genesis 44:18), and Judah was not confused about who sat on the throne. Joseph spoke and ruled for Pharaoh, so meeting Joseph was meeting Pharaoh’s word. In the next verse Yahushua says it is the Father in Him who “doeth the works” (John 14:10). The same holds for the command that all men “honour the Son, even as they honour the Father” (John 5:23). The verse before it gives the reason: the Father “hath committed all judgment unto the Son” (John 5:22).

**“Every knee belongs to Yahuah alone.”** Yahuah swore, “unto me every knee shall bow” (Isaiah 45:23), and the argument says that if knees bow to the Son, He must be Yahuah. Egypt bowed to Joseph at Pharaoh’s order, and it took nothing from Pharaoh. Paul’s sentence does not end at the Son. It ends “to the glory of God the Father” (Philippians 2:11).

**“He was subject only in the flesh.”** This is the usual answer to every verse that places the Father above the Son. But 1 Corinthians 15:28 is not about Galilee or Gethsemane. It is written of the risen Son after death itself is destroyed (1 Corinthians 15:26), and it says He “shall” be subject. Paul looks ahead to the end, and the exception still stands.

**“A type cannot prove a doctrine.”** That is correct, and Joseph does not need to. Paul’s plain statement carries the doctrine. Joseph shows the shape of it, written in Genesis long before any council met.

## Go Unto Joseph

When the famine came, “the people cried to Pharaoh for bread,” and Pharaoh answered, “Go unto Joseph; what he saith to you, do” (Genesis 41:55). The grain had been laid up “under the hand of Pharaoh” (Genesis 41:35), and Joseph was the one who opened the storehouses (Genesis 41:56). Yahushua described the same order: “my Father giveth you the true bread from heaven” (John 6:32), and “no man cometh unto the Father, but by me” (John 14:6). The Father is the source. The Son is the way.

Pharaoh’s answer has two halves, *go* and *do*. Much of the modern pulpit preaches the first and drops the second. The voice on the mountain said of the Son, “hear ye him” (Matthew 17:5), which is what Moses said Israel must do when Yahuah put His words in the Prophet’s mouth (Deuteronomy 18:18-19). Yahushua said His commandment came from the Father (John 12:49). So He asks, “Why call ye me, Master, Master, and do not the things which I say?” (Luke 6:46).

The order matters. Jacob’s house was kept alive first, and it lived under Joseph’s word afterward. Deliverance comes through the blood before anyone walks in the commandments. The walk does not buy the rescue, and the rescue does not cancel the walk.

## Where the Shadow Stops

Joseph was not a priest. His only tie to a priesthood was marrying the daughter of an Egyptian priest (Genesis 41:45). The text never says he was anointed. Pharaoh was a pagan king, so the picture lies in the rank and not in the man. Shaving before Pharaoh also fit the custom of Egypt, and the Hebrew word behind “Bow the knee,” *abrek*, is rare and its meaning is debated. These limits are the nature of a shadow, for “the body is of Messiah” (Colossians 2:17).

## Conclusion

Joseph at thirty came up from the pit, was clothed in linen, was given the ring, and was set over all Egypt, with one exception. The Son received all things from His Father, and the same exception stands. Revelation keeps it to the end. The Lamb takes the scroll “out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne” (Revelation 5:7), and He says He is “set down with my Father in his throne” (Revelation 3:21). All things were given to the Son. The throne remains the Father’s.
