Islam deserves credit for what it kept. It confesses one God, refuses images, prays at set times, and will not eat swine. Yet the symbol the Muslim world flies is a piece of creation, and Torah spoke to that directly.

## The Lights Are Servants

Yahuah made the sun, the moon, and the stars "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years" (Genesis 1:14). They are instruments, clocks set in the sky. Moses warned Israel not to lift up their eyes to the sun, moon, and stars and "be driven to worship them" (Deuteronomy 4:19). Torah set death as the penalty for any who served "the sun, or moon, or any of the host of heaven" (Deuteronomy 17:3). Job counted it a denial of God above if his heart was secretly enticed by "the moon walking in brightness" (Job 31:26-28). King Josiah put down the priests who burned incense "to the sun, and to the moon" (2 Kings 23:5).

The moon was no small god in the world Abraham left. Ur of the Chaldees and Haran, the two cities named in his story (Genesis 11:31), were both centers of the worship of the moon god Sin. Yahuah called Abraham out of that worship. Whatever a Muslim intends by it, a banner bearing the moon honors the servant rather than the One who set it in its place.

## One Light Doing the Work of Three

The deeper error is in the calendar the crescent stands for. Genesis 1:14 names the lights together for days and years. Islam reckons by the moon alone and never sets its year by the sun, so its year runs about eleven days short. Ramadan drifts backward through every season, falling in winter one decade and in summer another. Torah fixed the feasts in their seasons: "Observe the month of Abib, and keep the passover" (Deuteronomy 16:1), and Sukkot "in the end of the year, when thou hast gathered in thy labours" (Exodus 23:16). A calendar that cuts the moon loose from the sun cannot hold a single feast in its season.

Even the crescent itself is the wrong mark. The month renews when the moon passes the sun. The sighting of a thin crescent is a local event that one town sees and another misses, yet a *moed*, an appointed meeting, needs one day for all. Torah never commands a crescent sighting. Islam built its holiest season on it.

## The Verdict

The star and crescent is a borrowed imperial emblem that honors a created light and stands for a calendar that has let go of the sun. Islam kept the first commandment in word, then raised a piece of the sky over its houses of prayer.

Revelation shows where the moon belongs. The woman clothed with the sun has "the moon under her feet" (Revelation 12:1). In the city to come there is no need of sun or moon, "for the glory of God did lighten it," and the Lamb is the lamp thereof, the *luchnos* that carries the Father's glory (Revelation 21:23).
