*Witness One in the investigation [**The Trees of Judges**](/investigations/the-trees-of-judges) — back to the case.*

## The Man Is the Branch

Yahushua put the man in the vine: “I am the vine, ye are the branches” (John 15:5). Of the three good trees, the vine pictures one part of a covenant man’s walk: the entry. The fig shows how he keeps the Law. The olive shows what Yahuah pours into him. The vine shows how he gets in at all. The vine is also the first of the three to bear. As supporting evidence from the land, its first-ripe grapes come in midsummer (Numbers 13:20), and the walk begins where the fruit of the year begins.

Jotham’s vine named the one thing it would not give up: “my wine, which cheereth God and man.” The wine is *tirosh*, new wine. To cheer is *samach*, to make glad, to rejoice. The vine’s gift is gladness, and it is gladness on both sides at once. This study follows that gladness through the man’s entry into covenant.

## The Wine Is the Blood

Scripture calls wine “the pure blood of the grape” (Deuteronomy 32:14). At the last meal Yahushua took the cup and said, “this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” (Matthew 26:28). The cup is the covenant door.

Every covenant entry in Scripture came through blood. In Egypt, “when I see the blood, I will pass over you” (Exodus 12:13). At Sinai, “Behold the blood of the covenant” (Exodus 24:8). Now every believer has “boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Yahushua” (Hebrews 10:19). The vine’s wine is that blood, and no man walks in covenant who did not come in under it.

## How the Wine Cheers God

Under the Law, wine cheered Yahuah at the altar. It was not drunk. It was poured out beside the sacrifice as a drink offering, “of a sweet savour unto Yahuah” (Numbers 15:10). The blood of the grape poured out pleased Him.

Yahushua showed what that pleasure is. “Joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth” (Luke 15:7). When the lost son came home, the father said, “let us eat, and be merry: For this my son was dead, and is alive again” (15:23-24). Zephaniah saw the same: Yahuah “will rejoice over thee with joy” (Zephaniah 3:17). The entry of one man under the blood is what cheers God.

## How the Wine Cheers Man

The wine cheers the man too. It is “wine that maketh glad the heart of man” (Psalm 104:15), and *glad* is *samach*, the vine’s word. The man who has just come in carries that gladness out with him. The eunuch was immersed and “went on his way rejoicing” (Acts 8:39). The jailer at Philippi “rejoiced, believing in God with all his house” (Acts 16:34). Isaiah said it first: “I will greatly rejoice in Yahuah … for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation” (Isaiah 61:10).

Yahushua’s first sign was wine at a wedding (John 2:9-11). Scripture calls the covenant a marriage: “I am married unto you” (Jeremiah 3:14). A man’s entry by the blood is his entry into that marriage, and the wine at Cana is the gladness of the wedding feast.

## Why the Vine Would Not Trade

To take the throne, the vine would have to leave its wine and go “to be promoted,” *nua*, to sway over the trees (Judges 9:13). It would trade the gladness of entry for the gladness of rule.

Yahushua stopped His own disciples from making that trade. They came back thrilled that “even the devils are subject unto us.” He answered, “in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven” (Luke 10:17, 20). Do not rejoice in power. Rejoice that you got in. That is the vine’s answer to the trees.

## The Gladness Stays by Keeping

Entry is not the end of the walk. Yahushua told the branches, “If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love; even as I have kept my Father’s commandments.” Then He tied it straight to the vine’s gift: “These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full” (John 15:10-11). The gladness of entry stays full in the man who goes on to keep the commandments. That keeping is the fig, the next witness.

Much modern teaching says that once a man has entered, nothing can put him out. The vine says otherwise. “Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away” (John 15:2), and the branch that does not abide “is cast forth … and they are burned” (15:6). Those branches were in the vine. They had entered. A vine is good for one thing, and Ezekiel says its wood is fit only for the fire if it bears nothing (Ezekiel 15:2-4).

## The Wrong Cup

There is a counterfeit gladness. Moses warned of a vine “of the vine of Sodom,” whose wine is “the poison of dragons” (Deuteronomy 32:32-33). Paul drew the line: “Ye cannot drink the cup of the Master, and the cup of devils” (1 Corinthians 10:21). The harlot of Revelation makes the nations “drunk with the wine of her fornication” (Revelation 17:2). Her wine cheers for a night. The covenant wine cheers God and man forever.

## The Sign of a True Believer

The vine gives the first sign of a true believer: he came in under the blood, and he is glad. His joy is not in power or position but that his name is written in heaven (Luke 10:20). And his gladness is not a feeling that fades. It stays full because he keeps the commandments: “that my joy might remain in you” (John 15:10-11).

John put the sign in one line: “hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments” (1 John 2:3). The blood brings a man in, and “if we walk in the light … the blood of Yahushua … cleanseth us from all sin” (1 John 1:7). A man who claims the cup but has no gladness in Yahuah’s commands, or whose gladness comes from another cup, has not shown the sign of the vine.

## How Messiah Walked It

Messiah is the vine walk in full. He is the vine, and the wine is His own blood. Like the drink offering poured out beside the sacrifice, He “poured out his soul unto death” (Isaiah 53:12). He drank the true cup to the bottom: “Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done” (Luke 22:42). On the stake they offered Him a false cup, “wine mingled with myrrh,” and “he received it not” (Mark 15:23).

And He did it for the vine’s own gift. He “for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God” (Hebrews 12:2). The joy set before Him was the gladness of God and man when men come in. He drank the cup so that the cup could cheer.

## The Vine at the End

At the last meal Yahushua said He would not drink the fruit of the vine again “until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father’s kingdom” (Matthew 26:29). Revelation shows that day, and it is full of the vine’s word: “Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come.” “Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb” (Revelation 19:7, 9). The cup that let the man in is lifted again at the wedding, and God and man are glad together.
