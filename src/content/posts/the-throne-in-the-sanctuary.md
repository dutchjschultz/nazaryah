## Introduction

> **Hebrews 8:1–2**
>
> *Now of the things which we have spoken this is the sum: We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens; a minister of the sanctuary, and of the true tabernacle, which Yahuah pitched, and not man.*

Hebrews places two things in the heavens in the same breath: a throne and a sanctuary. Many readers picture them as two separate places, a royal court on one side of heaven and a temple on the other. Scripture paints a simpler picture. The throne of Yahuah stands inside His sanctuary. It always has.

## Part I — The Earthly Copy

### A Pattern From the Mountain

Moses did not design the tabernacle. He copied it. Yahuah told him to make everything "after the pattern of the tabernacle" shown to him on the mountain (Exodus 25:9, 40), and Hebrews says plainly that the earthly priests served "unto the example and shadow of heavenly things" (Hebrews 8:5). Whatever stood in the earthly tabernacle was a picture of something real in heaven.

### The Seat Between the Cherubim

The innermost room held the ark of the covenant, covered by the mercy seat, with two golden cherubim over it. Yahuah told Moses exactly what that spot was for:

> **Exodus 25:22**
>
> *And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony.*

From then on, Israel described Yahuah as the One who "dwelleth between the cherubims" (1 Samuel 4:4; 2 Kings 19:15; Psalm 80:1). Psalm 99:1 says He "sitteth between the cherubims." A king who sits is a king on a throne. The mercy seat was Yahuah's throne on earth, and it sat in the holy of holies.

### The Word for Mercy Seat

> **כַּפֹּרֶת (kapporet, H3727)** — the mercy seat; the covering over the ark where atoning blood was sprinkled. Greek: **ἱλαστήριον (hilasterion, G2435)**, used for the same object in Hebrews 9:5.

Hebrews 9:5 describes "the cherubims of glory shadowing the mercyseat." That is the same scene Hebrews 8:1 calls "the throne of the Majesty." The seat of mercy and the seat of rule are one seat.

## Part II — The Old Testament Thread

### One Room, Two Names

The prophets join throne and sanctuary without any gap between them. Jeremiah says it in a single line:

> **Jeremiah 17:12**
>
> *A glorious high throne from the beginning is the place of our sanctuary.*

David says the same: "Yahuah is in his holy temple, Yahuah's throne is in heaven" (Psalm 11:4). Isaiah saw Yahuah "sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple" (Isaiah 6:1). The throne was not down the hall from the temple. Its train filled the temple.

## Part III — New Testament Fulfillment

### The Furniture Around the Throne

When John is called up into heaven, he sees a throne first (Revelation 4:2). Then he looks around it, and every piece he names is tabernacle furniture. Seven lamps of fire burn before the throne (Revelation 4:5), the golden lampstand of the Holy Place. A sea of glass lies before the throne (Revelation 4:6), the bronze laver, which Solomon's temple called the "molten sea" (1 Kings 7:23). The golden altar stands "before the throne" (Revelation 8:3), the altar of incense. The ark of his testament is seen "in his temple" (Revelation 11:19). And one voice comes from both places at once: "out of the temple of heaven, from the throne" (Revelation 16:17).

The altar of incense settles the matter. In Exodus, Yahuah told Moses to set that altar "before the vail that is by the ark of the testimony, before the mercy seat" (Exodus 30:6). In Revelation the same altar stands "before the throne." Moses' words and John's words describe the same position. What Moses called the mercy seat, John calls the throne.

### The Priest Who Sat Down

This is why Hebrews 8:1 matters. Yahushua is not described as standing in an outer room waiting for a later day to go in. He is "set on the right hand of the throne," and the throne is in the holy of holies. He entered "once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption" (Hebrews 9:12), "into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us" (Hebrews 9:24). After offering one sacrifice for sins forever, He "sat down" (Hebrews 10:12).

This answers the teaching, still current in some churches, that the Messiah ministered only in the outer room of the heavenly sanctuary until 1844 and then moved into the inner room to begin a judgment. Hebrews puts Him at the throne, in the presence of Yahuah, from the day He ascended. A priest seated at the mercy seat has nowhere further to go.

### One Throne, One Occupant

Hebrews 8:1 also shows how many sit on the throne. The throne belongs to "the Majesty in the heavens." Yahushua is at its right hand, serving as "a minister of the sanctuary." One rules; the other ministers beside Him. Revelation 5:7 keeps the same order: the Lamb comes "and took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne." The Lamb approaches the One on the throne. He is not that One.

Nor is there a third person seated there. The "seven Spirits of God" in Revelation 4:5 are not a figure on a throne. They are the seven lamps burning before it, the lampstand of the sanctuary. The heavenly throne room holds one throne, one Majesty upon it, and one High Priest at His right hand.

## Part IV — Where the Believer Stands

Hebrews tells believers to "come boldly unto the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16). That boldness is not a man walking up to the throne on his own. Yahushua said it plainly: "no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6). The picture is a sanctuary picture. When Messiah died, the veil of the Holy Place was torn (Matthew 27:51), opening the outer room to the believer as a priest. Only the High Priest goes into the inner room, by His own blood.

The believer draws near from the Holy Place, at the altar of incense that stands "before the mercy seat." There his prayers rise as incense (Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3–4), and they reach the throne of favor through the One who "ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Hebrews 7:25).

## Conclusion

The throne and the sanctuary were never two places. The sanctuary is the house, and the throne is its center. Revelation ends with that same throne no longer hidden behind a veil but standing among the people: "the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him: and they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads" (Revelation 22:3–4). One face, one name, and a sanctuary where the throne is finally in plain view.
