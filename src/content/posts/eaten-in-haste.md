Most meals are eaten sitting down. The first Pesach was eaten by a people dressed to walk out the door, and every part of that meal pictures the believer leaving the world.

## Dressed to Leave

Yahuah gave the instructions in detail: "with your loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and ye shall eat it in haste" (12:11). No one ate that meal planning to stay. Paul uses the same picture for the believer, whose feet are "shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace" (Ephesians 6:15).

## Behind the Blood

Each house killed its lamb and struck the blood on the two side posts and the lintel with a bunch of hyssop (12:7, 22). Then came the command: "none of you shall go out at the door of his house until the morning" (12:22). The deliverance was inside the blood. When the destroyer passed through Egypt, Yahuah would "see the blood… and pass over the door" (12:23). No one in that house earned his place there by being better than his neighbor in Egypt. He was safe because he was behind the blood Yahuah provided.

## Bitter Herbs

The lamb was eaten roasted, "with unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs" (12:8). Egypt had "made their lives bitter with hard bondage" (1:14), and the bitterness was eaten on the way out so it would not be forgotten. A believer leaving the world takes with him a clear memory of what the world's service cost him.

## The Leaven Goes Out

Pesach on the fourteenth day was followed at once by Hag HaMatzot, seven days beginning on the fifteenth (Leviticus 23:5–6). For those seven days no leaven could be found in Israel's houses (12:15, 19). The order matters. The blood came first, and the cleaning came after. Israel did not clear out the leaven to earn the blood; they cleared it out because the blood had already covered them.

Paul reads the feast that way for believers: "Purge out therefore the old leaven… For even Messiah our Pesach is sacrificed for us: therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:7–8). He tells the assembly at Corinth to keep the feast, and he names the leaven: malice and wickedness. Yahushua named another kind: "the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees" (Matthew 16:12). Sin and false teaching both go out of the house once the blood is on the door.

## A Night to Be Observed

Israel went out at the end of four hundred thirty years, "even the selfsame day" (12:41). Moses called it "a night to be much observed unto Yahuah… of all the children of Israel in their generations" (12:42). The meal for leaving was made a memorial for every generation, so that each one would remember it was brought out and did not walk out on its own strength (13:3).

## Revelation

The Lamb whose blood marked the door is the Lamb John sees before the throne: "thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to Elohim by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation" (Revelation 5:9). The meal eaten standing up in Egypt looks ahead to a meal at rest: "Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9).
