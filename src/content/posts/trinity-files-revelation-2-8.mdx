**Revelation 2:8**

**The First and the Last**

*Smyrna's title — who died, and is alive*

— The Standing Stone —

> **Behind "LORD" in your Bible lies a hidden name — in the Hebrew it is Yahuah** *Psalm 83:18*; **Yahuah is the Father** *Isaiah 63:16*; **Yahuah is the only God, beside Him there is no other** *Isaiah 45:5*; **therefore Yahuah the Father is the only true God, leaving no room for a second or third person** *1 Corinthians 8:6*.

Reference Piece

**The Titles He Was Given**

*A title of Yahuah on the Son is worn by gift — a shared title was never a shared identity*

[Foundation: The Titles He Was Given →](/foundations/the-titles-he-was-given)

***1 — The Claim***

To the assembly at Smyrna, Yahushua announces himself again as "the first and the last." On Yahuah's own lips that title is a claim to be the only God: "I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God" (Isaiah 44:6), and again, "I am the first, I also am the last" (Isaiah 48:12). Both times the title is fenced by His sole deity — there is no God beside Him. So when the Son speaks the same words, they are read as the same claim: that Yahushua must be Yahuah.

***2 — The Title Comes With Its Own Definition***

As at the opening of the book, the title is not left to stand alone. The same clause that carries it tells you who the speaker is:

> **Revelation 2:8**
>
> *These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive…*

"Which was dead, and is alive." The One who holds the title died and was raised — which the eternal Yahuah, of whom death was never true, did not and could not do. The Father's deathlessness is His own, underived, held by no one else: He "only hath immortality" (1 Timothy 6:16). The Son is "alive for evermore," but the same breath says why — he "was dead" first (Revelation 1:18). One holds life in Himself; the other was given it back out of a grave. And where Isaiah fenced the title with "beside me there is no God," the Son never does — he fences it with a grave. The words are Isaiah's; the meaning is set by the empty tomb. He is first as "the first begotten of the dead" (Revelation 1:5), and last as "the last Adam" (1 Corinthians 15:45) — the first and the last of a new creation, not a second timeless God beside the One who has no beginning.

***3 — Why Smyrna Hears It***

And the title is handed to this assembly on purpose. Of the seven churches, Smyrna is the dying one — facing prison and the sword, told, "be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life" (Revelation 2:10). To a people about to die, Yahushua does not announce himself as the God who cannot die. He announces himself as the One who was dead, and is alive — the firstborn who went down into the grave ahead of them and came up the other side. The death they dread, their Master has already walked; the crown waits past it.

Conclusion

**The Verdict**

> **"The first and the last, which was dead, and is alive." The One who died is not the deathless God — he is the first to rise, and never die again.**
