Leaving the world is seldom a single clean break. After the fourth plague, Pharaoh stopped saying no and started bargaining. Each offer looked like a step toward freedom, and each one left part of Israel in Egypt. The same four offers come to every believer who sets out to leave.

## Bargain One: Worship Here

"Go ye, sacrifice to your Elohim in the land" (8:25). Pharaoh would allow the worship as long as it stayed on Egyptian ground. Moses refused: the sacrifices Israel offered were an abomination to Egypt, and Yahuah had commanded a three days' journey into the wilderness (8:26–27). Worship that never leaves the world's ground is not what Yahuah asked for. Paul asks what fellowship light has with darkness, and gives the answer: "come out from among them, and be ye separate" (2 Corinthians 6:14, 17).

## Bargain Two: Not Too Far

"I will let you go… only ye shall not go very far away" (8:28). Leave, but stay close enough to come back. This is the believer who leaves the world but keeps one foot near the border. James calls it plainly: "the friendship of the world is enmity with Elohim" (James 4:4). Yahushua said that anyone who puts his hand to the plough and looks back is not "fit for the kingdom" (Luke 9:62).

## Bargain Three: Leave the Children

"Go now ye that are men, and serve Yahuah" (10:11). The men could go if the families stayed behind as a guarantee of their return. Moses had already answered: "We will go with our young and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters" (10:9). A believer who leaves while his children stay to be raised by Egypt has not fully left. The Law puts the teaching of the children on the parents, at home, on the road, lying down and rising up (Deuteronomy 6:7).

## Bargain Four: Leave the Goods

"Go ye, serve Yahuah; only let your flocks and your herds be stayed" (10:24). The people could go, but what they owned would stay under Pharaoh's hand. Moses' answer is the end of all bargaining: "there shall not an hoof be left behind; for thereof must we take to serve Yahuah our Elohim; and we know not with what we must serve Yahuah, until we come thither" (10:26). What a man owns either serves Yahuah or keeps him tied to Egypt. "Ye cannot serve Elohim and mammon" (Matthew 6:24), and "where your treasure is, there will your heart be also" (Matthew 6:21).

## All or Nothing

The four bargains offered worship, then distance, then the men, then the people, each one a little more than the last, and each one keeping something back. Moses would not trade any of it, because Yahuah had not asked for part of Israel. He asked for all of it. Paul writes the same demand for the believer: present your bodies "a living sacrifice," and "be not conformed to this world" (Romans 12:1–2). Pharaoh's offers still sound reasonable today, and they still leave part of a person in Egypt.

## Revelation

Revelation describes those who left Egypt with nothing held back: "These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth" (Revelation 14:4). No hoof was left behind, no child left with Pharaoh, and they kept no road open back to Egypt.
