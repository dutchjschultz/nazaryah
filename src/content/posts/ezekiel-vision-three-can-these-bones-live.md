The vision is undated, but its place sets the time. In chapter 33 a survivor arrives with the news, “The city is smitten” (33:21), in 585 BC. Everything after that looks forward: the Shepherd (34), the new heart (36), and the dry bones (37). The captives have lost their city, temple, and king. This vision is Yahuah’s answer.

## The Vision

The hand of Yahuah sets Ezekiel down in a valley full of dry bones. Yahuah asks, “can these bones live?” and Ezekiel answers, “O Adonai Yahuah, thou knowest.” He prophesies to the bones, and they come together, bone to bone, with sinews, flesh, and skin, but no breath. He prophesies to the wind, the breath enters them, and they stand up, an exceeding great army. Yahuah explains: the bones are the whole house of Israel, who say their hope is lost, and He will open their graves, put His spirit in them, and bring them home. Then Ezekiel joins two sticks into one in his hand.

## The Valley

The word for valley, *biqah*, is the plain where Ezekiel first saw the glory and fell on his face (3:22-23). The place of glory is now full of the dead.

Asked if the bones can live, Ezekiel hands the question back: “O Adonai Yahuah, thou knowest” (37:3).

## The Word, Then the Breath

Ezekiel is told to prophesy first: “O ye dry bones, hear the word of Yahuah” (37:4). The bones come together, sinews and flesh come up, and skin covers them, “but there was no breath in them” (37:8). Then he prophesies to the breath, “Come from the four winds, O breath,” and they live and stand on their feet (37:9-10).

In Hebrew, breath, wind, and spirit are one word, *ruach*. Verse 14 names it: “And shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live.”

The four winds are every direction. Israel had been scattered to all four, and the breath that raises them comes from all four. Zechariah used the same words for the same scattering: “I have spread you abroad as the four winds of the heaven” (Zechariah 2:6).

## Eden Again

The valley follows the order of the first creation. Yahuah “formed man of the dust of the ground,” and only then “breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul” (Genesis 2:7). First the body, then the breath. In the valley it happens the same way: bones, sinews, flesh, and skin, and then the breath (37:8-10). What Ezekiel watches is a new creation.

The Greek Old Testament used one verb for that breathing in both places, *emphusaō*, to breathe into: Yahuah breathing into Adam (Genesis 2:7) and the breath blowing on the slain (Ezekiel 37:9). The New Testament uses that verb only once. After His resurrection Yahushua stood among His disciples, “he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost” (John 20:22). The breath of Eden and the breath of the valley came into the room.

Some take that verse to mean Yahushua must be Yahuah, since He breathed the way Yahuah breathed into Adam. The valley answers that. In Ezekiel 37 the one who calls the breath is a man: Ezekiel prophesies, and the breath comes (37:9-10). No one says Ezekiel is Yahuah. The breath is Yahuah’s, and He gives it through the one He sends. Yahushua said the same of Himself: the Comforter is the one “whom I will send unto you from the Father” (John 15:26), and Peter said He poured it out “having received of the Father the promise” (Acts 2:33). The breath comes from the Father, through the Son.

## Who the Bones Are

The vision is built from the people’s own words. They were saying, “Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost” (37:11). Yahuah took their saying, turned it into a valley, and answered it.

Yahuah says it plainly: “these bones are the whole house of Israel” (37:11). Both halves. The ten northern tribes, called Israel, Ephraim, or Joseph, had been scattered by Assyria in 722 BC, so long that only dry bones were left. Judah had just fallen to Babylon. Both said the same thing: “Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost.”

Hosea had pictured the north this way. Ephraim “hath mixed himself among the people” (Hosea 7:8) and was named “not my people” (Hosea 1:9). Yet “there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living El” (Hosea 1:10). Yahuah’s answer to the bones: “I will open your graves … and shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live” (37:12-14).

## The Two Sticks

Ezekiel writes “For Judah” on one stick and “For Joseph, the stick of Ephraim” on another, and they become one in his hand (37:16-17). “They shall be no more two nations, neither shall they be divided into two kingdoms any more at all” (37:22). One King holds them: “David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd: they shall also walk in my judgments, and observe my statutes, and do them” (37:24). David had been dead four hundred years. This is David’s Son. Raised by the breath, joined under one King, then walking in the statutes, the same order as 36:27.

## How the Sticks Become One

Yahushua: “other sheep I have, which are not of this fold … and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd” (John 10:16). John: He would die to “gather together in one the children of Elohim that were scattered abroad” (John 11:52). Paul takes Hosea’s promise to scattered Ephraim and applies it to believers called “not of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles” (Romans 9:24-26).

So the sticks are Judah and Ephraim made one, and it happens as believing Judah and the scattered of Ephraim, found among the nations, are joined with the strangers into one olive tree (Romans 11:17), “fellowcitizens with the saints” (Ephesians 2:19). The pulpit splits the house back into two sticks, Israel and the church. Ezekiel saw one stick.

## Not 1948

Ezekiel attaches terms no political event has met: Yahuah’s spirit in the people (37:14), one King from David’s line (37:24), no more idols (37:23), and His sanctuary among them forever (37:26-28). These are covenant terms, met by the Shepherd, not by a vote of nations.

## Two Graves Opened

Yahushua described both stages. Now: “the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of Elohim: and they that hear shall live” (John 5:25). Later: “all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and shall come forth” (John 5:28-29). Ezekiel’s valley holds both. The Nazaryah study [*Two Passages Against Four Doctrines*](/blog/two-passages-against-four-doctrines) walks through John 5.

## Bringing It Home

Believers still stand in that valley saying “our hope is lost.” The order has not changed: the word first, then the breath.

Verse 8 is the warning: every part in place, and no breath. Paul called it “a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof” (2 Timothy 3:5). Men can assemble a religion. Only Yahuah can breathe into it.

And the raised do not lie still. They stand up and “walk in my judgments, and observe my statutes” (37:24).

## Where It Lands

Revelation quotes the valley: “the Spirit of life from Elohim entered into them, and they stood upon their feet” (Revelation 11:11; Ezekiel 37:10). At the end, “the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead” (Revelation 20:13). No grave is left closed.
