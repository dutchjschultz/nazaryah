This vision is not dated, but its place in the book tells the time. In chapter 33 a man who escaped from Jerusalem walks into the camp of the captives and says it in four words: “The city is smitten” (33:21). That was 585 BC. The temple had burned and the king was in chains. The vision of the dry bones is Yahuah’s answer to a people who believed it was over.

## The Vision

Ezekiel is set down in a valley of very dry bones and asked whether they can live. He prophesies, and the bones come together with sinews, flesh, and skin, but no breath. He prophesies to the wind, the breath enters, and they stand up, an exceeding great army. Yahuah says the bones are the whole house of Israel, and then has Ezekiel join two sticks, Judah and Joseph, into one in his hand.

## The Valley

“The hand of Yahuah was upon me, and carried me out in the spirit of Yahuah, and set me down in the midst of the valley which was full of bones” (37:1). The word for valley, *biqah*, is the same word used for the plain where Ezekiel first saw the glory of Yahuah, years before. There the glory stood, and he fell on his face (3:22-23). The place where he once saw glory is now covered with the dead.

Then comes the question. “Son of man, can these bones live?” Ezekiel neither says yes nor no. “O Adonai Yahuah, thou knowest” (37:3). He hands the question back to the only One who can answer it.

## The Word, Then the Breath

The first thing Ezekiel is told to do is speak: “O ye dry bones, hear the word of Yahuah” (37:4). And as he speaks, “there was a noise, and behold a shaking, and the bones came together, bone to his bone” (37:7). Sinews come, then flesh, then skin.

Then verse 8: “but there was no breath in them.” Everything looks right, and nothing is alive.

So Ezekiel prophesies a second time, not to the bodies but to the breath: “Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live” (37:9). “And the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet, an exceeding great army” (37:10).

In Hebrew, breath, wind, and spirit are one word: *ruach*. It carries Ezekiel out in verse 1, blows as the four winds in verse 9, and enters the bodies in verse 10. In verse 14 Yahuah names it: “And shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live.”

The four winds are every direction. Israel had been scattered to all four and the breath that raises them comes from all four. Zechariah used the same words for the same scattering: “I have spread you abroad as the four winds of the heaven” (Zechariah 2:6).

## Eden Again

Look at the order Ezekiel watched: first the body, then the breath. That is the order of the first creation. “Yahuah Elohim formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul” (Genesis 2:7). The valley follows the same two steps. What Ezekiel is watching is a new creation.

The Greek Old Testament tied the two scenes together with one verb, *emphusaō*, to breathe into. It is the word for Yahuah breathing into Adam (Genesis 2:7), and it is the word for the breath blowing on the slain in the valley (Ezekiel 37:9). The New Testament uses it only once. After His resurrection Yahushua stood among His disciples, and “he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost” (John 20:22). The breath of Eden and the breath of the valley came into that room.

Some take that verse as proof that Yahushua must be Yahuah, since He breathed the way Yahuah breathed into Adam. The valley answers that. In Ezekiel 37 the one who calls the breath is a man. Ezekiel prophesies, and the breath comes (37:9-10). No one says Ezekiel is Yahuah. The breath belongs to Yahuah, and He gives it through the one He sends. Yahushua said so Himself: the Comforter is the one “whom I will send unto you from the Father” (John 15:26). Peter said He poured it out “having received of the Father the promise” (Acts 2:33). The breath comes from the Father, through the Son.

## Who the Bones Are

Yahuah explains the vision Himself, and He builds it out of the people’s own words. They were saying, “Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost” (37:11). So He showed Ezekiel a valley of dried bones and said, “these bones are the whole house of Israel.” Not one tribe. The whole house, both halves of it.

By Ezekiel’s day the nation had been split in two for more than three hundred years. The ten northern tribes, called Israel, Ephraim, or Joseph, had been carried away by Assyria in 722 BC and scattered among the nations. Judah had just been cut down by Babylon. Both halves lay in the valley, the long dead and the newly dead.

Hosea had pictured the north exactly this way. “Ephraim, he hath mixed himself among the people” (Hosea 7:8). Yahuah told Hosea to name his son Lo-ammi, “for ye are not my people” (Hosea 1:9). But in the very next verse came the promise: “in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living El” (Hosea 1:10). Ezekiel’s valley is that promise made visible.

## The Two Sticks

Right after the valley, Ezekiel writes “For Judah” on one stick and “For Joseph, the stick of Ephraim” on another, and they become one stick in his hand (37:16-17). Yahuah explains: “I will make them one nation in the land … and one king shall be king to them all: and they shall be no more two nations, neither shall they be divided into two kingdoms any more at all” (37:22). The bones show the house raised; the sticks show it joined, under one King: “David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd: they shall also walk in my judgments, and observe my statutes, and do them” (37:24).

David had been dead four hundred years. This is David’s Son. And the order is the same as the new heart of the chapter before, “I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes” (36:27): the breath does not replace the walk. It makes the walk possible.

## How the Sticks Become One

The New Testament shows how this joining happens. Yahushua said, “other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd” (John 10:16). One shepherd, the same as Ezekiel 37:24. John adds that Yahushua would die “not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of Elohim that were scattered abroad” (John 11:52). Gathered together in one: two sticks in one hand.

Paul took Hosea’s promise to scattered Ephraim, “I will call them my people, which were not my people,” and applied it to the believers Yahuah called “not of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles” (Romans 9:24-26). The northern tribes had been sown among the nations, and they came back the way the nations came in: through the Shepherd. Believing Judah and the scattered of Ephraim, found among the nations, together with the strangers who joined them, are grafted into one olive tree (Romans 11:17). They are “fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of Elohim” (Ephesians 2:19).

The pulpit splits that house back into two sticks: Israel over here, the church over there. Ezekiel saw one stick in his hand, under one King, walking in one Law.

## Not 1948

This chapter is often preached as the founding of the modern State of Israel in 1948. But Ezekiel attaches terms that no political event has met. Yahuah’s own spirit is put in the people (37:14). One King rules them, “David my servant” (37:24). They no longer defile themselves with their idols (37:23). And Yahuah’s sanctuary stands in their midst forever (37:26-28). These are covenant terms, met by the Shepherd and the spirit, not by a vote of nations.

## Two Graves Opened

Yahuah’s promise in the valley was, “I will open your graves, and cause you to come up out of your graves” (37:12). Yahushua described that opening in two stages. “The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of Elohim: and they that hear shall live” (John 5:25). That is now, the spiritually dead hearing the word. Then He went further: “the hour is coming, in the which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and shall come forth” (John 5:28-29). That is the resurrection. Ezekiel’s valley holds both. The Nazaryah study [*Two Passages Against Four Doctrines*](/blog/two-passages-against-four-doctrines) walks through John 5 in full.

## Why This Vision, and Why Now

The captives saw a dead nation, and Yahuah did not argue with them. He agreed the bones were very dry, then showed that dead is not the end of what He can do. The vision comes right after the city’s fall and right after the promise of a new heart in chapter 36. The promise said what He would do. The valley shows how.

## Bringing It Home

Many believers have stood in that valley and said “our hope is lost.” The answer has not changed: the word first, then the breath.

But verse 8 is a warning too. It is possible to have every part in place, bones, sinews, flesh, and skin, and still have no breath. Paul described the same thing: “Having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof” (2 Timothy 3:5). Men can assemble a religion. Only Yahuah can breathe into it.

And when the breath comes, the bones do not lie there. They stand up and walk.

## Where It Lands

Revelation quotes this valley almost word for word. The two witnesses lie dead in the street, and then “the Spirit of life from Elohim entered into them, and they stood upon their feet” (Revelation 11:11), the same words as Ezekiel 37:10. And at the end, the graves open for good: “the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them” (Revelation 20:13). The valley that began with a question, “can these bones live?”, ends with no grave left closed.
