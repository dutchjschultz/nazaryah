This is Part Two of the first burden (9:1). Part One ended with Yahuah promising to whistle for His scattered flock and gather them under a shepherd from Judah. Chapter 11 shows what happened when the Shepherd came: the flock did not want Him. The burden closes at the price they paid to be rid of Him, fulfilled in 31 AD.

## The Burden, Part Two

The chapter opens with fire in the cedars of Lebanon and shepherds howling. Zechariah is told to feed a flock marked for slaughter, whose owners kill them and whose sellers thank Yahuah for the profit. He takes two staffs, Beauty and Bands, and cuts off three shepherds, but the flock loathes him. He breaks Beauty, and only the poor of the flock recognize the word of Yahuah. He asks for his wages, they weigh out thirty pieces of silver, and he throws it to the potter in the house of Yahuah. He breaks Bands, the brotherhood between Judah and Israel. Then he is told to play a worthless shepherd, because Yahuah will raise one up who eats the flock instead of feeding it.

## The Flock of the Slaughter

Chapter 11 opens with a lament: “Open thy doors, O Lebanon, that the fire may devour thy cedars” (11:1). Cedar of Lebanon was the wood of kings and of the temple itself (1 Kings 5:6). The proud cedars burn, the shepherds howl, and the pride of Jordan is spoiled (11:2-3). The chapter states the cost first, then tells how it came about.

Zechariah is told to “feed the flock of the slaughter” (11:4). The men who own the flock kill them “and hold themselves not guilty,” and the men who sell them say, “Blessed be Yahuah; for I am rich” (11:5). Religious men grew rich off the flock and thanked Yahuah for it.

Zechariah takes two staffs. One he calls Beauty, meaning favor or pleasantness. The other he calls Bands, meaning cords that tie together (11:7). He cuts off three shepherds in one month. The text does not name them, and the study will not guess. But the flock does not want him: “my soul lothed them, and their soul also abhorred me” (11:8). So he breaks Beauty, the covenant of favor (11:10). Only “the poor of the flock that waited upon me knew that it was the word of Yahuah” (11:11).

## Thirty Pieces of Silver

Then the shepherd asks for his wages. “So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver” (11:12). In the Law, thirty shekels was the price paid when an ox gored a slave to death (Exodus 21:32). They valued the shepherd at the price of a dead slave. Yahuah answers with heavy sarcasm: “Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them.” Zechariah throws the silver to the potter “in the house of Yahuah” (11:13).

Matthew records the same silver, the same temple floor, and the same potter. Judas threw the thirty pieces down in the temple, and the priests used them to buy the potter’s field (Matthew 27:3-10). Matthew names Jeremiah there. Jeremiah also went down to the potter’s house and also bought a field (Jeremiah 18:2; 32:9), and Matthew draws from both prophets together. Then the second staff, Bands, is broken, “that I might break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel” (11:14).

## The Worthless Shepherd

The burden ends with a warning. Zechariah takes up “the instruments of a foolish shepherd,” because Yahuah will raise up a shepherd who will not visit the lost or heal the broken, but “shall eat the flesh of the fat” (11:15-16). “Woe to the idol shepherd that leaveth the flock! the sword shall be upon his arm, and upon his right eye” (11:17). Reject the good Shepherd, and a worthless one takes his place. Yahushua said it the same way: “I am come in my Father’s name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive” (John 5:43).

## Bringing It Home

Part Two asks one question: what price do you put on Him?

Israel’s leaders weighed Him out at the price of a slave. Every generation does its own weighing. The shepherds who said “Blessed be Yahuah; for I am rich” are still around, feeding on the flock and calling it blessing. The poor of the flock who wait on Yahuah are still the ones who recognize His word when they hear it.

And the warning stands. Anyone who will not have the true Shepherd will get a worthless one, who takes the fat for himself and leaves the broken unhealed. The flock that loathes the good Shepherd does not end up free. It ends up with the idol shepherd.

## Where It Lands

The Shepherd who was sold for thirty pieces is the Lamb who “redeemed us to Elohim by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation” (Revelation 5:9). They priced Him at a slave’s wage. Yahuah gave Him as the price for every nation. In the end the Lamb “shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters” (Revelation 7:17), the true Shepherd doing what the worthless one never would.
