This reading companions the breakdown New Wine, Old Bottles, and nothing is argued here; the words run as they stand, with the meaning of each image carried alongside it.

> **LUKE 5:33–39 — KJV**
>
> And they said unto him, *[the complaint was made at Levi's feast, by men who had just watched Him eat with tax collectors and with the people they would not sit beside]* Why do the disciples of John fast often, and make prayers, and likewise the disciples of the Pharisees; *[the law of Yahuah appoints one fast in the year, the tenth day of the seventh month, and affliction is its stated purpose; the schedule under dispute was twice in the week, and a Pharisee is on record naming it to Yahuah as his own practice]* but thine eat and drink? *[the fast Yahuah chose was to loose the bands of wickedness, to deal bread to the hungry, to bring the cast out into the house and to cover the naked, and that was being kept at the very table they were pointing at]*
>
> And he said unto them, Can ye make the children of the bridechamber fast, *[the groom's own companions, the men who stand with him through the feast; Samson was given thirty of them and the feast ran seven days]* while the bridegroom is with them? *[Yahuah tells His people their Maker is their husband, and says that as a bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so would He rejoice over them]* But the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then shall they fast in those days. *[the answer appoints a day for fasting rather than ending it, and names the hour it begins]*
>
> And he spake also a parable unto them; No man putteth a piece of a new garment upon an old; *[a garment is what a man is clothed in before Yahuah; when a priest stood before Him in filthy garments they were not mended, the word was to take them off him, and a change of raiment was promised]* if otherwise, then both the new maketh a rent, and the piece that was taken out of the new agreeth not with the old. *[two losses, not one — the new garment is cut up to mend the old one, and the old one tears worse than it was]*
>
> And no man putteth new wine into old bottles; *[new wine is Yahuah's to give and Yahuah's to pour, named with the corn and the oil, poured beside the offering by command, and a blessing is in it while it hangs in the cluster; the bottle is a sewn skin, and no passage in the Hebrew Scriptures assigns a skin bottle any meaning, so none is supplied here]* else the new wine will burst the bottles, and be spilled, and the bottles shall perish. *[wine still working pushes out against whatever holds it, and a skin that has already carried a batch has spent its give; the Hebrew Scriptures show those split skins once, laid out as evidence before men who ruled on what they could see and asked not counsel at the mouth of Yahuah]*
>
> But new wine must be put into new bottles; and both are preserved. *[the passage hands down its own outcome, and the outcome is preservation; the wine is kept and the container is kept, and nothing in the sentence is thrown away or condemned]*
>
> No man also having drunk old wine straightway desireth new: for he saith, The old is better. *[Moab was at ease from his youth and settled on his lees, never emptied from vessel to vessel, so his taste remained in him and his scent was not changed; the words here are the man's own, and the reason he gives is that he is used to what he has]*

Put together, the passage answers the question it was asked, and the question was about how often other men skipped meals. A wedding was going on, the groom was in the room, and mourning has no place at a wedding, which is what a fast is for. A day for fasting was named out loud, and it begins when the groom is taken away. The garment and the wine belong to that same wedding: a covering is exchanged rather than patched, and what is still working is not poured into a container with no give left in it. The verdict the passage states about itself is preservation — new wine in new bottles, and both are kept.

The warning stands where the passage leaves it. Men ruled on what they could see, as Israel ruled on patched clothes and split bottles laid out in front of them without asking counsel at the mouth of Yahuah. The last man in the scene turns down what is new and gives no reason but his own taste, which is what a vessel never emptied from one vessel to another always keeps. The bridegroom was taken away, and the fasting He named began. The wedding He named has not happened yet.
