## What Yahuah Is Doing

Yahuah means to be known. Not guessed at and not argued over, but known, by every nation on the earth.

His way of doing that has never changed. He puts somebody in place to carry it — somebody who lives it out in the open where the rest of the world can watch.

Isaiah is the record of who was holding that assignment. It changes hands twice before the book is finished, and the whole book is readable once a person knows which hand it is in.

## When This Is Happening

Still the third stretch — the work of one man, around 27 AD, and everything that follows from it.

Two passages carry it. Chapter 50, where He speaks about Himself, and the passage that begins at 52:13 and runs to the end of 53. The chapter break there is in the wrong place; 52:13 is where it starts.

## The Ear That Hears

Chapter 42 left a blind servant and a deaf messenger. Chapter 50 answers both.

> “He wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned.”
>
> — Isaiah 50:4

Every morning, the ear opened. The one thing the first servant could not do is the first thing said about this one.

## He Did Not Turn Back

> “I was not rebellious, neither turned away back. I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting.”
>
> — Isaiah 50:5–6

Set that against a nation that turned away over and over. He did the opposite, and it cost Him skin.

Verse 7 says how He managed it: He set His face like a flint. Not because it did not hurt, but because He was not going to be turned.

## Still Called the Servant

> “Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high.”
>
> — Isaiah 52:13

The passage everyone knows as chapter 53 opens with the word servant. Whatever happens in the next twenty verses happens to somebody holding that assignment.

What happens is that He is wrecked. Marred more than any man (52:14). Despised and rejected, a man of sorrows (53:3). Nobody wanted to look at Him.

## Who Is Doing the Redeeming

> “All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and Yahuah hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”
>
> — Isaiah 53:6

Follow the verbs. Yahuah lays it on Him. A few verses later, it pleased Yahuah to bruise him (53:10).

The common picture has the Son buying us back from a Father who had to be talked into it. This passage runs the other direction. The Father is the one redeeming, and what it cost Him was His own Son.

Verse 11 keeps the title in place at the high point of the whole book: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many.

## What He Gets Out of It

> “When thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days.”
>
> — Isaiah 53:10

Seed. Offspring. A household that did not exist before and exists now because of what He did.

That is the line Part Six is built on, and it is answered one chapter later.

## Where It Lands

What was done to Him does not get erased afterward.

> “And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne … stood a Lamb as it had been slain.”
>
> — Revelation 5:6

The marks are still on Him at the throne. The record of chapter 53 is permanent.

## In One Sentence

The one who listened every morning did what the nation would not, took what the nation had coming, and came out of it with a family.
