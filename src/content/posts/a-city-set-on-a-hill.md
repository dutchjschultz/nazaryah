## Where It Sits

The salt and light saying is wedged between the Beatitudes and the declaration about the Law, and the order is not accidental. First Yahushua describes who these people are, using the words of the prophets. Then He tells them what they are for. Then He tells them the standard they live by, down to the stroke of a letter. Function is announced before the commandments are opened, so that nobody could mistake obedience for a private hobby.

## Salt Is Covenant Language

"Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted?" (Matthew 5:13). To a crowd raised on the offerings, salt was not a figure about flavor. It was a requirement at the altar: "every oblation of thy meat offering shalt thou season with salt; neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy God to be lacking from thy meat offering: with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt" (Leviticus 2:13). The portions given to the priests are called "a covenant of salt for ever" (Numbers 18:19), and the kingdom given to David is described the same way (2 Chronicles 13:5).

Salt marks a covenant that holds. So the warning that follows lands harder than it reads in English. Salt without savour is not merely useless seasoning; it is an offering that cannot be brought, and the text says it is cast out and trodden under foot of men.

## Lamps Do Not Make Light

"Ye are the light of the world... Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick" (Matthew 5:14-15). The word behind candle is luchnos, a lamp, and the candlestick is the lampstand. Every Hebrew listener knew the lampstand in the Holy Place, hammered out of one piece of gold, fed with beaten oil, tended morning and evening (Exodus 25:31-37; Leviticus 24:2-4).

A lamp produces nothing. It holds oil and carries a flame it did not make. Yahuah is the source: "Yahuah is my light and my salvation" (Psalm 27:1). The assembly is the stand the light is set on, which is why the saying ends where it does, with men glorifying the Father and not the lamp.

## The City and the Mountain

"A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid" (Matthew 5:14). Isaiah had already put a city on a hill and told what happens there: "the mountain of Yahuah's house shall be established in the top of the mountains... and many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of Yahuah... and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law" (Isaiah 2:2-3). The nations are drawn to the visible city, and what they come for is the Law. The same assignment runs through Isaiah 42:6, 49:6, and 60:1-3.

## What the Good Works Are

"Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 5:16). Moses had already described that exact scene, and he did not leave the works undefined: "Keep therefore and do them; for this is your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the nations, which shall hear all these statutes, and say, Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people" (Deuteronomy 4:6). Nations watching. A people obeying statutes. Recognition going upward.

Moses drives it to the point four verses on: "And what nation is there so great, that hath statutes and judgments so righteous as all this law?" (Deuteronomy 4:8). The good works men see are the commandments being kept in public.

## Where the Pulpit Buried It

Let your light shine is preached as warmth, friendliness, and a good attitude at work, with the good works left carefully undefined. They have to stay undefined, because defining them would require naming statutes, and the same pulpit teaches that the statutes were taken out of the way.

Strip the definition out and the saying still sounds pleasant, but it can no longer do what Deuteronomy 4 says it does. Nations do not look twice at a friendly person. They look at a people living by a law nobody else keeps, and then they ask who gave it.
