## What Yahuah Is Doing

Lamentations is written in the ashes of Jerusalem, burned by Babylon in 586 BC. In it Yahuah keeps both halves of His word: the curse He set before Israel for refusing His commandments (Deuteronomy 28:15), and the road home He promised when they turned back (Deuteronomy 30:1-3).

The five chapters follow a shape the gospel would later fill: a death, a burial, a rising, a sentence declared finished, and a prayer for renewal. Paul said the things of the Law and the Prophets were “a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Messiah” (Colossians 2:17). Lamentations is a shadow. Messiah is the substance that casts it.

## Shadow and Substance

- **The shadow** — Jerusalem dies, one man goes down into the pit, Yahuah draws near and redeems him, the sentence is declared finished, and the people pray to be renewed.
- **The substance** — Yahushua dies, is buried in a hewn tomb behind a stone, is raised by the Father, the price is declared paid, and He lives forevermore.

Each stage covers a whole chapter or a whole section, not one verse. For the sake of space, this study quotes only a few lines from each. A short walk through the full chapter opens each stage, to show that the theme runs from the first verse to the last.

## The Death

**Across the chapters.** Chapters 1 and 2 are all death, from the first line to the last. Chapter 1 says five times that the city has no one to comfort her (1:2, 9, 16, 17, 21). Her children are carried off (1:5), her priests and elders die searching for bread (1:19), and the sword bereaves outside while death waits inside the house (1:20). Chapter 2 uses the Hebrew word for “swallowed up” five times (2:2, 5 twice, 8, 16), as the grave swallows the dead. Children faint in the streets and die in their mothers’ arms (2:11-12), and young and old lie together on the ground (2:21). The chapter ends with no one escaped and no one left (2:22).

**The shadow.** In chapters 1 and 2 the city dies, and she says why: “Yahuah is righteous; for I have rebelled against his commandment” (1:18). Jerusalem died for her own sin.

**The substance.** Messiah died for sin that was not His. “He was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities” (Isaiah 53:5). Daniel said Messiah would be cut off, “but not for himself” (Daniel 9:26). The city carried her own sorrow. Yahushua carried ours (Isaiah 53:4).

## The Burial

**Across the chapter.** The first part of chapter 3 is one long burial, from verse 1 to verse 20. The man is led into darkness and not into light (3:2). He is made old and his bones are broken (3:4). He is walled in, chained, and his prayer is shut out (3:7-8). He is hunted like prey by a bear and a lion (3:10). His teeth are broken with gravel, and he is covered with ashes (3:16). His soul is far from peace, and he says his strength and hope have perished (3:17-18). After the turn to hope (3:21-41), the chapter sinks once more into the pit (3:42-54) before the final rising (3:55-66).

**The shadow.** In chapter 3 one man speaks, and he speaks from a grave. He is set in dark places “as they that be dead of old” (3:6). His ways are walled in “with hewn stone” (3:9). His life is cut off in the pit, and “a stone” is cast upon him (3:53). Waters cover his head, and he says, “I am cut off” (3:54).

**The substance.** Every piece of that grave is filled in at the tomb of Yahushua.

- Cut off: “he was cut off out of the land of the living” (Isaiah 53:8).
- Hewn stone: Joseph laid Him in a tomb “hewn out in the rock” (Matthew 27:60).
- A stone upon him: a great stone was rolled to the door of the tomb and sealed (Matthew 27:60, 66).
- Waters over the head: Jonah cried from the deep with the waters around him (Jonah 2:5), and Yahushua named Jonah’s three days as the one sign He would give (Matthew 12:40).

The road to that grave is in chapter 3 too. The man is the mockery of his people (3:14), and at the cross the passers-by mocked (Matthew 27:39-44). He is filled with wormwood and gall (3:15, 19), and they gave Yahushua vinegar mingled with gall (Matthew 27:34). He gives his cheek to the one who strikes him (3:30), and they struck Yahushua and spat in His face (Matthew 26:67).

## The Rising

**Across the chapter.** The rising comes in two waves. The first is the turn in the middle of the chapter, where hope comes back with the morning and Yahuah is named good to those who wait for Him (3:21-33). The second closes the chapter: the man is heard, answered, redeemed, and his cause is pleaded before the Judge (3:55-66). The chapter goes down to the pit twice and comes up twice.

**The shadow.** From the bottom of the pit the man calls, and the answer comes from Yahuah alone. “I called upon thy name, O Yahuah, out of the low dungeon. Thou hast heard my voice… Thou drewest near in the day that I called upon thee: thou saidst, Fear not… thou hast redeemed my life” (3:55-58).

**The substance.** Yahushua “offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard” (Hebrews 5:7). He did not raise Himself. Elohim raised Him up, “having loosed the pains of death” (Acts 2:24), and Paul names who did it: “Elohim the Father, who raised him from the dead” (Galatians 1:1). One in the pit calls, and One on the throne answers. The pattern shows two, not three.

## The Sentence Finished

**Across the chapter.** Chapter 4 is the record of a sentence carried out, step by step. The gold is dimmed and the stones of the sanctuary are poured out in the streets (4:1). The children beg for bread and no one gives it (4:4). Those slain by the sword are counted better off than those slain by hunger (4:9-10). Then the verdict is spoken: “Yahuah hath accomplished his fury” (4:11). The prophets and priests are named as guilty (4:13), the last hopes in Egypt and the king fail (4:17-20), and the chapter closes on the punishment accomplished (4:22).

**The shadow.** Chapter 4 ends, “The punishment of thine iniquity is accomplished, O daughter of Zion; he will no more carry thee away into captivity” (4:22). The sentence is served.

**The substance.** On the tree Yahushua said, “It is finished” (John 19:30). The price was paid by Yahuah, who “spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all” (Romans 8:32).

## The Prayer

**Across the chapter.** Chapter 5 is prayer from the first verse to the last. It opens, “Remember, O Yahuah” (5:1). Every line is spoken by “we” and “us.” It lays the whole case before the throne: the land lost, the people orphaned, the women defiled, the elders dishonored, the joy gone (5:2-18). Then it lifts its eyes to the throne that lasts forever (5:19) and asks to be turned and renewed (5:21).

**The shadow.** The book ends with the whole people praying: “Turn thou us unto thee, O Yahuah, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old” (5:21).

**The substance.** The Hebrew word for prayer is *tefillah*, from the root *palal*, which means to judge, to step in between, to mediate. It is [a courtroom word](/blog/one-throne-1-the-mediators-word). When Phinehas stood between the people and the plague, Scripture says he “executed judgment” (Psalm 106:30), and the verb there is *palal*. To pray, in Hebrew, is to bring a case before the Judge.

In Lamentations that case could not get through. “When I cry and shout, he shutteth out my prayer” (3:8). “Thou hast covered thyself with a cloud, that our prayer should not pass through” (3:44). The people had no one standing between them and the throne. Messiah is that one now. “There is one Elohim, and one mediator between Elohim and men, the man Messiah Yahushua” (1 Timothy 2:5), and He “ever liveth to make intercession” for those who come unto Elohim by Him (Hebrews 7:25). One Elohim on the throne and one man standing between: two, not three. The prayer of chapter 5 now has a way to the throne.

## Bringing It Home

Jerusalem went into the grave for her own sin.

Yahushua went into the grave for ours, and Yahuah brought Him back up.

Everyone who belongs to Him follows the same road, buried with Him in baptism and raised by the same Father when He comes (Romans 6:4; 1 Corinthians 6:14).

## Where It Lands

In Revelation, the One who went down into the pit speaks: “I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death” (Revelation 1:18).

At the end, death itself is cast into the lake of fire (Revelation 20:14), and the people who prayed “renew our days” hear the answer from the throne: “Behold, I make all things new,” and “there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying” (Revelation 21:4-5).
