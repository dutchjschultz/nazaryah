*Witness Seven in the investigation [**Spoken in Hebrew**](/investigations/spoken-in-hebrew) — back to the case.*

## The Claim

Pulpits teach that Saul of Tarsus received a new name when he was saved: "From Saul to Paul." Some add that he chose Paulus, meaning "small," as an act of humility. Neither idea comes from Luke. Scripture records a man who carried two names from birth, one Hebrew and one Roman, and a writer who used whichever fit the setting.

## No Renaming on the Road

When Yahuah renames a man, Scripture says so, gives the new name, and usually gives the reason: Abram to Abraham (Genesis 17:5), Jacob to Israel (Genesis 32:28). Acts 9 has no speaker, no declaration, and no new name. Ananias greets him as "Brother Saul" (Acts 9:17), and Luke keeps calling him Saul for more than a decade of believing service (Acts 9:22, 26; 11:30; 13:1–2). Then, at Paphos:

> **ACTS 13:9 — KJV**
>
> *Then Saul, (who also is called Paul,) ... set his eyes on him.*

The Greek reads *Saulos de, ho kai Paulos* — "Saul, the one also Paul." *Ho kai* is the ordinary formula for a man known by a second name. It does not say renamed. It says *also*.

## Saoul and Saulos

The Greek text of Acts keeps two forms of the name. Luke's own narration uses *Saulos* (G4569), the name with a Greek ending. But wherever someone speaks to him directly in his own tongue — the voice on the road (Acts 9:4; 22:7; 26:14) and Ananias (Acts 9:17; 22:13) — the text keeps *Saoul* (G4549), the bare Hebrew *Sha'ul* (H7586, "asked"). The risen Messiah never once addresses him as Paul. Witness Eight takes up what that voice reveals about the speech of heaven.

## Why Paul at Paphos

Luke's usage changes before Sergius Paulus, the Roman proconsul of Cyprus (Acts 13:7), where the mission turns outward to the Roman world. *Paulos* (G3972) is the Latin family name *Paulus* in Greek letters. Paul was born a Roman citizen (Acts 22:28), and a citizen of Jewish birth commonly carried a Latin name beside his Hebrew one. Saul of Benjamin carried Paulus for his dealings with Rome, and signed his letters to Roman cities with the name that world knew.

## Where the Renaming Came From

The renaming story came from the Latin church fathers, more than three centuries after Acts. Jerome guessed that Saul took Paulus as a trophy of the proconsul's conversion, while admitting that no Scripture records why. Augustine added that *paulus* means "little." Both guesses assume a change Luke never records. "From Saul to Paul" is a sermon built on a parenthesis.

## The Same Pattern Across the Assembly

Saul is not an exception. The writers repeatedly give a man's Hebrew name and then a second name for the Greek or Roman reader:

- **Joses, called Barnabas** (Acts 4:36) — the Hebrew *Yoseph* with an Aramaic surname built on *bar*, "son," which Luke interprets as "son of consolation."

- **Simeon, called Niger** (Acts 13:1) — the Hebrew *Shim'on* with the Latin *Niger*, "black." The same verse lists Manaen, the Hebrew *Menachem*, and ends with Saul, still called Saul.

- **Thomas, called Didymus** (John 11:16; 20:24; 21:2) — *Thomas* is the Hebrew *ta'om* (H8380), "twin" (Genesis 25:24), and *Didymos* is the Greek word for twin. John gives one word twice, once in the disciple's tongue and once in the reader's.

- **Cephas**, "which is by interpretation, A stone" (John 1:42); **Tabitha**, "which by interpretation is called Dorcas" (Acts 9:36); **John, whose surname was Mark** (Acts 12:12).

## The Interpreted Title

The formula "which is, being interpreted" means the writer has stopped to translate. The most consequential instance is not a name but a title:

> **JOHN 1:41 — KJV**
>
> *We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ.*

*Messias* (G3323) is the Hebrew *Mashiach* (H4899), "anointed"; *Christos* is the Greek word chosen to translate it. The disciples found the Mashiach, and John rendered it for Greek readers. A Greek author writing for Greeks has nothing to interpret. These texts keep stopping to translate because Hebrew is underneath them.

## The Name That Is Coming

Scripture does promise a new name, but not on the Damascus road, and not by a translator's hand:

> **REVELATION 2:17 — KJV**
>
> *To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna, and will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it.*

That name will need no interpreter.
