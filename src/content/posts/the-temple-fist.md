## What Came West

Kung fu came to America in the early 1970s through Bruce Lee's films and the television series *Kung Fu* (1972–1975), whose hero was a Shaolin monk wandering the American West. Strip-mall schools followed, and the image of the fighting monk became part of Western culture.

That image comes from a real place. The Shaolin Temple stands on Mount Song in Henan, China. It was founded around 495 AD for an Indian Buddhist monk and became a center of Chan Buddhism, the school later known in Japan as Zen. Shaolin monks trained in fighting, and the temple's name became tied to Chinese martial arts. A later legend claims the Indian monk Bodhidharma taught the monks their first exercises, but that story is first written down more than a thousand years after he lived.

## What Comes with the Fist

The fighting skill is not the problem. What travels with it is. Many schools open and close each class by bowing — to a shrine, to a portrait of the founder, or to an image of the Buddha. Students are taught to gather and move *qi*, the "life energy" of Chinese religion, and the training is often presented as a path of inner enlightenment, not just self-defense.

## What Scripture Says

Scripture does not forbid learning to fight. David praised Yahuah, "which teacheth my hands to war, and my fingers to fight" (Psalm 144:1). The builders of Nehemiah's wall worked with one hand and held a weapon in the other (Nehemiah 4:17). Yahushua told His disciples that he who had no sword should buy one (Luke 22:36).

What Scripture forbids is mixing service to Yahuah with service at another altar. Paul put it plainly: "Ye cannot drink the cup of the Master, and the cup of devils" (1 Corinthians 10:21). Bowing in a training hall to a Buddha shrine is not a gesture of manners; it is homage at a temple altar. And the breath of life is not a force to be gathered and moved. Yahuah "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). Breath is a gift from the Maker, not an energy to master.

## The Verdict

Learning to defend one's household is lawful. The temple frame is not. A believer may learn to fight, but he refuses the shrine bow, the *qi* teaching, and the promise of enlightenment through the body. If a school will not train him without those, the school belongs to Shaolin, not to him.

The saints in Revelation triumph by a different strength: "they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony" (Revelation 12:11).
