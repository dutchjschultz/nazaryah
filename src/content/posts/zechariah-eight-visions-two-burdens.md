## The Claim

Zechariah looks like the hardest book in the minor prophets: horses in a valley at night, a flying scroll, a woman sealed in a basket, chariots coming out from between two mountains of brass. But the book is built on a plain frame, and the frame runs forward.

The front half covers two years of Zechariah’s own life, and he dates it three times: the call, the night of visions, and the fasting question. All eight visions come on that one night, the second of the three dates. The back half drops the dates, opens twice with the same heading, “The burden of the word of Yahuah” (9:1; 12:1), and runs from the Greek conquest to the King on a donkey, to the shepherd sold and pierced, to the day Yahuah is King over all the earth.

Eight visions for the men rebuilding the temple. Two burdens that carry the reader to the end. Once that is known, every chapter can be placed in its own time.

## Why It Looks Scattered

Zechariah gets quoted in pieces. “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit” (4:6) hangs on church walls. The King on a donkey (9:9) gets read once a year in the spring. The thirty pieces of silver (11:12-13) and the pierced one (12:10) get pulled into the Gospels and left there. The feet on the Mount of Olives (14:4) get drawn onto end-times charts.

One verse is lifted further out than any other. “What are these wounds in thine hands?” (13:6) is preached as a prophecy of the nail prints. Read in place, the man being asked is a false prophet who has been caught, denies he was ever a prophet, and explains his wounds away (13:2-6). The verse is about the cutting rites of false prophets, not about the Messiah.

Read front to back, the book does not scatter. It runs.

## The Five Blocks

### Block One — Zechariah 1:1-6

*The call to return. 520 BC.*

**The years.** The eighth month of the second year of Darius, king of Persia (1:1). The captives have been home from Babylon for nearly twenty years, and the temple still lies unfinished. Haggai is preaching in the same months, and Ezra names the two prophets side by side (Ezra 5:1).

**What happens.** Six verses, one message: do not be like your fathers, who heard the prophets and would not listen.

> Turn ye unto me … and I will turn unto you.
>
> — Zechariah 1:3

**How it ends.** The people answer that Yahuah did to them exactly what He said He would do (1:6). The book opens on a people who have learned the lesson of the captivity.

### Block Two — Zechariah 1:7 to 6:15

*Eight visions in one night. 519 BC.*

**The years.** The twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, the month Sebat, in the same second year of Darius (1:7). Three months after the call. Every vision in this block is seen that one night.

**What happens.** The visions come in a row, each one answering a fear of the men rebuilding:

- **[Horsemen among the myrtle trees](/blog/zechariah-vision-one-riders-among-the-myrtles)** report that the whole earth sits at rest, while Jerusalem lies unbuilt, and Yahuah says His house shall be built in it (1:8-17).
- **[Four horns and four carpenters](/blog/zechariah-vision-two-four-horns-and-four-carpenters)**: the powers that scattered Judah are themselves cut down (1:18-21).
- **[A man with a measuring line](/blog/zechariah-vision-three-a-city-without-walls)**: Jerusalem will spill past its walls, with Yahuah as a wall of fire around it, and many nations will be joined to Him (2:1-13).
- **[Joshua the high priest](/blog/zechariah-vision-four-filthy-garments-taken-away)** stands in filthy garments with the adversary at his right hand. The filthy garments are taken away, clean ones put on, and Yahuah promises to bring forth His servant “the BRANCH” (3:1-10).
- **[A golden lampstand between two olive trees](/blog/zechariah-vision-five-not-by-might-nor-by-power)**, fed with oil that no man pours (4:1-14).
- **[A flying scroll](/blog/zechariah-vision-six-the-flying-roll)**: the curse going out against the thief and the man who swears falsely by the Name (5:1-4).
- **[A woman in a basket](/blog/zechariah-vision-seven-wickedness-in-the-ephah)**, named Wickedness, carried off to the land of Shinar to have a house built for her there (5:5-11). Shinar is Babylon’s plain. Wickedness is sent back to where it came from.
- **[Four chariots](/blog/zechariah-vision-eight-a-priest-upon-his-throne)** come out from between two mountains of brass and go out to the four quarters of the earth (6:1-8).

**How it ends.** Crowns are made and set on the head of Joshua the high priest, and the word over him points forward.

> Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH … and he shall be a priest upon his throne.
>
> — Zechariah 6:12-13

A high priest named Joshua, in Hebrew *Yehoshua*, the same name the Messiah would carry, is crowned as a sign of a priest who will also be a king. The counsel of peace, Zechariah says, “shall be between them both” (6:13). Two, not three: the One who sends, and the Branch who builds.

### [Block Three — Zechariah 7 and 8](/blog/zechariah-did-ye-fast-unto-me)

*The fasting question. 518 BC.*

**The years.** The fourth day of the ninth month, Chisleu, in the fourth year of Darius (7:1). Nearly two years after the night of visions.

**The question.** Men come asking whether they should keep weeping in the fifth month, as they have done for seventy years over the burned temple (7:3). That fast was not commanded in the Law. It was a custom the captives made for themselves.

**The answer.** Yahuah does not answer about the fast at all. He goes back to what He already said: execute true judgment, show mercy, do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the stranger, or the poor (7:9-10). Their fathers made their hearts hard as stone “lest they should hear the law” (7:12), and that is why the captivity came.

**How it ends.** The fasts men made for their sorrow will be turned into “cheerful feasts” (8:19), and the nations will come looking for Yahuah. Ten men out of every language will take hold of the skirt of one man of Judah and say, “We will go with you: for we have heard that Elohim is with you” (8:23).

### [Block Four — Zechariah 9 to 11](/blog/zechariah-first-burden-the-shepherd-king)

*The first burden: the King comes, and the shepherd is sold. About 332 BC to the Messiah’s death in 31 AD.*

**The years.** No more dates. The burden opens with judgment on Hadrach, Damascus, Tyre, and the Philistine cities (9:1-8), the same coastal road Alexander the Great marched down in 332 BC. Greece is named outright: “thy sons, O Greece” (9:13). The burden runs forward from there to the King.

After Alexander, Greek kings ruled Judah, and in the 160s BC Antiochus IV set an idol in the temple until the Maccabees drove the Greeks out.

**Why the burden.** No one was attacking the returned captives. Persia was paying for the temple (Ezra 6:8). Their fear was that they had no king. The last king of David’s line had been carried to Babylon, and his grandson Zerubbabel was only a governor (Haggai 1:1). The burden answers that David’s line is not finished. The King is still coming, through Zerubbabel himself (Matthew 1:12-13), and they are to turn now, because the heart that sells the flock is the heart that would sell the King.

**The King.** In the middle of the conquests, a different kind of king arrives.

> Behold, thy King cometh unto thee … lowly, and riding upon an ass.
>
> — Zechariah 9:9

Matthew and John both quote it on the day Yahushua rode into Jerusalem (Matthew 21:5; John 12:15).

**The shepherds.** Chapter 10 says the people wandered like sheep because there was no shepherd (10:2). In chapter 11 Zechariah is made to play the shepherd of a flock marked for slaughter. When he asks for his wages, they weigh out thirty pieces of silver, the price of a slave gored by an ox in the Law (Exodus 21:32), and Yahuah calls it sarcastically “a goodly price that I was prised at of them” (11:12-13). The silver is thrown to the potter in the house of Yahuah. Matthew records the same silver, the same potter, the same field (Matthew 27:3-10).

**How it ends.** The shepherd breaks his two staffs, Beauty and Bands, the second one breaking the brotherhood between Judah and Israel (11:10-14). Then a worthless shepherd is raised up in his place (11:15-17).

The chapter opens with fire on the cedars of Lebanon (11:1), the wood the temple was built from. Forty years after the silver was weighed, in AD 70, Rome burned the temple.

### [Block Five — Zechariah 12 to 14](/blog/zechariah-second-burden-the-one-they-pierced)

*The second burden: the pierced one, across the age, to the day of Yahuah. From 31 AD to the end.*

**The years.** From 31 AD, when the shepherd is pierced and struck, to the day Yahuah is King over all the earth. The first burden ends at the cross, and this one begins there. This is the block the reader is standing in, and it runs to the end.

**The pierced one.** The nations gather against Jerusalem and break themselves on her (12:2-9). Then a spirit of favor and of prayer is poured out, and the people look on the one they pierced, and mourn for him as for an only son and a firstborn (12:10). John quotes the verse at the cross (John 19:37).

**The fountain and the struck shepherd.** A fountain is opened “for sin and for uncleanness” (13:1), idols and false prophets are cut off (13:2-6), and then the word turns back to the shepherd.

> Smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered.
>
> — Zechariah 13:7

Yahushua quoted it the night He was taken (Matthew 26:31). After the scattering, a third part is brought through the fire and refined like silver, and they call on His name (13:8-9). That refining is the life of the age between the comings.

**The city falls.** “The city shall be taken … and half of the city shall go forth into captivity” (14:2). That is AD 70, when Rome took Jerusalem and its people were “led away captive into all nations” (Luke 21:24).

**The day.** From the fall of the city, Zechariah leaps to the end, the way the prophets often do. Yahuah comes with all His holy ones, His feet stand on the Mount of Olives, and the light at evening time does not fade (14:4-7). Living waters go out from Jerusalem, half toward the eastern sea and half toward the western, summer and winter (14:8).

> And Yahuah shall be king over all the earth: in that day shall there be one Yahuah, and his name one.
>
> — Zechariah 14:9

**How it ends.** The nations go up year after year to worship the King and keep Sukkot (14:16). Even the bells on the horses read “Holiness unto Yahuah,” every pot in Jerusalem is holy, and there is no more Canaanite in the house (14:20-21).

## How the Breaks Are Marked

**The dates.** Three of them, and they climb: the eighth month of the second year (1:1), the eleventh month of the second year (1:7), the ninth month of the fourth year (7:1). Each one opens a block.

**The burdens.** The dates stop at chapter 8. Twice after that a block opens with the same heading, “The burden of the word of Yahuah” (9:1; 12:1). The first burden ends with the shepherd sold and replaced. The second ends with Yahuah King over all the earth.

**The shepherd.** He is the thread that ties the back half together: missing in 10:2, sold in 11:12, pierced in 12:10, struck in 13:7. Follow the shepherd and the order of the burdens is plain.

## Nine Runs for Fast Placement

Five blocks place a chapter roughly. Nine runs place it exactly.

**1:1-6** — Return to me, and I will return to you.

**1:7 – 2** — Horsemen in the myrtles, four horns, the measuring line.

**3 – 4** — Joshua’s clean garments, the lampstand and two olive trees.

**5 – 6** — The flying scroll, the woman in the basket, the chariots, the crown.

**7 – 8** — The fasting question, answered from the Law.

**9 – 10** — The King on a donkey, and the scattered sheep.

**11** — The shepherd priced at thirty pieces of silver.

**12 – 13** — The pierced one, the fountain, the struck shepherd.

**14** — The day of Yahuah, the living waters, Sukkot for the nations.

Run one is Block One. Runs two through four are Block Two’s single night. Each of the rest belongs to one block.

## One Audience the Whole Way Through

Zechariah speaks to Judah and Jerusalem, and he never trades them for a second people. What changes is who joins them. “Many nations shall be joined to Yahuah in that day, and shall be my people” (2:11). Ten men from every language take hold of one man of Judah (8:23). The families of the earth go up to keep the feast (14:16).

The people are not replaced, and the nations are not given a separate program. The nations come in, the house gets larger, and the feast gets more crowded.

## The Pierced One and the One Name

Zechariah 12:10 is one of the most quoted verses for the Trinity. In the Masoretic text Yahuah says, “they shall look upon me whom they have pierced,” and the argument is that Yahuah Himself was pierced, so the Son must be Yahuah.

The verse itself does not stay with “me.” In the same sentence, “they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son” (12:10). John, quoting it at the cross, writes “him whom they pierced” (John 19:37), and so does Revelation (Revelation 1:7). The pierced one is the Son for whom the mourning is made, the only son and firstborn, and the apostles read it that way. The full comparison of the texts is laid out in the Nazaryah study [*Zechariah 12:10 — They Shall Look on Him Whom They Pierced*](/edited-after-the-cross/zechariah-12-10).

The book closes the question two chapters later. When the day comes, “there shall be one Yahuah, and his name one” (14:9). Not a shared name. Not a name in three. One.

## What the Timeline Rules Out

**A war map for the evening news.** The nations that break themselves on Jerusalem in chapter 12 are the same last gathering Ezekiel calls Gog, and the same habit turns them into a forecast of modern borders. The army of 14:2 is different. It takes the city, which Gog never does (Revelation 20:9). That one came in AD 70.

**A second God at the end.** The last chapter names one King and one name (14:9).

**A law with an expiration date.** Block Three says the captivity came because the fathers refused to hear the law (7:12). Block Five ends with the nations keeping Sukkot after the day of Yahuah (14:16). The appointed times are still on the calendar on the last page.

Some take that verse, with its penalty of no rain, as proof of a thousand-year age after the return. Why the prophets describe the new world in the covenant’s own words is laid out in [*Three Verses After the End*](/blog/three-verses-after-the-end).

**A scattered book.** Zechariah is only confusing to a reader who skips the dates and the two headings.

## The Whole Book in Five Sentences

The returned captives are told to turn back to Yahuah so He will turn back to them.

In one night Zechariah sees the temple rebuilt, the high priest clothed, wickedness carried back to Babylon, and a priest crowned as a king.

Asked about a fast men invented, Yahuah answers with the Law their fathers refused.

After the Greek conquest, the King rides in on a donkey and the shepherd is sold for the price of a slave.

The pierced one is mourned, the shepherd is struck, Jerusalem falls in AD 70, a third is refined through the age, and on the last day Yahuah is King over all the earth with the nations keeping Sukkot.

## Where It Lands

Zechariah’s pictures return all through Revelation. The horses go out over the earth, the two olive trees stand beside the lampstand, the pierced one is seen, and living water flows out of the city at the end. Every one of the eight visions is traced into Revelation in [*One Night, Seen Again*](/blog/zechariah-in-revelation-one-night-seen-again).

Revelation opens on the same verse Zechariah gave the cross: every eye shall see Him, “and they also which pierced him” (Revelation 1:7). And it closes where Zechariah closed, with a city where nothing that defiles can enter (Revelation 21:27), and His name is in the foreheads of His servants (Revelation 22:4).
