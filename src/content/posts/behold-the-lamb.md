Job was told, “Behold now behemoth” (Job 40:15). Centuries later, John the Baptist told the crowd, “Behold the Lamb of God” (John 1:29). Two earlier studies showed Leviathan as the unseen king of the proud and Behemoth as the pride a man can see. This study reaches one more conclusion, and each reader is invited to check it against the Word: those two are a counterfeit of Yahuah and His Lamb. Leviathan is the unseen power behind pride, and Yahuah is the unseen King behind humility. Behemoth is pride outright, and the Lamb is humility outright.

## The Counterfeit Lamb

Revelation shows the Lamb “as it had been slain” (Revelation 5:6). Then it shows the beast with a head “as it were wounded to death,” and the whole world wonders after him (Revelation 13:3). The Greek uses the same word for both, *sphazō*, to slaughter. The beast has a death and a comeback of his own.

The second beast gives the game away. He “had two horns like a lamb, and he spake as a dragon” (Revelation 13:11). He looks like a lamb and talks like a dragon. That is the whole counterfeit in one verse. Even the marks are copied: the Lamb’s people carry His Father’s name in their foreheads (Revelation 14:1), and the beast’s people carry his mark (Revelation 13:16).

## Two Unseen Kings

Leviathan is “a king over all the children of pride” (Job 41:34), but only Yahuah is “my King of old” (Psalm 74:12). The copy goes further. The dragon gives the beast “his power, and his seat, and great authority” (Revelation 13:2). Yahushua said of His Father’s gift, “All power is given unto me” (Matthew 28:18). Yahuah gives, and His Son receives. The dragon gives, and his beast receives. The dragon wants to be Yahuah, and the beast wants to be the Son.

That is the center of the counterfeit. Pride has an unseen power behind it, and so does humility. Behind Behemoth is Leviathan. Behind the Lamb is Yahuah, “whom no man hath seen, nor can see” (1 Timothy 6:16). Yahushua said so Himself: “the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works” (John 14:10). Paul calls the Son “the image of the invisible God” (Colossians 1:15). The Lamb is the part anyone could see. Yahuah is the power no one could see.

Paul even uses the same word for both sides. There is “the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience” (Ephesians 2:2), and “it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do” (Philippians 2:13). Leviathan is king over the children of pride, and Yahuah dwells “with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit” (Isaiah 57:15). Neither pride nor humility stands alone. Pride is the serpent at work in a person. True humility is Yahuah at work in a person.

So the picture has two sides. Leviathan stands behind Behemoth. Yahuah stands behind the Lamb. The first pair is a copy of the second, and a copy can only imitate what it did not make.

## The Four Marks Turned Over

The four marks of pride found on Behemoth are each turned upside down in the Lamb.

**Height.** Behemoth’s tail stands like a cedar. The Son “humbled himself” (Philippians 2:8).

**Hardness.** Behemoth’s bones are brass and iron. The Son “became obedient unto death” and prayed, “not my will, but thine, be done” (Philippians 2:8; Luke 22:42). There was “no form nor comeliness” about Him to catch the eye (Isaiah 53:2).

**The trade.** Pride swaps Yahuah’s glory for what it can see and feed on. The Son said, “I seek not mine own glory” (John 8:50).

**Self-trust.** Pharaoh said, “I have made it for myself” (Ezekiel 29:3). The Son said, “I can of mine own self do nothing” (John 5:30).

The mute beast is turned over too. Behemoth is mute because he cannot reason. The Lamb was silent because He chose it: “he openeth not his mouth” (Isaiah 53:7). One cannot speak. The other would not.

## The Seen Lamb Beats the Unseen King

The Lamb is what Yahuah promised Abraham He would provide (Genesis 22:8). He is Yahuah’s answer to both enemies. Leviathan stays hidden in the deep, but the Lamb was lifted up where anyone could look at Him. Behemoth shows off his strength, but the Lamb showed His wounds. Open humility did what no hook or sword could do: “they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb” (Revelation 12:11).

That is where deliverance begins. Those delivered by His blood then walk behind Him: they “follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth” (Revelation 14:4) and keep “the commandments of God” (Revelation 14:12).

The proud kings will make war with the Lamb, “and the Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Master of masters, and King of kings” (Revelation 17:14). Job was told to behold the beast. John told the crowd to behold the Lamb. Scripture’s last word on the matter is one more behold: “Behold, I come quickly” (Revelation 22:12).
