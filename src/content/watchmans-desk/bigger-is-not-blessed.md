Christianity is the largest religion on earth, about 2.3 billion strong. Islam follows at 2 billion, Hinduism at 1.2 billion, Buddhism at over 300 million. Many believers point to that number with pride, as if the size of the movement proves Yahuah is behind it. Christian nationalism takes the same idea one step further: a big, powerful Christian nation, holding the reins of government, must be a blessed nation.

Scripture has never measured that way. Not once.

## Chosen for Being Small

Moses told Israel plainly that Yahuah did not choose them because they outnumbered anyone. They were "the fewest of all people" (Deuteronomy 7:7). He chose them because He loved them and kept His oath to their fathers. The nations around them were "greater and mightier" (Deuteronomy 7:1), and even the land was handed over "by little and little" (Deuteronomy 7:22).

The Torah goes further and makes it law: "Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil" (Exodus 23:2). A crowd heading in one direction proves nothing about the direction.

## David Counted His Strength

David once wanted to know how big he was. He ordered a census of Israel and Judah, not because Yahuah told him to, but to measure his own strength. When it was done his heart struck him, and he confessed, "I have sinned greatly in that I have done" (2 Samuel 24:10). Seventy thousand men died in the plague that followed.

Counting heads to measure strength was not bookkeeping. It was trust moved from Yahuah to the size of the army. That is exactly what a church does when it points to its membership numbers as evidence it is right.

## Yahuah Shrinks the Army

Gideon had 32,000 men. Yahuah said that was too many, because Israel would boast that their own hand had saved them (Judges 7:2). He cut the army to 300. Jonathan took on a Philistine garrison with one armourbearer, saying nothing stops Yahuah "to save by many or by few" (1 Samuel 14:6). Every time Scripture lines up the big and the small, Yahuah sides with the small so no one can take the credit.

## The Road Built for a Crowd

Yahushua described two roads. One is wide and spacious, and "many there be which go in thereat." It leads to destruction. The other is narrow, and "few there be that find it" (Matthew 7:13-14). He called His people a "little flock" (Luke 12:32). When crowds walked away from His teaching, He let them go and asked the twelve if they were leaving too (John 6:66-67).

A religion of 2.3 billion is, by definition, the crowd. Being in the majority is a reason to examine yourself, not a reason to feel safe.

## The Peace Prophets Are Back

Christian nationalism promises safety and greatness: elect the right people, take back the country, and peace and prosperity will follow. Jeremiah heard the same sermon in his day. The prophets told Judah there would be no sword, no famine, only "assured peace" (Jeremiah 14:13). Yahuah's answer was blunt: "I sent them not" (Jeremiah 14:14). They cried "Peace, peace; when there is no peace" (Jeremiah 6:14).

The true prophet calls people back to the commandments. The false prophet promises the nation it will be great. That has not changed in 2,600 years.

## Great Has Always Been Babylon's Word

Babel was built to make a name and keep the people together (Genesis 11:4). Yahuah scattered it. Nebuchadnezzar looked over his city and said, "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built?" (Daniel 4:30), and lost his kingdom before the words were out of his mouth.

Revelation finishes the pattern. All the world wonders after the beast (Revelation 13:3). The harlot's name is "Babylon the Great," and she sits on waters that are "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Revelation 17:5, 15). Size, power, and multitude are her marks, not the bride's. She falls "in one hour" (Revelation 18:10), and all her numbers cannot hold her up.

Opposite her stands the Lamb on mount Sion with a company described by one thing only: they "follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth" (Revelation 14:4). On that day no one will ask how many were in the pews. The only question will be whom they followed.
