## The Bedtime Story

A popular channel made for falling asleep plays a long, soft narration on the unsolved mysteries of science. A calm voice walks the listener through one hundred of them. It starts at the edge of the universe and ends inside the human heart. It is meant to be heard in bed, in the dark, with the eyes closed.

It is beautifully made. It is also a near-perfect picture of what a whole generation now believes. In one hundred chapters, the Maker of heaven and earth is never mentioned once. He is not argued with. He is not dismissed. He is simply not in the room.

Every chapter ends the same way. No one knows. It remains a mystery. The answer is hidden. One hundred times the listener is told that no one knows, and then he is told to rest.

## Every Door Opens Onto Nothing

The narration opens with the biggest question it has: why is there something instead of nothing? It admits that science has no answer. It admits that no telescope and no particle collider can ever measure it. Then it moves on.

It asks what started the universe, and says the first cause is a mystery “still hidden in the dark.”

It looks at how finely the laws of nature are set for life, and it admits the universe looks suited for life to an uncanny degree. A small change in any one of them and nothing could live. Here the narration walks right up to the obvious answer and turns away. Its way out is a multiverse: countless unseen universes, so that this one being right for life is just a lucky draw. Then it confesses that those other universes can never be observed, and that the idea “stretches the definition of science.”

It turns to the galaxies and admits that most of what holds them together cannot be seen. Dark matter and dark energy together make up about ninety-five percent of the universe in this model. No one has ever seen either one. The narration itself says the name dark energy is only a placeholder for what scientists do not know.

This is the point a reader must not miss. This generation is not being taught to reject the unseen. It is being taught to believe in a great deal of the unseen: invisible matter, invisible energy, invisible universes. The only unseen thing it is never offered is the One who made what is seen.

Scripture called this long ago. *“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse”* (Romans 1:20). The word rendered Godhead is *theiotes* (G2305), divine nature: the power and character of the one Maker on display in what He made. It is not a council of three persons, though pulpits often press the word to mean one. Two verses later Paul gives the result: *“Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools”* (Romans 1:22).

The letter to the Hebrews answers the narration’s first question in one line: *“the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear”* (Hebrews 11:3). The question science says it can never test, Scripture answered in its first verse: *“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth”* (Genesis 1:1).

## The Ending They Offer

A story is known by its ending. The narration is honest about the ending its universe offers, and it offers three. In the first, everything spreads apart until the stars burn out and the universe sits in cold darkness forever. In the second, it all falls back in on itself in a great crunch. In the third, an unseen force grows stronger until it tears apart galaxies, then stars, then planets, then the atoms themselves.

That is the whole hope of the worldview: a long dark, a crush, or a rip.

The smaller chapters say the same thing on a human scale. Civilizations rise and fall, and “nothing lasts forever.” The longest human life on record stopped at one hundred twenty-two years, and no one can say why. Aging is “a riddle written into our very cells.” Love, music, tears, memory, the mind itself: each one is wonderful, and each one is filed under unknown.

Solomon walked this same road three thousand years ago and named it. He studied everything done “under the sun” and wrote one word over all of it: vanity (Ecclesiastes 1:2–3). The Hebrew word is *hebel* (H1892), a breath, a vapor, a puff of air that is there and then gone. He added, *“For in much wisdom is much grief: and he that increaseth knowledge increaseth sorrow”* (Ecclesiastes 1:18). Knowledge with no Maker at the top of it does not end in wonder. It ends in grief.

## The Comfort Offered Is Surrender

At the close, the narration turns to comfort. It tells the listener that mystery itself is “a kind of lullaby.” It tells him there are no answers to chase, and that mystery is something to embrace rather than fear. Then it wishes him good night.

This is the enemy’s angle laid bare. The adversary does not need a generation to hate Yahuah. He only needs it to fall asleep without Him. A soft voice, a starry sky, and a hundred unanswered questions will do the work. The thief *“cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy”* (John 10:10), and the first thing he steals is hope. *“The god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not”* (2 Corinthians 4:4).

Paul warned Timothy to stay clear of *“oppositions of science falsely so called”* (1 Timothy 6:20). The warning is not against looking at the stars or measuring them. *“The heavens declare the glory of God”* (Psalm 19:1), and watching them is honest work. The warning is against knowledge that has cut itself off from its Source and still calls itself complete.

Many pulpits have not answered this generation. They have joined it. Rather than stand on Genesis 1, they took the same story and painted a hymn over it, calling the big bang the moment God spoke and the long ages His method. The generation heard the same lullaby with a church verse attached and found no reason to wake up for it. Scripture does not need to be fitted inside the narration. The narration needs to be judged by Scripture (Isaiah 8:20).

## What the Narration Could Not Explain

Several of the hundred mysteries are answered plainly in Scripture.

The narration says no one knows why living things need sleep. *“It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep”* (Psalm 127:2). Sleep is a gift from a Giver, handed each night to a creature that cannot keep itself going.

It says no one knows why time runs only one way. Yahuah set the lights in the heaven *“for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years”* (Genesis 1:14), and He is the One *“declaring the end from the beginning”* (Isaiah 46:10). Time runs forward because it is going somewhere.

It says no one knows how life rose out of lifeless matter. It did not rise. It was given. *“And Yahuah God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul”* (Genesis 2:7).

It says no one knows why the universe began so ordered and has been running down ever since. Scripture names that running down *“the bondage of corruption”* and says the creation was placed under it *“in hope”* (Romans 8:20–21). The running down is real. It is not the last word.

## What the Messiah Brings

Where the narration ends in a long night, the Messiah brings a morning.

Hebrew has a word for hope, *tikvah* (H8615). Its root picture is a cord, something stretched out and held onto. It is the very word used for the scarlet line Rahab tied in her window, the cord that marked her house for rescue when the walls of Jericho fell (Joshua 2:18, 21). Hope in Scripture is not a mood. It is a cord tied to a promise, and it holds when the walls come down.

Peter writes that Yahuah *“hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Yahushua Messiah from the dead”* (1 Peter 1:3). A living hope, not a fading one, resting on a grave that is empty. The creation that now groans *“shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God”* (Romans 8:21). The ending is not cold dark. It is *“new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness”* (2 Peter 3:13). *“Behold, I make all things new”* (Revelation 21:5).

That hope is entered through the blood of the Son, the way Yahuah Himself provided. And it is not idle. *“Every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure”* (1 John 3:3). The one who holds the cord walks in the commandments of the One who tied it: *“hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments”* (1 John 2:3).

## Good Night or Good Morning

The channel signs off with good night. Scripture signs off differently. *“Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning”* (Psalm 30:5). Paul, writing to believers surrounded by a dark empire, put it this way: *“The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light”* (Romans 13:12).

A generation is being sung to sleep with questions. The watchman’s work is not to sing along. It is to say that the night has an end, that the day breaks at dawn, and that the One who made the stars has already said how the story finishes.
