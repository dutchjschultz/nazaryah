Most days I listen to ordinary people take apart the miracle sellers and the prophets-for-pay. I am not there for the takedown. I am there for the language. The man doing the correcting tells you more about what he believes than the man he corrects ever does.

Three phrases came through this week. Each was said by someone defending something true.

<p class="wl-quote-block">Yahushua is my gift.<br>False teachers steal people's faith.<br>Nobody can steal the glory of Yahushua.</p>

Scripture never calls the Son the gift. It says the Father gave Him up. Handed Him over. Spent Him. The gift named outright is life. When the language turns to redemption it turns to money: a ransom, a purchase, a price. On the mountain the ram was not Abraham's gift. Abraham's gift was his son handed back alive, and an oath sworn over him. The ram was what it cost. Call the Son the gift and He becomes both giver and thing given, and the Father is no longer needed in the sentence.

The second is heavier than it sounds. Faith does belong to a man. Thy faith hath made thee whole. But what he owns is the leaning, not an object that can be carried off. Nothing in Scripture is ever stolen from a man except the word itself. The adversary taketh away the word out of their hearts, lest they should believe. A false teacher cannot reach in and take the leaning. He moves the wall. When Israel refused the land, Yahuah did not rebuke the ten men who brought the bad report. He rebuked the people who chose to believe them. The lean was theirs. So was the answer for it. Say a man's faith was stolen and you have excused him, and removed the One he should have leaned on.

The third is true and settles nothing. Glory in Hebrew is kavod, and kavod means weight. A man's glory is the full weight of who he is, out where people see it. Nobody can pick that up and walk off with it, not from the Son, not from anyone. Scripture never warns that glory might be stolen. It warns that glory might not be given. The crowd at Caesarea handed Herod a weight that belonged higher up. He kept it, and did not leave the platform alive. He was not struck for stealing. He was struck for keeping. Theft was never the danger. Direction is. The glory being guarded here is not His own. The Father gave it to Him, and He passes it on, and every knee and tongue send it home. Guard it at the Son and you have stopped it one step short of the Father.

Three sincere men. Three defenses of something real. And in every one the Father is quietly absent from the room. Not the one who gives. Not the wall. Not where the weight comes to rest.

Not one of those men was teaching the Trinity. They did not have to. The doctrine had already written the sentence.
