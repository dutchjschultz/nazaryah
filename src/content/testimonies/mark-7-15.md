## Where the Argument Collapses

The word food is not in this verse. It is not in the English and it is not in the Greek. The verse says nothing from without a man. The modern reading puts the word food in, then argues from the word it put in.

## The Claim

Yahushua says nothing going into a man can defile him. If that is true, the food laws are over, and it no longer matters what a man eats.

## The Verse

> There is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him: but the things which come out of him, those are they that defile the man.
> 
> — **Mark 7:15**

## What They Were Arguing About

Mark tells us the argument before Yahushua says anything.

> And when they saw some of his disciples eat bread with defiled, that is to say, with unwashen, hands, they found fault.
> 
> — **Mark 7:2**

Defiled, that is to say, unwashen. Mark tells the reader what he means by the word. He means hands. That is the same word used in verse 15.

In verse 5 the Pharisees say the charge themselves. They ask why the disciples eat bread with unwashen hands.

Bread is the only food named in the chapter. It was lawful bread. Nobody argued about it.

No unclean animal is named anywhere in the chapter. No list is quoted. Neither side asks what may be eaten.

## The Rule Came From Men

Verse 3 says where the hand-washing rule came from. The tradition of the elders.

Torah never tells a common man to wash his hands before eating bread. Priests washed before going to the altar (Exodus 30:19–21). A man washed after touching something unclean (Leviticus 15). Neither one is this rule. Men added this one.

Yahushua answers it as a man-made rule:

> Laying aside the commandment of Yahuah, ye hold the tradition of men.
> 
> — **Mark 7:8**

There are the two sides. The commandment of Yahuah on one side. The tradition of men on the other. He is defending the first and knocking down the second.

The modern reading has Him knocking down both.

## Why We Hear Food

When we hear wash your hands, we think of the kitchen. That is what the words mean today.

In Scripture, washing had little to do with meals. Feet were washed when a guest arrived at the house (Genesis 18:4). Priests washed before serving. A man washed after touching what was unclean.

We carry our meaning back into the chapter without knowing we did it. That is what makes the jump from hands to food feel natural. It is our habit, not the text.

## Nothing Does Not Mean Nothing

Take the word nothing with no limits at all and see where it goes.

If nothing that enters a man can defile him, then it does not matter what he watches. It does not matter what he listens to. Those enter from outside too.

But He said this:

> Take heed what ye hear.
> 
> — **Mark 4:24**

And He said this:

> Whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.
> 
> — **Matthew 5:28**

That came in through the eye, from outside the man, and it reached his heart. Adultery is on the list He gives at the end of this same chapter.

So nothing has limits. Nobody reads it without limits. The limits come from the argument He was answering, and Mark gave us that argument in verse 2.

## The Timing Does Not Work

This conversation happens years before the tree.

The same teaching says the Law ended at the tree, nailed to it. Both cannot be true. If the food laws ended here in Mark 7, there was nothing left to nail. If they ended at the tree, then Mark 7 was not the end of them.

There is another problem with it. He had already said this:

> Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven.
> 
> — **Matthew 5:19**

If He cancelled a command that was still standing, He fell under His own words.

## The Verdict

Mark 7 is one argument about one thing. Men made a rule that unwashed hands make a man dirty. Yahushua answered that rule.

The verse does not touch the food laws of Leviticus 11. It does not weaken them. It does not mention them.

The word food is not in the verse. No animal is named in the chapter. Nobody in the room asked the question.

A verse cannot answer a question nobody asked.
