On Monday, May 25, Americans will perform the most religious ceremony in the nation's civic calendar. A flag on every grave at Arlington. A wreath at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. Taps. A 21-gun salute. The eternal flame at JFK's grave, tended by state functionaries.

Every element is Roman. Strewing flowers on graves is the late-spring Lemuria rite, a propitiation for restless dead. The eternal flame is the Sacred Fire of Vesta — the pagan hearth of the city, extinguished by Theodosius in AD 394, revived under the Arc de Triomphe in 1923, and now burning in every Western capital. The unknown soldier occupies the place Rome reserved for the deified emperor.

The holiday is built to overwhelm reflection. This brief arrives five weeks early because once the weekend begins, the emotional pull of family gatherings and televised ceremonies makes the questions almost impossible to ask. Now is the time.
